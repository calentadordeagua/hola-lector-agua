<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 4:<br/>Approaching Summer, Premonition of a Fierce Battle</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">M</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">ID-JUNE</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> was almost here. There hadn’t been any new special exams since the one held at the end of April, and we had basically gone back to our normal lives here at school. I saw no signs of the White Room agent that was supposed to be after me making a move either. The only inconvenient thing that happened to me was Amasawa’s visit, but there was no imminent danger of expulsion. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, it did seem like the incident had left a deep impression on Kei and me, considering that even now we were still holding off on trying to kiss again. Even in situations when the mood was getting fairly good, it was like there was this invisible wall separating us. While I wanted to remove that wall and to make progress, there was probably no need to rush things. With time, Kei should be able to break past that wall on her own and naturally move on to the next step. Which might be more effective, in the sense of helping her to grow both emotionally and mentally.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Summer was approaching steadily. At the same time, I was experiencing a daily routine that was almost too fulfilling for a high school student. The temperature outside was slowly beginning to rise, just like in years past. On particularly sunny days, the temperature started reaching as high as thirty degrees. We truly were in the transition period between spring and summer.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Having spent an extended period of time living the carefree student life now, there were some topics that I heard people talk about quite often. One of these seemed trivial: which season was your favorite? However, this proved to be a surprisingly deep and interesting topic of conversation. Even people who grew up in the same place and were raised in the same way had different answers to that question.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had experienced all four seasons here at this school, and I was looking forward to the hot season coming once again. Now that I thought about it, I recognized that summer was my favorite season. Maybe that was why the blue skies looked so beautiful and dazzling during this time of year.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Good morning, Ayanokouji-senpai.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As I was walking along, looking up at the bright blue sky, I was greeted by a voice coming from in front of me. It was a student from Class 1-D, Nanase Tsubasa. She seemed to be going to class by herself, as there were no friends around her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, morning,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Considering the fact that she had been walking ahead of me, she’d probably just so happened to turn around and see me. Either that or she had some business with me and had been waiting for this.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is there something up in the sky?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The reason I didn’t notice Nanase was because I was concentrating on looking up at the blue sky. She must have been observing me for a while if she had noticed me doing that.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nothing. I was just looking at the sky,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“At the sky?” she repeated.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Nanase, now standing side-by-side with me, turned her gaze up toward the sky, like me. It was a clear blue today, without a single cloud in sight.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s really nice out, isn’t it?” said Nanase.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. And by the way, it’s been a while.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though we’d passed each other while out and about, we hadn’t actually had a conversation like this in a long time.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. We haven’t talked for about a month and a half or so now,” said Nanase.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Nanase had colluded with Housen in his plan to get me expelled from school. It would be no surprise if, like Amasawa, she’d felt it difficult to approach me under normal circumstances. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I feel like I did something really terrible to you, Ayanokouji-senpai,” said Nanase, while still looking up at the sky.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Apparently, she had more on her mind that I had imagined she would.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you resent me?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t really have a reason to. It was part of a special exam, right? So there’s nothing to talk about. Besides, I saw you try to protect me anyway.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Although Nanase had been complicit in Housen’s schemes, she eventually leapt to my defense, without regard for the danger. I remembered that she had confronted Housen, who was being quite hostile.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is that special exam over yet? I didn’t hear anything about a deadline,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, it’s still ongoing. The deadline isn’t until the start of the second semester,” said Nanase.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So from the sounds of it, the special exam would be ongoing for a while. However, if that were the case, I found the fact that Nanase and Housen had been silent for the past month and a half was somewhat curious.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Aren’t you concerned about the fact that I haven’t tried to make contact with you?” asked Nanase.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’d be lying if I said that didn’t concern me. It makes me worry that there might be some scheming going on behind the scenes or something.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Considering what happened the last time, I was convinced that even if we did devise a new plan, it wouldn’t work so easily. Besides, it’s extremely difficult for us to try and force you into a corner in the course of your daily life now that you know our objective, Ayanokouji-senpai,” said Nanase.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So you’re waiting for another special exam that’ll involve multiple grade levels, huh? What about the other students in your grade, though?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not entirely sure, but I think they do already know that Housen-kun made a move.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, now that Housen has failed, you’ve all decided that you can’t make any careless moves? I guess that’s one benefit of my injury.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t know if that’s worth your left hand, though,” said Nanase.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Of all the first-year students, Housen Kazuomi was one who really attracted attention, for better or for worse. The fact that he was the first to act might have been a stroke of good luck, in a way. The question was, who knew about this hidden special exam? It was easy for me to just ask Nanase about that, but…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I tried looking at her a few times, but she kept averting her gaze, so I gave up on that and faced forward again. Even if I tried asking Nanase that question, it wasn’t like she was going to answer. The students in the other three classes were still staying hidden so I wouldn’t know out their identities. I was sure Nanase wasn’t going to sell them out, to maintain fairness. The only thing Nanase had done was make me aware of the existence of this special exam in general, to offset any disadvantage to Class&#160;1-D.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I sincerely appreciate your understanding,” said Nanase, speaking as though she understood what I was thinking once I had remained silent for a while.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Since we were both headed to class anyway, I decided to talk about something completely unrelated.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Seems like you’ve gotten completely used to things here at this school,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">From the way she was acting, it looked like she had lost the innocence she had when she first arrived, now blending in with the rest of the students.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. I think the students in my class, myself included, are starting to get better at tolerating the special circumstances of this school. I’m not sure how much the upperclassmen know about this, but we first-years had our second special exam at the end of May,” said Nanase.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Just as we second-years had our own battles to fight, so too did the first-years have their own.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I haven’t had this information directly confirmed by anyone, but I hear that a student has been expelled,” said Nanase.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We second-year students had also heard news about someone getting expelled. One student had been expelled from the last special exam.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It seems like you are also aware of it, then. As I expected. The expulsion was one boy from Class 1-C,” said Nanase.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We’d found out because there was one student’s name missing from the list in OAA. He’d had had an A in academics, so he must have been hit with some kind of penalty. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“People are always going to talk when an expulsion occurs,” I answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Friends who were laughing together just the day before can just up and vanish at this school. It’s ruthless. What happened made me realize once more that I need to live my life here in a way that leaves me with no regrets,” said Nanase.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Although her class might consider what happened to everyone else as not their problem, they didn’t know when the time would come that they’d have to deal with something similar. It was especially important for them to have some sense of alarm, like Nanase. That being said, I didn’t know anything about the Class Points situation in the other grade levels. Meaning that I had no information at all about who won or who lost.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What kind of results did your class get in the special exams?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Unfortunately, we didn’t do so well. We came in last place the previous time and in third place this time. However, this time around, it was an exceptionally close match between us, Class A, and Class B, so the gap in Class Points is quite narrow,” said Nanase.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It seemed like Nanase felt that they were capable enough to not get left in the dust by Class A and Class B, but if anything, hold their own against them. On the other hand, Class C sank to the bottom of the rankings, largely because someone from their class had been expelled, most likely.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, is Housen behaving himself more these days? Or…?” I began, before being cut off by Nanase.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’d be lying if I said he wasn’t exhibiting any problematic behavior. However, he wasn’t involved in this most recent expulsion scandal. That’s because it seems as though he’s obsessed with you, Ayanokouji-senpai.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Nanase, who had been looking up at the sky for a long time, turned directly to me for the first time since our conversation began, looking at me with a wry smile.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Though this is definitely hindsight talking, I do think that Housen-kun has calmed down somewhat, thanks to you, Ayanokouji-senpai. I think the strong emotions he’d previously directed solely at the students in our grade are now being directed at our senpai, too. Lately, he’s been saying things like, ‘I wanna throw down with the second-years as soon as I can!’ Which makes me glad.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Well, that’s… I supposed it was certainly good news for the first-year students, anyway. The Housen and I would occasionally lock eyes when I passed his large, conspicuous form, but if I recalled correctly, the look he gave me felt something like, “Bring it on already.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sooner or later, there might come a time when we have to fight your grade,” I answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We had only worked together once so far. But if Nagumo’s policies were strongly endorsed by the school, the day we’d end up competing with each other wasn’t that far off. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I intend to live my life here at this school without regrets,” said Nanase.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s nice.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Just like Nanase had said earlier, a friend who was laughing with you one day might very well be gone the next. This was the sort of school where things like that could happen. That was why we had to cherish each and every day that went by, and not take anything for granted. Because each day that passed could never come back. They would be in the past.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ayanokouji-senpai, please try and live your life here at this school without regrets, too,” said Nanase.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She said in a way that implied my days here at this school were numbered. And there seemed to be some kind of powerful conviction in her eyes as she looked at&#160;me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course. I’ll make sure I don’t have any regrets,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After she heard my reply, Nanase responded with a nod, seemingly satisfied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well then, if you’ll please excuse me.” She bowed to me and went her own way, just as we neared the school building.</span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">4.1</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">C</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">ONSIDERING THAT THE</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> first-year students had had their second special exam at the end of May, it wouldn’t be too surprising if we, the second-year students, had one announced any day now. It was about time for us to be ready for something like that. Then, as if to evaluate our resolve, our usual morning homeroom began.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It seems like everyone is present. That’s good.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After Chabashira had finished taking attendance, she started fiddling with her tablet, most likely to project something up on the monitor. Shortly afterward, she must have finished what she was doing, because the screen went completely white. When that happened, the students looked up at it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We have been together for quite a long time now. So I’m sure you probably have some idea of what this is about,” said Chabashira.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">A new special exam was about to begin. Even though everyone clearly had something they wanted to say, they all waited to hear what Chabashira was going to announce. After a brief period of silence, she let out a little chuckle as she took in the gazes of nearly all of the students in the class.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, we will certainly be discussing a special exam. However, to keep you in suspense for a while longer, we’ll hold onto that discussion until later. First, I’m going to talk to you about summer vacation,” said Chabashira, casting her gaze down at the tablet.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">An image appeared on the monitor—first, a picture of a luxury cruise ship. Our class certainly had memories of a similar ship.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Now, I’m going to start by explaining a bit about what this summer vacation will be like,” said Chabashira.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The students looked at each other for a moment, their expressions conveying the joy they felt at the sweet-sounding words that had just come out of Chabashira’s mouth. However, the combination of the words “ship” and “vacation” ultimately dredged up some very different memories that had been deeply ingrained in us. Those memories reminded us that there was no way this school would let us indulge in such sweet fantasy. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As we were reminded of that fact, the picture on the monitor changed from an exterior view of the ship to an interior view. The itinerary was also displayed, in similar fashion.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“From August 4 through August 11, you’ll be able to enjoy your summer vacation freely and to the absolute fullest on this luxury cruise liner. That’s a total of eight days and seven nights. You can watch plays or indulge in tasty meals. And there will be nothing even remotely like a special exam held on this cruise, either,” said Chabashira.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In other words, we were being promised a true, genuine vacation for about one whole week. The students who had serious doubts felt themselves relax a bit. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, that slight easing of doubts disappeared as soon as the on-screen image faded away. It was almost like poison had been delivered right into our eyes.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“However, in order to fully enjoy this cruise, you must successfully complete the upcoming special exam,” said Chabashira.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After those few brief moments of dreamlike bliss, my classmates were swiftly yanked back to reality. The act of lifting the students’ hopes, only to drop them back down right after, would normally have resulted in great disappointment. However, the students instantly changed gears in their minds, adopting an attitude that showed that they were ready and willing to accept this upcoming battle.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It seems you really have been learning,” said Chabashira, an approving smile appearing on her lips, as if to express her admiration of us.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It wasn’t like she had mentioned the vacation part first simply out of sheer meanness or anything. She probably wanted to prove that even though we were still Class D, we were different from how we were a year ago. After all the trials and tribulations we’d endured, we’d learned to brace ourselves and keep our spirits up. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The very first person to ask a question was none other than Horikita, whose seat was directly in the middle. “When will this special exam begin?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, typically when we make these announcements, the special exam would be given that very same day or the next. Unfortunately, in this instance, it’s still a ways off. The next special exam is going to be held during the summer break,” said Chabashira.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So, the school was going to hold the special exam during summer break, after the first semester had finished? If that was the case, what concerned me was the fact that it seemed far too early for the teachers to be explaining the rules right now. What was the purpose of this announcement, if the exam was still more than a month away? </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">At any rate, whether we liked it or not, there was one particular special exam that sprang to the students’ minds when they considered what had been said so far. Then, just as everyone seemed to be thinking the exact same thing, Chabashira spoke once more and transformed those thoughts into reality.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re going to participate in an ‘Uninhabited Island Survival’ test, and will be competing against each other.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Survival on an uninhabited island for a special exam. The battle between the classes that took place during summer break during our first year had been deeply etched into our brains. Each class competed against one another using the limited number of Class Points that we were given during that exam. Also, there were additional rules such as trying to guess who the leader for each class was and getting points by occupying zones. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, we have to do that again this year…” mumbled Keisei, who must have been recalling what had happened then. He would normally have quietly listened to the entire explanation of the special exam, but had probably spoken up because our class had quite a challenging time back then, including some fierce infighting between the guys and girls. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sure you’re thinking back to the uninhabited island survival exam that you took last year. However, this year’s exam is going to be different from the previous ones. This will be more rigorous, more grueling than any other special exam. Of course, the Class Points and Private Points that you can earn in this exam will be significant, though,” said Chabashira.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We’d been free to fight any way we wanted to in the Uninhabited Island Survival battle last year. If you were determined to win, you needed to be frugal with your assets, but if you were fine with giving up on trying to win, then you were allowed to spend your time relatively freely. That was the kind of exam it was. There were no harsh measures such as expulsion, at least not as long as the students didn’t violate any important rules.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She said that this exam was going to be more rigorous and more grueling, but what changes would be implemented from last year’s exam? I didn’t need to worry about getting an answer to that question, though, because I was sure Chabashira was going to tell us right away.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Let’s start by going over the schedule. You can download it to your own devices later and look it over, so there’s no need for you to take any notes here,” explained Chabashira.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She put up the schedule for the special exam, just as she said she would, the monitor lighting up again now that something was being projected to it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">July 19: Assemble on the sports field. Depart by bus. Board the cruise ship at the harbor. Depart.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">July 20: Special exam begins. Explanation of special exam, distribution of supplies, etc.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">August 3: Special exam ends. Rankings will be announced onboard the cruise ship. Rewards will be handed out accordingly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">* Private Points for the month of August will be allotted after the results of the uninhabited island special exam are applied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">August 4: Start of free time on the cruise ship.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">August 11: Arrive at harbor. Return to campus. Dismissal. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The closing ceremony, which would mark the end of the first semester, was going to be held on Friday the sixteenth. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The most ideal solution would be to form two groups with three people in the same class, and then have them combine into a group of six later. However, Chabashira had mentioned earlier that it wasn’t going to be easy to bring groups together how we wanted once the exam began. If we didn’t get any guarantees that we would be able to form groups freely beforehand, the damage would be great if we tried something and failed. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, it was also a fact that this particular special exam had tremendous destructive power. Supposing if the top three spots were dominated by a single class, the total number of Class Points gained would be as high as 600. And supposing if our class had managed to pull that off, it would essentially be like our magic express ticket to move on up to Class B in one shot.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But, if we try and do this with only the people from our own class, I’m sure there are going to be gaps in talent that we aren’t going to be able to make up for. And besides, if we were to form a group with students from our own class, and… Well, what if the other classes work together? In the worst-case scenario, we might end up being the only class getting left behind.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It went without saying that managing to take first place in this exam only with people from our own class would be ideal, but that was it. It was nothing more than an ideal. If any one class chooses to fight alone, they create the risk of being ganged up on by the other three classes. And if that solitary class were to lose, well, there’d be no “high return” in that situation whatsoever.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It would be one thing if it were simply a concern about if we couldn’t win. But if we were to get eliminated early, then we’d run the risk of getting expelled. Meaning, if we aren’t incredibly confident that… No, as long as we don’t have a completely certainty of winning on our own, forming a group of six people, blended together with students outside our class is a prerequisite.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This special exam, where the other classes were going to be both friend and foe, was shaping up to be something truly unprecedented. If you considered that, then I suppose you’d conclude that including students right away from the beginning with the goal of unifying your objectives, would be an important strategy. However, there was no guarantee that we would be able to easily align ourselves with the other classes. Even if you understood that there’s no apparent advantage in forming groups only with students from your own class, in the end, it was normal to want to outpace the other classes if possible since there were going to be a large number of Class Points moving around. That was especially true for the lower-ranked classes. So, the starting point for us would be deciding which way to go, while operating on the premise that we’ll begin with forming a group, with all that in mind.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How are Sakayanagi-san, Ryuuen-kun, and Ichinose-san going to play this?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In order to get us to come to a decision, Horikita decided to start the conversation with our whole class with that particular question, directing it at Yousuke.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I’m sure that Class A won’t be stressing at all about trying to team up with other classes since they have a significant lead over the rest of us. No matter what group wins, it won’t exactly be a problem for them as long as the gap in Class Points doesn’t close too much. On the other hand, the rest of the classes, us included, definitely want to close that gap somehow,” replied Yousuke.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sudou, who had been listening closely to the conversation, spoke up, offering an idea that wasn’t bad.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“’Kay then. How about formin’ an alliance with us three? B, C, and D? If Class A is so far ahead of the rest o’ us, it wouldn’t be a bad thing if we were to start by workin’ together with B and C to try and close the gap, right?” said Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The idea was that we would an establish a common enemy, and we’d work together to attack Class A on all sides.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“They do say ‘the enemy of my enemy is my friend,’ after all. Trying to isolate Class A isn’t a bad idea. And there’s a good chance that Ichinose-san would go along with this proposal,” replied Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But if we suggest isolating Class A, we’re going to need to be prepared to bear some serious resentment. Considering Sakayanagi-san’s personality, I’m sure she’ll devote any and all of her resources to hunt even us, D, the lowest class, down,” said Yousuke.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Normally, you would think that someone in their position would be focused on knocking down the class that was on their tail in second place, Class B. But, just like Yousuke had said, Sakayanagi tended to persistently chase after anyone she had selected to be her prey.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We need to close the distance between ourselves and the upper classes quietly, as much as possible,” he added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If B, C, and D are going to fight together, it’s better that we’re not the ones who propose the idea,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The idea was to set up someone else to be the spokesperson for this plan, and let them suffer Sakayanagi’s, and the rest of Class A’s, ire. The difficult part wasn’t talking about it, but actually going through with it. The trouble with this special exam is that not everything could be solved through discussion from just within your own class. It didn’t matter how heated our debates got from this point onward. Nothing was going to move forward. If we didn’t understand what Class B and Class C were thinking, and if we couldn’t unify our intentions and put plans into action, then we ended up with nothing more than armchair theory.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It wasn’t going to be easy for our three classes to simply come together and hold a discussion. Ichinose wouldn’t be an issue, but it was hard to imagine that Ryuuen would be willing to agree to it so easily. Besides, if Sakayanagi got wind of this information, then she would naturally&#160;act.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Seems like we’re going to have to make a real difficult decision…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though we had a grace period of more than a month to form groups, if we went about things slowly, the other classes would be making their own moves, trying to form their own groups, one after another. We wouldn’t sit around and not take a firm stance.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’d sure be a huge help if one of the other classes proposed a similar plan themselves…,” said Yousuke.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The students of Class D were racking their brains. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Just figuring out how we should make a group is proving to be quite a headache,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In addition to the important task of forming a group, there were still other things that needed to be done too. There was the matter of the cards, which had various effects. Tomorrow morning, each and every student was going to be given one of these unique cards, which cannot be transferred between classmates. Moreover, once an item had been transferred once, its possession becomes fixed, and couldn’t be returned. Meaning the only way to get a card for sure is to simply trade with a student from another class, or by buying and selling. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It seems highly likely that everyone will start making their moves tomorrow.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. And gathering up effective cards for your group is also going to be a point that we’ll need to think about.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Today was the day when the school gave us the go ahead to start making groups for the upcoming special exam. Naturally, the situation was going to take a major turn. That included our class, D, as well.</span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">4.3</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">A</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">FTER CLASS HAD ENDED</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> the phones of students were who classified as exceptional in terms of academic ability or physical ability started ringing. Horikita approached me, while keeping an eye on the situation.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Seems like they’ve started making their move right away. I suppose it’s only natural for them to try and pull in the most talented students to their side,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Regardless of whatever policy the class you’re in had adopted, there wasn’t any harm in kicking things off with some tentative reservations.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Have you gotten any calls or texts?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“None.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. Well, the number of people who actually have your contact information is extremely limited,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You knew that, and yet you deliberately went ahead and said something meant to agitate me. Your phone seems surprisingly quiet, doesn’t it?” snapped Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita had come back at me with some harsh words of her own. I decided to look at my phone, which wasn’t ringing.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Unfortunately, my battery’s dead. I haven’t charged it for about two or three days now,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you don’t use your phone so often, you won’t need to charge it as often,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As much as I’d like to deny that and say it wasn’t the case, she wasn’t wrong. If you didn’t use your phone that much, you’d usually forget about charging it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Shouldn’t you go ahead and give warnings to our classmates? If they carelessly start forming groups on their own, it might spell trouble later,” I cautioned.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t need to explain anything to them. I’ve already given my instructions. I summed things up in a very easy-to-understand message and sent it to everyone. You just didn’t notice since your phone is dead,” said Horikita, showing me her phone’s screen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">—Do not finalize any group assignments until we’ve had the chance to discuss it within Class D. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">—If you need to finalize a group assignment as soon as possible for some reason, please contact Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It seemed like Horikita had seen something like this coming and established some minimum-level ground rules. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s not like these rules are binding, though. Ultimately, it’s up to the individual to decide, based on their discretion,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was certainly true that it was up to the individual to decide who to partner up with and to whom not. You couldn’t really meddle when it came to things like personal chemistry, and besides, expulsion was at stake here. Even if all four classes worked together, there was no ideal combination that would keep absolutely everyone safe from the threat of expulsion. With that in mind, all you could really do was offer some advice.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I always carried around a portable charger with me, so I plugged in my phone and got up from my seat. I figured there might be some students eavesdropping on conversations happening in our classroom.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Any word from Ichinose? It wouldn’t be surprising if she threw out a suggestion for our grade level to all work together,” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Haven’t gotten anything from anyone yet so far. And there haven’t been any suggestions coming from Class A or Class B, either. If our entire grade level were going to be joining together as one, we should assume that there would be an attempt to come to a mutual understanding of some kind at this current stage,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If students just went ahead and started forming groups based on their own wants all willy-nilly, it would gradually become more difficult for us to coordinate. If we weren’t going to have some kind of discussion at the outset, that was essentially establishing the reality that the classes in our grade level were going to be fighting one another. If Horikita wanted there to be some cooperation between our classes, she should be making her move now.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita didn’t show any visible signs of displeasure at the fact I got up from my seat. Instead, she got up and followed me. It seemed like she apparently still had more she wanted to say. After we got out into the hallway and she made sure that no one else was around, she started talking to me again.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Regarding this special exam… Would you be able to get first place all by yourself?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t be absurd. All we know right now is that there’s going to be a test on an uninhabited island,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I just figured that someone like you, who got a perfect score in math, wouldn’t even need a group,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">What kind of logic was that? It seemed like she just tried throwing that out there, just asking for the impossible. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“As long as we get first place, our class will see positive results. I don’t care if the first-years and third-years get second- and third-place, they can have them. I’d rather they take those places than someone else from our grade level,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Easier said than done.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, in that case, I suppose we could form groups in such a way to prevent as many expulsions as possible, and that would make things easier, but…” I replied, pausing briefly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But if we steered towards making strong groups to win, we would inevitably be creating some weak groups, as well.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But it’s not like everyone is going to be able to pay up the number of Private Points to save themselves,” I added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, that’s true. I’d like to collect as many Private Points as possible for the students who are still feeling anxious. But if the students who I borrow any points from end up getting penalized and expelled, that’ll be almost too terrible to imagine,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There was hardly anything more fruitless than trying to save others only to end up taking a fall yourself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you hate the thought of that happening, then you’ll just have to only request points from students who have extra,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That would certainly work. But in that case, the number of students we could reach out to was quite limited.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There are ways to do this without letting anyone get expelled, though I doubt anyone would want to do it,” I&#160;added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You mean the idea of having people bow out right at the start, deliberately?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Apparently, Horikita had also noticed the few holes in this exam. According to the rules, only the first five groups that are taken out are expelled. In that case, if we set up five groups specifically to be sacrificed and deliberately let them get taken out, we wouldn’t have to worry about any students after them getting expelled. However, to carry that idea out, a total of thirty million Private Points would need to be prepared. And, more importantly, whatever grade levels made up those bottom three spots in the exam would be handing over Class Points to whoever placed in the top three. And even if people from the same grade level took both the top and bottom ranks, the rewards would be decreased a little bit, so it was inevitable that by taking on the role of deliberately getting out, there were going to be some losses. I supposed that the reason the top three places were tied to the bottom three could be considered the school’s way of preventing anyone from cheating all that easily.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I guess we just need to survive on our own power somehow,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, it really does seem that way. May I consult with you again later?” asked Horikita, stopping in her tracks.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“As long as it’s something I can help with, sure.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’ll be plenty. Thank you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita seemed like she must have had something to talk about with someone else because she went back inside the classroom. After I watched Horikita go back inside, I decided to head for the exit.</span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">4.4</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">W</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HILE I WAS WALKING</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> in the hallway, making my way to the main entrance…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“’Sup!”</span></p>

He had a wide grin plastered on his face. I guessed that something good must have happened to&#160;him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey dude. I tried sendin’ you a text, but I didn’t get an answer. So I figured I’d just come over.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry. My battery ran out.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No worries, man. Hey, I’m just gonna borrow you for a sec. ’Kay?” said Ishizaki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Am I being mugged or something right now?” I asked.</span></p>

I mean, hell, is there even anyone in this school who could mug you?” he replied, responding to what he thought was a joke with one of his own. “What, do you have something you gotta do or something?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, I was just about to head back to my dorm.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, I figured. In that case, ain’t no problem, right? Let’s go,” said Ishizaki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ishizaki beckoned to me with an impassive smile on his face. He started walking on ahead, going faster and faster, inviting me to follow. If I just stood there and let him walk off, I’d probably lose sight of him in a flash. However, if we carelessly conducted a conversation here in the hallway and caused a commotion, we’d only end up attracting attention. Since I had time right now anyway, I&#160;decided to follow Ishizaki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But the minute I turned the corner, an impossibly large wall suddenly appeared right before my eyes. No, wait, that’s not what it was. It was Yamada Albert, Ishizaki’s classmate. He was wearing sunglasses. Then, with an air of intimidation about him, he placed his right hand on my shoulder.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey,” he said in English.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Hey,” I replied similarly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t really understand what was going on right now, so I figured I’d just respond to him with the same thing he said to me. But what was happening here? This mugging that was supposedly just a joke now seemed to be a little more real.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hello, Ayanokouji-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Right there, besides the gigantic Albert whom I had earlier thought was a wall, stood Hiyori.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had thought for sure that Ryuuen would show up too, but that didn’t seem like it was going to happen.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Move? Where to?” I asked.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, I’ve got a bad feeling about all this, so can you please just let me leave now?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had the feeling that this was in no way going to be a good turn of events, so I asked for their permission to make my exit.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, the hell man? You’re free, ain’tcha? We ain’t lettin’ you go.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re…not letting me go?” I repeated.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Albert, who was standing behind me, used the strength from his massive frame to hold me, binding my arms behind my back. Then Hiyori grabbed hold of my arm as well, pulling herself close. The two of them had captured me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sorry, Ayanokouji-kun. We can’t let you escape.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh…?”</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><img alt="" height="100%" id="INTERIORIMAGES_3" src="/hola-lector-agua/images/books/y2v2/OEBPS/Images/INTERIORIMAGES_3.jpg" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style=" top: 0px; vertical-align: top; height: 100%;"/></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Finally, my mugging theory was starting to look increasingly plausible.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">…Okay, that’s enough of me making that joke.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Anyway, the three of them seemed like they were planning to bring me to some other location.</span></p>
