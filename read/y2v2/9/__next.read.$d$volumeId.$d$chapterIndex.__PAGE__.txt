<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 3:<br/>Days Passing By</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">A</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">PRIL, A MONTH</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> where some head-scratching events had happened, had come to an end. It had already been two weeks since the beginning of May. As usual, the student from the White Room didn’t show any signs of making any major attempts on me yet. It seems like they had left Tsukishiro’s control, but what in the world were they thinking? At any rate, I didn’t really have any particular complaints as long as they let me spend my days in peace. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">One mid-May morning, I met with Horikita in the lobby. The significant amount of attention I had attracted due to my test results was now beginning to quiet back down. Even my classmates passing me by in the lobby didn’t give me any strange looks or anything. Of course, I’m sure that there were still many students who had their own private thoughts on the matter, but for the time being, it looked like the situation had mostly been resolved. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">While I waited for Horikita, I opened the OAA app, which had just been refreshed with new data. The OAA was a system which reflected our performance every month, and it would give us a glimpse of what the new order would be like for our second year. I had gotten a perfect score in math, but my total score across all five subjects was three-hundred-and-eighty-three points. As a result, in terms of overall performance, my academic evaluation was an A-, which was slightly higher than expected. The rest of my scores were similar to what I had in my first year.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.00em;">2-D Ayanokouji Kiyotaka</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Second Year Results</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Academic Ability: A- (81)</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Physical Ability: B- (61)</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Adaptability: D+ (40)</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Societal Contribution: B (68)</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Overall Ability: B- (62)</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Students who had gotten As in their academic ability evaluations last year, like Horikita and Mii-chan, hadn’t really changed, still keeping those As. It was likely that students who’d gotten a total score of four hundred points or higher on the test earned As or better on their evaluations. The OAA system had shown that there was noticeable improvement in every student’s results across the board, and as I had mentioned the other day, Sudou was one of the eminent examples of this. The improvement in his evaluations, even when compared to the rest of our grade, was truly remarkable.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.00em;">2-D Sudou Ken</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Second Year Results</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Academic Ability: C (54)</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Physical Ability: A+ (96)</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Adaptability: C- (42)</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Societal Contribution: C+ (60)</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Overall Ability: B- (63)</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Considering the fact that the overall ability result for his first year was a C with a total of forty-seven points, his growth was astounding. His evaluations, boosted further by his prominent physical ability, had taken a sharp turn for the better all around. Even though this was just the evaluation given in OAA, his overall ability was ranked higher than Keisei and Akito.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If he could improve in academic ability and societal contribution in the future, then he might be able to join the ranks of people like Yousuke and Kushida. You could say that was the appeal of a student with truly outstanding ability. However, even though we were told that our evaluations would be reset, regarding our adaptability and societal contribution scores, it seems that things have… </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Well, to be precise, it seemed safe for us to assume that the school had used some of last year’s data as a metric as part of their evaluation process. After all, it wasn’t as though a student’s friendships and their communication skills suddenly changed across the board just because they moved up a grade level. That being said, if Sudou continued to apply himself diligently for the next month, or the next six, his societal contribution scores should improve to be fair enough, at the very least. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In addition to Sudou, many other students had grown in terms of overall ability across the board, compared to last year. They were mostly students who were lacking in adaptability or societal contribution, or both, but it was safe to say that they had grown by leaps and bounds.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry to have kept you waiting,” said Horikita, as she came down to the lobby, arriving slightly before the time we had arranged to meet.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wasn’t really waiting long,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We didn’t need to talk about anything in the lobby and we started making our way towards school. It was easier to talk outside because things tended to progress more smoothly, regardless of the content of our discussion. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I have to say thank you, once again. Thanks to your quick-wittedness, I didn’t draw too much attention from the rest our class. I also got the impression that the story had gotten around to the other classes as well, in similar fashion,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chances were that the other classes would become more wary, but honestly, there had been hardly any impact. Sakayanagi from Class A has already known about me for a long time now, and Ryuuen has experienced a personal beatdown by my hands, so he knows that math isn’t the only thing that I’m good at. As for Ichinose, I got the feeling that she thought that I wasn’t ordinary, based on what she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It was no big deal, really. I was just doing what I thought would benefit our class in the future. If I told everything that you were just selfishly holding back of your own accord, that wouldn’t have gone over so well with everyone, now, would it? By the way, what would you have done, if I hadn’t been there, anyway?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Dunno. Who can say?” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I dodged the question, but ultimately, I would have tried to frame the story in an analogous way that Horikita had. I’d give an excuse at first to evade the topic on the day in question, saying that it was one of Horikita’s strategies. Then, at a later date, I’d mention the topic again, mentioning something similar. Horikita seemed to have figured things out though, without me having to bother with explaining it to her verbally.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, let’s just say that you owe me one then,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And I will quietly accept that I now owe you a favor, thanks,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita then shot a glance over at my left hand.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is your hand okay?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s getting there. It’s still going to take some time to fully heal, but since it’s not my dominant hand, it’s not that big a deal,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, in that case, that’s good, but… Have you had any contact with Housen-kun since then?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nah, not really. I did pass by Housen and Nanase once, but we didn’t actually talk.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Both Housen and Nanase had looked over at me, but neither one of them had tried to say a word.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even though they didn’t actually apologize or anything, I wonder if perhaps they’re aware that they did something wrong, after all,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Not sure. I didn’t really feel like they were.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Neither of them?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Entirely unfazed by the sheer audacity of pulling a stunt like that—those first-years certainly had guts.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wonder if what they said about getting twenty million points if they have you expelled is true,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We don’t have conclusive proof at the moment. But they most likely wouldn’t have done something like that without that kind of reward,” I reasoned.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was unthinkable to do something so pointless, risking both severe injury and expulsion, otherwise. The only possibility in that case would be that they were students sent from the White Room.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Whether or not it’s true will become clear in time,” I&#160;added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But that’s… That’s not a pleasant development at all. Even though it’s an entirely absurd idea, if it’s some form of special exam, all four classes will likely know about it, right?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nanase said as much too. She told us that to make us pay attention to all the classes in her grade.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That meant there were at least three people, from the three remaining classes, who knew about me. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Amasawa-san, from Class A… We owe her a debt of gratitude for partnering with Sudou-kun, but there’s no doubt she was an accomplice to Housen-kun, right?” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I responded with a subtle nod. Amasawa Ichika, a student from Class 1-A, was almost certainly one of the people who knew about the twenty-million-point special exam. We didn’t know who the remaining first-year students from Class B and Class C were, though.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So as of right now, only three people have taken action to try and get you expelled?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“As far as I can tell, yeah, seems that way.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If so, that’s a little strange, don’t you think? …Housen-kun doesn’t seem well-liked by the students in his grade level, to put it mildly. Do you think that the other students would really just sit back, twiddling their thumbs, and watch him outmaneuver them to snatch up the twenty million points?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was something that had been bothering me too. But it was difficult to narrow down the reasons why. Was it because they thought Housen and Nanase couldn’t get me expelled…? Or perhaps they never planned to participate in this special exam all along? Maybe they never believed the exam was legitimate in the first place.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita, who was walking alongside me, probably wouldn’t have an answer for any of those questions. So I thought I’d try changing directions a bit.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why do you think there’s no indication that the first-year students are sharing information?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Since that was something we were going to talk about anyway, I decided to go ahead and ask Horikita for her opinion.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes… I suppose that if their entire grade were notified that getting you expelled was a form of special exam, then it would only be a matter of time before not only the first-year students, but also the second- and third-year students, heard about it. If our class had learned about such an absurd special exam, I’m sure we would have protested strongly. That’s most likely why they are keeping us from knowing about it… Right?” reasoned Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was most definitely correct. But there was something deeper, beyond that correct answer, that was more concerning. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wonder if the school administrators really approved such an extremely absurd special exam…” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s a good question. I tried to indirectly confirm whether or not they had with our homeroom teacher, Chabashira-sensei, but there was no sign that she knew about it,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In truth, I hadn’t checked with her at all, but I was almost certain that she wasn’t informed of it. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If we go from there, there are two possibilities we can consider. The first is that what Nanase and Housen said was actually completely nonsense. In that case, there was no special exam that involved getting me expelled at all. But, as I said before, it’d hard to imagine that they would do something so risky without any reward, so we can eliminate that possibility,” I reasoned.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes,” replied Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The other is that it’s possible that this isn’t really a special exam at all. To put it in more precise terms, it’s possible that someone enticed the first-year students into this by offering to pay them twenty million points if they have me expelled.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. If someone personally put a bounty on you, then this story starts to make more sense,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">What these people were doing was questionable, but I was sure it wasn’t a violation of school rules. And I was sure that, as Horikita began to work out the situation, she would come to understand something. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita continued to process the situation, gradually nearing the truth. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, are you saying that someone in our grade or someone in a higher grade posted a generous sum of points for that purpose?” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Since Horikita had no point of reference to work from that would lead her to deduce the possibility of Tsukishiro being the perpetrator, the ideas that she could come up with were inevitably limited.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Though we can’t deny the possibility that this was some sort of game set up by some of the first-year students themselves, it’s hard to believe they could have arranged something like this when they’ve only just started school here. They have no trust or capital to work with. I’d say that the odds of that are slim,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, we’re looking at someone who had both the ability to pay out twenty million points and is trusted by the first-year students.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As Horikita worked through this scenario, a certain person likely came to her mind.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…The student council president,” she concluded.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The words that came out of her mouth fit surprisingly well into place.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Student Council President Nagumo couldn’t possibly have something to do with this. Could he?” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not so sure about that. While I’m sure he doesn’t like me at all, I’m skeptical that he’d be willing to come up with twenty million points to get me kicked out of school. And using first-year students when he doesn’t know what kind of people they are or what kind of abilities they have? That’s odd too.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If he really wanted to use someone to get me expelled, he would have been much better off using one of the third-year students, who were under his thumb.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s possible that he’s connected somehow, though,” I&#160;added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t have enough information to state with certainty that he wasn’t involved in some way. And since he had the title of student council president, the first-year students wouldn’t doubt him at all.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s possible you’ve made him jealous without even knowing it, actually. Student Council President Nagumo was fixated on my big brother. But my brother was always going on about you, Ayanokouji-kun. It wouldn’t be surprising if he had some mixed feelings about that, like I do,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If Nagumo was involved, that was likely the only connection.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, even though it’s taken a while for us to get to this point, let me get to the main thing I wanted to discuss with you. I’ll be going to the student council office after class today. I’m going to meet with Student Council President Nagumo and ask him about joining,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After many twists and turns, we were finally making progress on the matter of Nagumo, which had been Horikita’s brother Manabu’s one lasting regret.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But if President Nagumo doesn’t give me his approval, you can’t hold me responsible, okay?” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I already told you before. The president’s stance is that he won’t turn down anyone who comes to him,” I&#160;answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Yes, I suppose you did.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita had been highly emotional at the time Manabu graduated, but she did seem to remember what we had talked about. Although Nagumo had said himself that he wouldn’t turn away anyone who came to him, I was sure that wasn’t all. She was Horikita Manabu’s younger sister, who followed him everywhere. It was unthinkable that he’d ignore a person so precious.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The reason you want me to join the student council… You said that it was to monitor Student Council President Nagumo, but it’s not like you just want me to stand around and keep watch on him, right?” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She was asking for instructions on what she should do after joining the student council.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sure you’ve noticed this already, at least to some degree, but your brother and Nagumo have completely different ways of thinking. Your brother, precisely because he valued tradition, did not find Nagumo’s reforms agreeable. Before he left, he said something to me. He said that the class is a unit, and everyone has a shared destiny. And that he doesn’t want that framework to change,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s certainly true that the current student council president is trying to do the exact opposite of that,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But I’m not passing judgment on which one of them is right. What I can say for sure right now is that I’m interested in seeing the reforms Nagumo is trying to pass.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That’s right. Manabu’s way of thinking wasn’t wrong, but Nagumo’s way of thinking was by no means wrong either.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So that’s why you didn’t give me any specific instructions?” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yep.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Then why do you still want me to serve on the student council? If you wanted to watch what he’s going to do, there’s no need for me to join the council and monitor him in the first place, is there?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If Nagumo does steer things in the wrong direction, there’s going to be a need for someone to stop him then, won’t there?” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">And the person who should do that wasn’t me, but Horikita Suzune, the younger sister of Horikita Manabu. Of course, it was a rather one-sided imposition, which was exactly why I made it my condition for our competition before.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There are still some things I don’t like, but I’ll chalk this up as a net gain,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was sure it had something to do with the topic of the bounty that Horikita herself had mentioned earlier. Joining the student council would improve her chances of being able to obtain information about that.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I suppose I’m in no position to impose conditions on you when I had lost our wager, but would you please sit with me?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sit with you?” I repeated.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. I want you to come with me, so I can prove to you that I met with Student Council President Nagumo directly,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Meaning, she wanted to prove she wasn’t lying in the unlikely event that she was denied entry into the student council.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If Student Council President Nagumo is somehow involved with the matter of your bounty, then we might get some kind of reaction out of him,” added Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Indeed. We certainly might be able to get some clues regarding the twenty million points.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“All right. So, after school?” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Having made plans with Horikita, our day had begun.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s that,” I muttered to myself.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, if you don’t really have any plans or anything…do you mind if I come visit your room?”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As soon as Amasawa’s eyes met with Kei’s—Kei having only just shown her face—she gave a mischievous grin. Kei, on the other hand, was looking back at Amasawa with a sour look, like she had just swallowed a bug.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“My oh my, this won’t do. A young man and young woman, together, all alone, with the door locked?” Amasawa teased, sounding almost excited, as she took off her shoes.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s not like we can’t be alone. There are lots of people in relationships who do this sort of thing, everywhere,” argued Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, sure, I suppose that’s true. It’s just that when I look at you two, I get the feeling there’s something dirty going on,” said Amasawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I would have liked her to have shown us some evidence for saying so, but I supposed that considering the fact that we were just about to kiss earlier, I couldn’t really get upset about Amasawa’s point. As soon as Amasawa entered the living room area, she cast a watchful gaze over towards the bed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Your clothes aren’t all rumpled. And the bed doesn’t seem too messy, either. I guess that means you weren’t doing anything, after all,” said Amasawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“O-of course we weren’t! Anyway, why the heck did you barge in here all of a sudden, anyway?!” huffed Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Amasawa’s appearance had caused Kei, who had been meek and sweet until now, to get really angry. I was guessing her anger was accompanied by a hint of anxiety too. She must have heard that Amasawa might expose our relationship if we offended her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And here I thought for sure that you were engaged in some illicit affair… I mean, I thought you were having sex,” said Amasawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Amasawa had already been skirting the line of inappropriateness. Now, she went deliberately over the line, bringing up the topic of sex. And on top of that, she didn’t direct that last comment to me, but rather, to Kei. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kei choked up, at a loss for words. It was less that she was blushing, and more that she went completely beet red. She turned to me with a strained look on her face, which seemed to be saying, “What is she talking about?!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Amasawa seemed to be probing us all through this encounter. And every time she did so, she would look over to check how Kei was taking it. Having realized she wouldn’t get anything out of me, she was instead using Kei to gather information. Unable to let Kei bear this burden any longer, I spoke up.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s forbidden by the school rules,” I told Amasawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">My intention was to try and put Kei at ease after Amasawa had upset her by responding to Amasawa’s probing as calmly as possible. However, Amasawa didn’t seem to flinch in the slightest after hearing what I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“School rules? That’s just a simple formality, isn’t it? Just lines on paper? There are tons of kids all over the school who are obviously in relationships, making out with their SOs and stuff. If you go to the convenience store, you can even find contraceptives. To tell you the truth, I actually tried buying some. And you know what? The clerk just pretended not to see it. I guess in a situation where it’s like, this is banned, that’s banned, and you have young people all running wild… Well, if students ended up getting pregnant, that’d be a big problem, wouldn’t it?” said Amasawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She proceeded to reach inside the plastic bag with her left hand, took out some contraceptives, and placed them on the table. It seemed like she’d done so to prove that she actually did go out and buy some. Well, it was certainly true that if products like these weren’t available, the end result of an illicit sexual relationship would be pregnancy. I supposed that meant it was a sort of unspoken rule in this school that while having sex was ostensibly forbidden, if you were going to go ahead and do it, you needed to make you sure didn’t not get caught, and also to use protection. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kei was at a complete loss for words now. Her eyes darted back and forth between Amasawa, me, and the contraceptives on the table.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Here. Take these as a gift from me… Well, actually, I&#160;suppose they’re my way of apologizing,” said Amasawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t remember you doing anything you need to apologize for,” I answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, come on. The injury on your hand. I was involved in that, remember? I cooperated with Housen-kun,” said Amasawa, without a hint of guilt or shame in her voice.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Rather than having me make her come out and say it, she freely admitted it herself, huh?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I-Is that true?” said Kei, who couldn’t help but be taken aback by what she just heard.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I really hoped that Kei would refrain from making any unnecessary remarks right now. With just one statement of shock, she’d be handing information over to Amasawa. Amasawa would be able to judge how much I told Kei, and whether she was someone worth talking to.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ayanokouji-senpai. I think that maybe you’ve got the wrong idea about me,” said Amasawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The wrong idea?” I repeated.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not your enemy, Ayanokouji-senpai.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m guessing you’ve probably noticed how I feel already, but I can’t believe that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Really? Just because I put an idea into Housen-kun’s head?” said Amasawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If Amasawa hadn’t contacted me, things would have gone quite differently. It would’ve been difficult for anyone to lay the blame for Housen’s self-inflicted injury at my feet, and the whole incident would’ve ended with his self-destruction. Well, I was sure Housen would’ve come up with something else on his own, but regardless, there was no doubt whatsoever that Amasawa’s involvement situation was what turned his idea into a viable strategy.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Let me have a guess at what you’re thinking right now, senpai. I modified Housen-kun’s plans to have you expelled, thereby increasing the chances that you would get kicked out of school. A person like that saying that they’re not your enemy? I’m sure you’re thinking, ‘Yeah, right, don’t make me laugh.’ Am I right? You know, I think you’ve really underestimated me, Ayanokouji-senpai,” said Amasawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t remember ever underestimating you. I think I’ve given you enough credit,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Really? You sure? I don’t think so.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kei was still stunned, but she regained some of her composure after hearing what Amasawa and I were talking about.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“H-hey, wait, hold on a second. Someone was trying to have Kiyotaka expelled…? Huh? What do you mean?” asked Kei, flustered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She’d been informed about the injury to my hand, but not in detail. After seeing Kei’s flustered reaction, Amasawa smiled broadly, deeply interested.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh? Ayanokouji-senpai, you didn’t tell your girlfriend. Does that mean you haven’t told her about the twenty million, either?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wh-what is she talking about? Twenty million what?” said Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was safe to assume that Amasawa had deliberately started this conversation in order to probe my relationship with Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You can ask your boyfriend over here about the details later. Right, senpai?” said Amasawa, turning to me at the end.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Now that she’d said something like that, I was going to have to explain things to Kei afterward.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Housen-kun and I were going to use that knife to get Ayanokouji-senpai expelled. I’m sure you realized that fact when we went shopping together, right, senpai?” said Amasawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Having told me all that, she now started to work on making me change my mind about her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That was the first time I had seen kitchen utensils here at this school. But I didn’t hesitate at all when choosing the knife. And after checking with the clerk a few days later, you found out that that someone else tried to buy the same knife. Which was why you were able to make a quick judgment call and prevent Housen-kun from injuring himself… Isn’t that right?” said Amasawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The answer I had arrived at was the one that Amasawa had left traces to lead me to. But they were traces that she had left behind on purpose, choosing not to conceal them. She had assumed that I would arrive at the correct answer, and that I would prevent Housen from pulling off his scheme before he could do it. It was certainly true that if Amasawa had played her part perfectly in that situation and covered her tracks, things might have turned out differently.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re too kind,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I just thought it would be sad. You being expelled, without even knowing the reason why, all because of a bounty on your head,” said Amasawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wondered if a normal high school student would have been able to think that far ahead. I had my doubts about that.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Amasawa Ichika. Considering her thought process, if someone told me she was the White Room student, I’d find that quite believable. But if that were the case, then telling me this much was basically the same thing as telling me her identity. What was the advantage in letting me know her identity right here and now? Or was she perhaps a genius like Sakayanagi, refining her skills in a place unrelated to the White Room? In any case, Amasawa had gone up in my mental ranking of people to watch out for. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah, I’m pretty thirsty. I think I want a coffee or something,” said Amasawa, demanding a drink in a coaxing, wheedling way, as if she were wanting something.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kei had a look of blatant disgust on her face when she heard that, not even bothering to hide her displeasure at Amasawa’s attitude. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, go fix a cup of coffee for Amasawa,” I told Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? Me?!” stammered Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you don’t want to, I can do it. Then you can talk with Amasawa.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…I’ll do it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Given a choice between fixing some coffee and talking with Amasawa, Kei seemed to have weighed her options and chosen the better of the two. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sugar and milk, please!”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, and please don’t put garbage in it because you don’t like me, okay?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wouldn’t do something like that!” shouted Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Amasawa chuckled in amusement, having deliberately made a statement meant to offend. She was, without a doubt, a devilish little imp… Well, actually, we could omit the little part. She was a full-fledged devil. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kei had temporarily left our field of vision for a short while, leaving Amasawa and I alone in the living room. It was just the two of us. Amasawa glanced down at the textbooks and notebooks placed on the table.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wow, this all seems pretty contrived, doesn’t it? These study guides and stuff placed here, I mean. Talk about forced.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I guess since you were kind of biased already, you can see it that way.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Since Amasawa already had her doubts about everything Kei and I were doing right from the very beginning, it was pointless for us to try and cover anything up.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hmm, what’s this? Let’s see here. What was the convention adopted by UNESCO in 1972?” said Amasawa, reading one of the problems from a textbook.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ding, ding, ding! That’s the correct answer,” said Amasawa, giving herself a round of applause for getting it right.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, wait a second! Don’t go writing stuff in my notebook!” she shouted, warning Amasawa not to do something like that without permission.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wow, senpai, your girlfriend…seems a little hot-tempered,” said Amasawa, casually whispering into my ear.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It would be bad if Kei saw us in this position…but somehow, we managed to avoid being seen. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kei returned from the kitchen with a cup of coffee in hand, not even making an attempt to hide her displeasure. She had made sure to include sugar and milk, just as requested.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thank you oh so much, Karuizawa-sempaaaai!” replied Amasawa with a broad grin.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well then, now that I’ve given you my apology gift, I think I’ll be heading back now. Feel free to help yourself to that food,” said Amasawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Having apparently finished what she came here for, she turned her back to us, preparing to leave.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? Wait, what? You’re not even going to drink it?! You had me make it for you, though!” shouted Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I don’t particularly mind hanging out here and relaxing for a while, but is that what you want?” asked Amasawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…W-well, I mean…I’d like for you to leave,” said Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yep, that’s what I thought. Anyway, I’ll be going now!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So she’d deliberately had Kei make her coffee, just for fun. I guessed this was what it meant to know no fear. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Amasawa, standing up straight, left as quick as the wind. As soon as she had left, the room instantly became quiet again. However, the sweet atmosphere of just a little while ago was gone, leaving things feeling awfully strained and gloomy.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Kiyotaka, what was with that girl?!” shouted Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s something I’d like to know too.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Ugh! She really ticks me off!”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, explain it to me. What’s this twenty-million-point reward about? Does it have anything to do with your injury, Kiyotaka?” she asked. </span></p>
