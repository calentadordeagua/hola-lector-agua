<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 6:<br/>Invitation</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HE PRELIMINARY BATTLE</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> for the uninhabited island special exam was heating up all over the place more and more, day by day. There were now only a few days left in this preliminary period. The group-building process, which would be over in about a week’s time, had reached its climax, with more than 90 percent of the school’s students now part of groups made up of two or more people, therefore putting them into cooperative relationships where they were effectively sharing their lot with another. Students who had invited me to join them, like Ishizaki and Matsushita, eventually gave up on trying to recruit me as time went on.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was perfectly natural though, because the longer they delayed in forming their own group, the more danger they would be putting themselves in. I wondered what the remaining students, now less than 10 percent of the student body, would decide to do by next Friday. Just as I was thinking about that, I received an e-mail. It was just after nine thirty in the morning on Saturday now. The sender of the message was none other than Ishizaki, from Class 2-B.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">While I had thought to myself how I had been getting contacted by him a lot lately, I noticed that the content of his message seemed different than usual. It seemed like he wanted me to come to the café because Ryuuen was calling for me. The absence of the words “if possible” in his message seemed to suggest that this was a mandatory meeting, from the looks of it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I could refuse of course, but then I supposed that Ishizaki would get the blame. I had plans to meet up with the Ayanokouji Group today, but fortunately enough, our meeting time was set for one in the afternoon. So doing something now shouldn’t create a conflict. After I had finished getting ready and left for Keyaki Mall, fifteen minutes had already passed. Considering that it had only taken me fifteen minutes to get ready, I figured that I should be able to make it time to meet with them.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The group-building process was now in its final stages, which meant that Ryuuen, who had been silent up until this point, was about to make his move. At this current point in time, Ryuuen had still not yet formed a group with anyone. It wasn’t as though there was no chance that he might try and recruit me, but in truth, I saw that as highly unlikely. While I kept that line of thinking in mind, I was curious to see what exactly he was going to talk to me about, aside from that.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">While I was on my way over to Keyaki Mall, I ran into Kanzaki, who seemed to be on his way back from the convenience store. I saw two 2-liter bottles peeking out of a plastic bag that he was holding.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re heading over to the mall at this hour?” asked Kanzaki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s not going to be any time to take it easy once the island exam starts, after all,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had a little time, so I came to a stop so that we could chat.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It seems like your class is making a lot of progress in forming groups, but you’re still solo, huh?” he asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, unlike the other students, I don’t have a lot of friends,” I answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had thought I’d try and play things off with a joke, but Kanzaki remained stone-faced.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m guessing that you are Horikita are essentially the reserve players meant to substitute in to help the students who are struggling in Class D, right? After all, exceptional people can get results no matter what group they’re in,” said Kanzaki. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I supposed that’s the only way that Kanzaki could see it, since he had a much higher opinion of me now and had been wary of me for a while now.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m guessing that you’re taking on a similar role yourself then, Kanzaki, since you’re still alone yourself for the time being,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kanzaki, like me, hadn’t yet teamed up with anyone, and was still acting on his own in his class.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ayanokouji, Ichinose seems to place a lot of trust in you. Is it really okay for us to trust you?” he asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If I say that you can trust me, would you?” I asked in return.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I could consider it, at the very least,” he replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As the air around the plastic bottles in his bag cooled, water droplets appeared on the outside of the bottles from condensation. This midsummer heat, which easily exceeded 30 degrees Celsius, was bearing down on us without mercy.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even though our alliance has been called off, I don’t consider Ichinose an enemy,” I replied, telling Kanzaki the truth.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That statement can mean different things depending on how you interpret it. Do you think of Class C as your enemy?” he asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had thought that I could deftly fool him, but it seemed like Kanzaki’s wariness was several steps sharper than I had imagined.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Kanzaki, what is it you want me to say?” I asked in return.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He seemed different from usual now. The vibe I was getting from him now seemed to suggest that he was in a hurry to do something. If I anticipated where he was coming from and what it was that he was trying to get out of me, then I could see what he was aiming for a little bit more.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you trying to get some kind of statement out of me, and planning to make Ichinose hear it?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…You’re a lot sharper than Ichinose thinks… Well, no, you’re a sharper guy than both of us think. Far more. Since the first time we met, I had this strange feeling that there was something about you I couldn’t put my finger on. But now I can finally see it. You’re the one behind Class D’s rapid success,” said Kanzaki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Who can say?” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, I’m going to come out and say it. I want to ask you for your help. Ichinose trusts you quite a lot. Which is exactly why I want you to tell her that as she is now, she’s not good enough,” said Kanzaki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When he took a step closer to me, closing the distance between us, a drop of water dripped out of the plastic bag and onto the ground.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And you think Ichinose will change the way she thinks if I do that?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes,” he replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry, but I can’t help with that. I would like to see how Ichinose handles things.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Meaning that you want to see us, your enemies, fall into ruin?” said Kanzaki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I guess it’s not wrong for you to read into what I said that deeply, but…” I replied, trailing off.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I thought for a moment. No one knew at this point in time what fate awaited Ichinose, of course. However, when she did finally plummet to the bottom, then…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I hesitated for a moment, wondering whether I should tell Kanzaki what I was thinking, but quickly decided against it. The situation wouldn’t improve even if I did something unnecessary here, which I hadn’t taken into account. In fact, it would probably only lead to the introduction of an unnecessary foreign contaminant. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Fundamentally speaking, each person has to do things for the sake of their own class. Isn’t that right?” I told him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Yes, I suppose so. Asking you for help might have been an unfair move on my part,” said Kanzaki, seeming as though he was reflecting on what he had asked earlier.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I nodded my head in response.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I had intended to come up with an answer on my own. But I thought that if I could avoid putting that into action, and if there was an easier option available, I could have taken that easy way out instead,” said Kanzaki, before walking back to the dormitory.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I’m sure that he was panicking because he didn’t actually have any leeway in his class anymore, but they did also say that a cornered rat will bite a cat. I’m sure that Kanzaki could also stand in our way as a formidable opponent in the upcoming special exam.</span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">6.1</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;"> </span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">ARRIVED AT THE CAFÉ</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> in the mall a little ahead of the time we were supposed to meet. While I was finishing up paying for my drink, I saw two guys I’d normally never expect to see together seated by one another. One of them was Ryuuen, the very same person who had asked me to come here today. And the other was…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You mentioned one more person would be coming. So you were referring to Ayanokouji then?” said Katsuragi Kouhei, from Class 2-A.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He directed his gaze toward me with a rigid expression on his face. I wouldn’t say that Ryuuen and Katsuragi were necessarily like oil and water, but the two of them were by no means on good terms with one another.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What kind of meeting is this supposed to be, anyway?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What, you plannin’ on standin’ while we talk? Take a seat,” said Ryuuen, with an odd smirk on his face.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I did as Ryuuen had instructed, sitting down in an empty chair. There was a unique atmosphere in the air here, the likes of which I had never experienced before. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I knew there was something different about you, compared to the average student, but it seems you have even more hidden talents than I imagined, Ayanokouji. To think you would get a perfect score on the mathematics test,” marveled Katsuragi, who I hadn’t spoken to since we started our second year, immediately bringing up my math score. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ku ku. Come now, Katsuragi, don’t admire him too much. That’s all ancient history,” said Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ancient history? You sure seem relaxed even though there’s an unexpectedly powerful enemy in your midst. Are you that exhilarated by defeating Ichinose and being promoted to Class B?” said Katsuragi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Save it. Ichinose is self-destructing all on her own. She ain’t even close to bein’ on my radar,” said Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sure enough, this entirely unexpected pairing was already causing the mood to sour. Things were looking tense.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…And? I’d like to know why you called me here,” said Katsuragi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That confirmed Ryuuen was indeed the instigator of this meeting, so I waited along with Katsuragi for him to speak.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What’re ya rushin’ me for? Go on, relax a little,” said Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s no way I can relax. If people see me with you, that alone will cause me trouble,” said Katsuragi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He was concerned about being seen by people around us, so it wasn’t strange that he was urging Ryuuen to get to the point. Even though it was the morning of a day with no classes, there were sure to be more than a few students watching. If students from our grade level saw us, they wouldn’t be able to hide their shock.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What is Class A after in the next special exam?” asked Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What are they after? The same thing that everyone is, surely,” said Katsuragi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you gonna try and grab all the Class Points yourself? Or somethin’ else? That’s the question. From what I can tell from OAA, you’ve mostly been formin’ groups with Class C. But looks like Kitou’s on his own. Plus, Ichinose, Shibata, and Sakayanagi bein’ together just smells fishy to me, no matter how ya look at it. You guys workin’ together?” asked Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had been curious about that too. Aside from the group of three that Ryuuen had mentioned specifically, Hashimoto and Kamuro from Class A had formed a group with one of Class C’s best and brightest students, Ninomiya. Then, on top of that, there was the fact that the “One More” card, which Amikura had originally, was now in the hands of Hashimoto from Class A. I couldn’t imagine that was all mere coincidence.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re free to interpret the situation however you wish, but I cannot confirm anything,” said Katsuragi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I ain’t looking for any of this diplomatic back-and-forth crap. Gimme some honest answers,” said Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, I’ll give you an answer that’s easy for you to understand. I do not intend to tell you a single thing,” said Katsuragi, clearly and decisively.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though Katsuragi and Sakayanagi had opposed each other, he naturally demonstrated that there was no way he was going to share confidential information about his class with Ryuuen, an enemy.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It is only during the day on which the test begins that you will know how Sakayanagi intends to fight this battle. No one will know anything of her strategy until she says something herself. If you really must know, then you just have to ask her directly,” he added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, so you don’t know simply because she doesn’t trust ya, huh?” said Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That might be the case,” said Katsuragi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As Ryuuen himself had said, information didn’t necessarily trickle down to Katsuragi. He had mentioned earlier that Katsuragi and Sakayanagi were at opposing ends, but actually, it was more that Katsuragi was the sole person in Class A who wasn’t part of Sakayanagi’s faction. That fact was so well-known that I didn’t even need to bother asking to check. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In any case, this whole conversation so far had been nothing more than the opening act.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How the mighty have fallen, eh, Katsuragi? This time last year, you were a worthy playmate. But now you’re just a shadow of your former self. You’re the same as the rest of the garbage. Guess that’s just the end of the road for somebody whose faction lost out, huh?” said Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If I recall, you got worked over by Ishizaki once yourself</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">” Katsuragi fired back.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Come on, don’t ya wanna crawl your way back up now too? I mean, that dude who was holding you back, Totsuka, is gone now, right?” prodded Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Suddenly, Katsuragi slammed his right fist down on the table. Yahiko had idolized Katsuragi. When Ryuuen mentioned his name, Katsuragi, who had remained calm up until this point, let his anger show through.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If your goal was to make me angry, then you’ve succeeded, Ryuuen. Satisfied?” asked Katsuragi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What? Aw, still can’t get those widdle feewings out of your system? I’m kinda relieved,” said Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He clapped his hands together three times, giving Katsuragi a mocking round of applause. He then continued.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t ya think it’d be an interestin’ turn of events if we could get Sakayanagi expelled in the next special exam?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…What?” asked Katsuragi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If she’s gone, then Class A’s gonna be without a leader, obviously. If that happens, you can get to bein’ in charge,” said Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t know what it is you’re plotting, but that’s impossible. Supposing even if you managed to defeat her on the island somehow, she has ample Private Points, enough to save herself. And on top of that, if the situation called for it, she could use her Protection Point anyway,” argued Katsuragi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Getting Sakayanagi expelled would be an extremely arduous task, considering she had funds and a Protection Point.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, it’s true that if we’re gonna get her kicked out of school, we’re gonna need to hit her at least twice. Well, I’m just kiddin’ about the island exam thing, anyway. That was a joke. Survivin’ on the island’s gonna be about clawing your way up on your own power, not knockin’ down your enemies,” said Ryuuen.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I could he was starting to get closer to the main reason he’d set up this meeting, little by little. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The rewards for first through third place would give me enough points to get within striking distance of Class A, but the rules are a bit tricky. So I thought I’d do a little something to take care of that beforehand,” said Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And that’s why you called Ayanokouji and I here?” asked Katsuragi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You got it,” said Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Whatever this strategy was, Katsuragi wasn’t going to easily agree to go along with it. Though I was sure he had strong feelings about Sakayanagi, making an enemy of her would essentially mean defying Class A, which he was still a part of. Maybe it would have been a different story if this were happening at the start when Katsuragi and Sakayanagi were vying for supremacy in their class, but doing something of the sort at this juncture would only hurt him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even so, I’m pretty curious ’bout why Ichinose teamed up with her. Did Sakayanagi manage to win her over with her smooth talk? Or was teamin’ up with an incompetent loser like Ichinose the only thing Sakayanagi could even think of? Whaddya think?” asked Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I have no way of knowing, and it’s none of my business. And besides, if Sakayanagi heard what you just said, I’m sure she’d throw it right back in your face. There aren’t exactly that many people willing to work with you, after all. You’re a problem child,” said Katsuragi, responding in a manner that didn’t show antagonism towards Sakayanagi, but was rather like an ally of hers.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, in that case, I’d say that everyone here is a ‘problem child,’” said Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">All three of us were simply acting alone right now. None of us had grouped up with anyone. However, why would Ryuuen deliberately try to aggravate Katsuragi? No matter how much Ryuuen tried to stoke his feelings of hostility toward Sakayanagi, it was obvious from the way things were going that Katsuragi wasn’t going to betray her that easily. Or perhaps… Was Ryuuen doing this to confirm once and for all that Katsuragi wouldn’t sell Sakayanagi out? Just to be sure?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“All right, Katsuragi. Y’know, the way you’re so pointlessly honest and upright, it ain’t bad,” said Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You won’t get anything from trying to agitate me, Ryuuen,” said Katsuragi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">At this point, Ryuuen must have finally decided to get down to business, for he adjusted himself in his seat, sinking back into his chair.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“One of the most important things in this upcoming special exam is that we don’t get any of our Class Points snatched away by anybody else. I ain’t exactly hopin’ to let the first-years or third-years line their pockets with our points. That’d suck. So, to make sure that doesn’t happen, I figure I need to have at least some allies, right? Like a minimum number of ’em. Because you wouldn’t have enough power to win it all alone, with just what you got in your own class,” said Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He had contacted us to make a proposal at a time when everyone was almost finished with forming groups.</span></p>
