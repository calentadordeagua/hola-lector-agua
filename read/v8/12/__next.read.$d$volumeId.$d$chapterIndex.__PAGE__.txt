<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 6:<br/>Ubiquitous Things</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">S</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">UNDAY CAME AND WENT</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> in a flash, and it was now Monday, the fifth day of the test. All four hours of our morning lessons were devoted to exercise. We had to walk or run an eighteen-kilometer course that was going to be used for the long-distance relay race, and then be back for our afternoon lessons. Since it was a relay race, each person would only be running a kilometer or two. But we were in the mountains, and the way could be rocky and steep.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We walked for about five kilometers, exhausting our stamina. The other day, we’d only worked up a light sweat. The difference between then and now was incredible.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Seriously, how much higher does this slope go?! This is friggin’ nuts, dude. This is way too hard,” spat Ishizaki as we passed a sign telling us to be wary of wild boars. He turned back toward me, with a look in his eye that suggested he’d spied something unpleasant. “Speaking of wild boars, are the ones here pretty big? Like this guy?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Truly amazing. I underestimated you, Ayanokouji.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hashimoto and a bunch of other guys started complimenting me, which just made me extremely uncomfortable. Knowing they were going to use this joke to mess with me for a while was profoundly awkward. Albert even went so far to applaud. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But soon, they were out of time to tease me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though the road that led to the summit was paved to allow vehicles, the incline was extremely steep. Simply walking up it was going to strain our legs. On top of that, since we’d been getting up early to make breakfast, we were more tired than the upperclassmen. We only got a break on Sunday because the school showed some mercy.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How long is it gonna take for us to get back?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The average person’s walking speed is four kilometers per hour. It’s a distance of eighteen kilometers. If we walk the entire time, it’ll take four and a half hours.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’ve gotta be screwing with me. We ain’t gonna have any time left to eat lunch!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Then we have to run, Ishizaki. The more we run, the sooner it’ll be over,” said Class B’s Moriyama, rather bitterly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Though our whole large group had started out together, most of the second-year and third-year students were moving faster than us. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t talk crazy. No way in hell I can run eighteen kilometers.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t tire yourself out by talking for no reason. We’re all here because you agreed with my strategy, right?” warned Keisei, panting heavily as he spoke.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Students with decent stamina could have started to sprint right away, but doing that continuously for eighteen kilometers certainly wasn’t the best idea. Keisei’s strategy was that we would walk for the first nine kilometers until we reached the turnaround point and run from there. We’d mostly be going downhill by then, something that Keisei had factored into his proposal.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We ain’t even started runnin’ yet. Like hell we’ll be able to hold out until the turnaround point.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Shut up… Just be quiet and walk.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Keisei wasn’t very good with physical exertion. His legs must have been hurting him already, because his composure was starting to fray. With thirteen kilometers or so to go, we might not be able to make it back within the stipulated time. It was only natural to want to keep talking to a minimum and focus on walking. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That said, this exercise had made me begin to understand who the runners were. Yahiko and Keisei were definitely not suited to this task. Kouenji, who was lagging behind us, could probably be an asset to the group. But I doubted he’d take it seriously enough to run.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Be quiet and walk, huh? You’re actin’ all high and mighty for someone who looks like a zombie, Yukimura.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ishizaki kept at it. It didn’t seem like there’d be any cutting back on the chatter.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m saying this for the sake of the group, as the representative. Please don’t talk.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, as the representative? Screw you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Maybe because of all the stress he was under, Ishizaki just kept verbally assaulting Keisei. Moriyama and Tokitou from Class B couldn’t let that slide.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Enough, Ishizaki. Yukimura’s right.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sensing someone behind me was getting further away, I turned to find that Kouenji had veered off the byroad and gone into the forest. No one else had noticed. They were solely focused on looking ahead. Ishizaki wasn’t our only problem child. I couldn’t see Kouenji anymore and there wasn’t any sign that he was coming back; this probably wasn’t a minor detour.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, it is what it is…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I considered quietly chasing after Kouenji, but everyone might think I’d ditched too. So I spoke up.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Kouenji went off on some other path back there. I’ll go call him back.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? What in the hell is that weirdo doin’?!” With no students around who seemed capable of stopping him, Ishizaki’s voice seemed to just get louder and louder. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t let him distract you, Ishizaki. If you don’t ignore Kouenji, you’ll only be hurting yourself.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Keisei’s strategy was to treat Kouenji as if he were invisible. A sound strategy, but ignoring him completely was easier said than done. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry, Kiyotaka. Can I leave this to you?” asked Keisei apologetically.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I could tell Keisei didn’t have the energy to go back and look for Kouenji. I told him I’d do it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, this is Kouenji we’re talking about. Won’t he be kind of a handful? Want me to help?” offered Hashimoto. However, I politely declined his offer.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No matter who goes, we might not be able to bring him back. Getting as many people as possible to finish the run would look better to the school. I don’t see how I could get lost on this route, anyway.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re probably right. Come back the moment you think it’s a lost cause, though.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I nodded my assent and went in pursuit of Kouenji. I hadn’t planned on actively making a move, but I wasn’t often in a position to speak privately with Kouenji. If I was going to talk to him, this was probably my only chance.</span></p>

<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">6.1</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HE NARROW PATH</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> was just a dirt road. Despite the poor terrain, I picked up my pace. If Kouenji was on foot, I calculated I could catch up to him in a minute or two. However, he must have gone faster, because there was no sign of him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What a pain in the neck…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Going faster was one thing, but running down a dirt road was troublesome. I increased my pace, looking for any tracks Kouenji might have left behind on foot. After a hundred or so meters, I finally spotted him up ahead.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As I looked at his back, I remembered a similar situation back on the island, when Airi had been there. Kouenji had left us in the dust back then too.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Kouenji,” I called, closing the distance between us.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, ho, if it isn’t Ayanokouji Boy. This isn’t the correct route, is it?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m here to keep you from potentially getting expelled via the solidarity rule. Why did you take this detour?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I caught a glimpse of a wild boar. I dare say I was interested. So I gave chase.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That certainly wasn’t what I’d expected to hear. I refrained from asking what he planned to do if he found it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Rest easy. I shall return in time. It shouldn’t take someone like me even thirty minutes,” he added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Looked like I had no choice but to trust him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Incidentally, do you have any other business with me?” asked Kouenji. He must have sensed something was up because I hadn’t walked away. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“About the exam. I want you to help the group.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m getting so tired of hearing people say that to me. I feel like my ears will start to bleed if I have to listen to those words just once more.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t doubt Keisei and the others had been trying to persuade him when I wasn’t around. And yet, Kouenji hadn’t budged a single inch.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You don’t have to get an outstanding score. Just do what you’re supposed to.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You do not get to decide that. I do. You know that, don’t you? I will see you later,” said Kouenji, motioning that he was about to leave. But I grabbed his arm and stopped him. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He tried to take a step forward, leaving me no choice but to hold fast and dig in my heels. I expected him to resist, but for some reason Kouenji went slack.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Heh. I see. So that’s how it is then, Ayanokouji Boy,” said Kouenji, turning toward me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do you mean? What’s how what is?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The person who tamed the Dragon Boy.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Dragon…who?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m talking about that mischievous rascal, Ryuuen.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What does Ryuuen have to do with me?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re quite good at playing dumb. You don’t let your intentions slip through at all when you feign indifference.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I really don’t understand how you arrived at this conclusion.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Because of your touch on my arm. I can tell by the heat being transmitted through your skin.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I’d already figured Kouenji wasn’t normal, but apparently he was even more of an eccentric than I was. He got all that from me grabbing his arm?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry, but this is a huge misunderstanding,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Really? Judging by the way Delinquent-kun looks at you and acts around you, and the reactions of all those around you, I would think it’s a certifiable fact.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kouenji didn’t have a single shred of evidence, but he sounded overwhelmingly confident in his observations. It’d be pointless to attempt any further deception.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Heh. Relax. I have no intention of revealing what you’re keeping hidden. Even if you do happen to be exceptional in your own way, you’re still a small fry to me. One of many. So, whether I’m right or wrong, there should be no problem as long as I don’t spill the beans. Correct?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I do want to clear up this misunderstanding.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Too bad. That’s not going to happen, Ayanokouji Boy. Even if you have third parties vouch for you, assuring me that you had nothing to do with any of it, my mind is made up. I’m certain of what I know.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. Well, can we get back to the matter at hand?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re talking about me doing my part as a member of the group, yes?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Can you?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ve said this repeatedly. I refuse.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He answered clearly and decisively.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I will act in accordance with my own wishes. That is my philosophy. Will I take the exam, or won’t I? What grade will I achieve? All of those things depend on how I feel in that moment.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had considered various means of persuasion, but haphazardly trying anything out here would probably backfire. I was going to have to leave things to chance, but there was a strong possibility that doing so would cause the least harm. It was clear as day that Kouenji wanted to avoid being punished with expulsion. I had no choice but to gamble on that. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There was nothing I could do for now but watch Kouenji walk away, chasing the wild boar. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t think there’s anyone in the world who can make that guy do anything.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It didn’t matter if it was Horikita’s brother, or Nagumo, or even his own classmates. Such was my honest impression of my classmate, whom I had known for about a year&#160;now.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">6.2</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">A</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">FTER LEAVING KOUENJI,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> I returned to the course.  Even though I’d been gone for less than ten minutes, I was probably in last place now. I didn’t see any students from the group in front of or behind me, so I decided to rush a bit and catch up. A short while later, I sighted a group of first-year students—Keisei and the other guys. Tokitou noticed me first right away, and then everyone else looked over to me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, for the record, I did find him, but…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No dice, huh?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hashimoto, who predicted that would happen, wore a bitter smile on his face. The other students didn’t blame me either and just groused about Kouenji’s absence. Somehow or other, we eventually made it to the turnaround point, happily badmouthing Kouenji as we went. Chabashira was waiting for us there, arms crossed. I hadn’t seen her in a few days, but it seemed she’d been tapped pretty regularly to help out with various lessons.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The second-years and third-years have turned back. You guys are all that’s left,” she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What time is it, sensei?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s almost eleven o’clock now.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That meant we still had an hour to go until our lunch break. If this were a flat road, it wouldn’t be too hard to make it back in time. However, we had already trudged nine kilometers up an incredibly steep, winding slope. Our stamina had been depleted considerably. If we didn’t run at a solid pace, this exercise was going to take a bite out of our lunch break.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m going on ahead. I don’t want to be late for lunch.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait. We’ll be taking roll call before you head back down. Each person has to state their class and name.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">A board was brought out, probably for the purposes of recorded students who’d made it to the turnaround point. Once Ishizaki signed, he turned on his heel and ran, leaving the group behind. Looked like it would be every man for himself, group be damned. Albert followed afterward.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Let’s go, Kiyotaka.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You go on ahead. I’d like to wait and make sure Kouenji comes back.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s all well and good, but…we only have an hour left.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m fast enough. Don’t worry.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Short-distance running and long-distance running are two different things, you know. Well, I guess I’m not really one to talk,” said Keisei, letting out a self-derisive chuckle before running awkwardly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m going,” said Hashimoto.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yep.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hashimoto, the last member of our group, stretched and then ran off. Chabashira and I were the only two people left. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It doesn’t seem like you have anything you want to discuss with me,” she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m just waiting for Kouenji. If we don’t get the tail end moving, it’ll be trouble.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Trouble?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It wasn’t really a big deal. If you had stamina to spare, like Ishizaki, who was quick to take the lead and clear the race, you’d never notice the students who gave up partway through. This wasn’t a timed trial. We just had to clear the course by the set time; whether we did that in one hour or four didn’t matter. Keisei didn’t have much stamina himself, but it was obvious he was pushing himself hard to keep from holding us back.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">About twenty minutes later, Kouenji finally showed&#160;up.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This seems to be the turnaround.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He’d gotten around a bit, apparently. Traces of leaves and dirt were stuck to his jersey.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re the last one, Kouenji. You have forty minutes left.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That seems to be the case. I wish I’d taken my time&#160;a bit more, but my encounter with the wild boar ended a&#160;bit sooner than expected.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wild boar?” asked Chabashira.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She clearly had questions about that particularly strange and sudden part of what he’d just said, but Kouenji turned around and ran off.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Roll call, Kouenji. You’ll be disqualified otherwise,” said Chabashira. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“My name is Kouenji Rokusuke. Remember it well, </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">teacher</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">,” said Kouenji, without even turning around. His boisterous laughter echoed down the hill.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is that okay, sensei? He didn’t state his class.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, he gave his name. Let’s cut him some slack.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“All right. I’ll be heading off myself.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wondered how much time had passed. Coming up on the sign warning us about wild boars again, I saw two male students’ backs. One of them was Keisei, about what I’d expected. Rather than having exhausted his stamina, though, he seemed to be leaning on another student, who was propping him up. <p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">6.4</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">T WAS NOW</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> the sixth day, Tuesday. On that day, I started to hear something a little odd from the guys.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was the current topic of conversation. I got the feeling that most of them were looking forward to dinnertime. While being surrounded by guys made me feel more relaxed, it wasn’t exactly as fun. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Agh, goddamn it. I feel like I’m losin’ my mind being around guys all the time.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If I were at an all-guys’ school, I’d be dead, man.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Those were just some of the opinions I heard.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Dude, havin’ only guys around </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">stinks</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">. Like, literally.”</span></p>

Keisei was complaining about his pain. </span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Grab a breather. I’ll take your spot.” There was no helping it. I’d clean Keisei’s section for him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry, Kiyotaka.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, we help each other out when we’re in trouble.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Problem solved.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Until…</span></p>

So I have to do something, even if it’s reckless.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If the two options Keisei had just listed </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">were</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> the only options available, then acting recklessly probably would be the better choice. If you had no other paths to take, you just had to forge a new one for yourself. But Keisei didn’t seem capable of forging anything right now, let alone a whole new option.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We needed someone who was capable of both understanding the group and also taking action for the sake of others. I looked over at Hashimoto, who was quietly continuing to clean. He’d stopped Ishizaki from snapping at Kouenji on the second day, and he’d responded perfectly to the situation during the marathon practice. I had the impression he could to hold the group together firmly, at the right distance. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was unclear how much Sakayanagi and Katsuragi valued him, but I imagined he was a highly capable man—if I evaluated him as a hypothetical enemy, anyway. He was harder to read than the aggressive Sakayanagi and the conservative Katsuragi, which made him a tougher opponent.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Look, just don’t forget that I’m here. If you’re in trouble, I’ll help.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thank you, Kiyotaka. Just hearing you say that puts me at ease.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If those words helped Keisei, I was happy to say them.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">6.5</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">D</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">URING OUR NEXT CLASS,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> it was clear the situation in our group had not improved. Keisei, feeling responsible, couldn’t successfully give orders as the representative, and Ishizaki wouldn’t even talk to anyone except Albert anymore. Even during mealtime, the only period when it was possible to create some kind of peace and harmony, our group never came together. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I decided to forget about them for the time being. There was nothing I could do for this group, anyway. I&#160;could give some advice to the struggling Keisei and the belligerent Ishizaki, but I had no intention of taking any direct action to help. Getting in deeper would defeat the purpose of trying to fade into the background. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Remembering Haruka and Airi, I decided to investigate what was going on with the girls again. However, it wasn’t like I could just keep directly contacting Kei over and over again. She had responsibilities of her own, and if I kept contacting her, it might cause others to have their suspicions about the nature of our relationship.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Besides, the information I wanted wasn’t related to the first-years. I wanted information on the second- and third- years. I needed to confirm Nagumo’s true intentions toward Horikita’s brother.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This meant my number of helpful contacts was very limited indeed. I took a small risk for that reason, attempting to contact Kiriyama by leaving a clue for him to find…but Kiriyama was in Nagumo’s group. Even if he resented Nagumo, he probably couldn’t help me this&#160;time.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had to attack from a direction Nagumo didn’t expect. One person came to mind. I had Kei investigate a certain second-year girl for me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That person was named Asahina Nazuna.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She was in Class A, alongside Nagumo Miyabi, and was personally quite close with him. I’d seen Asahina eating meals with her friends in the cafeteria many times. Even now, I was monitoring her movements from afar. She wasn’t on the student council but had a relatively high level of influence within her class. She also seemed to have a lot of influence over Nagumo. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There were other guys and girls who were close to Nagumo, but I chose Asahina for two reasons. The first was that, contrary to her rough exterior and way of speaking, she had a reputation for being dutiful and conscientious. She also didn’t worship Nagumo.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The other reason was that the two of us had just so happened to bump into one another by accident. The difficulty in trying to dig up information on Nagumo came from the fact that virtually every single second-year supported him. If I clumsily attempted to make contact, I ran the risk of exposing information about myself instead. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had to narrow my possible contacts down to someone who was least likely to leak information. This made our “accidental” meeting a powerful weapon. Information that only I know. Information that only Asahina could understand. I would use what that accident had birthed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">What accident do I mean? Well, it came from a good luck charm.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Asahina had accidentally lost it a while back, and I had just so happened to pick it up. At the time, I’d returned it without thinking much of it. To my surprise, though, the item was apparently very important to her. I could tell because she had brought it with her even to this camp. She always wore it on her person, with great care. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">An accidental connection can sometimes be stronger than one created intentionally. Taking advantage of that connection, I wanted to ascertain whether or not she could be a useful source of information regarding Nagumo. The very circumstances of this camp made contacting her easy. All that remained was how to change our indirect connection into a direct one.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If I openly approached Asahina, then someone—even if it wasn’t Asahina herself—might report it back to Nagumo. I wanted to avoid that. I’d been waiting for the right time, but Asahina spent almost all of her dinner hours with other people. I couldn’t find a chance to be alone with her. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But today, that golden opportunity finally presented itself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m going to the bathroom. Be right back.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Just like that, Asahina got up, mid-meal. No other students accompanied her as she left, which was strange for a girl. I quickly followed her. I couldn’t bother her while she was in the bathroom, so I patiently waited for her to come back out. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We’d probably have five minutes to talk at most. If I took up much more of her time, she might grow annoyed. I had no idea how much I could get done within those five minutes, but I had to remind her that we’d met earlier by chance. Emphasis on </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">chance</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Soon, Asahina exited the bathroom. As usual, she was wearing her charm on her left wrist.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I pretended to be passing by.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hmm?” I muttered, quietly enough that Asahina might have thought I was just saying that to myself or that I was calling out to her. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When I said that, Asahina turned around. If I didn’t respond quickly, she would probably think I’d been talking to myself and keep on walking. I decided to act.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, sorry. I was just thinking I’d seen that charm before. Please don’t mind me,” I said, motioning as if to continue walking. If she didn’t respond, I was prepared to start a conversation myself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“They don’t sell this charm at the school anymore, though,” she replied. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But she </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">did </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">reply, so without hesitating, I kept the conversation going.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, that so? Did you happen to drop it a while back, by any chance?” I asked. She should understand what I meant right away.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait, are you…the person who found my charm?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Maybe… I picked it up on my way back to the dorm, uh…when was that, again?” I replied, pretending not to remember.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, I think I’m right. I see. So it was you,” said Asahina, smiling happily. She drew nearer. “Thank you. When I realized I’d lost it, I was so upset. Ever since then, I’ve been scared I’ll lose it again, so I’ve been wearing it more often.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She looked bashfully at her wrist.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This charm, well, I bought it after I started school. It’s not like I have any strong attachment to it in itself or anything. It’s just…how do I put it? It’s like emotional support. When I have it at hand, I just feel safer, you know? When I lose it, I get all anxious that something bad is going to happen, like it’s a bad omen or something.&#160;I was so relieved that someone found it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Well, bringing good luck </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">was </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">essentially a charm’s purpose.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I never imagined you’d be the one who found it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you know me?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, I know about you. You got a lot of attention in that relay race against Horikita-senpai. Oh, and Miyabi. Wait, you might not know his first name. Student Council President Nagumo talked to you a while back, right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait. Were you there too?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Of course, I knew the answer to that. Ichinose had also been present.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, yeah.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Asahina seemed like the person who’d put up her guard if I told her I knew she’d been there, so I pretended not to remember. Just like picking up her charm, this meeting, where we just happened to cross paths, had to be accidental too.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m pretty fast, but to be honest I don’t really have anything else going for me. Maybe I’ve caught President Nagumo’s eye through some kind of misunderstanding,” I told her, making my voice sound troubled. Asahina nodded back repeatedly, showing that she understood.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nagumo really respects Horikita-senpai. Or, like, he’s made Horikita-senpai a sort of goal. I think he was probably jealous that he didn’t get to challenge him during the relay.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I couldn’t sense any ulterior motive in Asahina’s words. For better or worse, she seemed the honest type. I decided to step things up a bit.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How can I get Nagumo-senpai to stop paying attention to me?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, how about you beat him at something? You could cut that smug Miyabi down to size. Personally, I’d love to see him lose for once,” she replied, chuckling. She was probably joking, but I pretended to take what she said at face value.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><img alt="" height="100%" id="ClassroomElite-Vol8_INTERIORIMAGES_7" src="/hola-lector-agua/images/books/v8/OEBPS/Images/ClassroomElite-Vol8_INTERIORIMAGES_7.jpg" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style=" top: 0px; vertical-align: top; height: 100%;"/></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. That might be one option,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Asahina’s eyes immediately darted back to me, an awestruck look on her face. Then a few seconds after, she burst into laughter.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah ha ha ha! Come on, I was joking. Couldn’t you tell?” she asked, laughing almost to the point of tears, lightly smacking me on the shoulder.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So if Nagumo’s ever defeated, that would be trouble, huh?” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Just in case she still thought I was joking, I decided to strengthen my tone. If Asahina was the type of person who would pull back and report to Nagumo now, then this was the end of the line. Even if she reported back to him, it’d end with her thinking that I was just some uppity first-year.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait, are you serious?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So you were joking, senpai?” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, look. This isn’t something a first-year can do anything about,” she replied, apologizing for joking around. But I continued speaking in the same tone.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Among all the second-years I’ve met until now, you seem to be the most straightforward one, Asahina-senpai.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The most straightforward?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s hard to get any information from the second-years, outside of ‘Nagumo Miyabi rules us all.’”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s some dangerous talk. I’m a second-year too, you know. Miyabi and I have a pretty deep relationship, understand?” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s not about being shallow or deep. What matters is how much you’ve been influenced by him.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Since they were in the same class, they couldn’t be enemies, not truly. No matter what Asahina privately thought of Nagumo, she wouldn’t want her class to suffer for it. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I think those are similar things, though,” she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, think of this as nonsense from a first-year.” I bowed. “Please excuse me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah, wait. Somehow, I feel like I’m the bad guy here,” she replied, releasing a deep sigh. Her smile disappeared. “I see you’re not joking around. As an apology, please let me repay you for picking up my charm. If there’s anything you’d like to know, I’ll tell you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you sure? It might be seen as rebelling against Nagumo-senpai, you know.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“To be honest, I don’t think anything drastic will happen just because I talk to you,” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She seemed to be certain that telling me something about what was going on with the second-years wouldn’t have a major impact. In other words, anything she told me would be information that didn’t matter, even if it was leaked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Out of the girls in your grade level, how many would you say are especially close with Nagumo-senpai?” I&#160;asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Girls who are close with him? Like, all of them. They trust Miyabi more than other guys, y’know.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I already knew he was no ordinary opponent, but this was an extraordinarily broad range of skills.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What about the people who act as Nagumo-senpai’s eyes and ears?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You really think I’d tell you that?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, as a senpai, it’s okay to help a first-year out a bit,&#160;right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wow, you’re cheeky,” she replied, laughing. She didn’t seem upset, though. “Well, if I do say so myself, the second-years have a really strong sense of unity. Honestly, we were more successful at dividing into groups than the first- and the third-years, you know? After they explained the test to us back on the bus, Miyabi said to immediately share that information with the other classes.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As I’d suspected—though they’d started out as enemies, the four second-year classes seemed on the way to becoming actual allies. Asahina told me the names of the four class representatives. The four classes kept in contact with one another and had even discussed the formation of their small groups with each other, to a certain degree. Apparently things had gone similarly for the girls.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What about when you met up with the first- and third-year groups? Did you decide things randomly then&#160;too?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Nagumo had proposed a draft selection system, which the first-year guys had carried out.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“For the most part, yeah.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“For the most part. So something was different?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Asahina crossed her arms, appearing deep in thought.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why do you ask?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I could tell Asahina was starting to have doubts. The silence lingered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you not going to tell me?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, I will. It’s just…when we were deciding on the large groups, some of the second-year girls made a small request. Or more like…they wanted some adjustments. At that time, the small group was made up of members that Miyabi could count on.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If the group formed based on Nagumo’s orders, then he’d likely given them a special role. You wouldn’t think that unless you knew what was going on within the second-years. From an outsider’s perspective, it would simply look like friends coming together.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are there any first- or third-years who were selected to join that girls’ large group that stand out in particular?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even if you ask me that, I hardly know anything about the first-years. From the third-years, I guess there’s Tachibana-senpai, who was Horikita-senpai’s secretary. Ah, but their representative is a different person, though. Miyabi said nothing weird would happen. He said he was going to do this fair and square.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You have a lot of faith in Nagumo-senpai, huh?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita’s brother also seemed to place a certain degree of trust in what Nagumo said. If I were to believe him and Asahina, then this line of reasoning might be considered a feint. Nagumo had promised to fight fair and square, but at the same time, he was making us jump at shadows. We feared he might be up to something behind the scenes, and he was using that to break our concentration.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“He always sticks to his word. He won’t use dirty tricks. Besides, even if that group of girls does try to lay some kind of trap, it won’t have any effect on Horikita-senpai and Miyabi’s fight.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s right. It would be completely irrelevant.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Asahina was spot-on. Nagumo had proposed that only his group and Horikita’s brother’s group compete. The girls had nothing to do with it. Even if some of the second-year girls who were close with Nagumo happened to be in Tachibana’s large group, it was irrelevant. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So was he making it look like he had something underhanded going on, while actually fighting above the board? That would mean the seemingly meaningful words he’d uttered when he met up with the third-year student from his group, Ishikura-senpai, were just a fake-out too.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I supposed that if you were investigating him normally, you’d get the sense that these clues were popping up and then disappearing all over the place. An interesting way of doing things. This was different from Sakayanagi and Ryuuen: a unique strategy.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What I can tell you, I suppose, is that whoever cares too much is the one who loses.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You were a big help,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was grateful to Asahina for humoring my unreasonable request to discuss her class’s internal affairs. Of course, she’d probably done so because she didn’t think I could be an obstacle for Miyabi. She couldn’t even imagine me being his opponent.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, do your best to try and give Miyabi a run for his money, boy,” Asahina. “I’ll be rooting for you, even if it’s only a little bit.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, just one more thing…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hmm?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If I combined what I’d just learned with the information I got from Kei, my understanding of the situation was becoming much clearer. I decided to push a little harder.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">6.6</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">O</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">N THE NIGHT</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> of the sixth day, a sour mood hung over the entire group. If we let the day end like this, the group might fall apart entirely tomorrow. These rancor between us would just continue to intensify, making it all the harder to secure a high score on the test that was now just two days away. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Returning to the room after my bath, I found the atmosphere tense as ever. Ishizaki had walled himself off and was now refusing to speak to anyone else. Keisei, blaming himself, had fallen silent and retreated inward too. The Class B students kept trying to chat in an attempt to liven things up, but the awkward atmosphere was too much for them to bear, and they eventually went silent.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Eventually, after confirming that it was getting close to lights-out time, Yahiko flipped the switch and sent the room into darkness, seeking to bring this day to a merciful end.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, Ishizaki. You got a minute?” Hashimoto broke the long silence as we sat in the pitch darkness.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No,” replied Ishizaki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Judging from the sound of rustling sheets that followed, he’d probably turned his back to Hashimoto, refusing to engage. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“At this rate, our group will be in extreme danger. We have the advantage of our team not being too big, but that also disadvantages us when it comes to the exam itself. In the worst-case scenario, Yukimura and someone else will be expelled,” said Hashimoto.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">You’re going to be the one who goes down with him when that happens, Ishizaki. You know that?</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> was the unsaid implication.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Shut it. Even if I get expelled, whatever. I don’t care. Shit happens.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Dude, come on…” Hashimoto sighed deeply, as if giving up, at this refusal of his helping hand.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I couldn’t see Hashimoto’s face in the darkness. Had we passed the point of no return when it came to our group’s unity? Maybe it was time we gave up.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I played soccer in elementary school and junior high. Our school was pretty prestigious, so our team competed in national championships each year. I wasn’t an ace player or anything, but I played in most games and did pretty well,” said Hashimoto. He didn’t direct the words to any one person in particular but addressed the room as a whole.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait, you’re not part of the soccer team now though, are you? It doesn’t look like you got injured or anything,” Yahiko pointed out, his voice cutting through the dark.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, I’m not. I know it’s not really popular these days, but I used to smoke.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So they found you out and kicked you out of soccer?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No. I hid my smoking habit well. Only my family&#160;knew.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even if smoking is disgusting, it’s not a reason to quit soccer.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yahiko’s doubts were justified. If no one had known, then it couldn’t have been an issue.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I guess I just felt this sense of alienation or something. Everyone else would be working together to win the national championships, and part of me would just kind of observe them coldly. I knew I didn’t belong. Also, I didn’t really like soccer all that much. I decided to quit playing and just focus on my studies. I was pretty smart to begin with, so it wasn’t that hard to keep getting good grades.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What, now you’re bragging? I ain’t listenin’ to this,” interjected Ishizaki nastily.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“For better or worse, my only saving grace was that I could do reasonably well out in the world, I suppose. But there are still times when I feel regret. Whenever I see Hirata and Shibata practicing out on the field, I think it could have been me out there. Even though I didn’t even like it that much. Strange, huh?” said Hashimoto. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He chuckled self-deprecatingly to himself. “What about you? What was your childhood like, Ishizaki?” asked Hashimoto.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? Why you askin’?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No reason.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hmph. Well, I ain’t got anything to say.” Ishizaki refused, to no one’s shock. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Keisei opened his mouth, the next to join our conversation in the darkness.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ever since I was little, all I ever did was study. Maybe I was influenced by my older sister. She was a lot older than me, and she wanted to be a teacher, which meant I always had to play the role of the student. My sister was pretty ridiculous. She was always giving me these stupidly difficult problems to solve, even when I was in elementary school.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So that’s why you’re so good at studying, huh?” replied Hashimoto, responding as though he were trying to draw out the conversation.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. And on top of that, I’m terrible at sports. No matter what I did, I generally came in near last place, just barely squeaking by. I decided to just focus on developing my strengths rather than conquering my weaknesses. I&#160;used to think there was no point in training your body if you weren’t planning to become a professional athlete. Enrolling in this school made me begin to doubt that for the first time…but I still believed that I could study hard and be considered an appropriate pick for Class A,” said&#160;Keisei. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He stopped speaking for a moment, as if he were remembering that time. Being put in Class D must have caused him immense despair.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But then all these things I couldn’t accept just kept happening. I don’t agree with the solidarity system, and living on the deserted island made even less sense to me… In my own class, Sudou was like the polar opposite of me. He was great at sports, but he couldn’t study. At first, I thought I’d been saddled with some ridiculous baggage. But on the uninhabited island and during the Sports Festival, Sudou’s skills were far more useful than mine. I&#160;could see him shine, right beside me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There was a hint of regret in his voice.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s still a part of me that can’t accept it, to be honest. But I’m slowly beginning to realize that if all you can do is study, or all you can do is sports, then that’s no good. That’s the thing about this exam. If you can’t do </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">both</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> of those things, then you can’t score high. Am I wrong? Ishizaki?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Seriously, are you askin’ me—?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I feel completely humiliated. Just like back on the uninhabited island and during the Sports Festival. I’m dragging the group down. I hurt myself, which has meant being a burden on others, and more importantly, I dragged down the group’s morale. I have absolutely nothing to show Ishizaki, who, despite his complaints, has been contributing to the group just as much if not more than the average.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ishizaki seemed about to mock him, but then he swallowed his swords. I couldn’t see his face. But it was precisely because we were in the darkness, unable to see each other, that we could be this vulnerable.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sorry, Ishizaki… I’m sorry that your representative, who should be setting an example for the rest of you, is in this condition.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I could tell Keisei was trying to hold back tears. None of us were insensitive enough to cut in and say something. These were bitter tears.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Stop screwing around. Why are you apologizing? I mean, I’m the one who blamed you,” said Ishizaki. He let loose a chuckle, seemingly aimed at himself, before continuing on. “I mean, you were the one who agreed to be representative when no one else was willin’.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even if we’d tried to push the role on Keisei, he could have refused it. As a matter of fact, Ishizaki had done so himself. He probably realized the good faith gesture Keisei had made by accepting the role now.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It pissed me off, gettin’ ordered around by you. But if you didn’t give those orders, our group probably would’ve been way worse off. For makin’ breakfast, and for the marathon.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No doubts there,” said Hashimoto, laughing. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Some students do well in academics, and some don’t. Some excel in sports, and some struggle. When all these different students, with their own interests and backgrounds, come together to form a single class or group, they bring their problems with them. All the friendships and rivalries and everything of the sort. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yahiko and the other students started chatting themselves, speaking up little by little. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was the first night we started to feel like a real group.</span></p>
