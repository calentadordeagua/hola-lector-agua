<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">T WAS PAST SIX</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> in the morning when lighthearted music began to reverberate throughout the room from speakers, clearly a signal for us to get out of bed. The room was still dark. I couldn’t even see sunlight through the thin curtains.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The hell? God, shut up.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ishizaki’s grumblings were the first words that we heard. Some students lay in bed even after hearing the music, but most of us slowly started to rouse, sitting up in bed, putting on our glasses, and so on.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I guess we’re getting started,” Hashimoto muttered with a sigh.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“For the time being, it’d be best if we all get up. If even one of us is absent, we’ll get hit with a deduction,” said Keisei, putting on his jersey. As long as we shared a room, collective responsibility was unavoidable.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, Kouenji’s not here.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Good morning, gentlemen. Were you about to search for me?” said Kouenji, entering the room covered with a thin layer of sweat, wearing a pleasant smile. Apparently, he’d gotten up even before we did.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Doesn’t seem like you went to the bathroom or anything.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Heh. It was such a nice morning, I went ahead and did my training.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What training? There’s no telling what we’re facing today. I can’t approve of you pointlessly exhausting your stamina,” said Keisei. Not that Kouenji was listening. On the contrary, he offered a gleeful rebuttal.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even after a full session of training, I have unimaginable reserves of stamina to spare. This is nothing, really. Besides, if you are so worried about stamina, shouldn’t you have warned the group yesterday?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I didn’t…think there’d be any kind of training happening.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, no. I’m afraid that’s not going to cut it. I remember sharing a room with you back on the cruise ship. Surely you recall that I am the sort of man who never skips out on training,” spat Kouenji, as if it were outrageous Keisei would forget such a thing.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Enough with the high-and-mighty act, Kouenji,” said Ishizaki. He wasn’t trying to defend Keisei—everyone was just fed up with Kouenji, who’d been behaving selfishly from square one. He was probably used to being a disruptive element.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We didn’t have time for this now. The thing I most wanted to avoid was being late on the first day. Someone like Hirata would have gotten the group in order, but without a clear leader…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Enough already. Promise us you’ll cooperate.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do you mean, ‘promise’? Have you sworn allegiance to this randomly selected group? I don’t see it that way.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well I don’t wanna cooperate, either,” said Ishizaki, scanning the room and unintentionally settling his gaze on me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Because of Class A? Is that the reason why you hate this?” asked Hashimoto, coming down from the top bunk to stand next to me. Ishizaki’s gaze settled on him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Tch. It ain’t just ’cause of A. It’s everyone,” said Ishizaki. He turned back to Kouenji. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You seem to be heading down the same delinquent path as Red-Hair-kun,” Kouenji said. “It’s been amusing to watch, but now that I’ve dealt with you directly, I’ve frankly had my fill. Shouldn’t you hurry along to the meeting place? Leave before your incompetence is exposed.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The fact that the only person here who grasped what was happening was Kouenji just added fuel to the fire. He was choosing his words to agitate Ishizaki, and it was working.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“All right, bring it on! Yaaaah!” shouted Ishizaki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Keisei checked the clock as Kouenji mentioned time and started panicking. “We don’t even have five minutes until assembly. Leave the fighting for later.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ain’t my problem. If we’re late, it’s his fault!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It looked like no amount of water could douse the fires of Ishizaki’s anger now. On the contrary, the flames were just growing hotter. Keisei could see what was happening, but he didn’t know how to manage their feelings in a way that would let him wrap this up. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’ve got a one-track mind. That’s probably why you got demoted to Class D,” Yahiko commented, only adding more fuel to the fire. The Class B students just watched, waiting for the situation to blow over.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This is pitiful. I don’t know whether we’ll make it with these clowns.” Hashimoto sighed. “Well, guess there’s no helping it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The way he said it made me think he was washing his hands of the matter, but then he punched the wood of his bed frame with his balled fist. Everyone except Kouenji reacted to the sound.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Just calm down, you lot. It’s fine to duke it out, but this is the worst possible time and place. Get it? If our furniture gets banged up, we’ll be held responsible. And if someone’s face gets bruised, we’ll be in trouble for it. Right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Having cut through the silence with a sound other than his voice, Hashimoto said what needed to be said. Ishizaki, who had been hollering about how our problems didn’t concern him, had to understand now that he was putting even himself in jeopardy.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, Four Eyes-kun. What’s your name again?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yukimura.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s right. It’s just like Yukimura-kun said. There’s no time. So how about you bury that anger deep, deep down, and we go to assembly? If you’re still mad after breakfast, you can decide if you want to beat each other up. That’s what being a group is about, right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You should be happy, Kouenji. You get to live a little longer.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, my, yes. I happen to be a pacifist,” said Kouenji.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Just what you’d expect from Class A, huh? I didn’t know exactly where Hashimoto fit in the class hierarchy, but he’d expertly solved this problem for us. The fire was still burning, but it was contained for now. We left the room, still cradling a bomb with a lit fuse.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The boys from all three grade levels gathered in one classroom. Approximately forty people, give or take. You could almost say it was like we made one class. The first-year students all extended brief morning greetings to the second- and third-years. Not too long afterward, the teacher entered the room.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m Onodera, the instructor in charge of third-year Class B. We’re going to take roll call now, and then you will go outside and clean your designated areas. After that, you will clean the school building. These cleaning duties will be part of your morning routine for the next week. In the event that it rains, you will be exempt from working outside but not let off work; you’ll just spend twice as much time cleaning indoors instead. As for your lessons—they won’t just be taught by school instructors. There will be individuals coming in to cover a variety of different topics. Please give them a proper welcome and behave yourselves.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">With that brief explanation, our group went off to clean.</span></p>

<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">3.1</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HE SCENT OF SOFT RUSH</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> from the tatami mats tickled my nostrils. The space before me made me feel strangely nostalgic. The teacher had escorted us to a spacious dojo, where it seemed we’d be working alongside some other groups.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Starting today, you will practice </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">zazen</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> here in the mornings and evenings.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Zazen</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">? I myself have never before engaged in such an activity,” said the Professor from the other side of the dojo. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The man in charge, hearing this comment, approached the Professor.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wh-whatever do you want with one such as I?” asked the Professor, looking up at the man. He looked unnerved by the silent, almost intimidating aura the man radiated.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The way you speak. Is that something you were born with? Or is it a hometown dialect?” asked the man.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Mayhap I would say that is not the case…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re not a time traveler from the Muromachi Period or the Edo Period, are you?” asked the man.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? No, of course, I myself have never engaged in the luxury of time travel…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. I don’t quite understand what you think you’re achieving by talking like that, but here’s some advice. Fix that ridiculous speech pattern and grow up.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wh-what?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What would someone think of you if you spoke that way to them upon your first meeting? Do you need me to elaborate?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t know why the Professor chose to speak that way, but even I could tell it was a deliberate affectation. He certainly wouldn’t be permitted to speak that way in the real world, or at the very least, in a formal setting. There were no rules against it, of course, but it went against socially accepted codes of morals and manners. You could dig in your heels and defend it as a personal quirk, but very, very few people could successfully get away with that. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“All right, listen up, and listen well. There are people out there who sling their words and actions around without consideration for others in an attempt to stand out. To show that they’re special. This is true not only for the young, but also for the elderly too,” our instructor said sternly. “You don’t need to change your personality to be a part of society. You’re free to express your individuality, of course, but you must take other people’s feelings into consideration as you venture out into the world. These lessons will help you cultivate that mentality, using techniques such as </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">zazen. </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">By bringing your words and movements to a halt, you will be unified with the people around you, merging with the group. Consider others, and finally, think. What kind of person am I? What can I do?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He deliberately directed his gaze over toward the Professor, as if to say, ‘Get it now?’</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I-I have felt fe—ack, gotta be careful.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He might not be able to get rid of his speech pattern right away, but practicing </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">zazen</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> might teach the Professor to look inward—for instance, at why he’d slipped just&#160;then.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The groups were seated individually and then given a brief explanation. This room was called the </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">zazen</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> dojo. While in here, we were to ball either our right or left hand into a fist and wrap the other hand around it. We had to keep it that way whether walking or standing. Also, we needed to keep it at the height of our solar plexus. That was a stance known as </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">shashu</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">. Depending on which sect you were in, there were different rules about how to form your hands. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We received an explanation on one more thing regarding </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">zazen</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">. Simply, it was nothing more than one form of meditation. Practicing </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">zazen</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> wasn’t about emptying your head but about forming an image. There was also something known as Ten Bulls, a series of poems and accompanying pictures that illustrated the path toward enlightenment. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was new to this </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">zazen</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> stuff, myself. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“After you’ve crossed your legs, place your feet on your thighs. Practice a lot, because sitting in the lotus position will have an effect on your exam results.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ow. Wait, for real? I can’t even get one leg up.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you are unable to do it at the start, you may instead use half-lotus position, with just one leg crossed.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The man in charge demonstrated the pose. I was able to cross my legs without any problem, so I decided to go with the lotus position. Surprisingly, it seemed a lot of students couldn’t do it…though Kouenji, who was starting to intrigue me, had his legs crossed effortlessly. He wore a faint smile, looking as though he’d already entered a state of zen. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Since his posture needed no correction, the man in charge moved past him without complaint. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“He can really do it, huh,” said Tokitou in a hushed voice. He was also able to pull off the lotus position.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“He doesn’t appear to dislike this kind of thing. That’s a relief.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Our instructor was a scary-looking guy, but this was Kouenji, after all. He might have simply refused to do the lesson. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Now that all of the students had grasped the general idea, </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">zazen</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> time began. However, since we’d spent quite a bit of time having things explained to us, our first session was limited to just five minutes.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">A</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">FTER MORNING CLEANING</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> and </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">zazen, </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">it was  around seven o’clock—breakfast time. But we were led outside instead of into the large cafeteria we’d used yesterday evening. There, we found a spacious eating area prepared for us. Several groups had already arrived.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The school will provide a meal for you today, but starting tomorrow, provided that the weather is clear, you will be making breakfast with your group. You must discuss how you wish to divide the work with your whole group.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“For real? I ain’t ever cooked a meal before,” grumbled Ishizaki. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But if that was the rule, then there was no avoiding it. Preparations for breakfast were underway while we received instructions on how to prepare food from tomorrow onward. It looked as though the breakfast menu had already been set, and handouts on how to prepare the items were being distributed. At least we’d know what to cook.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ugh, is this all there is?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The meal was simple. A Japanese breakfast, consisting of soup, rice, and three other dishes. For students with hearty appetites, this wasn’t going to be enough. While it seemed we could choose to swap in other dishes, we’d need to prepare everything ourselves.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thank goodness for that island test. Compared to that, this is luxury,” said Keisei, sounding somehow relieved, as he began eating.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If we’re going to do this fairly, let’s have each grade level take turns at cooking,” said a third-year boy who appeared to be a representative, addressing his mealtime rotation proposal to Nagumo.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. I’ve got no objections. I’d like to start with the first-years.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How about it, first-years? Any objections?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There was no way anyone </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">could</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> object. Assuming we had clear weather for the remainder of our time, we’d be making breakfast six times. The order in which we’d be cooking would be different, but that wasn’t reason enough to complain. I wouldn’t say it was unquestionably the kind of thing underclassmen should knuckle under and accept, but I was fine with it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We accept,” said Keisei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Since we’ll be cooking breakfast, what time should we wake up tomorrow?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“To make sure we have enough time, let’s wake two hours early,” proposed Keisei. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ishizaki vigorously rejected the idea. Keisei’s proposal meant he’d have to be up and ready to go by 4:00 a.m.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Still, we don’t have any choice. If we can’t prepare breakfast, it’ll be awful.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Then you guys do it. I’ll be sleeping.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ishizaki had never made such statements while under Ryuuen. But in this group, he’d already risen to the top of the hierarchy. It was interesting how his behavior had changed when his status did. Being celebrated as one of the </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">distinguished</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> few who had overthrown Ryuuen probably had something to do with it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I couldn’t really blame him for being standoffish with me, given I knew what had really happened. Also, being placed in the same group as me must have shaken him. His actions and words weren’t just hurting other people; they were hurting him too. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ishizaki and Albert weren’t cut out to be leaders or strategists. They were more suited to be third in command., the ones corralling the rest of the students. Ryuuen should have made sure they kept those roles—though, to be honest, Keisei and Yahiko were similar. They weren’t as foolhardy as Ishizaki, but they weren’t qualified to be leaders, either. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I would’ve thought Class B would take a more active role, but they’d been unusually quiet so far. Maybe they weren’t as proactive as I thought, with the exception of certain individuals like Kanzaki and Shibata. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That made Hashimoto the person most qualified to hold the group together. His prestigious Class A position, together with his ability to assess situations clearly and communicate effectively, were all key. However, he didn’t seem willing to lead.</span></p>

<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">3.3</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">A</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">FTER OUR PLAIN—</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">no, our </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">healthy</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> morning meal— the real lessons began. Our large group gathered in a classroom that was a bit more spacious than those in the Advanced Nurturing High School. I wondered if it was supposed to resemble a college classroom. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There was no assigned seating, so it was inevitable that students from the same grade level would cluster in small groups. You could sit in the corner by yourself, but that might gain you unwanted attention from other grade levels. You might even get a warning. Since the second- and third-year small groups hadn’t arrived yet, we first-years had our pick of seats.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Would it be better to sit up front?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, we should probably wait before taking out seats. Shouldn’t the seniors take their seats first, and then we grab whatever’s available?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Keisei didn’t want to run the risk of getting chewed&#160;out. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t go being selfish again, Kouenji. You might end up sitting off by yourself.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If we’re free to choose our seats, I believe I shall sit wherever I please.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Despite saying that, Kouenji showed no sign of sitting down. So he wasn’t </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">entirely </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">an agent of chaos after all. He did actually listen quietly during our normal lessons too. The man just lived by his own rules. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Seems like you first-years are struggling a bit,” said a second-year student. “Need any help?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We’re okay,” replied Keisei, lightly bowing in response. “Ugh. Why do I have to be the representative?” he muttered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After all, communicating with the second- and third-year students was the representative’s responsibility. Keisei seemed to be under a large amount of stress. If I left him hanging like this, it would only be a matter of time before he exploded.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">3.4</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">N THE AFTERNOON,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> we had PE—or rather basic physical fitness conditioning. We were told the main focus would be marathon training, and that a long-distance relay race was scheduled for the last day, undoubtedly part of the final exam. We’d practice outside for a few days, and then later on the track.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huff huff,” panted Keisei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The many tasks we’d already completed since morning had depleted our stamina, and he was struggling. I could have helped him with a knowledge-oriented problem, but when it came to physical fitness, there was nothing I could do but watch.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Surprisingly, Ishizaki and Albert had more stamina than the average student. They made it through practice easily, perhaps because, delinquents though they were, they didn’t smoke.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“All I’ve done since morning is analyze things.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">For whatever reason, I was growing tired of this. Putting aside the question of whether I intended to play an active role, I’d come to the realization that I wanted to improve our group’s performance enough to keep us safe from expulsion. If we came in last place and scored beneath the minimum threshold set by the school, then Keisei would be expelled. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The likelihood of him taking me down with him was almost infinitesimally low but not zero. If we lost, he might resent me for not offering a helping hand despite seeing him struggle. Should I provide the bare minimum of assistance necessary to save him? Or should I work to put the group on the right track? Maybe I should just observe everything and hope that the problem resolved itself? No—I quickly eliminated that last option.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kouenji’s presence would be a cause for concern too. I should probably make a move sooner rather than later.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I slowed down in order to run alongside Kouenji, who was jogging nonchalantly. Even as I approached, he didn’t even glance at me. He wouldn’t leave his own private world unless I forced him to.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, Kouenji. Can’t you go a little easier on them all?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“By them, you are referring to the group, Ayanokouji Boy?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. They’re confused. Not everyone is as incredible as you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ha ha ha, I certainly am one of a kind. However, wouldn’t it be the absolute height of stupidity to hold myself back in order to keep pace with common riffraff?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well…I don’t know if that’s right. But…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What are you trying to say?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It would be nice if the group could score reasonably well. I’d like to avoid expulsion.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If that’s what you want, then you must work hard to make it happen, hmm?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m saying this to you because I intend to work hard.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kouenji didn’t respond, leaving our pounding feet as the only sound we heard. He’d gone right back into his own little world. Guess talking to him was a wasted effort after all.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Half-assed threats or pleas were meaningless when it came to Kouenji. Reflecting on everything that had happened so far told me that much. It didn’t matter if the class joined forces to plead with him or if the teachers put their weight behind us—if he didn’t want to do something, that was that. He was an obstinate, utterly self-involved person.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">3.5</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">P</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">ERHAPS BECAUSE</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> it was our first day of classes, or because our marathon training had been so demanding, the rest of the lessons consisted solely of explanations for what to expect this week. It was, however, made clear to us that the primary goal of these lessons was to teach us socialization skills. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Of course, the first-years had no idea what that meant. The second-years, on the other hand, seemed to take it calmly in stride. The experience gap between us was impossible to ignore. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ugh.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Our final lesson for the afternoon, </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">zazen</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">, had ended. Keisei collapsed, unable to move.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you okay?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’d like to say I am, but my legs feel all numb. Please…give me a minute.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Looked like the lesson had been unexpectedly hard on Keisei. He remained stiff and motionless for about two minutes, waiting until the numbness in his legs subsided. Ishizaki hadn’t done so well with </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">zazen</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> either. He bent forward in agony.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Damn. Okay, food and bath. Yeah, bath. Give me a hand, Albert.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Albert approached Ishizaki, grabbed his arm, and pulled him up.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Gah! More gently! Let go!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Thud!</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> Ishizaki collapsed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Gaaah!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I couldn’t help but be amused by watching that interaction play out. The rest of our group, however, just found Ishizaki and those like him a pain in the neck. Keisei moved to leave, ignoring them, but I purposefully held my ground.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“They’re an amusing duo, huh?” I asked, deliberately drawing Keisei’s attention.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Kiyotaka, just leave them alone. They’re goofing around. Don’t look at them too much or you’ll draw their attention.” Keisei moved in front of me, blocking my view. “He might not be as bad as Sudou, but Ishizaki’s still the type to punch first and ask questions later. This might end up being like Ryuuen all over again.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Still, we’re in the same group. I’m sure they won’t mind a certain amount of contact, right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I pointed. Ishizaki noticed us and glared. Keisei flinched, but Ishizaki just left the dojo, dragging Albert with him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re surprisingly bold, Kiyotaka.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was because I knew what was really going on with Ishizaki and his posse. I wanted to find a way to indirectly tell Keisei not to worry too much about it. As long as he was in charge of our group, he had to maintain a certain degree of control over the students from other classes.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Keisei, we need to peel another layer off this school.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“A layer? What do you mean?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We might need to befriend Ishizaki and Albert to at least some degree.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s ridiculous. We’re in the same group, but we’re still enemies. I can’t befriend them.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Like Keisei, I’d also believed there was no way rival classes could ever get along when I first enrolled here. In fact, the school encouraged us to compete with each other. Lately, however, I’d begun to imagine another way forward.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It seems Student Council President Nagumo has been able to bring people together, irrespective of class,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s…because he’s charismatic,” said Keisei. “Or he’s just special. I don’t have that kind of talent… In fact, that’s not something anyone outside of Class A could even do, right? We don’t know what Nagumo-senpai’s planning, or if his methods will work all the way to graduation. But no matter how much he unites the second-years, the ones who graduate from Class A will have the last laugh. The rest will be left in tears.” With that, Keisei left the dojo.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">3.6</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">A</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">FTER DINNER,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> I decided to head back to the room  before the others. There were a few people gathered in the hallway, both guys and girls, making me think something was wrong.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry, sorry. Are you okay?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. No need for concern.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yamauchi, a member of my class, reached his hand out apologetically. It looked like he’d knocked over Sakayanagi Arisu from first-year Class A. She didn’t take Yamauchi’s hand but instead tried to get up herself.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><img alt="" height="100%" id="ClassroomElite-Vol8_INTERIORIMAGES_3" src="/hola-lector-agua/images/books/v8/OEBPS/Images/ClassroomElite-Vol8_INTERIORIMAGES_3.jpg" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style=" top: 0px; vertical-align: top; height: 100%;"/></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She couldn’t do it unaided, though, so she grabbed her cane, which lay on the ground. Then, leaning against the wall, she slowly got back up. It didn’t take her long, but with everyone staring at her, it probably felt like an interminably long time to Sakayanagi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yamauchi awkwardly retracted his hand.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, uh. Guess I’ll be going?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. Don’t mind me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakayanagi smiled lightly and looked away from Yamauchi. Everyone began to disperse, looking relieved the issue hadn’t escalated.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sakayanagi-chan sure is cute, but she’s also clumsy,” muttered Yamauchi. Apparently, it hadn’t even occurred to him she might’ve fallen because </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">he </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">bumped into her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Somehow, Sakayanagi’s gaze had found mine. “Are you okay?” I asked her as I approached.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thank you ever so much for your concern. However, it’s nothing major.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll talk with Yamauchi later.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, he didn’t do it on purpose. I just fell, that’s all,” said Sakayanagi with a chuckle. But her eyes weren’t laughing. “Well, then. Please excuse me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kamuro was usually by her side, but wasn’t here. She’d probably been placed in a different group. I had no way of knowing how things were going with the girls, and I didn’t care, either. However, as Sakayanagi started to leave, she stopped and glanced back at me. Did she feel me staring?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I remembered something I wanted to discuss with you, Ayanokouji-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She tapped her cane once, a thin smile on her lips.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Class B is quite unified. Ichinose Honami-san has earned the trust of her comrades by giving it her all. However, what if they’re trusting her too much?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That has nothing to do with me,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But Sakayanagi continued, not caring what I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There was a rumor going around about her. She’s said to possess a tremendous number of points, despite her lack of significant achievements—enough points to even warrant an investigation by the school, I hear. It shouldn’t be possible for a single student to earn so many points alone. She’s likely the treasurer for Class B. Don’t you think?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Who knows? Only Ichinose or her classmates could answer that question. Why tell me this?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m saying, is it wise to entrust her with all of those private points? For example, if she suddenly needed a large number of points to protect herself after making some mistake, or to save a classmate, no one would blame her for it. Perhaps she’s acting as treasurer for that purpose.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Probably, yeah.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“However, if she squandered a large sum of points purely to serve her own goals, the school might investigate on the basis of fraud.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In any case, this wasn’t about me. This applied to no one else but Ichinose and the students of B Class. If Ichinose really was acting as their treasurer, then the students who had deposited their points with her would be the ones who had the right to complain about it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I can’t imagine Ichinose using private points for selfish reasons, though,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I suppose that’s true. At the very least, no one doubts her. Yet.” In other words, she was implying that might change. “I do so look forward to finishing this special exam and going back to school.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Looking satisfied, Sakayanagi turned and walked away without looking back.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">3.7</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HERE WAS ABOUT </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">one hour left until ten o’clock, which was lights-out time. None of us really had anything to talk about, so we sat around in our shared room in silence. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Breaking the ice was proving to be surprisingly difficult. Even if you made an effort to initiate conversation with someone from another class, it could easily feel forced. Ideally, someone would bring up a topic we could all chime in on, but seemed like it might be too much to hope for.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Trying to force conversation would be off-putting. If only someone would strike up a conversation.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">A knock came at the door. Apparently we had a visitor.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Who could that be?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Everyone looked perplexed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Maybe it’s a teacher,” said Ishizaki disinterestedly. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was certainly a possibility. Keisei got up, went to the door, and asked who it was. The answer was surprising.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you still awake?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Student Council President Nagumo! Is something wrong?” asked Keisei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I came to check on you since we’re in the same group. Can I come in?” asked Nagumo.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There probably wasn’t a single first-year student brave enough to refuse him. Keisei immediately complied. Apparently, Nagumo hadn’t come alone. Vice President Kiriyama and two other third-year students accompanied him: Class B students named Tsunoda and Ishikura. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Once inside, Nagumo scanned the room.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It appears all the rooms are identical, senpai,” he said to Ishikura with a grin.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It looks like it. Now, how exactly do you plan to deepen our bonds of fellowship by bringing us to the first-years’ room?” asked Ishikura. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The question was directed at Nagumo, but Keisei, not understanding what he meant, spoke up. “Bonds of fellowship?” he asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I already told you, didn’t I? I came to check on you, since we’re in the same group. We don’t have TV or computers or smartphones. There’s nothing remotely resembling entertainment. But it’s not like we have nothing to play with,” said Nagumo, pulling a small box from his jersey pocket.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Cards?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re probably thinking, ‘Cards, in this day and age?’ Well, cards are a staple game at camps like this.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Nagumo sat down in a random spot, peeled the plastic tape from the sealed box, and opened it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Take a seat, senpai. Sorry first-years, but there isn’t much space. You stay in your beds,” said Nagumo as some of the first-years began to get up.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not playing,” said Tsunoda. He turned his back to Nagumo.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Come on, don’t say that. Let’s play. It might help us chat a bit more freely,” said Nagumo.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Tsunoda stopped in his tracks, seemingly giving up, and took a seat. Ishikura sat beside him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We should bet something to make the game more exciting. Any ideas?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The first-year students, nervous in the presence of upperclassmen, said nothing. They didn’t know how to address the student council president, and Nagumo, of course, had anticipated they would shrink back that way.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We decided on the order in which people will making breakfast, right?” he said. “Let’s bet on that. If, for example, you lose constantly, you’ll be on meal duty until the end of camp. On the other hand, if you never lose, then you never have to cook breakfast.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, Nagumo. Shouldn’t we discuss that with the entire group?” said Ishikura.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s only breakfast duty. Come on, give me that much. Please.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He was the acting student council president for our school, and yet he was so completely casual in how he talked, even to seniors. The other third-years seemed unable to respond. They probably knew of the tension between Nagumo and Horikita Manabu and didn’t want to get involved.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Fine. Let’s decide by playing cards.”</span></p>

Keisei shuffled while he looked at us, seeking one other student to participate. Since there were no volunteers, Hashimoto raised his hand with a look of resignation.</span></p>

<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">3.8</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">O</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">UR GAME OF</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> Old Maid with first-, second-, and third-years had begun. Yahiko, who had been watching the situation unfold, signaled to Keisei that it was time to switch.</span></p>

I didn’t know how much skill was involved, but you probably needed at least some luck not to draw the joker.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s good that you’re putting in effort as president, but I don’t like you picking fights with Horikita.”</span></p>

For the first-years to win, Hashimoto had to pull through. The number of cards in his hand was decreasing, but all that mattered was who held the joker.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“All right.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After another second-year student took third place, Hashimoto also got rid of his cards.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, ho, looks like the firs-years survived this time. Congrats.”</span></p>

I mean, we </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">are</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> your seniors. I’d like you to take over our&#160;duties.” </span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay.”</span></p>

<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">3.9</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">“S</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">ORRY, FIRST-YEARS,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> but I finished first.”</span></p>
