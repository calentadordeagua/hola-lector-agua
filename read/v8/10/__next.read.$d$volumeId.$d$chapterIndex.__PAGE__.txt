<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 4:<br/>A Premonition of Defeat</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">W</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">E NORMALLY HAD</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> Saturdays off, but lessons were still being held at this outdoor school. However, the timetable was slightly different from weekdays. Our lessons were only in the morning. Once those were finished, we had free time.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The special exam had started on Thursday. It was already the third day of camp, and discord was beginning to manifest within the group. It started early, just after five o’clock in the morning.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Aah, I’m so goddamn tired!” shouted Ishizaki at the outdoor cooking area. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So is everyone else. Ah, please measure the ingredients correctly—don’t mess up the amount of miso,” said Keisei, flipping through the breakfast menu the teachers had given us.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Shut it. Why do I even gotta help make food in the first place?!” Ishizaki groused, though he kept stirring the miso to make it dissolve.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Look, we don’t have a choice. We could get hit with a penalty if our whole group’s not here.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Whatever, dude, like hell I care. God damn it. Oh.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What was that?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nothin’.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, that was something. Where’s the salt you were just holding?!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I put it all in.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Keisei turned off the fire in a panic. He tasted the soup and choked. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You put in way too much! </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Ack!</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> It’s not even edible.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If we’d given that soup to the seniors, it would have invited a </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">lot </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">of criticism. Not to mention it was probably unhealthy too.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You have to start over.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Screw that. You do it. Or what about Kouenji?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s not like I know!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re in the same class, aren’t you?!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hashimoto shot a backward glance at the two of them fighting over the miso soup, his hands occupied by skillfully wielding a frying pan on the camp stove.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Dude, you’re really good.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ve always cooked my own meals,” said Hashimoto without a trace of arrogance. As he continued to cook up a storm, Albert approached him silently, carrying a bowl filled with whipped eggs.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thank you. If you’re up for it, could I ask you to cut up some vegetables too?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Despite his bulky frame, Albert skillfully brought the kitchen knife down on the cutting board, dicing vegetables with ease. We’d be feeding a lot of people, so Hashimoto kept churning out fried eggs. Clearly, he and Albert were our team’s trump cards, as far as cooking went. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Meanwhile, I’d lucked out by landing the easy job of preparing the raw vegetables and tableware. There were a </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">lot </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">of vegetables to be prepped, and although I couldn’t help with the frying, I felt I could at least pitch in on the dicing and chopping. I stood beside Albert and tried to communicate with him silently, just using my eyes.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Can you handle cutting? Vegetables?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Probably, yeah.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Somehow, we seemed to understand each other—at least enough that Albert handed over a kitchen knife. Thankfully, living in the dorms had led me to develop some skill with a knife. I began to dice vegetables, keeping pace with Albert.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Where had Kouenji gotten to, anyway? It’d already been half an hour since he went to the bathroom. Classes A and B each sent one student to look for him, but since they hadn’t returned, it was a safe bet they hadn’t found&#160;him. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In the end, Kouenji didn’t return until breakfast. When he did return, all he would say was that he’d been holed up in the bathroom due to a stomachache. Needless to say, his relationship with Ishizaki was wrecked beyond hope of repair at this point. </span></p>

<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">4.1</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">S</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">OMETHING HAPPENED</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> while I was studying morality during our third period that Saturday. I heard a girl’s cheerful voice outside, and peering out the third-floor classroom window, saw Ichinose race spiritedly through the yard. She’d had a difficult time getting the groups coordinated on the first day, but she seemed cheerful now.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakayanagi had enthusiastically declared she would crush Ichinose, but I saw no sign of that happening. Of&#160;course, I could only see what was on the surface.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><img alt="" height="100%" id="ClassroomElite-Vol8_INTERIORIMAGES_4" src="/hola-lector-agua/images/books/v8/OEBPS/Images/ClassroomElite-Vol8_INTERIORIMAGES_4.jpg" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style=" top: 0px; vertical-align: top; height: 100%;"/></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As I watched, I was able to discern, to a certain extent, the people who were members of Ichinose’s group. Surprisingly enough, I spotted only one person from Class C among them. The Class B students were all unfamiliar to me, with the exception of Ichinose. Had they gone with the same approach as the boys—picking the minimum number of people from Class B in order to have an even mix of people from all four classes? </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wasn’t really sure who the Class A and D students were, but I did spot the girl who had suffered a severe injury by running into Horikita as a part of Ryuuen’s ploy during the Sports Festival. Fortunately, she must have made a complete recovery, for she seemed to be running just fine. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The only student from Class C, meanwhile, was a girl named Wang Mei-Yu.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She’d come to Japan from China during elementary school and had remained here ever since. At least, that’s what I’d heard. Her nickname was Miichan, though only close friends ever called her that. All I knew was that she did well in class and was especially proficient in English. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Though there were some slight differences in their scores, overall, she was as academically adept as Keisei. Strangely enough, she was just as athletic as him too—which was to say she was currently dead last by a wide margin, despite struggling desperately to keep up with the group. She staggered along, breathing heavily and looking up at the sky, seeming ready to collapse. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ichinose noticed Miichan lagging behind and slowed down. She matched Miichan’s pace to run supportively alongside the other girl, encouraging her. Soon after, another girl came up beside them. It was Shiina Hiyori from Class D. She didn’t seem especially athletic either, but she had a smile on her face as she ran alongside the other girls.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">According to Ryuuen and the people around him, Shiina had taken on the role of leader of the Class D girls. If that was true, then I was looking at a girls’ group with two class leaders in it. With that in mind, it wouldn’t have been strange for Horikita and Sakayanagi to be together too—but they were apparently in different groups.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Feeling a surge of curiosity about how those groups had come together, I gazed out the window instead of focusing on the lesson. When our instructor spoke up, though, I could tell that things were about to become difficult.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We will now begin self-introductions. However, you won’t simply be introducing yourselves. Please keep in mind that this will be one part of your lesson. From now on, you will all be giving a speech every day. The themes will differ between grade levels, but the four fundamental criteria on which you will be judged are volume, posture, content, and communicativeness.’”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The word </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">speech</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> had indeed cropped up in the syllabus we’d been given back on the bus, making this undoubtedly one of the subjects we’d be tested on as part of this special exam. I was willing to bet every member of our large group would have to deliver a speech they’d composed themselves at some point. This part of the exam would be hell for people who lacked public speaking skills. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The instructor went on to notify the first-years that they would be giving speeches on what they’d learned in their first year of school and what they’d like to learn in the years to come. The second- and third-years had to speak about their plans for the future, like attending university or getting a job.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“For real? Talk about a shit exam,” spat Ishizaki. I understood how he felt, but he was being much too loud. Even the teacher seemed to have heard him, though he didn’t take Ishizaki to task over it. We could do as we pleased, but we needed to remember that our actions would ultimately affect the group. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When free time rolled around, a young man approached the first-year group. Ishizaki, who had had his legs sprawled on top of a desk, instantly corrected his posture. The new arrival was Kiriyama from second-year Class B, who served as vice president on the student council under Nagumo Miyabi. He used to be in Class&#160;A, but had been demoted after losing to Nagumo, and it seemed that, deep down, he wished for Nagumo’s downfall. Horikita’s brother had put us both in touch.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I think you should adjust your attitude a bit,” he said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“S-sure. Well, I wasn’t really makin’ any fuss or anythin’.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not just talking about you, Ishizaki. That applies to you, too, Kouenji.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Much as he might crave Nagumo’s downfall, Kiriyama still had to play the part of a dutiful vice president. He had to address anything that might affect the large group’s overall scores.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We’re going to be evaluated based on the test on the final day, right? I don’t think taking these lessons seriously is that important.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The written test isn’t everything there is to this special exam. Haven’t you considered the possibility that our instructors will be taking your behavior in class into account? And how exactly do you plan to score well on the test if you don’t take the lessons seriously?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Simple is best. This is </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">me</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> we’re talking about, no?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. You’re saying getting a high score is easy for you, eh? Well, we’ll see if you can make good on those boasts when the special exam comes around. You’re part of this group—shouldn’t you want to avoid acting in ways that make your teammates uneasy?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“A group that’s made uneasy by my actions is a group that has no value.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You don’t get to decide that, Kouenji.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Then who, may I ask, does?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No one person. The whole group does. Every student here decides.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ishizaki couldn’t help but grin after hearing that, probably because he loved seeing Kouenji get told off. However, common sense wasn’t going to work against Kouenji.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m worth far more than the rest of you combined. An average person cannot correctly judge the exceptional.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re too ignorant and infantile to even be called a high school student,” Kiriyama said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kouenji didn’t flinch, but before I knew it, nearly half of the second-years had begun to encircle us. Even Ishizaki’s smile faded, his face growing stiff. Threatening words could be heard being murmured around us.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Besides, it’s not just Kouenji. There’s a number of you who’ve been causing all sorts of problems.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He probably meant Ishizaki, but I honestly couldn’t think of anyone else. We’d all been taking the lessons seriously, in our own way. Kiriyama was probably lumping us first-years together because he wanted us to focus, letting us know that we’d be earning our seniors’ ire if we continued to behave impertinently. Kouenji was merely the straw that broke the camel’s back.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I think that’s enough, Kiriyama.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Unable to stand by and watch the situation unfold, Ishikura, a third-year, stepped in to help. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I know you’re just trying to coach them, but the way you’re doing it could come off as bullying. If that happens, you’ll be the one in trouble. The first-years understand the situation well enough. Isn’t that right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ishikura looked to us for confirmation, and every one of us, including me, nodded. Except for Kouenji, of course.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Excellent, Ishikura-senpai. You really got a handle on things, didn’t you?” said Nagumo happily. He’d been watching the whole thing go down from the sidelines. “You really are too good to be kept in Class B. Maybe you’re just really unlucky, Ishikura-senpai.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Luck, huh? Much as I hate to admit it, I think it’s just that my skills aren’t up to par.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t think that’s true. The only reason you haven’t been able to rise to Class A is because there’s a genius like Horikita Manabu in your way. I know you’ve fought the good fight for three years. There’s a 312-point difference between Classes A and B right now. Even though graduation is around the corner, I think you’re closing in on&#160;them.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you saying you’ll lead this group to victory?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Exactly. If you’re willing to trust me, Ishikura-senpai, we’ll win this special exam. And, as I’m sure will bring you no small amount of delight, I’ll help you get to Class&#160;A. We might even be able to get Horikita-senpai removed from the school. Hmm?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Unfortunately, Horikita doesn’t seem to be a group representative. And you aren’t either, are you, Nagumo? There’s nothing you can come up with that would be enough to drag him down.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It doesn’t matter if he’s the representative or not. There are many ways to crush him,” said Nagumo with a laugh.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry. But I can’t trust you. Not with the fate of Class&#160;B.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s unfortunate.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Nagumo had simply laid out all his plans in front of everyone. Was he genuinely that guileless? Or was he just trying to make himself </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">appear</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> guileless? I doubted it was the former. </span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">4.2</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">A</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">T DINNER,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> I decided to make some minor moves.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Or so I say, though all I was really trying to do was get a better handle on the girls’ situation. Ichinose and Shiina being in the same group had caught me slightly off guard, and I wanted to understand what was up with the other groups.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wanted to better understand the girls’ situation, because Ichinose and Shiina being in the same group had caught me by surprise. I wanted to understand what was up with the other groups. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kei was eating in the same place as she had since the beginning, which made it easy to get in touch. I hadn’t even asked her to do that. She truly was reliable. I, on the other hand, had been grabbing random seats that just happened to be open, wanting to avoid openly engaging with Kei, just in case.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Very few students knew about my strange relationship with Ryuuen and other members of Class D, or with Kiriyama and Kei. Besides, there were enemies on the inside of whom I needed to be cautious. I checked my timing and then sat down near Kei. Just as I was racking my brain over how I would get her to notice I was there—</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hmm.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kei made some kind of noise, I think as a greeting? It&#160;was very soft. Apparently, she’d noticed me, even though she was enjoying a meal with her friends. In that case, I would wait patiently until she got rid of them.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She proceeded to eat her meal slowly, letting her friends head back to the room ahead of her. I’d been considering postponing this meeting if there was a chance of being interrupted. She couldn’t shake her friends, but she’d cleverly manipulated them into leaving. Finally, no one was paying attention to us, and our conversation could start. Of course, we’d immediately cut it short if someone did come.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So? It’s the third day, and you finally feel like asking for my help?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. I have too little information on the girls.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No surprises there. Someone with a communication impairment like yours can only make contact with so many girls.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She was giving me the cold shoulder, right off the bat. A small price to pay if it helped Kei feel she had an advantage, and keep our relationship going…but I decided to be a little mean in response.</span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><img alt="" height="100%" id="ClassroomElite-Vol8_INTERIORIMAGES_5" src="/hola-lector-agua/images/books/v8/OEBPS/Images/ClassroomElite-Vol8_INTERIORIMAGES_5.jpg" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style=" top: 0px; vertical-align: top; height: 100%;"/></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you’ll make it through this special exam even if you get no help from me?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“O-obviously. I mean, who do you think I am?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. Then there’s nothing to worry about.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, maybe you can analyze my situation just to make sure there’s nothing to worry about. Okay?” said Kei, looking anxious.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Tell me how the girls’ groups are divided.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah, before we talk about that, something’s been on my mind.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Make it quick.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If we spoke too long, people might become suspicious.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I’d say it’s pretty important… What’s going on with that Ryuuen guy?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you worried?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, yeah. Even the girls are talking about it. Like why he quit being the leader and all. No one knows what really happened.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I wouldn’t ever call Ryuuen ‘gentle as a lamb,’ but he’s been somewhat domesticated.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So raking him over the coals worked?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Raking him over the coals?”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I hate to admit it, but I guess the two of us share first place,” Sudou said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t think anyone would object to that…but we </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">still </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">weren’t done.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I had the honor of watching your valiant battle. But my, how naïve you are,” said Ishizaki from Class D.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? Don’t make me laugh, Ishizaki. You ain’t a match for me,” said Sudou with a dismissive laugh. Ishizaki was pretty much on Yahiko’s level.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I ain’t your opponent.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You fool! Class D possesses the ultimate trump card!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No way. You don’t mean Ryuuen?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No!” shouted Ishizaki. “Albert! You’re up!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The moment Albert’s name was called, there was an uproar. The thought of Albert had certainly crossed everyone’s minds, but they’d avoided mentioning him. Now that unspoken rule was broken.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, that’s not fair!” Even Sudou, who’d been dubbing himself a king moments ago, couldn’t hide his nervousness.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Deal with it. If we’re measuring who’s number one in our grade, then Albert is on our team!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ishizaki had a point, but no one could deny a fight that crossed into international territory put us at a disadvantage. Japan’s professional baseball players, for instance, were highly skilled—but if you looked at foreign players in the major leagues, the difference in physicality was obvious. Foreigners were just made differently from us, both in terms of build and genetics. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Albert approached silently. Sudou and Katsuragi were well built, but they couldn’t compare to his muscular frame. Also, for some reason, Albert was still wearing sunglasses in the bath. Maybe they had some kind of anti-fogging solution applied to the lenses, because he moved with no problem.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Damn, he’s huge…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Albert had a bath towel wrapped around his hips, so Sudou’s muttered words had to be referencing his physique. Now that I saw them side by side, the difference was clear. It was like the difference between a junior high student and a college student. It followed that the same should apply to the weapons they wielded. Though it might be precious little help to him here, all Sudou could do was pray Albert wasn’t packing more firepower than&#160;him. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Bring it on!” Sudou shouted, showing no fear. As the king, he couldn’t run away.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Albert didn’t say a word, but he was plenty intimidating, even so. He let Ishizaki remove his bath towel. The veil had been lifted. Everyone, not just King Sudou, watched with rapt curiosity. Was this a weapon worthy of a final boss? Or, in a stunning upset, would he be wielding something minute? </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was a clash between beasts—the most primal kind of battle.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Go, Albert!” cheered Ishizaki as Albert’s fighting prowess was made clear. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Th-this is…?!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Unveiled before the eyes of the current king was Albert’s true form, which he’d kept hidden so far. The crowd fell silent.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I…lost.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Two simple words from King Sudou. He collapsed to his knees, struggling with overwhelming defeat. Unlike the contest with Katsuragi, there was no need for judgment here. The difference was just that extreme.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This means Albert’s…the final boss!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yamauchi, Shibata, and the others folded like Sudou had, their spirits crushed. No one was capable of challenging Albert. The winds of despair began to howl. Albert slowly bent over, maneuvering his large bulk as he picked up the towel and walked off. Everyone fell to their knees in despair, recognizing their horrible defeat.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ha ha ha. You are all amusing yourselves like </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">children</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">, it seems.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kouenji’s voice cut through the dreary mood like a knife. He’d been observing the commotion from inside the hot tub.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The hell, Kouenji? Aren’t you frustrated, too? Look at the state Sudou is in right now!” shouted Yamauchi. Sudou was still too anguished to be able to stand back up.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I know. But Red Hair-kun put up a good fight.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The hell, dude? Are you tryin’ to say you can square off against Albert?” said Sudou, the life gone from his eyes. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I am a perfect being. As a man, I possess the ultimate body.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t dodge the question. What are you saying, </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">specifically</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kouenji ran his hand through his hair, without getting out of the tub.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There is no need for a competition, precisely because I know that there is </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">no one </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">superior to me. So, there is no need to war over something so pointless.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So you say. But it’s not true, is it?” said Yamauchi, baiting him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, Kouenji showed no signs of nervousness.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You truly are a fool. However, it might be fun to play along with you on occasion.” He swiped his hair back from his face, looking as though he intended to accept the challenge. “Now then, shall I assume that Aaaalbert-kun is my opponent in this little competition?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Why did he say Albert’s name like that?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No. It’s Katsuragi-san!” shouted Yahiko.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Leave me out of this, Yahiko…” said Katsuragi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s no way Kouenji can win if he goes up against Albert! On behalf of the Japanese people, I’m begging you, Katsuragi-san, you must defeat him!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Well, I supposed Yahiko and Kouenji </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">were</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> in the same group. Even though he’d been seated nearby, Kouenji probably didn’t know in detail the kind of fighting power that Sudou and the others held. If Katsuragi, who was an even match for Sudou, stepped forward…then maybe he stood a decent chance of victory.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“For crying out loud… All right, just this once,” said Katsuragi, exasperated. He stood to represent the Japanese people, his package swaying left and right as he did. The guys gazed at it as if they were looking upon something divine.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“A-as I thought, he really is huge. I mean, even he can’t go up against Albert, but if Kouenji—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Heh. I see. So you’re not called a king for nothing, eh?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Please just end this.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“However, you are no match for </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">me</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">.” But Kouenji made no attempt to get out of the bath.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, hey. You ain’t </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">scared</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> or nothin’, are ya, Kouenji? Or are you all talk, hidin’ your thing away in the tub?” Ishizaki said, trying to needle Kouenji into making a move.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not so foolish as to direct my blade toward an unworthy opponent.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Heh. In that case, we’ll break your spirit ’til there’s nothin’ left. Right, Albert?!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Albert, the great foreign threat, stood next to Katsuragi. When he did so, a strange phenomenon occurred: Katsuragi’s thing looked small in comparison. Upon seeing that, Kouenji’s expression changed dramatically for the first time.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Bravo!” He clapped his hands. “I see, I see. As I would expect of the one representing the rest of the world, it would appear that you aren’t all talk.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you get it now, Kouenji? How much of a clown you are?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ve had enough of this,” said Katsuragi. After he finished washing off his body, he entered the tub and kept his distance from Kouenji. Everyone ignored him, now completely absorbed in the battle between Kouenji and Albert.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Normally, it is not my policy to show it to men. But this is a one-time deal.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kouenji stood, grabbing a towel and wrapping it around his hips to conceal his weapon.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“S-so you really gonna do it, Kouenji?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The ultimate eccentric and the king were going head-to-head. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ve known the outcome of this battle from the start. Now everyone here shall bear witness too.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kouenji struck a pose as he removed the towel that had been concealing his lower half. In that instant, a dazzling light hit everyone’s eyes. A mighty sword, complete with a dyed blond lion’s mane. No, it was too enormous to simply be called a sword.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I heard Albert mutter softly in English beside me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, my God,” he said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And thus, I have proven that I am a perfect being.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The guys who’d just borne witness couldn’t even utter a sound.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you even human?” said Sudou. That was all he could say in the face of a power so overwhelming, it crossed national borders. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If Sudou and Katsuragi were rifles and Albert was a bazooka, then Kouenji was a tank. Nobody could win against such overwhelming firepower. Its colossal size, armor, and destructive power would take down anyone in its path. There wasn’t a single student in this massive bathhouse that could defeat Albert…which meant none of them who could stand up to Kouenji either. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Then, just as everyone was about to hand him the crown…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ha. Hold it, Kouenji.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">A voice emerged from the vicinity of the tub that Kouenji had been in moments ago.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“R-Ryuuen…?” someone stammered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ryuuen, the former leader of Class D himself, was warming up in the whirlpool bath, near Kouenji. There was fire in his eyes. He must have been watching Albert and Kouenji’s battle.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You can’t possibly think you’re a match for me,” Kouenji said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No. Not even I can win against that thing you’re packin’. However, there might be at least one person here who could give you a good fight.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Everyone started looking around at each other as he said that, even though there was no way such a person could exist. Then I realized what he meant.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ryuuen had caught me in his trap.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, ho? And </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">who</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> might that be?” asked Kouenji. His interest must have been piqued.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Can’t say. But if I’m not mistaken, there is still one person here covering himself up with a towel, hiding his true power.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Having dropped that bomb, Ryuuen entered the bath and turned his back to us. Fortunately, only a few people seemed to buy what he’d said…and yet, everyone’s gazes were intensifying. Somehow, I felt like not just the current occupants of the bathhouse but people all throughout Japan were now paying attention.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No way. A guy like you? No way, dude,” said Yahiko, glaring at me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you really taking what he said at face value?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t intend to, but…it’s kind of curious that you’re the only one still covering yourself up all this time.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Curious or not, I just never had any intention of joining this game.” I took a step back.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay, fine. But let us check, just in case.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yamauchi and Yahiko approached, as though they were trying to flank me. Ryuuen wore a smug smile on his face.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">I’m gonna make you taste defeat.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That’s what his look and smile said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Just as I thought…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ryuuen, who had no way of knowing what my member looked like, had intentionally set this up. He was intent on making me lose, one way or another. It was a malicious sort of attack, very much like Ryuuen. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I could use all my strength and bolt from the bathhouse, but then I’d have to forego bath time while here at the camp. Sooner or later, the veil would be lifted. I&#160;could try to turn the tables and punch out every student here, but that strategy was hardly worth consideration. Either way, I lost. There was no way I could avoid this incomprehensible battle anymore. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kouenji, seeing I wasn’t budging an inch, laughed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ha ha ha! No need to feel embarrassed, Ayanokouji Boy. Even if you happen to be wearing </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">protection</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">, that’s something a lot of Japanese children do. It’s an important thing to protect.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re not protecting anything though, Kouenji.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s because I possess overwhelming strength, you see. I have no need for armor.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had to get out of this. Think. Find a means of escape—</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You guys, do the chant. The chant.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Despite having dropped out of this competition himself, Ryuuen spurred everyone else on from where he sat in the bath, setting off another of his traps. He countered my strategy, ensuring I could no longer escape.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Take it off! Take it off! Take it off!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">All the guys started chanting, calling for me to take it off. The identity of the person goading them on didn’t matter one bit to them. I was trapped, all thanks to Ryuuen. And all I’d wanted was to wash away my fatigue after a tiring day…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“All right.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sometimes, you just had to fight. I had no choice but to admit that now was one of those times. And as a man, if you had a weapon, you should wield it. Winning or losing didn’t matter, and nor did pride.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As a man with a weapon, if you have to fight, then you should fight. What was important here wasn’t winning or losing, nor was it something like pride.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Fine, whatever.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you want me to put you out of your misery, Ayanokouji? Read you your last rites?” asked Sudou. I&#160;waved him away.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Everyone kept calling for me to take it off, so I removed the towel wrapped around my hips, and then…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The chanting suddenly dropped. There was complete silence, as if the unruly noise earlier were just a dream.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“F-for real, dude? Ayanokouji, he…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t believe it…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The guys spoke in whispers. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, well. I’m honestly impressed, Ayanokouji Boy. To think there exists a Japanese person who can hold their own against me. Truly, a margin of a few millimeters might as well be nonexistent.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s like a showdown between two T-rexes…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The guys looked on with admiration and exasperation.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You are all living witnesses to history,” said Kouenji, tossing his towel over his shoulder with a laugh. “However, victory is mine. If they are both T-rexes, as you put it, then the difference lies in the number of prey they’ve devoured. In other words: experience.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Without supplying any further details, Kouenji dipped back in the tub.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">T WAS 1:00 A.M.,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> well past lights-out, and I was lying awake in my bed. Everyone else was sleeping soundly. I&#160;should have been sleeping in order to prepare for tomorrow. That reason I was still awake lay in the single piece of paper beneath my pillow with the number 25 written on it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The note’s simplicity didn’t leave much to the imagination. The memo represented 25:00—in other words, one o’clock in the morning. I didn’t have any clue about who had put the note there, but I was awake now so I could find out. If it were just a simple prank or something completely different from what I was imagining, then, well, that would be that. I could just use this time to calm down and think about things.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">What lay at the heart of this special exam? The bigger picture was slowly coming into view, bit by bit. Of course, this was all speculation, since we hadn’t been told exactly how the test would be scored. But I did know for a fact that there were going to be several things included in this exam. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Zen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We would be graded on everything from our manners at the start of </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">zazen</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> to the posture we held during it. If we behaved inappropriately or did anything that got us smacked by the zen stick, we’d probably have points docked for it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Long-Distance Relay Race.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That sounded simple. A test of our speed. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Speech.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Every person in the large group would deliver a speech on four criteria, aforementioned. The grading system for this had already been disclosed. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Written Exam.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I expected this would focus on the subject of ethics. It sounded like it would be a standard essay-based test.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There were other things that concerned me, like “cleaning” and “cooking meals,” but I couldn’t determine how those would be scored yet. In some cases, things like tardiness or causing trouble might also be included in our assessments. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">A lot of students were probably agonizing over how to approach this completely different special exam. A strategy, while vital, could only be devised once you understood something’s true nature. The goal of this exam was ostensibly to make us work as a group, support each other, and earn a high average score. Simple, on the face of it. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But though it might sound easy at first, you could tell just from watching the groups be formed that this was going to be an uphill battle. It was extremely difficult for students who were normally hostile toward one another to truly cooperate. Horikita and Hirata, to speak of our own class, or Ichinose and Katsuragi, to speak of others, were probably focusing on encouraging such cooperation. Wielding influence within your group and having leadership skills made all the difference. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Selecting the members of your group was important, of course, but it was nearly impossible to tell right off the bat which students were capable of scoring well in this exam. Although Keisei’s academic prowess was nothing to scoff at, and thus, he could be expected to do well in that regard, he seemed to struggle with even the two five-minute sets of </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">zazen</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> on the first day. Some students couldn’t cross their legs at all. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was still too early to tell whether academic or athletic skill would be a good measure of how students would do from here on out. Rather, it was the most adaptable students who were likely to come out on top.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Additionally, it was probable that more than a few students were opting to pursue strategies that deviated from the basic one of encouraging cooperation. I’d sensed when the rules were explained to us that even the school had struggled to prepare for this rather unorthodox special exam. This had been true of every special exam, of course, but there had always been loopholes in the rules to exploit. Blind spots that the school couldn’t see. Such as how Ibuki and Horikita had traded blows back on the uninhabited island, even though violence was prohibited.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Of course, if you did happen to get caught committing breaking the rules, then the consequences would be huge. The majority of the students probably wouldn’t risk it, with immediate expulsion at stake. Besides, the situation was far too complex for a mere rule violation to secure you a victory. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Could you find a barely existing blind spot, exploit a loophole, and take a shot that would somehow outdo the people following the traditional strategy? A high hurdle to overcome. I’d tried a lot of things in the special exams we’d had so far. On the uninhabited island, I had Horikita retire and switched up leaders. On the cruise ship, I had the trick with my phone. I purposefully drew attention to myself during the Sports Festival. And I shut Kushida down during the Paper Shuffle. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But this time, I’d decided not to take action. I would just sit and observe, continuing to collect information. I had determined that was what I had to do in order to fade into the background and graduate like any ordinary student. Even if that meant Class C took some hard blows this time around, I wouldn’t do anything. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I also wanted to show Sakayanagi and Nagumo, who both had a certain degree of interest in me, that I had no intention of fighting…though I had my doubts about how well that would work. Horikita’s brother couldn’t really blame me for anything if all I did was observe. However, there was one measure I could take, and it was defense. If there were a student out there who was trying to get me expelled, then it was only natural that I would defend myself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was already past 25:00 now, and I had yet to see anything out of the ordinary. If that was the case, should I go back to sleep?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But just then, something happened.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The hallway door cracked open slightly and a little bit of light came through. It was Morse code. They were communicating via the flickering light. Since it was extremely dark in the hallways at night, several flashlights had been made readily available to us, and the person out there was probably using one. Light made no sound. The perfect way to signal me to come meet with them. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I got out of bed and silently stood. Our rooms weren’t equipped with toilets. Getting up to go to the bathroom in the middle of the night was a perfectly natural thing to do.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">4.5</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HE CORRIDOR WAS</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> pitch-black, but I could tell someone was moving from the faint sound of footsteps. I followed that sound, and the person holding the light turned out to be Horikita Manabu.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh, to think you’d contact me. Isn’t that kind of conspicuous?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In order for him to place a note on my bed, he’d have to know where I slept. In that case, there were only a couple people who might have helped him. It was probably either Ishikura or Tsunoda, the third-year students who’d played cards with Nagumo. They could have told Horikita which bed was mine.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“More than a few students meet in secret when others are fast asleep. There are probably two or three schemes being implemented during this exam, after all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Everyone—first-, second-, and third-years alike—was pulling out all the stops in order to win. That being said, such clandestine meetings rarely led to anything good. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you know why I called you here?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Because Nagumo is acting weird. I can’t think of any other reason.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Exactly. I thought you might have something on him, since you’re in the same large group. Plus, I wanted to respond to the message you sent me back on the bus.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll say this up front: prepare to be disappointed. There’s no sign Nagumo is up to anything strange.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was lying. There </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">were </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">several things about Nagumo’s behavior that concerned me. He’d directly challenged the elder Horikita to a contest in front of a huge crowd of people, and losing that contest would be a pretty poor showing for the second-years. Both his upperclassmen and underclassmen would view him with skepticism in the future. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If you were going to do combat in public, you should do so only when you were sure the odds were in your favor. I didn’t sense that was the case here. Since Horikita’s brother had instructed Nagumo to fight fair and square, I’d expected Nagumo to be strict about making sure everyone in our large group paid attention to our lessons…but there was no sign of him doing anything of the sort.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was probably making Horikita Manabu anxious. If not, he wouldn’t have taken the massive risk of contacting me like this.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Then you think Nagumo is going forward without any kind of scheme?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Who knows? I think not involving any third parties really limits what you can actually do.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even if you could remind people not to talk in class, doze off, or be late, that wasn’t going to improve test scores by leaps and bounds. At most, it might avoid having points docked. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Currently, our large group is more unified,” Horikita’s brother calmly assessed. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">True, his group was mostly composed of Class A students, including those from the first year. Whatever the test was, there was a strong chance they’d win. That had to be why he felt anxious about Nagumo’s inaction.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What are the odds he’ll go back on his word?” I said. “Maybe he wants to see you lose no matter what it takes.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nagumo shows no mercy to those who defy him. He’s employed underhanded methods like Ryuuen more than once. He’s also directly responsible for the second-years’ unusually high expulsion rate. However, he’s never once broken a promise.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You believe he’s serious about not involving any third parties?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita’s brother nodded decisively. He and Nagumo had served together on the student council for nearly two years, and he must have seen how the other boy operated. I had my doubts, but hearing the certainty in his voice gave me that answer. An answer that applied not just to Horikita’s brother, who was before me right now, but perhaps to all the second- and third-year students too.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After hearing those words of absolute certainty from him, I felt doubt at first, but then I had arrived at my answer. I could say that for Horikita’s brother, who was in front of me right now, and perhaps for all of the second-year and third-year students as well.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I should give Horikita’s brother some advice here and now. But it probably wouldn’t do much good. He’d already determined that his best defense was to trust in his enemy’s principles.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This has been a waste of time, apparently,” said Horikita’s brother, turning his back to me. “Oh, to answer your earlier question… The student council can influence the special exams. The council is supposed to represent the students’ perspectives and can therefore make significant revisions to penalties or meddle with the rules. However, the council cannot make these decisions on a whim.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">With that, Horikita left.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“He might lose,” I muttered, unable to keep it in.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Well, perhaps that was the wrong word. Horikita’s brother would make no mistakes. He would doubtlessly manage his group well. But even so, we clearly didn’t fully understand this exam. It was possible this would drastically change the course of our third semester.</span></p>
