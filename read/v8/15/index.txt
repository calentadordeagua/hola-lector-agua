<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 9:<br/>Blind Spot</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">T WAS THE FINAL DAY</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> of camp, the day when our groups would be ranked according to the special exam. During this last week, the twenty-six or so small groups had passed time in their own unique ways. Some groups had gotten to know one another better and worked well together. Others were on the verge of collapse. Others had completed their daily tasks with indifference, growing neither closer nor further apart.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">At first, our group had been a disaster. However, we’d grown significantly closer to one another by the end. I&#160;mean, we weren’t perfect. At best, we were a ragtag lot. This was a temporary alliance—come tomorrow, we’d be enemies again. There was a certain sense of sadness at the thought of our group activities coming to an end.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“For the time being, we’ve done all we can. No matter what the outcome, we have no regrets.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I think so too. Thanks for being our representative, Yukimura.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ishizaki and Yukimura shook hands.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No matter what happens, let’s give it our all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Let’s do it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The other guys complimented each other and shook on it. Afterward, we headed to our assigned classroom. There was nothing to worry about as far as group unity. Our biggest concern was Kouenji. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Right now, he seemed calm, quietly following us. But no one could predict when he might suddenly cut loose. The second- and third-years from our large group had already arrived, so we hurried to our seats in a bit of a panic just as the chime sounded. The teacher entered and began to explain the contents of the exam.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though we’d gathered in our large group, the exam would be administered to the small groups by grade level. The large groups would affect the overall ranking when the scores were tallied. No matter how vast the camp grounds, if everyone tried doing all the same activities at the same time, we’d be crammed in together.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The four sections of the exam were as expected. They included </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Zen</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">,</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;"> Speech</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">,</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;"> Long-Distance Relay Race</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">,</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;"> </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">and</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;"> Written Exam</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">.</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;"> </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We first-year students would begin with </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">zazen</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">. Then we headed to the next classroom for our written exam. We’d follow that up with the long-distance relay race and, finally, we’d give our speeches. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The second-years had a higher hurdle to jump right out of the gate, since they were starting with the long-distance relay. The third-years, apparently, were beginning with speeches.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">9.1</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">A</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">FTER WE FINISHED BREAKFAST,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> we headed to  the </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">zazen </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">dojo. We were exempted from cleaning this morning. The exam began as soon as all of the first-year guys had gathered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Without further ado, we will begin. There are two criteria in determining your scores: your manners and posture upon entering this dojo, and the presence or absence of any disorder during </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">zazen</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">. After you’ve finished, you will stand by in your assigned classrooms until you are given instructions for your next exam. Starting now, I will call out names. Students whose names have been called will line up and begin taking the test. Class A, Katsuragi Kouhei. Class D, Ishizaki Daichi—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The instructor continued to read names aloud. After Katsuragi came Ishizaki. That was an unexpected development. The crowd murmured its surprise.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hurry up, Ishizaki. Next, first-year Class B, Beppu Ryouta.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Confused, Ishizaki got in line, panicking slightly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is this a different order then usual?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Keisei mentally prepared while panicking himself. We hadn’t expected this. We’d performed </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">zazen</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> several times over the past week, but always in our small groups. This time around, the school was assigning spots randomly. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That meant students outside our comfort zone would be close to our personal bubble. That might seem like a trivial difference, but on the day of the exam, when people were tense to begin with, it made everything that much harder. The school’s goal to shake things up was working.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Keisei was flustered. Then a large hand rested on his shoulder—Albert, offering a gesture of consideration to calm him down. Getting the message, Keisei looked like he had regained a little bit of his composure.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry. If I’m like this during the very first exam, it’ll affect our group’s morale.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Rather than consider the pressure of being the representative as a negative, Keisei thought of it as a positive. When his name was finally called, he responded clearly and entered the dojo. I was called before Albert, the second to last. Several instructors inside the dojo stood around holding clipboards and pens.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">On top of that, there was a strange number of cameras installed throughout the dojo, perhaps to add certainty to the scoring. I already had the basics of </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">zazen</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> down in my head, so I wasn’t going to slip up. Since it was very likely the scoring system was set up in such a way where you’d start with one hundred and have points deducted based on performance, I calculated that I would definitely have a perfect score.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After all, there wasn’t any need for me to hold back in performing </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">zazen</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">. A slight distance away from me, Kouenji was participating too. He made no mistakes—in fact, he had beautiful posture. His movements were flawless. He’d never once seemed to take practice seriously, but I guess this was what you’d expect from him, huh? </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Since we kept our eyes closed during the assessment, I couldn’t be sure about the finer details. But I was sure we’d done well.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">9.2</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">A</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">FTER WE FINISHED</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> with </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">zazen</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">, everyone began to  disperse in silence. We were still being graded until we actually left the dojo. Under the watchful gaze of the instructors, the students silently exited and headed toward their respective classrooms as instructed. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Once everyone in our group gathered, Keisei slumped in his chair.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ugh, my legs went numb during the test.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Did you make it?” asked Ishizaki. His legs might have suffered a similar fate, because he rubbed at them as he asked Keisei that question.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I think so. But I might’ve gotten a few points taken off.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, no use worryin’ about it. Nothing ya can do now. Right, Ayanokouji?” said Hashimoto.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s right. Next up is Keisei’s forte: the written exam. Just focus on that,” I told him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Nagumo’s words from last night were probably still in Hashimoto’s head. But he wasn’t going to confront me directly about them, because he had no idea what Horikita’s brother considered special about me in the first place. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Two other small groups of first-years were in the room with us. One of them was the group that Ryuuen was in, which Akito was the representative for. I could tell that Ishizaki and Albert shifted their gazes over toward Ryuuen, but Ryuuen, rather than look back at us, took his seat, alone. He didn’t talk to anyone. He was part of the group, but at the same time, he wasn’t. He gave off an air of being completely and utterly isolated. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh, that’s pretty weird,” muttered Hashimoto, standing next to me. It would be easy for me to ignore him, but I supposed I’d play along a bit.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What is?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ishizaki and Albert’s eyes. I don’t get the impression that they hate him. If anything, they look sorrowful. Like pets whose master has abandoned them.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t get it. Didn’t Ishizaki and Albert get fed up with Ryuuen?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s what they say, but…maybe there’s something more to Ryuuen stepping down. Don’t you think so?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hashimoto almost certainly had no evidence linking me to Ryuuen. It was likely he was trying to steer the conversation in that direction because Nagumo had expressed an interest in Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Dunno. I’m not really familiar with other classes’ goings-on.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. Sorry for asking you something weird.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After the ten-minute break ended, we moved on to the written exam, which proved to be nothing special. They tested what we had learned during our time at the camp. If you had a good grasp of the main topics we’d covered, you could almost certainly get a perfect score. If you were a student who struggled, you would get somewhere between fifty and seventy points. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">What should I do?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">While everyone else around me put their heads down and worked away, I tried to figure out how many points I should allow myself to lose. They probably weren’t going to make individual scores public, but I didn’t want to let the school see me get successive perfect scores. There were a lot of students who had been trying to feel me out as of late. Honestly, I would much rather hold back and not score too high.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I decided I would miss just one question that people would consider difficult. That would give me a solid 95 or so. After I finished penning down all my answers, I was tempted to look out the window. I didn’t want the teachers to think I was cheating, though, so I shut my eyes and waited for the end. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After the test, the groups assembled and assessed our scores. It wasn’t like calculating our scores ourselves was going to change the results, and yet, I couldn’t really help but wonder whether I got that one question right or wrong. I guessed forcing yourself to think differently was effective, to a certain degree.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Everyone in our group was here except for Kouenji, who’d left the classroom as soon as the test was over. Ishizaki, as expected, hadn’t understood most of the questions. My insurance was going to come in handy. Still, the written exam had been easy overall, meaning every group probably scored high.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Based on what I’d seen in the dojo, the written exam and </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">zazen</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> test might not have resulted in a significant point gap between groups. Everyone seemed to have performed </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">zazen </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">with a certain degree of precision. Both tests were about demonstrating what you’d learned, so if you could pull that off, there likely wouldn’t be a huge variation in scores. Of all the sections of the exam, it was the long-distance relay race that was likely to have the biggest impact.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If final scores were awarded based on where you placed in the relay, you might assume that the group who got first place would have earned a perfect score…but that was probably too simple, huh? I had a feeling they’d be taking our times into consideration too. In fact, you might even score well if you came in sixth place but made good time in the race. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When we left the classroom, I saw a number of vans parked outside. It seemed they’d be taking us to the spot where the batons would be handed out. When we got into the van, we received additional instructions from the teachers.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Every individual student must run a minimum distance of 1.2 kilometers.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Baton passes are only allowed every 1.2 kilometers.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If a student is unable to finish the race due to an accident, or if they fail to meet the minimum requirements, they will be disqualified. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After the teachers explained those three points, they dropped off Keisei, our first runner, and the van drove on. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Besides, if I ended up causing us unnecessary trouble because of a decision I made, there’d be hell to pay.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It seems you’re cleverer than I thought. But also quite boring.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If that was how he saw me, I was grateful for it. </span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, Ayanokouji-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Naturally, there were other guys waiting there, too. Hirata greeted me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh, you’re not the anchor?” he asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nope. Kouenji is going last. What about you? Is Sudou your anchor?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. He’s raring to go—wants to run a lot. But with fifteen people, that’s not really going to work.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sudou’s rivalry with Kouenji would probably flare up during the final lap of the race.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Personally, I wish we’d had more people,” I said. “It would probably have made things a little easier.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“At any rate, let’s both do our best. As long as we don’t fall below the school’s standards, no one will be expelled, after all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">While we waited, everyone was free to chat or quietly concentrate. Since water stations were placed every 1.2 kilometers, it was also possible to get a drink…though you increased your chances of a stomachache if you guzzled down water before you ran. One student, totally oblivious to the peril, sucked down the contents of a plastic water bottle.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah, I’m so nervous,” he muttered before turning around and locking eyes with me. It was the Professor. Maybe he wanted someone to talk to, because he approached me. “So, you’re in this spot too, Ayanokouji-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“A-Ayanokouji-kun? In this spot…?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I couldn’t believe my ears. The Professor I knew would have said something like “Honorable Ayanokouji-dono, art thou currently placed upon this land?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah. Well, I quit talking like that. I was doing it to play a character, but after getting that warning during </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">zazen,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> I thought I’d knock it off.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I couldn’t hide my shock at hearing the Professor speak so normally. It didn’t suit him at all. It was like the thing that made him </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">him</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> had disappeared. He felt like a placeholder now, like Student X, or Y, or whatever. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We went on to have a completely ordinary conversation, but to be honest, I didn’t remember any of it. Guess just changing the way you talked could make a huge difference. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">At any rate, I wondered if Keisei had successfully passed the baton. No matter how long it took, finishing the race was crucial. Cold as it might sound, even if our large group did place dead last or fell below the school’s standard line, no harm would come my way. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Of course, I did genuinely think it’d be better if no one got expelled. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As I wondered how many minutes had passed, I finally saw a student coming our way. However, it wasn’t Ishizaki. It was someone from the mostly Class B group led by Kanzaki. More students began to arrive, one after the other. After a close struggle with the runner in third, Ishizaki was in fourth.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Huff huff</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">. Take it, Ayanokouji! Go for first!” he shouted, handing the baton over. </span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll kill ya if ya hold back!” shouted Ishizaki with the last of his strength, before collapsing to the ground.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">His exhaustion was natural, considering he’d run more than three kilometers on a mountain path. I decided to slowly but surely close the gap with the runners in front. I ran a little bit faster than everyone else, making sure not to disrupt my breathing. Rather than trying to go on the offense right off the bat pace, I waited for their stamina to drain before overtaking them. By doing that, I fooled them into thinking I’d passed because they were too slow.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Despite the bumpy terrain, two kilometers wasn’t enough to leave me out of breath. Just like that, I overtook one runner ahead of me, putting me in third, only a short distance behind the second-place runner. Then I handed the baton off to Kouenji.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Nine people had handled this baton. Our fate was in this man’s hands.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Now then. Let’s work up a light sweat, shall we?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kouenji, brushing back his hair, took the baton in hand and ran with a nonchalant look on his face. He probably wasn’t going at full speed, but he was quite fast. If he kept to that speed, we’d probably be fine. That was, of course, so long as he didn’t slow to a stroll once he was out of sight. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In the end, despite all the anxiety he’d caused us, Kouenji wound up finishing in second place. I didn’t know if he couldn’t overtake the runner in first or if he simply didn’t want to. Probably the latter. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The speeches we’d have to give after this intense race were going to be hell for the first-years. We’d have to speak at length in raised voices after completely exhausting ourselves.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, nothing particularly noteworthy occurred. While I had some doubts about Kouenji’s somewhat…let’s say </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">dramatic</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> performance during his speech, everyone seemed to have completed the assignment without any real difficulty.</span></p>

<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">9.3</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">A</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">ND SO,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> the longest day of the special exam was over. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, I’m glad. I’m really, really glad.”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sure. For the boys.”</span></p>

In a worst-case scenario, students from all three grade levels might be expelled at the same time.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“As for the group that fell below the threshold…”</span></p>

The only thing that’s happening is that a Class B student will be expelled. If&#160;anything, this means you’ll pull even further ahead of your rivals, right?” Nagumo snickered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Um, please, some quiet, if you would. Ikari-san may also decide to invoke the solidarity rule and name someone else within her group. Ikari-san, please consult with me later. Next, I will announce which of the girls’ groups has taken second place.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Despite saying that it was unfortunate, he continued speaking, solemnly.
