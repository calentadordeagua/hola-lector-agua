<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 2:<br/>A New Special Exam:<br/>Mixed Training Camp</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">O</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">N A THURSDAY MORNING,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> shortly after the start of the third semester, several buses traveled in a row down the highway. The buses carried not only first-year students, but second- and third-year as well—the entire school jointly embarking on one big road trip.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The bus that carried us—the first-year Class C students—entered a tunnel. My ears were assaulted by the pressure change. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This was the second time I’d ridden a bus since enrolling at this school. No one had explained where we were headed or what we were doing. All we knew was that we had to wear jerseys, and it was strongly recommended we pack several spare jerseys and changes of underwear before we left. This didn’t sound like a vacation, though.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Since the bus ride was about three hours or so, everyone had brought along fun items considered acceptable by the school. Cell phones were a given, but people had also brought books, playing cards, and snacks and juice. Some had even brought along game consoles.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Our seats were designated, with our names written on them, and I was seated next to Ike Kanji. I’d intended to get along with him when I enrolled here, but our relationship was still at the “just fellow classmates” stage, and opportunities to hang out with him had decreased dramatically. Instead of talking to me, his literal seatmate, he knelt on his seat, turned around, and had a loud conversation with Sudou and Yamauchi, who were seated further away. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I occasionally heard the girls tell them off for being annoying, but they didn’t pay much mind. It was uproarious in here, anyway, so it was no wonder that they weren’t holding back. I felt a bit lonely, but there was nothing I could do about it. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">On the bright side…the recent round of exams had let me grow closer to students like Akito and Keisei. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The atmosphere on the bus was pleasant, but I could tell this wouldn’t be a mere field trip. If we’d done this in the middle of winter vacation, I might have held out hope of it being just a fun outing, but the third semester was already underway. It would probably be for us to anticipate an exam like the one we’d had on the deserted island, just so we could prepare ourselves mentally. It wasn’t as though Ike and the others hadn’t matured at </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">all </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">since then. Probably.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira observed the students doing as they pleased with a look of deep interest. She stood close to the driver, rather near my seat, as she stared at us. Since I didn’t want our gazes to meet, I decided to stare out the window. This was a long tunnel. It’d been two or three minutes since we entered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Just as I thought that, it began to get brighter. We’d made it through. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira, who had been waiting for us to emerge from the tunnel, started to move. At the same time, the pain in my ears increased. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry to interrupt your enthusiasm, but be quiet,” she said, addressing the students through a hand-held microphone. “I thought you might like to know where this bus is headed, and also what you’ll be doing once we arrive.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, yeah, ’course we’re curious. We’re not going to another deserted island, are we?” asked Ike.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira answered quickly. “I see that special exam is still seared into your collective memory. Allow me to give you some peace of mind; we don’t hold special exams on that grand a scale very often. Nor are we so cruel as to put you through something like that when it’s no longer summer. However, as you can already guess, there </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">is </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">a new special exam ahead. Compared to the island, though, you’ll be in significantly better living conditions.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So she said—but I didn’t trust her. The island aside, every special exam we’d faced so far had been insanely difficult from an average student’s perspective. Hanging over our heads was the ultimate penalty of failing one of these special exams: expulsion. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Now, the special exam that you Class D students will—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira paused. My classmates smiled with a touch of pride as, immediately after, she bowed her head in apology.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sorry. You are Class C now. Allow me to give you newly promoted students an overview of the special exam.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Having been formally promoted to Class C in the third semester, our class seemed to be trying its best to calmly face the upcoming exam. Receiving an explanation of how that special exam worked on the bus meant we could prepare countermeasures, or at least </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">try </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">to. We couldn’t just get up and walk around while the bus was in motion, but the bus was small enough that everyone could hear you if you spoke, and we could always use our phones to correspond privately with specific people.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ike and the other noisemakers shut up, listening to what Chabashira had to say. Proof that they </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">had </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">matured, if only a little. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We are taking you to a school camp in the mountains. We will arrive in just under an hour. The less time I have to spend explaining, the more time you’ll have to strategize,” said Chabashira.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">An hour until the exam started, huh? In other words, if it took twenty minutes to explain the rules, we’d have forty minutes left over to plan. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t students normally attend school camps and stuff in the summer?” asked Ike, who used to be a boy scout. He knew quite a bit about the mountain range we could currently see from our seats as we traveled down the highway. It was covered in white snow, even now. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Please be quiet and listen. Remember, this is the only time you have,” Chabashira rebuked, but she sounded gentle, rather than angry. Ike scratched his head sheepishly and apologized. A brief moment of laughter ensued.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">School camp. I’d never heard of it, so I looked it up on my phone.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“Typically held in the summer months on days when the weather is nice. Commonly held in locations where greenery is abundant, such as the mountains. Group activities are conducted with the express purpose of promoting the development of healthy bodies. Can also refer to an educational facility.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Just as Ike had said, it sounded like these school camps were usually held in the summer. Clearly, however, they could also take place any time of year.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Normally, opportunities to connect with senior students are few and far between, especially for those not involved in club activities. At this school camp, you’ll participate in inter-class group activities for eight days and seven nights, making this a step beyond the Sports Festival. The name we’ve given this special exam is the ‘Mixed Camp.’ Since I’m sure a verbal explanation won’t satisfy you, I’ll be distributing printed materials now,” Chabashira continued.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She handed a stack of printouts to the students sitting in the front row, who each took one and then passed the rest back. The syllabus consisted of about twenty pages. Since she hadn’t specifically told us not to look, I began to flip through it. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There were clear photographs of what I assumed to be the camp, including the dormitories, public baths, cafeteria, and so on. It was actually kind of exciting to look at these; I felt like I was paging through a travel guide. The all-important words </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">special exam</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">, however, curbed our enthusiasm.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Still, this was twenty pages of documentation on top of a verbal explanation. We’d only received a brief verbal explanation for the Paper Shuffle, which meant this exam was probably shaping up to be pretty annoying. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After confirming that everyone had received the handouts, Chabashira resumed speaking.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Feel free to keep reading, but I’m going to explain the Mixed Camp now. These materials will be re-collected before you get off the bus, so please make sure you clearly understand the rules. Questions will be accepted at the end, so pay attention. Do you understand?” asked Chabashira, looking at Ike as she spoke. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ike nodded. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The main goal of the Mixed Camp is to foster your mental development. In order to accomplish this, we’re going to start with the fundamentals of participation in civilized society—ensuring you can build stable relationships with people you don’t know very well. Each and every one of you will learn these skills.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So that was why they were making us do group activities with senior students, huh? </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As Chabashira had just said, students who were in clubs might have already developed relationships with their juniors or seniors. Even so, those interactions were mostly limited to the club activities themselves. As for students who </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">weren’t </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">in any clubs, hardly any of us had interacted with upperclassmen at all. You might think the school would want us to fraternize voluntarily, without need for exams or club activities to serve as incentive. Of course, it wasn’t that simple in reality.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So, how did they plan to get the upperclassmen involved in this? Unless the need for direct contact was a high priority, most students would probably keep their distance from one another, just like we had during the Sports Festival. Well, we </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">were</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> headed into the mountains to a place designated as a camp, so that might be easier said than done… </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">At any rate, if the rules for the special exam weren’t set in stone, it should be easy to find loopholes. There was a marked difference in maturity, both physically and mentally, between first- and second-year students. A year is a significant period of time in the life of a teenager. I&#160;couldn’t say how extreme the gulf would prove to be, but the reality of the situation was that we wouldn’t be on a level playing field.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Once we arrive at our destination, we will divide you by gender. Next, we’ll hold a school-wide discussion with all grade levels and then further divide you into six groups.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Divide men and women, then make six groups…” muttered Ike, as though he were trying to memorize the information. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira continued, “The minimum and maximum numbers of people in each group have already been decided. Take a good look at the guidelines on page 5 of your handout.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The students all looked at once. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Group numbers are calculated based on the total number of students, then separated by grade and gender. For instance, if there are 60 or more male students in the same grade level, the minimum number of participants in a group is 8, and the upper limit 13. If the total is 70 or more, the limits are 9 and 14. If the total is 80 or more, the limits are 10 and 15. However, in the event the number of students is under 60, please refer to a separate table.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Let’s assume the ratio of boys to girls in a class didn’t vary between grade levels. If there were 40 students in one class, with a boys to girls ratio of 1:1—20 boys and 20 girls—then that meant, basically, that there were a total of 80 boys across the four classes of a single grade level. That would mean between 10 and 15 people per group, with a total of six groups overall. But the fact that the syllabus referenced the total number of students first meant that these numbers would change depending on how many people had been expelled across all grade levels. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re probably already aware of this, but sorting you into six groups divided by gender means that students from different classes will be mixed together. For the duration of the test, your group members will be your allies. You’ll be in the same boat, as it were.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Isn’t it kinda ridiculous to ask us to be in groups with people from other classes? I mean, they’re our enemies,” muttered Ike loud enough so that Chabashira could hear&#160;him. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He’d probably been unable to contain himself any longer. But his own words must have jogged something in his brain, because he brightened. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, that’s it! We don’t really have to do that, do we? We can divide Class C into two groups. That’ll take care of it, right? Ayanokouji?” he asked, lowering his voice.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was certainly true that Class C could form two groups of ten. However, Ike’s idea just wouldn’t work. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“A good idea, but things aren’t that simple. The rules forbid forming a group with students from just one class. As long as groups abide by the approved headcount, you are free to team up with any class you wish. But each group must have a combination of students from at least two classes. More importantly, these groups aren’t selected through discussion. They must be agreed upon unanimously,” said Chabashira. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">What she’d just said was written plainly in the syllabus. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“Every group must consist of students from at least two or more classes.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So this means we have to make friends with the enemy?” Ike blurted out. It was less of a question and more of something that had just slipped out.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, exactly,” replied Chabashira, looking slightly exasperated. “Of course, you could form a group that consists </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">almost</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> entirely of students from your own class. If you can get just one student from another class, you’ll meet the requirements.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In other words, we could form a group of ten people—the lower limit—and then have nine of those students be from Class C. I couldn’t imagine that kind of group composition would be unanimously approved when we held our school-wide discussions, though. Very few students would be willing to join a group almost entirely composed of people from another class. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, I doubted a group like that would meet unanimous school-wide approval. Very few students would likely be willing to join a group that only had members from another class.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Also, was it better to have more people, or fewer? The same number? If the difference in numbers could create an advantage or disadvantage, a smaller group was a risk. But we couldn’t know which number would be superior without learning the test’s conditions. Our luck would depend on the nature of the exam.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sure you’re wondering if it’s better to have more people in a group or fewer. Numbers will certainly be significant to this test, as you’ll see if you turn to the section marked ‘Outcomes’,” said Chabashira wryly. “I will explain it now.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Apparently, it was easy to tell we’d all been thinking the same thing.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Excuse me, but could you please continue explaining the rules first? I want to learn about the outcomes, but before that, I’d like to know what we’ll be doing as a group,” asked Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I suppose you’re right. If I respond to every one of Ike’s concerns, we won’t get anywhere.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ike scratched his head apologetically again.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Each group will function like a temporary class. I say temporary, but your time together will be significant. Group members will take classes together, of course, but will also cook and clean, bathe and sleep together. You’ll be sharing a variety of life experiences.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Both guys and girls let out agonized wails when they heard the words ‘bathe and sleep together.’</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I really don’t feel like I could live with people from the other classes though,” grumbled Ike. I understood how he felt. Though we’d cooperated with another class during the Sports Festival, that had been a brief and temporary alliance. we hadn’t spent much time together. We hadn’t exactly been together through thick and thin.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, this exam was about to collapse those boundaries. Depending on how things went, some groups might even wind up with a mix of people from all four classes.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“As for outcomes, those are determined by a comprehensive test given on the last day of camp. The contents of this rather significant test are outlined on page 7 of your handouts. Give it a look.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Everyone checked that section.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.00em;">Ethics</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.00em;">Fortitude</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.00em;">Discipline</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.00em;">Initiative</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">These weren’t typical school subjects. This exam was going to be something removed from the course of normal classes like English or mathematics, which tested academic ability, huh? Unfortunately, I doubted the tests we were about to face would have clear answers. The four concepts outlined in the syllabus were abstract ones. I&#160;had yet to see any concrete details of how the test would be conducted. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The schedule laid out in the syllabus only proved my point. After waking up, we’d do morning assignments. Then we would gather at the dojo and engage in </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">zazen</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">, meditative discipline exercises (such as cleaning), followed by breakfast, then by various lessons in a classroom setting. After lunch, students would work on afternoon assignments, and then practice more </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">zazen</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">. Then we would have dinner, bathe, and go to bed. Quite the departure from our lifestyle up until now.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Incidentally, unlike our usual school routine, there would be additional lessons on Saturday mornings. We’d only have Sunday off.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“More details about your schedule will be announced upon arrival at camp. Right now, I cannot tell you what kind of exam you will face on the final day,” added Chabashira.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So we’d need to stay on our toes and play things by ear during the special exam. Maybe the final day would test us on things like </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">zazen, </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">including fine details like posture and manners. Words like </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Speech</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> and </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Production</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> jumped out at me as I scanned the syllabus. They didn’t feel like good signs.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Choosing your groups will be of the utmost importance. All six groups must work together and get through the week-long school camp as a unit. No matter the reason, you cannot withdraw from your group at any time, nor can members be changed. If a student has to withdraw due to illness or injury, then their group must simply deal with their absence and operate as though that student is still part of the group.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In other words, we’d get nowhere if there was discord within the group, or if we antagonized each other. It was looking more and more like we were going to have to juggle vying with other classes with balancing the internal functioning of our own group. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Going by the schedule, lessons began tomorrow morning, which was Friday. We’d be in session until Wednesday of next week. Next Thursday, all grade levels would take the final exam.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Once the first-year students have finished creating their groups, they will rendezvous with the second- and third-year students, who will form their own groups at the same time. In the end, we will have six final groups, each consisting of a mix of approximately thirty to forty-five first-, second-, and third-year students.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was already tough forming a group within the same grade level, but now we were adding other grade levels on top of that. The bus got weirdly quiet.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“To put it more simply,” Chabashira said, “think of groups formed of students in the same grade level as small groups. The groups formed from students in all grade levels, on the other hand, will be large groups.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Each of the six groups formed by students in the same grade level would be a small group. Then those small groups would join with the second- and third-years to ultimately form six large groups.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Outcomes will be determined based on the average exam results of all members of the six individual large groups. The strengths and weaknesses of the other grade levels will heavily influence your results.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">What concerned me here was the difference in group membership numbers. The large groups would consist of about forty people. Averaging out our results </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">should </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">help reduce inequality, but depending on how the smaller groups were formed, there might be significant differences in the number of people in the large groups.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">How we chose to form those large groups was now the most important thing. If this were an academic exam, a large group with only exceptional students in it would easily win. Conversely, students who were deemed unexceptional would inevitably be excluded from the top groups, thus forced to form a lower ranked group. However, this exam couldn’t be won by simply gathering the academically gifted in one place.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I think you’ve probably got the gist of things now. I’ll conclude with the most important piece of information—that is to say, the results of this special exam.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Good question. What did we stand to lose?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The members of large groups whose averaged scores take first through third place will receive both private points and class points. Large groups whose average scores place fourth or below should expect to have points deducted,” explained Chabashira.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The details were, naturally, outlined in the syllabus.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.00em;">Basic Rewards</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">1</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 0.75em; vertical-align: super;">st</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;"> Place: 10,000 private points. 3 class points.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">2</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 0.75em; vertical-align: super;">nd</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;"> Place: 5000 private points. 1 class point.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">3</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 0.75em; vertical-align: super;">rd</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;"> Place: 3000 private points.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">The aforementioned rewards will be distributed to each individual student.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So if a small group of ten people included nine people from the same class, that class would be awarded twenty-seven class points as a result of the group taking first place. Of course, that was describing an ideal scenario, but it would be best to aim to have as many students as possible from our class take first place. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, the more students from the same class there were in a group, the more points that class would have deducted if the group ranked fourth or below. And the larger the group, the trickier it would be to control everyone. The negatives at stake outweighed the relatively few positives.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">4th Place: –5000 private points. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">5th Place: –10,000 private points. –3 class points.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">6th Place: –20,000 private points. –5 class points.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">The aforementioned penalties will be deducted from each individual student.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Private points and class points couldn’t fall below zero, but the deductions would remain as a cumulative deficit that would be applied against any points awarded for future exams. It was something the school had never done before. If the rewards seemed relatively small, compared to the risks, it was because there was a trick to it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira read the next section aloud.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Depending on the number of classes represented in a small group, rewards may be multiplied. They may also be multiplied based on the total number of people in a small group. Please relax, though. These multipliers only apply to rewards gained from placing first, second, or third. They do not apply to the point deductions for fourth place and below.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">These potential reward multipliers went as follows.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If a small group comprised students from two classes, they would receive only the base rewards. However, a small group comprising students from three classes would be rewarded with double the amount of class and private points. A group comprising students from all four classes would be rewarded with triple the amount of class and private points.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Finally, the multiplier changed depending on the number of people in a small group. A group of ten people got the base reward. A group of fifteen people got 1.5 times the reward. In the special event that a small group had only nine people, their reward would be 0.9 times the default.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In other words, if a group comprising students from all four classes obtained first place, they’d hit the 3× multiplier. If that same group </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">also </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">had the maximum number of allowed members, i.e., fifteen, they’d earn an additional 1.5× multiplier. Rounded to the nearest whole integer, this meant each member of the group would be awarded 45,000 private points and fourteen class points.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So far, this seemed like a standard special exam; tricky but interesting. But what came next changed everything.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Also…” Chabashira said. “There is a significant penalty for the large group that comes in last place.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“A penalty…? No way.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. The penalty is expulsion.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">No surprises there.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Granted, not every member of the large group that comes in last will be expelled. If we did that, we’d be expelling nearly forty students at once. Expulsion will only apply to the small group within that large group whose average score falls below the threshold set by the school.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So overall rankings were calculated based on the average score of each large group, but when it came to expulsions, it was the average scores of the small groups that were taken into account. That was troubling.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If a small group fails to meet that standard, then that small group’s representative will be expelled.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How do we pick the representatives?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You will discuss it with the members of your small group and appoint somebody. That is all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The hell? Who’d willingly want to be the representative knowing they might get expelled?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I doubted there’d be many volunteers.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There are significant benefits to being a group representative. The representative and the representative’s classmates will receive double the rewards.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Did you say double?” muttered Horikita, who had been silent until now.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s right. To secure the highest possible payout from this exam, you would have to form a group comprising twelve Class C students and one student from each of the remaining three classes. If you then appoint someone from Class C as your representative and take first place…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wh-what do we win?” asked Yamauchi, unable to perform the calculations himself. He was practically hyperventilating.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’d get 1.08 million private points and 336 class points.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Th-three hundred thirty-six?!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If that happened, it would overturn the class rankings entirely. Even jumping all the way to A Class as a result of this exam wasn’t out of the question, depending on how the other groups scored. The more risks we took, the greater the rewards—and the odds of securing the highest achievable reward weren’t too low, either.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Once the small groups have been decided, you will have until tomorrow morning to appoint your representative. In the event a group cannot choose a representative, that group will immediately be disqualified, and every single one of its members will be expelled. Of course, no group would ever be so stupid as to make such a mistake.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So the school wouldn’t be picking representatives—it was solely up to us to decide who to appoint. A difficult decision, to be sure. If no one volunteered, we might wind up having to draw straws or play rock-paper-scissors…an inevitable conclusion in light of the fact we’d all be expelled otherwise, but in an already fraught situation such as this, it would put a strain on the group’s unity.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Finally, the representative who is expelled may choose one other person from within their group to share their fate. We’re calling this one the solidarity rule. Think of it as going down together.’”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“H-huh?! What’s with that?! That’s just crazy! So, if we appointed some random dude as the representative, they could take down the leader of another class?!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I couldn’t imagine it would be that easy. Selecting a representative would involve a certain degree of screening. We probably wouldn’t be picking obvious sacrificial pawns to fill the role, and if we did, it would be the group’s fault. It was unlikely we’d find someone willing to both sacrifice themselves for the group’s sake and take down a student from an enemy class in the process…unless, of course, we were talking about someone who’d been trapped in Class D for years and was on the verge of dropping out anyway. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But if there were students who were contemplating giving up in that way, their peers were probably aware of the fact.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t worry,” Chabashira said. “The representative can’t just take </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">any</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> student down with them. Only students whom the school deems contributory to the group’s failure can be selected for expulsion in this way. Unless you deliberately score poorly or boycott the test, you’ll be fine.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Well, that was something, at least. However, I had my doubts about the representatives for this exam. This was unlike any previous situation we’d been in—particularly the fact that this time, the exam was the same for all grade levels. The other classes were probably receiving the exact same instructions right now. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In other words, many different strategies were currently in the process of being formed. This wasn’t just a fight between the first-years. The second-years were battling other second-years and third-years other third-years. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I sent off a text message to a certain individual, hoping to clear a few things up. I wanted to know if the student council was somehow involved in creating this special exam.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“One more important detail. If a student is expelled, that student’s class will be penalized accordingly. The specifics of the penalty change depending on the exam. In the case of this test, one hundred points will be deducted from the class. If the class has insufficient class points to pay off the penalty, they will be in debt. Until that debt is paid off, their total will remain at zero.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The benefits were enormous, but the negatives were just as vast. The promise of doubling your points by being a group representative was tantalizing, but it came with the risk of being expelled. No one would willingly volunteer for such a role unless they were confident in the abilities of their small group. Nor, however, would they want the spoils of the representative’s victory to go to another class. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Then there was the solidarity rule, which was clearly designed to leave you at an impasse.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That concludes my explanation. I will now open the floor for questions.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata immediately raised his hand.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If someone were to be expelled…would there be any way to help that person, like a lifeline?” he asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you’re expelled, you’re expelled. Nothin’ ya can do ’bout it,” replied Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata rejected that outright. “That can’t be true. You were almost expelled once, Sudou-kun, but were saved thanks to Horikita-san’s quick thinking. It would be strange if there was nothing we could do.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira smiled.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s correct. You can buy a Revocation of Expulsion using private points as a last resort. Naturally, the price will be high. The Revocation of Expulsion costs twenty million private points </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">and </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">an additional three hundred class points. This lifeline will only prevent a student’s expulsion. It won’t cancel out the penalties suffered by the class as a result of that expulsion. Of course, if you don’t have the points to pay for the lifeline, then that option is unavailable.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">An exorbitant price. It meant a minimum of four hundred class points would be required to save a student on the brink of expulsion, which made it a lifeline we were unlikely to extend. The whole class would have to pay a great toll to save one person.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Regarding the twenty million points you mentioned, can the entire class pitch in?” asked Hirata, who was clearly considering a future where we might use the lifeline.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, everyone may pitch in. But this is completely irrelevant, since you have only a few points available.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">With that, Chabashira was done reviewing the materials.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There isn’t much time left before we reach our destination. You are free to use the remaining time however you like. Right before we arrive, I will re-collect the handout. Also, the use of phones will be forbidden for a week. I&#160;will be collecting those shortly. Other than that, you are free to bring daily necessities or playthings along with you, but not food. Perishables must be consumed before we arrive, or you’ll have to throw them away before you get off the bus. That’s all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Students who hadn’t reacted to the dangers of the special exam now let out agonized wails at that last comment. We’d already gone through this on the uninhabited island, but I supposed it was painful to have your phone confiscated for a whole week.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I have a question!” Ike raised his hand energetically. “You said that guys and girls would be separate, but, like, exactly how spread out are we gonna be?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira wore a wry smile. “There are two buildings at the camp. The boys will use the main building, and the girls will take the other. The buildings are next to one another, but you’ll be living apart from each other for a week. You will not be allowed to go outside during recess or after school without permission either.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So we won’t be able to talk to each other?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, boys and girls will take meals together in the cafeteria in the main building for one hour a day. The school issues no directives to students during that time period. You can do as you please then. Understand?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes!” Ike rejoiced, probably happy he’d be able to talk to girls. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I sat up slightly and glanced at Shinohara, who was sitting nearby. While she looked slightly exasperated, her face brightened at Ike’s words. Maybe their Christmas dinner had gone well.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If there are no further questions, that’ll be all,” said Chabashira, probably anticipating a lot of silly questions coming her way.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sensei. May I borrow your microphone?” asked Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course.” Chabashira handed it over and returned to her seat. Hirata took her place at the front of the bus.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Based on what sensei said, we’re running out of time. Still, I’d like to try to hear everyone’s opinions. How can we get through this exam? What kind of groups should we shoot for?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wouldn’t it be best if we tried to get as many of our classmates in as possible? Pick twelve toppers and then get one person from every other class. That’d be perfect,” said Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That would be ideal, but I doubt three students from other classes would want to join that group. They’d naturally be on their guard.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It would be obvious that such a group wanted to win badly. I couldn’t imagine single students from each of the other classes being willing to join them. Besides, if such a group ranked poorly, the damage to the class would be considerable.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, if all the smart people get in one group, then the rest of us don’t stand a chance,” said Yamauchi. Apparently, he still hadn’t figured out that this wasn’t about academics. “I mean, we really wanna get some private points ourselves.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">His complaints were understandable. This issue had come up during the exam on the cruise ship too. The large group who ranked first would earn private points, but the students in the bottom groups gained nothing. In fact, they would lose a great deal. Given that, most people obviously wanted to bet on the winning horse by joining a large group that was likely to win.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If everyone here agrees, I’d like to propose an even distribution,” Hirata said. “We don’t know which group will come out on top. If any of us gain private points from this exam, we’ll distribute those points evenly across the class. That should be fine, since point transfers are allowed.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">And if we got hit with a deduction, it wouldn’t be so bad, since everyone would be sharing the load.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah, I see. That’s okay.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Of course, the more exceptional students would complain about this, but considering what was at stake, a consensus seemed likely. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Heh.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After hearing Hirata’s proposal, Chabashira chuckled, facing away from him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I didn’t tell you this because you didn’t ask, but as a reward for your promotion to Class C, I will give you one bit of helpful advice.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Advice?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata looked cautious, not ready to accept this reward at face value.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“When the rules don’t forbid it, you’re certainly free to transfer points. Be it in the middle of an exam or in the course of your daily life—you can transfer points as much as you please, as long as you don’t break any rules in the process. However, private points are not pocket money. You’d do well to remember that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you saying we could, like, transfer to any class we wanted if we accumulated like twenty million points? Or&#160;is this about the lifeline?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m saying there are many different ways of using private points. Having as little as one point can help you in a time of need. Getting along and supporting each other isn’t always going to be the correct answer, you understand? For example, let’s say Ike made a mistake that would lead to him being expelled unless a million private points were paid right away—and the school disallowed transferring points in that instance. Ike would have to produce the million points himself or be expelled. What then? If you divide your points up equally, you might end up doing something you can’t take back.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ike gulped loudly when he heard himself used as an example.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You can’t count on other students to save you, because every single one of them might find themselves in need the next moment. The only one who can protect you is you, yourself,” said Chabashira. “People who work hard are rewarded. That much is obvious. Once you enter the real world, it’s exceedingly unlikely you’ll find people who’d gladly share their salary and bonuses with friends.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Perhaps we should be grateful for the advice…but this was just going to make it harder to unite the class. I didn’t doubt everything she was saying was true; I couldn’t imagine any teacher at this school would stir up trouble just because there was no precedent for something. Chabashira always played by the rules.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That said, there was more to this conversation.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was sure there had been prior instances of individuals saving up private points. But conversely, I was equally sure there had been incidents where students were saved because their classmates had accumulated a great many points. How did I know this? Well, from personal experience. In the past, Horikita and I worked out a solution to provide Sudou with points when it seemed he was about to be expelled. That then set a precedent. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ultimately, sharing points evenly among ourselves could be seen as a preventative measure to be deployed in unforeseen situations. Having individual students hold onto a large number of points raised the risk of that person embezzling those points or betraying the class. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira had just said something that disrupted her own class’s chances at cohesion. I couldn’t reject the possibility that it was just school policy, of course, but…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, shall we put it to a majority vote? I’d like to at least hear what everyone is thinking. Can the people who want to split the rewards please raise your hands? It’s okay if you change your mind later,” said Hirata. He lifted his hand at once.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Only a few hands went up. Most of my classmates looked worried. Coming together as a class was important, sure, but when push came to shove, you had to have a way to protect yourself. Most students probably had only somewhere between ten thousand to a hundred thousand private points. Given that, it was no wonder many wanted to keep those points as a safety net.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Students who lacked confidence in their own abilities, on the other hand, were more likely to want points to be shared across the class. There were a few more such students than I’d expected, but in the end, less than half the class raised their hands.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thank you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The majority was against point splitting. Hirata’s motion had failed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Was my advice unnecessary, Hirata?” Chabashira asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, I’m thankful for it. It’s valuable information at this stage.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">My phone vibrated. Thinking the certain individual whom I’d messaged might have replied, I took my phone out of my pocket—but it was his little sister, Horikita, who’d texted me. Naturally, it was about the special exam.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you have any ideas?”</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> she wrote. Jeez, always trying to make me do things.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“None,”</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> I replied. After thinking it over, I sent another message. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“Guys and girls are going to be separated in this exam. I can’t help with anything. Do your best.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">My attempt to offer her some encouragement. I was sure Horikita had a lot she wanted to say in return, but I didn’t want to listen. I closed the text thread and checked on another of my group chats: that of the Ayanokouji Group (named after myself, but not in a boastful way).</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Keisei, Akito, and even Airi and Haruka all happily discussed the exam. I read through their texts, closed the chat without adding any comments, and resumed listening to Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We don’t have enough time to come up with a strategy,” he was saying. “If the boys and girls are going to be separated, sending advice to each other will get tricky.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No way…” The girls looked understandably uneasy at the prospect of being unable to turn to Hirata, who held the class together and had always been a guy they could rely on.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Since the boys won’t be able to lend a hand, I think the girls should decide on a leader right now. Will you accept the role, Horikita-san?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata had probably been chewing over this since Chabashira began to explain the exam. He’d singled out Horikita because she was the only one capable of handling the role.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Very well. If anyone’s having difficulties, feel free to approach me at any time. I don’t mind,” replied Horikita. She showed no signs of displeasure, but, even though she was gradually becoming more of a person our classmates could depend on, she didn’t warrant anywhere near the level of trust that Hirata did. Of course, being Horikita, she was surely aware of that. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“However, I’m sure quite a few of the other girls will find me lacking,” she continued. “I don’t like to say this of myself, but I have a somewhat abrasive personality.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yeah, that really wasn’t something anyone would want to admit.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Which is why I’d like Kushida-san to assist me. What do you think?” said Horikita, directing this to Kushida, who sat near the front of the bus.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“W-will I even be useful?” asked Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course. Everyone in our class trusts you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Um… Well, okay. If you’d like, I’ll help.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thank you. In the event someone finds it difficult to speak directly with me, you can do so via Kushida-san. I don’t mind. I will respond to any issue, no matter how trivial.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">While Kushida’s trustworthiness was a matter of some concern, this strategy was unquestionably the best we had right now. The exam rules made it fairly difficult for guys and girls to meddle in each other’s affairs. Despite being held in the same general facility, our classes and tests would take place in separate locations. The guys would have no opportunity to participate in any of the battles being fought on the girls’ side. With our phones being confiscated to boot, our only opportunity for contact would be the one hour we had for dinner.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That being said, it was vital we be able to gather as much information as possible. I was going to need a gofer to funnel information to me from the girls. Kushida worried me. This left Horikita or Kei as options, and the former had a lot on her plate right now. I also had to consider the fact that she tended to read too much into my intentions and take unnecessary actions. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">More importantly, if she was going to be fielding consultation pleas from the other girls, she probably wouldn’t have energy to spare for much else. I supposed I’d have to use Kei after all, though I couldn’t force her to survey the entire group by herself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I sent the bare minimum of necessary information over to Kei’s phone. She immediately replied with a blank message to confirm receipt. Given we’d just learned that the unique nature of this test would force the guys and girls to do battle separately for a while, she must have assumed I’d be contacting her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kei probably wanted some advice herself right now. Considering the rules of this exam, particularly the ones about the representatives and their ability to take someone down with them, it wasn’t outside the realm of possibility that Kei might wind up as a sacrificial pawn. I&#160;couldn’t say she was doing well at the moment, whether in terms of her test scores or her behavior in class.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was why I was going to teach her a few tricks to protect herself. They weren’t things that every student could pull off, but they could keep you safer, if only slightly so.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As for me, I couldn’t really care less about the special exam. I had no intention of executing any winning strategies. All I wanted was to make it through safely and without incident.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This didn’t mean I wouldn’t be making any moves at all—like what I’d just done by giving Kei advice. In a worst-case scenario, this special exam could lead to several students getting expelled from C Class. I couldn’t protect the entire class by myself. I had to narrow down the list of people I wanted to safeguard.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Besides myself, I wanted to protect Kei, who’d become a staunch ally, and Hirata. Considering my involvement with the student council, I needed to ensure that Horikita stuck around, too. Then there were my friends: Keisei, Akito, Haruka, and Airi. They couldn’t be my top priority, but as their friend, I certainly hoped they wouldn’t be expelled. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Finally, given this rare opportunity for grade levels to intermingle, I should keep an eye on Nagumo’s movements. Any other minor skirmishes that occurred around me were beneath my notice.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">2.1</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HE BUS GOT OFF</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> the highway and began to drive up the paved mountain road. I wondered why our school outings always took place in nature, like this location or the ocean. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">At any rate, a new special exam was about to begin. Given they were confiscating our phones, this was certainly going to be that annoying kind of test where you had to gather information either on your own or by using your connections. And, since more information would be leaked the more carelessly you acted, prudence and discretion would be vital.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not cut out for this…” I muttered, letting my honest thoughts slip forth. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">No matter how many special exams we went through, I just couldn’t get used to them. I’d so rarely had to cooperate with other people before.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We will arrive shortly,” Chabashira said. “Once there, you will form your groups. Then, as soon as room assignments have been completed, you will have lunch, followed by free time in the afternoon.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That means… Yay! We don’t have to study!” Ike beamed at me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">True, but this wasn’t vacation. It was a school day. Even taking the travel time into account, it was odd that they were letting us off the hook this afternoon—it felt almost like a normal field trip, which couldn’t be right.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The bus pulled into the stop and slowed to a halt.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“When your name is called, hand over your phone and get off the bus. Ayanokouji. Ike—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira started with the boys, calling our names in syllabary order. I turned my phone off and put it in a plastic box that sat next to our teacher. As I disembarked from the bus, an unfamiliar teacher approached, instructing us to wait a short distance away from the bus.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Dude, it’s cold!” shouted Ike, hugging himself tightly. Probably because we were in the mountains. It was certainly colder here than at school. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, the sight before us made us forget the cold for a moment.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Whoa. What </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">is </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">this place? This seems a lot for a ‘school camp’…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Enormous grounds spread out before us, with some </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">extremely</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> old-fashioned school buildings visible in the distance. Our home for the next week. They were huge, probably because they had to accommodate the students of all three grade levels.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It felt similar to the test on the uninhabited island. As with that test, I really didn’t have much experience living out in the wilds like this. People like Ike, who had been a boy scout, were going to be useful here. So would those like Sudou, with their physical strength. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The girls filed off the bus next. It seemed like Horikita wanted to come talk to me, but unfortunately, we were already being queued up into separate lines. The boys and girls each headed for our respective school buildings, with the boys being directed to the larger one, referred to as the main building. Once inside, I found my nostrils tickled by the nostalgic scent of the wood-paneled interior. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This really is a traditional wooden school building, isn’t it? It’s old, but still beautiful. It must be exceptionally well maintained,” said Hirata. Everyone else agreed. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Along the way, we saw what appeared to be a classroom. There was no air conditioning, only a single stove placed in the center of the room. We’d most likely be having classes from tomorrow in rooms like that. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Finally, we arrived at what seemed like a gymnasium. The guys from Class A and Class B, who were already there, looked over our way. The Class D students showed up right after us, so the second- and third-year students were probably on their way in next. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We were ordered to form a line and await further instruction. Classes A and B looked calm and didn’t chat amongst themselves. They’d probably already finished strategizing on the bus, huh?</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">2.2</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">B</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">OYS FROM EVERY</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> grade level assembled inside the gymnasium. The bashful first-year students clumped together to wait in silence, and soon after, a teacher from one of the senior classes took to the stage, microphone in hand. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m going to assume you all understand the contents of the exam after the explanation you were given on the bus ride,” he addressed us. “Each grade level will now hold internal discussions and form into six small groups. The large groups will be formed at 8:00 p.m. today. That is all. As a reminder, the school will play no part in the formation of these groups, whether large or small. Nor will school officials act as mediators.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Okay, then. I wondered what the other classes’ strategies were? They should have some plans in place already, but…we’d see how it went.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Each grade level—first-, second-, and third-years—distanced themselves from one another, and the discussions began. I was curious about what the seniors were doing, but it was hard to make out any details from here. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As I attempted to observe them, though, there was already movement taking place within the first-year classes. I’d thought we’d try to feel each other out a little longer, but Class A got straight to work, forming one large group. A pretty attention-grabbing move, given the stalemate we were in.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There were twenty boys in first-year Class A. Fourteen of them formed up into a single group, then made the following declaration to the people in Class B and below. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“As you can see, we in Class A intend to form a single group in this fashion. We have fourteen people at present and need one more person to meet the requirements. We are looking for someone to join us.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The person who said this was a Class A student named Matoba. I saw Katsuragi among the fourteen boys in the group, but if Matoba was the leader, did that mean Katsuragi wasn’t the representative either? At any rate, this made it clear that Class A’s strategy was to form a group made up of as many of their own people as possible.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, hey! What the hell are you selfish punks doing? It ain’t fair if you’re the only ones who go and do something like that,” said Sudou, glaring angrily at Class&#160;A.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is it selfish? Our group would comprise students representing only two classes. Even if we take first place, our modifier will be low. I don’t think this is a greedy proposal at all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“W-well, sure. But it’s unfair that there’s fourteen of ya.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“On the contrary, it’s very fair. Since the remaining three classes can create three groups of fifteen people each, you can all make groups similar to ours. Right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Uh, I guess?” said Sudou, not quite understanding. He looked to Hirata for help.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That is correct, yes,” said Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Then this discussion is pointless. By the way, we’ve agreed that the six remaining boys from Class A will happily join whatever groups you make, no matter their makeup,” said Matoba, smiling broadly. He turned to Class B’s Kanzaki and Shibata as well.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Um… Well, I guess this isn’t really a bad deal at all. What do you think, Kanzaki?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry, I need more time to answer that,” Kanzaki said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I can’t imagine that Class A students would go so far as to deliberately drag the other classes down, but I suppose it’s best to be cautious…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Class A was pushing everyone to come to a decision right away, but Kanzaki didn’t seem inclined to rush. In response to his stalling, Matoba came back with a strongly worded statement. “You have five minutes. Please make your decision by then.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“A time limit? We’ve only just begun to discuss this. Class A gave us an opinion, not an order—you don’t get to make an unilateral decision here. A five-minute window is absurd.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Despite Class A’s claim that going along with their proposal meant each class could make their own group of fourteen people, it would be a lie to say that this was fair for all classes. If you thought about it, Class A was the only class that could afford not to care about a low reward modifier. They were currently ranked first and leading in points. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I suppose it wouldn’t be fair of us to make this decision solely by ourselves,” Matoba said. “But you misunderstand. We’re not saying we won’t negotiate after the five minutes are up. We’re just offering special terms for someone within that five minutes.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Special terms?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Matoba continued to control the conversation, precisely because the other classes had yet to solidify what they wanted. This was certainly close to what you’d call a preemptive strike.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Class A is going to form one group of fourteen people, meaning we require one student from another class. Setting aside the merits of this strategy, it’s certainly true that we’re selfishly pushing this idea on you. As such, the single person whom we will welcome into our group will receive special treatment,” he explained eloquently. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This had to be the strategy they’d come up with on the bus.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The student who joins our group will bear no risk whatsoever. Katsuragi-kun will act as the group’s representative, and in the unlikely event that we place last, he will take full responsibility. No one will be dragged down via the solidarity rule. Of course, this only applies so long as the special entrant doesn’t intentionally lower our scores or hurt our friends. If your exam scores are genuinely poor despite your best efforts, that’s fine.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So these were the special terms, huh?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you serious…?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The proposal had its merits. Gathering skilled team members in one place to create a group with a high point modifier might be necessary to advance a class, but it was those skilled people—the people at the very heart of the class—who bore the risks of such a strategy going sour. From the perspective of an average student, who grappled with fears of expulsion, Matoba’s proposed system of special treatment wasn’t a bad idea at all. It would guarantee they made it safely through this special exam.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Still, why wasn’t Katsuragi the one suggesting this? Was this the fallout of him losing status within the class?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We intend to take first place, meaning there is a strong possibility that our special entrant will be rewarded with private points. Aren’t many of you nervous about this special exam?” asked Matoba, looking around. His words clearly resonated with the less secure students. “However, if you cannot decide within the five-minute window, the special offer expires. In the unlikely event that our class does receive a penalty, we will not hesitate to drag that student down with us.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s an interesting proposal. But after the five minutes have passed, the value of joining your group plummets significantly. No one wants to join a team with such a high risk of taking them down with it,” said Kanzaki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, that’s right. Who’d be crazy enough to do that?” said a student who’d been briefly charmed by the idea of the special terms.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t mind what you think. But these are our terms,” said Matoba, taking a step back. His group moved with him, signaling the end of the discussion.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I think we’re better off ignoring them. Once five minutes have passed, no one will want to join their group. They’ll be back,” said Kanzaki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Suppose so,” said Shibata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Having said that, they moved calmly away. I didn’t see Kaneda and the other newly demoted Class D students making any strange movements either. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata, however, seemed to be the sole person who felt differently about Class A’s proposal. He approached Keisei, Akito, and me, asking, “What do you think?” in a low voice.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“About their strategy?” replied Keisei, taking the lead in the conversation.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. Surprisingly enough, I don’t think it’s a bad deal. It is absolutely essential that everyone in Class C makes it through. We’ve only just been promoted, and I don’t want any of my classmates to be expelled. If a student who’s nervous about this exam joins Class A’s group, they should be able to rest easy,” said Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As a defensive strategy, Class A’s proposal had its merits. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course, it remains to be seen whether Class A will keep their promise of special treatment. If they wind up taking last place, they might use the solidarity rule to take someone down with them, after all,” he added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata’s anxieties were understandable. Verbal agreements </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">were</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> binding in nature, but even if we confronted Class A about it in the wake of a betrayal, they could trap us in endless arguments that went nowhere. If they feigned ignorance, then things would get complicated. After all, their promise hinged on no one intentionally sabotaging the group. If a student’s exam scores were low, it would be difficult to prove whether or not it was intentional. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Still, we couldn’t exactly get it in writing; there weren’t any pens or paper at hand. The teachers had been ordered not to help, so asking them to bear witness to a verbal promise would probably be useless too.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That being said, Matoba’s words had piqued the interest of all of the first-year students. He stood to gain nothing by going back on his words. It might be okay to trust Class A on this.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…It might be possible to have them protect one person,” I stated, joining Hirata and Keisei’s conversation.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. If we make a move now, that just leaves the question of what B and D decide…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Accepting the offer would be seen as siding with Class A, who had opted for a heavy-handed approach. Even though we only had a tiny window, Hirata seemed to want to think this over until the last possible second. Roughly three minutes had passed since Class A’s sudden proposal. We didn’t know whether they were dutifully counting every second, but Matoba and the others seemed to be leisurely standing by. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Maybe they were expecting someone to raise their hand. Or maybe they were thinking up another strategy. We watched them carefully for the remaining two minutes, waiting for them to make their move—though what they would do, of course, depended on the leaders of classes B and below. </span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What are you plotting?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s simple. In return, I want the lion’s share of the reward for first place.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t think people would resist the idea, but then again, they all understood that taking in Ryuuen came with great risks. It was just that, well…I never imagined </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Akito would make a move with the intention of getting the reward. I suspected he was simply trying to come up with a reason to take Ryuuen in, since no one else was willing to do so. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What are you proposing, exactly? You’re not planning to use the solidarity rule to take someone else down with you if it comes to that, are you?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Unless someone tries to blatantly sabotage me, I won’t do that. And the rules say I can’t, anyway.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The members of the hypothetical groups fell silent after hearing Akito’s well-reasoned argument. And so, despite encountering a few bumps in the road, the first-years finally managed to form six groups. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This included my group, which was composed as follows:</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">From Class C, we had Kouenji, Keisei, and me. Three people.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">From Class B, we had Sumida, Moriyama, and&#160;Tokitou. Three people.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">From Class A, we had Yahiko and Hashimoto. Two people. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Then, from Class D, we had Ishizaki and Albert. Two people.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ten people in total.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Our group was clearly unlike the other five. We had a far more even mix of students from different classes, though I supposed the group Akito was representing wasn’t too far off. However, my group still needed a representative. None of us seemed to have great leadership skills, and no one was stepping forward to claim the role. With no one around to take the lead and guide everyone toward a consensus, we all sat there, not knowing what to say.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Anyway, we had to at least report to the school that we’d successfully formed our group. We could select the representative later. So the ten of us—group number six—headed over to report to the school officials.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even though we managed to avoid having Ryuuen, I’m still worried about getting a decent average score,” said Keisei anxiously.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wasn’t certain how good the other students were either. Personally, I’d wanted to avoid being in the same group as Ishizaki and Albert, but there was nothing to be done about it at this point. Ishizaki was blatantly refusing to meet my eyes, but that didn’t necessarily mean that a third party was going to notice anything was up. Most people would assume he simply thought nothing of me. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Kouenji is going to be a problem too,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If Kouenji would take things seriously, he’d be unstoppable. His athletic and academic abilities were impressive. But “take things seriously” was where things fell apart.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I mean, he’s Kouenji, but he wouldn’t really do something that gets us penalized, right? If he does, maybe it’s all over.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had a feeling Kouenji would score above average, but would do it in an aimless, noncommittal way. The only certain thing about him was that his motives were impenetrable. If he remained unmotivated, our future was uncertain. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When we finished giving our report, I noticed a group hanging around. It was the one with mostly Class A students, who should’ve been long gone. At first, I thought they wanted to find out how the remaining five groups had organized themselves, but it seemed that wasn’t the case. There were second- and third-year students waiting around, too. More importantly, so was Student Council President Nagumo Miyabi, who ruled the second-years with an iron hand. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After confirming that all the first-year students had formed their groups, he called out to me. “You guys were surprisingly quick. I thought that would take you longer.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It seemed the second- and third-year students were almost all done composing their small groups too.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I have a proposal for you first-years,” Nagumo said. “How about we form a large group right now?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Aren’t we going to decide that tonight, Nagumo-senpai?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s just the school being flexible. They didn’t expect that the small groups would form so quickly. Since all grade levels have finished, wouldn’t it be best if we moved on to the next step?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Apparently, his suggestion was unexpected—even to the teachers. Sensing that we were about to start forming large groups, the teachers began to move in a hurry. Since the student council president himself had proposed this, the other students could hardly refuse.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You don’t mind, do you, Horikita-senpai?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t. This is more convenient for us too.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Discussions were underway, with Nagumo at the center.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, how do we do this? Should we have a draft-pick system? One person from each of the first-year small groups plays rock-paper-scissors against the others. The outcomes determine the order in which people make their picks. Based on that order, they can choose which second- or third-year small groups they want, and thus we’ll get large groups. A fair and quick process.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The first-years don’t have much information to go on. That doesn’t seem fair.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s never going to be completely fair. We all have different amounts of information, in the end.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">A brief but important conversation between Nagumo and Horikita’s brother followed. There was no way a first-year could interrupt.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><img alt="" height="100%" id="ClassroomElite-Vol8_INTERIORIMAGES_" src="/hola-lector-agua/images/books/v8/OEBPS/Images/ClassroomElite-Vol8_INTERIORIMAGES_.jpg" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style=" top: 0px; vertical-align: top; height: 100%;"/></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do you first-years think? If you have any complaints, speak up,” said Nagumo, knowing full well that no one would talk back to him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We have no objections,” answered Matoba, apparently now representing the first-years.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. In that case, how ’bout we get started?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Nagumo smiled, and then joined up with his own small group. The second- and third-year students reformed their groups so that it was easier for us to tell them apart. Then the representatives of five small first-year groups stepped forward. Nagumo regarded us fondly, like we were adorable children.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Now all that’s left is </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">that</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> group.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">My small group was the only one that had yet to pick a representative, so naturally, none of us were stepping up to play rock-paper-scissors. I lightly pushed Keisei, making sure I wasn’t noticed. He looked puzzled but then reluctantly raised his hand.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The six representatives formed a circle and started playing. Keisei was selected to pick fourth. First up was Matoba’s group, which was mostly filled with Class A students. Second was Hirata’s group, consisting mostly of Class C students. Third was the Class D group, represented by Kaneda. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You can discuss among yourselves which group you’d like to pick.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Two groups that immediately stood out as top choices: the group containing the leader of second-year Class A, the student council president Nagumo, and the group led by Horikita’s brother, a third-year student. But someone like Hirata, who had many friends and acquaintances across different grade levels, might be able to spot an excellent group that didn’t appear to be one at first glance. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Matoba’s group, the first to pick, chose the third-year group containing Horikita Manabu without hesitation. Next, Hirata carefully assessed each of the eleven groups. In the end, he didn’t go with one of the two obvious choices I mentioned but picked a group of third-years that didn’t have a single person I recognized.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, Hirata, you sure that’s a good idea? Shouldn’t you pick the student council president’s or something?” Ike interrupted, unsurprisingly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, I’m sure. I think this is a good choice. Exceptional people have their appeal, sure, but they can bring trouble in their wake. Besides, the seniors I chose aren’t half bad,” replied Hirata, nodding with apparent confidence.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ike decided not to push the matter, a sign of the trust Hirata had cultivated with our class.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The majority-Class-D group was next. Kaneda consulted his classmates—which is to say he simply informed them which group he wanted to choose. There were no objections, and he made his selection immediately. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I would like to join second-year Gouda-senpai’s group, please.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Once again, Nagumo’s group had been passed over. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wonder why they’re avoiding Nagumo,” I muttered doubtfully. Akito, standing next to me, offered an answer.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Because aside from Nagumo-senpai, the other members are kind of iffy.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is that so?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I suppose they’re not </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">all</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> iffy, but there are a lot of C and D guys. The second-year group with lots of Class A students is the one Kaneda picked.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In other words, it wasn’t that Kaneda had avoided picking Nagumo—just that he had chosen reliable, strong allies. I </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">was</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> curious as to why Nagumo hadn’t formed a group of mostly Class A students. I knew he controlled all the second-years; bringing members of his own class together seemed like a more reliable option.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Finally, it was Keisei’s turn. “Is it okay for me to decide?” he asked the rest of the group.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t care. Don’t really get it, anyway,” said Ishizaki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It seemed Ishizaki, and by extension the Class D students, were fine with leaving the decision to Keisei. The Class A students had no real opinion on the matter either. The Class B students said nothing at first, but after thinking it over for a while, they made the following request.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Please pick Nagumo-senpai’s group.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Though Nagumo’s group was mostly composed of Class C and D students, I was guessing it was the presence of the council president that made them rate it so highly. After hearing the Class B students’ request, Keisei chose Nagumo’s group.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After that, discussion came to an end. The six large groups had formed successfully.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Horikita-senpai, since we’re in separate large groups, how about we have ourselves a little contest?” proposed Nagumo. Horikita shot him a sharp look.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I heard a somewhat exasperated sigh, and a third-year named Fujimaki stepped forward to admonish Nagumo. I recognized him as the person who’d taken charge during the Sports Festival, meaning he wielded a certain degree of influence. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nagumo. How many times have you done this? Enough already.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Whatever do you mean, Fujimaki-senpai?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You keep challenging Horikita to competitions. I’ve never interfered before, but this is a large-scale special exam that includes the first-year students. You cannot treat this like your own personal game.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why do you say that? Distinctions like first and third year no longer apply right now. Challenging someone under such conditions is hardly unusual, is it? There’s nothing in the special exam rulebook to forbid it.” Rather than cower before Fujimaki’s impressive bulk, Nagumo chose to taunt him. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We’re talking fundamental morals here. Even if something isn’t expressly against the rules, some actions are good, and some are bad. That’s obvious.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t really think so. If anything, it’s precisely seniors like you who hinder the growth of younger students by refusing to do battle with them, wouldn’t you say?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You may be student council president, but that doesn’t mean you can do as you please. You need to be aware of the fact that you’re overstepping your authority.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you think that’s the case, then please, </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">make</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> me aware of it. How about </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">you</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> be my opponent, Fujimaki-senpai? You’re third-year Class A’s number two, aren’t you?” replied Nagumo, stuffing his hands arrogantly into his pockets, acting as though Fujimaki were an afterthought.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was cheap provocation, but it seemed to successfully antagonize some of the third-years. A few students started to step forward. However, Horikita kept them&#160;back.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I have rejected your demands until now,” Horikita said. “Do you know why?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hmm, let’s see. I’d say it’s because your friends are scared you might lose, but that can’t be right. You are superior to every other person I’ve met, Horikita-senpai. You’re not afraid to lose. You never even </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">think</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> that you could lose.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The second-year students listening to Nagumo wore worshipful looks on their faces. He wasn’t a friend or a patron. He was a rival, a hated enemy, but also someone they deeply respected. It seemed he inspired a variety of powerful emotions, anyway.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In the two years he’d been at this school, Nagumo had accomplished many things that no ordinary person could. Not even the third-year students could comprehend the extent of his achievements. The first-year students even less so.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m the same as you, Fujimaki-senpai. I don’t want any futile conflict either.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The conflict you </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">do</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> want gets too many other people involved.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But that’s just how this school works. And I think that’s the best part of it… Well, I suppose it comes down to a difference of opinion. At any rate, I wish we could have had a little showdown in that relay during the Sports Festival, senpai. Alas, that didn’t happen. I’m still really frustrated about that, y’know?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I can’t imagine what a showdown between the second- and third-years would do to help us in this exam.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re probably right. You’re that kind of person, senpai. But all I want is a battle between the former student council president and the current student council president. You’re going to graduate soon. Before that happens, I want to see whether I’ve surpassed you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There was no telling if Nagumo would ever stop. It was like he was possessed by some kind of unceasing longing. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What would the battle even be?” Horikita asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The third-year students looked shocked at the implication he might be about to accept Nagumo’s challenge.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How about which of us can get the most students expelled?” replied Nagumo.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Everyone, from first-years to third-years, started murmuring amongst themselves.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Stop joking.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I really do think that would be interesting. But if you insist…how about which group can earn the higher average score? Simple and easy to understand.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Very well. I accept.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thank you. I knew you wouldn’t disappoint, senpai.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“However, this is a personal battle between you and me. Don’t drag anyone else into it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t drag others into it? Given the rules of this special exam, I would think getting someone to drag your opponent’s group down was a valid strategy.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s the very opposite of what the essence of this exam is meant to be. This is, at most, an exercise to test our group’s unity. The point of the test isn’t to exploit a weakness in an opponent’s group, even if you happen upon one by accident.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…What does that mean?” said Ishizaki, seemingly directing his question at Keisei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It means we play fair and square, based on our abilities and nothing more. To put it simply—no dirty tricks. No kicking our opponents while they’re down, like Ryuuen likes to do,” Keisei answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ignoring Keisei and Ishizaki, Horikita’s brother and Nagumo continued with their discussion.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you don’t agree to my conditions, I won’t accept your challenge,” said Horikita. In all likelihood, the ultimatum was meant as a way to hamstring Nagumo.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So I can’t win by attacking Horikita-senpai’s pawns, huh? Fine.” I’d expected him to object, but surprisingly, Nagumo agreed readily enough. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, Horikita’s brother wasn’t finished.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This isn’t limited to my group. I refuse to acknowledge any methods that cause harm to spill over to other students either. Our competition is invalid the moment I determine you’ve been meddling in others’ affairs.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Just as I’d expect, senpai. You miss nothing. I’d considered recruiting other groups to launch a joint attack, but…” Nagumo smiled audaciously. “Well, since I’m the only one who really wants to compete, I don’t mind agreeing to a certain number of conditions. All right. Fair and square, let’s see who scores higher and works better as a group or whatever. Ah, let me say this, though—no need to penalize the loser, yeah? After all, our pride is what’s on the line here.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita’s brother said nothing to either confirm or deny that. That probably meant he didn’t even intend to put his pride on the line for this.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">2.4</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">W</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">ITH THAT,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> the long opening act was finally at an end. But then Nagumo called out to our small group, stopping us in our tracks.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey. Now that the seniors are gone, it looks like you guys haven’t decided on a representative yet.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? How could you tell?” asked Keisei, sounding slightly panicked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“When I suggested everyone play rock-paper-scissors, it took a minute before you stumbled out. If you’d had a representative picked, he’d have stepped forward right away. I noticed one other group had a delayed reaction too. I’m thinking the groups that couldn’t decide on their representatives were probably the ones with a more balanced mix of three or four classes,” said Nagumo.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Nagumo probably didn’t know each and every one of the first-year students. Even so, he had correctly deduced how our groups were divided, which wasn’t something just anyone could do. Any delays on our part had been minor. We hadn’t discussed it; I’d just shoved Keisei and he’d immediately stepped up. Most people would never have noticed anything wrong.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I’d been trying to avoid exposing what could be construed as a weakness in our group. I guess my attempt had been in vain. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I thought the school didn’t mind if we decide on a representative later.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s right. But we want to know who the first-year representatives are. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We were now free for the rest of the day and, as such, abandoned all pretense of group unity to just do whatever we wanted. A natural-born leader like Hirata might have tried to get us talking, but…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As for me, my feelings were fixed. While it was unfortunate that I wasn’t getting to know any students from other classes better, it was also a relief to not have to deal with annoying small talk. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This might be a stupid question, but can Albert speak Japanese? He understands Japanese, right?” Hashimoto lay on his top bunk, directing the question to Ishizaki and Albert.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course he does. Right, Albert?” answered Ishizaki, peering down at his friend. However, Albert just continued to stare straight ahead in silence. “Or maybe he doesn’t.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Aren’t you guys classmates?” Hashimoto laughed. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t know, all right? Ryuuen-san’s normally the one giving orders,” replied an irritated Ishizaki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ryuuen-</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">san</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">, huh?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ishizaki’s respectful—and contradictory—use of the honorific was interesting.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is it true you guys had a fight and ousted him as leader?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“God, shut up. Of course it is. Calling him ‘san’ was just…old habits, that’s all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Rather than come together as a group, we were already sniping at each other. I decided to escape the escalating conflict and take a walk around the building.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">2.6</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">F</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">INALLY, IT WAS </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">mealtime. The first time we’d seen the girls since getting off the bus that morning. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The cafeteria was quite spacious, so much so that it even had stairs to a second floor with a view of the first. From what I saw, this place could apparently fit about five hundred people. And it was currently packed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s not easy to meet up with someone when we don’t have our phones.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita and Kei were probably looking for me, but I didn’t set out to search for them. If they did happen to run into me, their reactions would probably be polar opposites. Horikita would probably snap at me for avoiding her, while Kei would wait and see what happened, understanding that since I hadn’t sought her out, there was no reason for us to make contact right now. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Touching base with various students on the first day was to be expected. I doubted many people had eyes on me, but it was quite possible that Sakayanagi and Nagumo were watching. Even though I could say that Hirata and Satou had only been hanging out with us over Christmas, Nagumo understood that I had a special relationship with Kei. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wanted to avoid conspicuous contact.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I stayed solo, observing who was hanging out with who. But first, it was time to eat. The strict allotted hour was precious. I carried my tray over to my seat and sat down alone. Back at school, students would be separated by grade level to a certain extent, but here, first-, second- and third-years mixed together.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Many of the small and large groups stuck together, but there were more than few students scouting for information. More importantly, this was the only place and time we got to see the girls, making it the only time couples could spend together.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Siiiigh</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I heard a cute, exhausted sigh be released near me. It belonged to first-year Class B’s leader, Ichinose Honami. A bunch of guys and girls were crowded around her. I&#160;decided to sit nearby and listen in on their conversation, confident I’d remain relatively unnoticed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Pathetic to be proud of taking up so little space,” I muttered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ichinose and the others didn’t react to me at all. Well, the cafeteria was crowded. They couldn’t pay close attention to every single student.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Good work, Honami-chan. Was it tough?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah ha ha. Well, I suppose it was, yeah. I thought deciding on a group wouldn’t be so bad. But when people wanna fight, they’re gonna fight.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nothing we can do about that. The other classes are enemies.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But according to Kanzaki-kun, the boys came to a decision pretty quickly.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? Really? It took us until past noon.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The boys hadn’t had an easy time of it, but it sounded like the girls had struggled even more than we had. Maybe the instructors had anticipated that, which explained why there weren’t any classes on the first day.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you think someone might get expelled?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I can’t say there’s no reason to worry. Even though no one’s been expelled from the first-year classes, we can’t let our guard down.” It seemed Ichinose was acutely aware of the danger.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What should we do if someone gets taken down by the solidarity rule?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’ll be okay, Mako-chan. As long as we take this seriously, it’s not going to come to that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you sure?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If that time does come, we’ll all help each other out,” said Ichinose gently. Though she looked the most exhausted of them all, she remained stout-hearted as ever. “Ahh… I’m beat.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She laid her head on the table. Unfortunately, that change of position allowed her to notice me. </span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><img alt="" height="100%" id="ClassroomElite-Vol8_INTERIORIMAGES_2" src="/hola-lector-agua/images/books/v8/OEBPS/Images/ClassroomElite-Vol8_INTERIORIMAGES_2.jpg" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style=" top: 0px; vertical-align: top; height: 100%;"/></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ayanokouji-kuuun!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Oh, hey, Ichinose. Didn’t notice you there.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> If I said that, it’d come off as unnatural. It was probably best to just answer honestly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sounds like you were having fun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Some girls find power in gossip,” said Ichinose, flopping over once again. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t really understand what she meant. This listlessness was kind of surprising, coming from her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I suppose I probably shouldn’t do that, huh?” said Ichinose, sitting back up straight. I stopped her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s normal to do something like that when you’re exhausted,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry for making you kind of uncomfortable.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wasn’t uncomfortable at all, but I couldn’t speak those words aloud. “Sounds like you got put in a tough group,” I said instead.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I guess you could say it’s been tough just getting the groups together… Like, girls are really up-front about their likes and dislikes. Or rather, there are more than a few girls who are willing to just tell another girl they don’t like her to her face. I suppose guys tend to keep their personal feelings on the down-low, huh?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“They’re pretty up-front about hating Ryuuen, though.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I feel bad for laughing at that, but I guess there’s no helping it. Still, isn’t Ryuuen-kun tired of it too? Being disliked by everyone has to be draining.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She wasn’t wrong, but the logic probably didn’t apply to Ryuuen. If anything, he seemed to be taking it pretty easy now that he was no longer shouldering the burdens of an entire class.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t work yourself too hard.” Concluding that it would be pointless to linger, I stood up.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m okay, I’m okay. My energy’s the only thing I have going for me. See you later, Ayanokouji-kun.” Ichinose waved gently in farewell. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">One hour per day. That was the only time we’d have to talk to the girls. Even though boys and girls couldn’t directly intervene in each other’s affairs, I imagined this hour was designed for sharing information. The school likely intended us to use the time to gather information, strategize, and continue to fight, making it an area where well-liked and well-trusted students with strong communication skills excelled.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not suited to this at all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Just like on the deserted island, there was basically nothing I could do to help.</span></p>
