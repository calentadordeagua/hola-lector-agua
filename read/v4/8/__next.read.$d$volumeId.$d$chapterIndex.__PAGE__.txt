<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 2:<br/>The Gentle Days…</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">t’d been three days since the end of that special test on the island. No other exciting events had occurred aboard the luxurious cruise ship that the Advanced Nurturing High School had provided for us, and we were enjoying the respite. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It went without saying that time spent on a deserted island had caused a bunch of young, rambunctious students like us to lose our minds. We, the guys, were basically beasts—sex-starved carnivores. While we watched the girls chattering and dilly-dallying like herbivores, the boys waited for an opening like the predators that we are. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This luxurious cruise ship, equipped with everything, felt like a dream come true. This trip let us forget everything unpleasant. It wouldn’t have been strange to fall in love here. Rumor had it that many couples had come together on this trip. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Unfortunately, that wouldn’t happen to me. I lived a mostly solitary life. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The test hadn’t changed my situation… No, that wasn’t quite true. My environment had begun to change. Against my will, I’d been tasked with a major course correction. I’d originally come to this school for a certain reason. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Contact with anyone outside is forbidden until graduation.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That rule was the reason I entered. However, a certain man had been forcibly trying to establish contact with me. Chabashira-sensei, my homeroom teacher, told me that. Then Chabashira-sensei threatened to expel me from this paradise should I refuse to work toward Class A. Although I’d done nothing scandalous, a powerless creature like me had no choice but to accept. I didn’t know whether she was lying or not. Therefore, I had to assume it was the truth.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, I didn’t intend to dance to my homeroom teacher’s tune forever. While I collected the necessary information, I considered that I might need to take action. A little devil perched on my shoulder whispered softly in my ear: </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">It’s better to get them before they get you.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> I wondered about the many ways I could force her to resign. Hmm.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That troubling thought lasted only a second, though. My mind returned to its normal, blank way of thinking. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Sigh</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">. If only I had the power to rotate the earth on its axis myself…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If I could do that, I’d be able to live freely, without having to worry about the small things. I looked out the window and daydreamed about </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Dragon Ball</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">. Three days had passed since the end of the test. Immediately after our survival test’s conclusion, most of the students had been waiting for the other shoe to drop. They’d treaded lightly in case the school was up to something. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But there were no signs of anything malicious. All was gentle and peaceful, as if summer vacation had truly started and this was now an actual fun, pleasant trip. The students began to relax, and behaved as if the test really were over. They thought this second week was purely a vacation. Everyone became increasingly lax. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Despite our outward calm, we maintained a sense of readiness. People who know how to relax can be effective.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hmm? You haven’t been in the room all this time, have you?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata Yousuke, one of my cabin roommates, shook me from my reverie while I gazed at the sea. “I don’t really have any reason to go out. I don’t have anyone to hang out with, either.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s not true, though. I mean, you have Sudou-kun and the guys, and Horikita-san.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was certainly true that such people had categorized me as a “friend,” and I reciprocated. But those at the bottom strata of the “friends” category are treated differently from other, higher-ranked friends. When someone wants to hang out, low-level friends are only invited ten percent of the time. Naturally, I was that lowly friend.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You might make more friends if you were a little more proactive, Ayanokouji-kun. Though I suppose it’s none of my business.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata was very popular and admired by many students. All the girls adored him, and he had a girlfriend named Karuizawa. A man with so much happiness probably couldn’t understand the pangs of loneliness. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re a solid guy, Ayanokouji-kun. I think you might just need the right opportunity.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t need such kind-hearted yet cruel words. I didn’t need to hear girls say things like, “Oh, but I’ve heard you’re great,” because if I responded with, “Well then, let’s go out,” they would reply, “Well, I don’t know about that…” I didn’t need that. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">I spend time by myself because I can’t make friends or get a girlfriend. You freaking&#160;idiot.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m planning to meet up with Karuizawa-san and the others at 12:30 to have lunch. Do you want to come with? I think it’ll be really fun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Karuizawa and others?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. There are about three other girls. Want to come?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had to think about that. To be perfectly honest, I’d wanted to interact with Karuizawa for a while now. However, there wasn’t any need to hurry. Besides, if there were other girls, forget about being able to even initiate conversation. I couldn’t imagine I’d be able to liven things up. In fact, I’d probably have a dampening effect.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll pass. I don’t think I’m on especially good terms with Karuizawa’s group.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Since the end of the first semester, the relationships in our class had been firmly established. How would I go about building friendships with other people at this point? I could already picture Karuizawa and the other girls’ disgust. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though he didn’t fully understand my fear of other people, Hirata sat next to me. “I can understand that you’re feeling hesitant. That’s exactly why I want you to rely on me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata wore that eternal, optimistic smile of his. While it was a welcome offer, I shook my head. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You only have about ten minutes before you have to meet them. It’d be better if you just left me here.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s no need to hurry. Besides, I like spending time with you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">To an outsider, my words might have sounded like an excuse, but I was honestly satisfied with the current situation. Back when I had just started school, I’d wanted to make friends. I’d wanted to be able to make a hundred friends, but naturally, everyone settled down into their own separate factions. It was only a matter of course.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though I’d only been able to talk with the three idiots Horikita, Kushida, and Sakura, my school life wasn’t all that bad. I really believed that. But Hirata wasn’t the type to leave someone be if he saw they were alone.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, how about just the two of us have lunch together, you and me? Would that be okay?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Just the two of us alone. Sitting on the bed together. Hirata turned to me with a serious look. If he lightly pushed me down, things would get out of hand. “Well, I don’t particularly dislike the idea, but… Didn’t you already promise Karuizawa?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I can eat with Karuizawa-san and the others any time, but I haven’t really had many opportunities to eat together with you, Ayanokouji-kun.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Normally, a guy would do anything to get the chance to eat a meal with a girl. Typical male thinking. However, Hirata seemed able to prioritize eating with another guy without hesitation. It was enough to give me doubts. Perhaps he swung “that way.” Hirata was an effortless multitasker, but he never did anything without reason. “I’d feel bad if Karuizawa blamed you later, though.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I tried to gently turn him down, but it seemed to appeal to his conscience. I wondered if Hirata considered me a trembling newborn fawn that couldn’t even take its first step. “Don’t worry. I don’t think Karuizawa-san’s the kind of girl to hold a grudge.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">No, no. Karuizawa </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">definitely</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> seemed like that kind of girl. Even though she pretended to be nice in front of Hirata, she was domineering with other ladies. I wondered if Hirata had categorized her as not being “that type of girl.” It reminded me of that teacher from </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Yomawari Sensei</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">, who showed mercy to bad students.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay. I’ll turn Karuizawa-san down for today.” Hirata pulled out his phone to call Karuizawa. I tried to stop him, but Hirata shot me a look. “Is there anything you’d like to eat?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I can eat just about anything, I guess. Though I’d like to avoid heavy food if possible,” I muttered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The cruise ship had many restaurants. The offerings ranged from junk food like ramen and hamburgers to French cuisine. Since it was still daytime, I wanted to eat something light if possible. I heard Hirata flatly tell Karuizawa that he was cancelling their plans over the phone. I couldn’t hear Karuizawa’s voice clearly, but Hirata abruptly hung up.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you really okay with this?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course. Well then, shall we go to the deck? If we’re just having a light meal, eating there should be easier.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata opened the door, as if to guide me, while I relaxed in bed. Hirata was being his usual concerned self, but taking me out when I wasn’t really that enthusiastic was odd. It seemed kind of forceful for Hirata, especially since he could normally read any situation well. He likely had some kind of ulterior motive.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thank you for helping on the deserted island. I’m sorry I didn’t thank you enough for helping to find the culprit, Ayanokouji-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You don’t need to apologize. I wasn’t even useful. Horikita found the underwear thief.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even so, I’m grateful to you for helping.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Speaking of the underwear incident, I had something I wanted to ask. I looked around to confirm we were alone, then broached the topic. “Did Karuizawa get her underwear back?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. When she heard Ibuki-san was the culprit, things went smoothly.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Karuizawa had her underwear stolen on the island, and for a while there’d been an uproar. Because the underwear was in a guy’s bag, the relationship between the guys and girls of Class D had been in a precarious state. But Hirata put his quick thinking to use, and concealed the underwear. Because of this, things had cooled down. I was really glad. It had been an extremely delicate operation, so I was impressed. I’d half-believed that Hirata would bungle returning the underwear, despite his skills.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata’s quiet and peaceful resolution to the underwear fiasco proved that he was steadily climbing the stairway to adulthood. We took the ship’s elevator to the deck on the top level. Many of our classmates appeared to be enjoying summer vacation to its fullest. Both guys and girls could be seen in the pool in their bathing suits, boldly frisking around. The heavy, fraught mood that weighed on us during the test had lifted.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This display of wanton indulgence came from the students’ liberated desires, which had been suppressed back on the island. We didn’t need to use points for any of the facilities on the ship, or for food and drinks. Everything was free of charge. No one had to restrain themselves. You needed to borrow things like swimsuits and other equipment, but that was about it. It was heaven. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">By the time we reached the restaurant, over half of the seats had already been taken. We slipped past the crowd and secured two empty seats.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Honestly, there’s something I’d like your advice on.” Hirata spoke in a slightly apologetic way as he looked down at the menu. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Advice?” So he </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">did</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> have an ulterior motive, after all. That was probably why he’d wanted time to eat with me. Well, I was grateful. I didn’t sweat the reason for an invitation.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not really the best person to come to for advice. Can you give me the short version?” I wasn’t exactly a great talker or listener, but he had likely selected me for a reason. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wondered if you would act as a bridge to help reach Horikita-san. After all, Class D will have to come together and work hard in the future, and I think Horikita-san will be indispensable.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So that’s what he wanted to talk to me about, huh? When I nodded, Hirata continued with confidence.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The other day, Class D got a major boost thanks to Horikita-san. Class morale has shot up, and more than anything else, the number of people who idolize Horikita-san has increased. This is a big opportunity.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I suppose so.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita Suzune was a student in Class D, and the first friend I’d made upon starting school. I was her first friend as well, but she was a solitary, standoffish person. She was an honors student, highly accomplished both in literary and martial arts. Her weakness was her aloof, independent personality. She didn’t involve herself with anyone due to her high-handed attitude, and she wasn’t very good at socializing. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s why I think she should try and get along with everyone right now. If she cooperated with us, I think we could get up to Class C, and then B… No, we could even reach Class A.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">From anyone else it would have sounded phony, but Hirata had been singing Horikita’s praises since we started school. He’d been aware of her potential from the start, and didn’t seem to dislike her at all. I wouldn’t mind helping him out. The task was simple. I could at least bring Hirata and Horikita closer together, but that probably wouldn’t lead to a permanent solution.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But even if I can bring you both together, things won’t be that easy. Horikita is difficult.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If I asked her to soften her approach, she’d turn me down. If Horikita believed I was trying to manipulate her, things could end in disaster. She would distance herself even further. Her response to Kushida’s push for friendship in the café during our first semester was proof of that.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. I do understand, of course. Horikita-san only opens herself up to you, Ayanokouji-kun. I don’t want to force her confidence. That’s why I wanted to tell you my intentions, so that you can talk to her. Then, pretend I never spoke to you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So I was supposed to be the Horikita whisperer, huh? I would be the go-between, delivering Hirata’s opinions to Horikita. If I did that, Horikita would enter into an unseen partnership with Hirata, and never realize it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If I thought she’d listen to me, things would be simple enough. But it won’t be that easy. I usually just go along with whatever Horikita says. I’ve never really forced my opinion on her. If I just suddenly start voicing strong opinions, she’ll probably find it suspicious. If she finds out it’s </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">your</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> opinion, I think she’ll shut down completely.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But I can’t come up with anything else right now. I don’t think I have the confidence to speak with Horikita-san, let alone persuade her. This is my last resort.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t you think it’s a little soon for last resorts?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata clearly wanted to join forces with Horikita, but he’d have to face her directly. I understood it was difficult, but so was coordinating groups of people. Horikita would probably agree. No one else in our class cherished friendship and community the way Hirata did. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was still uncertain about his proposal. It seemed like he’d lost his nerve and was doubting himself. Hirata had also been acting strangely back on the island. This wasn’t a trivial matter. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I ordered a light sandwich and a drink. Students swam in the pool nearby, and others were eating while still wearing their bathing suits. Everyone appeared to be having fun. If Ike and Yamauchi were here, they probably would’ve foregone the food and feasted their eyes on the girls instead. Hirata didn’t pay any attention to the girls at all, but focused squarely on me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, you may be right. Like you said, Ayanokouji-kun. My plan was really short-sighted.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He recognized his error in judgment and gave an honest, flexible response. Another of Hirata’s many charms. But his desire to work together with Horikita was so strong, he showed no signs of giving up.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I should probably reconsider how to do this. Horikita-san seems very fastidious and hypercritical. How do you manage to get along with her, Ayanokouji-kun?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata wanted to become Horikita’s friend before establishing a working relationship. I thought facing her directly was the right thing to do. It was constructive, and I wanted to lend a hand, but…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, in truth, I don’t think I’m on especially good terms with Horikita. Recently, I wondered if we could even be called friends.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But it does seem like Horikita-san gets along well with you alone, Ayanokouji-kun. You’re special to her.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Special, huh? Hard to believe that someone with over forty friends would say that to someone barely acquainted with one person. Or perhaps it was because he was able to get along with over forty people that Hirata felt frustrated at missing a connection with one particular student.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s no need to get impatient, right? The first semester’s only just ended.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Bonds between people must fundamentally strengthen over time. Sometimes, you can place people together under sudden, harsh conditions, like the test on the island, and watch those bonds strengthen overnight. Of course, while you might see instant improvement via that method, those bonds can often be fragile. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Horikita isn’t the type to make friends easily.” I wanted Hirata to understand.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s probably true.” Hirata appeared slightly remorseful. Perhaps he felt like he was rushing things again. “I wasn’t thinking about her feelings. I was just thinking of myself.” Hirata shook himself from his reverie. Again, he smiled. “Sorry. I invited you out to eat and made it all about me. Well, let’s eat, shall we?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">With that, we started eating our recently-arrived food. However, Hirata looked up and seemed to notice someone approaching us. He shot me a nonplussed look.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah, so you’re here after all, Hirata-kun. Let’s eat lunch together!” Karuizawa approached us, leading a group of girls. She sounded happy and carefree.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Um… Karuizawa-san, I thought I canceled our plans…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata stumbled over his words, looking unsure. Meanwhile, Karuizawa and the girls pulled out the chairs from another table, pushed me away, and circled Hirata. Our lunch went from peaceful to noisy in seconds flat. I wasn’t much of a talker, but I wasn’t really concerned. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was used to dealing with situations like this. It was probably time for me to use the special skill I’d acquired since school began, my “Speedy Escape.” I grabbed my food and got up quietly, without making a sound. Hirata’s eyes met mine for a moment, but the girls closed ranks and he disappeared.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Maybe this was one of the select disadvantages of having so many friends. With so much time devoted to others, you didn’t really get any time for yourself. Even if Hirata had issues weighing him down, he couldn’t talk about them with Karuizawa and the others. Instead, he held them unspoken in his heart.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">A</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">fter Karuizawa</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;"> </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">monopolized Hirata, I decided to return to my room. I didn’t have anyone else to hang out with or talk to, anyway. I took the stairs rather than the elevator, and returned to my room on the third deck. When I got there, I noticed some wet blotches scattered about the hallway floor. The blotches appeared to head toward my room. When I followed the trail, I discovered an elegant man striding through the corridor, nude from the waist up, wearing nothing but a bathing suit.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“S-sir! Please don’t walk through the hallway while you’re still dripping wet!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">A young bellhop hurried toward the man, apparently eager to control this emergency situation. The bellhop was already holding out a towel, overly prepared, like he always walked around with a strategic towel ready.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ha ha ha! It appears you’ve found me, eh?” the man&#160;said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, I’ve found you. This is the fourth time. I’ve already told you, please dry yourself off after you leave the pool. If you don’t, you’ll disturb the other passengers.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Apparently, this man was a repeat offender. That explained the bellhop’s prepared towels. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Disturb? But I’ve no memory of disturbing anyone. I&#160;don’t dry myself off with a towel on principle ever since reaching the age of reason. Haven’t I already said? ‘A fine man, dripping with water,’ hmm?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yes. The man was Kouenji. He slicked back his wet hair, scattering droplets of water. The bellhop hurriedly used the towel to dry the spots on the carpet and wall. Kouenji stopped. I wondered if he found the bellhop’s flustered behavior amusing.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you have a pen and paper on hand?” he asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? A, uh, oh…due to the nature of my job, I do walk around with a notepad and pen, but…” The bellhop, clearly unsure where the conversation was headed, fearfully took out a ballpoint pen. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Did you know that a prominent celebrity’s signature can accrue an unexpectedly premium value over time? Abroad, some autographs are valued in the millions to even the tens of millions.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And…what of it?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After Kouenji finished scribbling something on the notepad, he thrust it back at the bellhop. It was far away, but I was able to make out the name “Kouenji Rokusuke.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wh-what is this?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Isn’t it obvious? A signature. Even though it’s written on such a cheap notepad, it will certainly hold tremendous value in the future. I’m presenting this to you as a token for your troubles. Please receive it gratefully and safeguard it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Apparently Kouenji believed the bellhop would receive this with gratitude, or perhaps a sense of awe-inspired devotion. But no one in their right mind would want this. If anything, his ballpoint pen and notepad were worth more.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Please don’t look so suspicious. I am the man who will carry Japan’s future on his back. At that point, I intend to vacation upon a larger ship, but I’m prepared to wait until then. Of course, it will be a luxury liner of significantly higher quality than the common ship upon which we are now riding.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Well, a luxury liner was still a luxury liner. Personally, I’d be satisfied as long as it wasn’t fated to sink like the </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Titanic</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">. Kouenji guffawed, seemingly satisfied. The bellhop, completely dumbstruck, continued staring at the wet spots on the floor. He had completely lost the will to keep trying to stop this man. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kouenji was alone all the time because our classmates steered clear of him, disgusted by his incredibly selfish personality. Many of our classmates had already experienced the same treatment as this poor bellhop. Hirata would probably try to speak with him, but he’d most likely be brushed off as well. Kouenji was like poison. Anyone who encountered him, friend or foe, suffered for&#160;it. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In order to avoid being dragged into something so bothersome, I slunk past the two of them. Getting too near a person of </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">such</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> high rank would be dangerous.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh ho? Why, if it isn’t little Ayanokouji, hmm? What a coincidence.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ugh. Kouenji called my name. There was no way he was </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">actually</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> calling me, right? The instant the bellhop noticed that Kouenji’s attention had shifted over to me, he appeared elated. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">I’m finally free!</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> is what his look seemed to say.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">No, no. How could a crew member act like that? He ought to continue serving the customer, no matter how annoying the customer was. It was like someone releasing their pet into the river without permission. Especially if that fish was a ferocious, invasive species like Kouenji, which would devour all the native fish in the river until there was nothing left.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you want something from me?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, no, I’ve no real business with you. I’ve simply acknowledged you because we are schoolmates. Also, though we are certainly not alike in terms of position, you are my roommate.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kouenji flipped his hair once again, scattering more water, which splashed my face and uniform. Of course, he didn’t seem to have the faintest clue what he was doing to his victims. Despite what was happening to me, the bellhop watched this tragedy unfold with a grin. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Oh yes, yes, I sympathize with the pain you’re feeling…not, </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">he seemed to say.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I will excuse myself. Please do take care in the future.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The bellhop delivered that parting shot as he made his escape, having fulfilled the bare minimum for his role. Of course, I didn’t exactly want to end up alone with Kouenji, either.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What were you talking with Kouenji about?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">For an instant, the bellhop’s expression changed to a look of anger, but when Kouenji turned around, the young man’s smile returned. He was like Asuraman or something.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, um. Well, as you can see, he was wet. I tried to offer him a towel, and—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, in other words, you were giving him a warning. I&#160;must have interrupted you, so I’ll be on my way and let you get down to business.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I passed the ball, if you will, a blazing fastball that crushed the bellhop and gave me a chance to escape.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This bellhop came to give </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">me</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> a </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">warning</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">?” Kouenji cried.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah, no. Well, that is to say…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I managed to escape from Kouenji and headed back toward my room. “But I’ll just bump into Kouenji again there, won’t I?” I murmured.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">My room would become less of a sanctuary and more of a hell. I’d been alone with Kouenji several times during our trip, and every experience was unbelievably uncomfortable. Wanting to avoid such an awkward atmosphere, I turned around. I wouldn’t return to my room just yet. I’d head back to where Hirata and Yukimura, my other roommates, would be. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">A map of the ship was on a nearby signboard, and pretty easy to understand. The fact that the map had been placed in a gilded frame might’ve been overkill, but also made it seem like something you’d find on a luxury liner. I drew a route that’d let me kill a bunch of free time, and immediately hopped onto the elevator. I got off on the second deck. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The ship had nine decks in total, plus a roof. The fifth deck was above ground, while the fourth deck was below. The first deck housed both the lounge and banquet area, while the rooftop had the pool, café, and other facilities. The third and fourth decks were for guest rooms. The guys were on the third deck; the girls on the fourth.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The guys and girls, teachers included, were neatly divided. However, there weren’t any special restrictions on our movements, so a guy could easily wander through the girls’ area. We were probably prohibited from staying on the girls’ level or coming into the area after midnight, though.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The ship also had various kinds of entertainment options, such as movies and a live theater venue. These were everywhere, from the first underground level up to the third underground level. On the fourth underground level—the lowest level at the bottom of the ship—was some kind of switchboard breaker room. That particular level was irrelevant to the students. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The lounge was open twenty-four hours a day. We were free to go there no matter how late it was, but a notice from the school urged us to refrain from going there as much as possible. As I walked through the second-level area, I noticed that the atmosphere felt quite different. The rooms were vacant, and I couldn’t tell what they were used for. So few students were in the hallways that it appeared deserted.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Just then, my cell phone vibrated in my pocket. Pulling it out, I saw that I’d received an email. A certain girl was calling me. It was convenient, since I’d planned on killing time anyway. With no reason to reject her, I took the call with pleasure.</span></p>

<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">2.2</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">“A</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">hh. Ahhh… Ahhh…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakura, who’d sent me the email, was making several anxious, pained sighs.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What’s the matter?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wah! A-Ayanokouji-kun?!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t think I’d spoken in an especially harsh or shocking way, but Sakura was so surprised that she looked like she’d been jolted with electricity. Her normally hunched posture straightened at once.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry for scaring you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“N-no, no. I’m just kind of strangely nervous is all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If she was </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">that</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> nervous about meeting a friend, her daily life must be a drag.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ayanokouji-kun, you’re roommates with Hirata-kun, Kouenji-kun, and Yukimura-kun. Right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“My roommates? Yeah, you’re right. What about them?” I wasn’t expecting her to ask that.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh. Well, to tell you the truth, I…I’m a little worried about the people I’m sharing a room with.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It sounded like she didn’t really have a good relationship with her roommates. Sakura wasn’t good at socializing, anyway. One look at her deeply troubled expression, and I understood.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re worried because even though you want to get along with them, you don’t think you can?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not sure. I have mixed feelings. I </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">do </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">want to get along with them, but I also want to be alone, too. I’m just hopeless, aren’t I?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Her voice trailed off and she shied away. Looking into her shimmering eyes, I understood. I didn’t know who else was sharing Sakura’s room, so I couldn’t really offer any advice. “By the way, who’re you rooming with?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh. Didn’t I tell you? Shinohara-san, Ichihashi-san, and Maezono-san.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She looked incredibly depressed while giving me the names. Those were all girls with strong personalities. Shinohara had a close relationship with Karuizawa; Karuizawa was like her boss, really. She was a reliable, trustworthy girl who didn’t run away from a challenge, even quarreling with the boys. But she could be rather relentless against people she didn’t like. I couldn’t imagine she thought much of Sakura, so she probably wouldn’t go out of her way to befriend her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ichihashi was usually rather mature, but also headstrong. I didn’t really know much about Maezono, but I had a bad impression of her. She had a bad attitude and seemed quick to pick a fight. She was probably the hardest type of person for Sakura to deal with. Even if Sakura tried her hardest to bridge the gap between them, if Maezono didn’t like Sakura’s attitude, she’d probably hate her. I wanted to pat Sakura on the head and tell her how great she was. I mean, she hadn’t cried until now. She was doing great.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But why come to me?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I…just thought any advice from you, Ayanokouji-kun, would be good?” Sakura muttered quietly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Apparently she’d come to unexpectedly rely on me. She murmured some words of apology.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I-I’m sorry to just come to you for help like this out of the blue. You’re so busy, Ayanokouji-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s no big deal. I don’t mind it when you come to me for advice. Whether I’ll be of much help is another story.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Since I wasn’t really friends with any of Sakura’s roommates, I couldn’t guarantee I’d be able to help. While I was thinking of something I could do, a door opened.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? Ayanokouji-kun and Sakura-san? What are you doing here?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida Kikyou from Class D exited the room. Sakura’s bright expression immediately disappeared, like the sun hiding behind the clouds. The atmosphere around us became uncomfortable. Perhaps Sakura wasn’t good at controlling her emotions, either. Sakura’s response to Kushida’s bright and cheery appearance was one of rejection, but Kushida continued talking without showing any signs that she noticed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah, did I interrupt? I didn’t mean to. I was supposed to meet up with some friends.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m going back to my room.” Sakura retreated as fast as her legs would carry her, as if trying to withdraw from Kushida in a panic. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh. I’m sorry. I guess that was bad timing. I probably shouldn’t have said anything.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida clasped her hands together in apology. There wasn’t really any reason for her to apologize, though. Sakura was just bad at dealing with people.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, that reminds me, I feel like this is the first time we’ve talked since getting back to the ship. I </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">did</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> see you hanging out with a bunch of girls earlier, from a distance.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida was the most popular person in Class D—no, probably in the whole school. On the day of the school entrance ceremony, she’d declared that she would make friends with everyone, and had almost accomplished that goal now. She was only missing a select few, like Sakura-san.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ve made plans to go see some girls from Class C today. Do you want to come, Ayanokouji-kun?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Eh. Is it okay for me to join?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? You’ll come?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This was shaping up to be a bad day. Kushida’s mask had slipped a little, and her true self seemed puzzled by my response. Well, there was always the diplomatic, socially acceptable way of putting things. In other words, I had to find a polite, diplomatic way to refuse.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m joking. Don’t you know by now that I’m not the sort of person who joins in on stuff?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh jeez, come on. You surprised me a little. Ayanokouji-kun, you’re funny.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“R-really?” Deep down, I doubted she truly found me funny, but I was scared to hear Kushida’s real thoughts.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I’ll be going.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As she bid me goodbye, both our cell phones started ringing at the same time. There was a loud, dinging sound that meant we’d received a message from the school. Usually it was a message with new instructions to follow, or some kind of modification to an event. Your phone would make a sound even if you had it on silent. Clearly, it was a message of high importance. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What is it?” Kushida asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She stopped in her tracks and looked puzzled, which was understandable. Even though they’d explained this system to us after school started, we hadn’t received any important messages during our summer vacation until now. Simultaneously, an announcement could be heard throughout the ship.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Attention. This is an announcement to all students. All students should have received a message from the school, as indicated in the contact line. Please check your individual mobile devices and follow the instructions accordingly. In the event that you did not receive a message, we apologize for the inconvenience. Please go to the nearest faculty member for assistance. Because the contents of the message are extremely important, please do not miss it. We repeat—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That was about the message we just got, right?” Kushida asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Probably.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I pulled out my phone and saw the following message:</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">A special test will begin soon. Place gather in the designated room at the designated time. Anyone who arrives later than ten minutes after start time may be penalized. Please gather in Room 204 on the second deck by 18:00 today. Because it takes about twenty minutes to reach the area, we ask that you please use the restrooms now if necessary. Either silence your phone or turn it off, and make your way over.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“A special test?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This probably wasn’t going to be a paper test or physical fitness examination, or something you’d see at a regular school—much like the survival test on the island. However, nothing in the message implied what awaited us. Were we supposed to be able to read into something, or should we just be prepared for anything? I didn’t know.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">More than anything else, certain points in the message weighed on me. They wanted us to gather in the room at 18:00, but we only had about twenty minutes to get ready and head over, which was an extremely short amount of time. Plus, it seemed like it had been decided rather quickly. Also, why was the designated meeting spot one of the ship’s private rooms? That couldn’t make a good exam room by any standard.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Can you show me your phone for a second?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida, without hesitation, showed me that she’d received the same message. The only difference was the designated time and place. In her message, the designated time was set at 20:40, but she was also told that it took about twenty minutes to reach the area. I also noticed that her designated room was just two rooms down from mine.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wonder why they called us in such a strange way?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I have no idea.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I only knew that I had a bad feeling about this. I hadn’t thought that our cruise would end like this. A place where all the first-year students would gather inside the ship… I’d already visited places like movie theaters, party venues, and buffets. I thought I could speculate about the contents of the test based on any suspicious activity I saw, but I’d seen no indication of anything of the sort. Why would they isolate students, limit us, and then tell us to start…whatever this test was?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I quickly messaged Horikita via chat on my phone. I saw that she had read the message immediately after I sent it, which was unusual. She’d often wait about half a day before reading and replying; several days in some cases. Was it because we’d received a message from the school around the same time? I tried asking her questions.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Yeah. I did.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why on </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">earth</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> was I lumped in with a bunch of </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">space cadets</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">?”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Our vacation had come to an end, and round two had just begun. Well, I’d expected this. Feeling depressed by this whole situation, I decided to return to my room.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The Professor often said he wanted to go to the 2D world, and that 2D women were perfect. Knowing that, I understood why he’d strongly reject real girls like Karuizawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“To be honest, I really hate it. No matter what I do, she’ll only drag us down.”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I supposed I should report to Horikita. I wanted to know if she’d received the same information as the Rabbit group. I sent details to her in chat, and while I waited on her report, I started to come up with a strategy.</span></p>

<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">2.4</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> returned to my room, dying for a moment of peace and quiet. In the middle of dozing, I thought I heard a noise, and propped myself up in bed. Neither Yukimura nor Kouenji were in the room.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sorry. Did I wake you?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata, who’d been putting his luggage back in order, looked up with a slightly apologetic expression. He was putting on his uniform, which made me think he was getting ready to leave.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nah, I wasn’t really in a deep sleep or anything. I’m pretty thirsty anyway, so it’s a good time to get up.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I went ahead and shut off the alarm that had been about to go off. I’d wanted to check on Horikita anyway, so there wasn’t a problem. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Would you like to come with me? I was thinking the school’s message should be coming pretty soon.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was just before 20:30, then. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The test seems really unusual. Well, that’s just a feeling I have.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It seemed like Hirata already knew the test’s specifics. I wondered if he’d heard about it from another student.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yukimura didn’t exactly like Hirata much, but maybe he thought telling Hirata would improve our chances of winning. If you understood the rules beforehand, it would be easier to glean more information when you were listening to the official explanation. Yukimura might have told Hirata in order to hear what Hirata thought afterward, and to see if there was anything new to think about. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Of course, that was surprisingly bold. I’d have liked to follow his example and team up with a superior student.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Did you notice anything, Ayanokouji-kun? If it’s okay, I’d like you to share it with me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Can’t really say. I haven’t been thinking about the exam like you, Horikita, and Yukimura have. I’m not smart, either, so I haven’t really noticed much.” I tilted my head to the side, as if nothing sprang to mind. </span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I was wondering why the explanations are so…scattered. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, I think you’re right, Hirata. It would definitely be more efficient to explain the test to all the students at the same time, rather than dividing us into groups and doing it in stages.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata’s doubts were right on the money. The school had adopted a clearly inefficient method. Perhaps we should consider why they would split us up, and for what reason. It was possible that our “thinking” was already being tested.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m planning to ask the teacher about that later.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wondered how things would shake out. Hirata normally acted on behalf of Class D. Considering he’d be lumped in with other classes, I couldn’t imagine what he thought of these rules, or what he’d do about them.</span></p>

<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">2.5</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">H</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">irata’s meeting place was on the second deck, only one level below us. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">At a glance, it looked like there were close to ten people here. Considering the time, even if some part of the group had already been briefed, it did seem rather strange. Did this meeting have some other purpose? Were they all checking to see who belonged to which group? If so, they didn’t need to spend all this time and effort. If you just talked to your classmates later, you could figure out the groups right away. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">They glanced up as we passed, but then immediately went back to their cell phones, like they were in the middle of something. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Who are those people?” I asked.</span></p>

I kept moving, thinking how easy this would all be if it operated like that. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When Hirata and I arrived, several guys and girls were gathered near the door. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Unless I’m mistaken, you’re also in the group meeting at 20:40, yes?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I heard a rather low, deep voice. Katsuragi, from Class A. He had an incredibly calm and collected demeanor for a first-year high school student. He was cool and composed, and had a good physique to boot. You could mistake him for a college student at first glance. Even though Class A boasted many excellent students, most had already acknowledged him as their leader.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, I am. And what exactly does that have to do with you?” answered the girl with long black hair, who faced Katsuragi without hesitation.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I knew it,” he said. “That’s good news. I wanted to speak with you again. I’m also in the 20:40 group. Starting tomorrow, we will be working together.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita Suzune stared back at Katsuragi. So Hirata had been placed with both Horikita and Katsuragi. Quite a team.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You wanted to speak with me? Funny. Didn’t you completely ignore me the other day?” she said.</span></p>

Three guys who probably belonged to Class A grouped around Katsuragi, as well as two girls from either Class B or C. They listened in on the conversation from a short distance away.</span></p>

I&#160;would give the same warning to both Classes B and C.”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You needn’t concern yourself. Class D has always been regarded as inferior. I’d welcome any change in our reputation.”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do you mean?”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Well, that was true. Bridging the gap between us would still be difficult. Of course, Horikita understood that. She also knew that the island was </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">my</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> victory, but I’d refused to take credit for anything. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">For a minute there, I thought there’d be a huge showdown before the test had even begun.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Roughly speaking, yeah. You’d best mentally prepare yourself.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah ha! Don’t worry, it’s okay! Like Hirata-kun said, all I can do is my best. Oh, I haven’t really had many chances to talk with Katsuragi-kun and Ryuuen-kun yet. I’d like to get along with you all, too.” Kushida spoke to everyone present without any anxiety, disgust, displeasure, or joy in her voice.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If we’re going to continue this pointless conversation, I’d prefer to just go inside. Besides, it’s about time.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">With those cold words, Horikita flipped her hair and turned her back on Ryuuen and his minions. I had to give it to her: Horikita didn’t back down. Weak-willed people tended to lower their heads and ingratiate themselves into their group, no matter what. They would beg forgiveness if they were isolated or ostracized. But Horikita wasn’t perturbed. She acted as she always did.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Apparently, I don’t need to worry all that much.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Of course, it wasn’t clear exactly how far she’d get like that, considering who was in her group. Even so, I didn’t think she’d get the wind taken out of her sails. That was just my intuition.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well then, good luck.” I left Hirata with those sympathetic words. He had his work cut out for him from now on.</span></p>
