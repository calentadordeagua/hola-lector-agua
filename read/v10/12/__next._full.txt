<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 6:<br/>Good and Evil</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HE MOMENT I SET FOOT</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> in the classroom the next morning, many pairs of eyes fell on me all at once. But those students quickly looked away from me, in all directions. Then several gazes fell upon me once again, from all over the room. This process began to repeat itself, over and over again. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was going to be expelled. The reality was that they had already begun taking action to get me expelled. This was precisely what I had sensed just yesterday—the feeling that something was out of place. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Akito, Keisei, and the rest of the Ayanokouji Group seemed no different today, though. The four of them weren’t such incredibly gifted actors that I wouldn’t be able to notice anything out of place about them. Since they were in the same group as me, I was sure the mastermind had naturally made sure no information was leaked to them. Of course, I didn’t intend to make the four of them worry about me. If I carelessly let something slip, then Kei’s involvement in my situation could be brought to light. I had no choice but to deal with this myself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Good morning, Ayanokouji-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh. Morning.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita, who had just arrived, didn’t seem to notice anything either.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sup.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Apparently, Sudou must have tagged along with her, since his greeting came at almost the exact same time.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Just so you are aware, this is a coincidence,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I didn’t ask,” I replied. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">For some reason, Sudou had his nose in the air, looking proud as he headed toward his seat. He probably had nothing to do with my current situation. Sure, he might want me expelled, but if he did go along with what Yamauchi was planning, then it would greatly impact Horikita’s opinion of him. Besides, he wasn’t a skilled enough actor to put up a good poker face.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…By the way,” said Horikita in a quiet voice, once it was just the two of us.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What did you do?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is it just me, or am I missing something? Can you explain whatever it is you’re talking about?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Regarding me. What did you do?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was a rather abstract question.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t know what you’re trying to say, but I’m not doing anything. I don’t exactly have enough free time to worry about you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You don’t have the time to worry about me? What do you mean?” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Look, don’t worry about it,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Class was just about to begin. Considering how Horikita was acting, she probably hadn’t gotten in touch with her brother yet. It seemed like the action was going to happen this afternoon.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">6.1</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">T WAS LUNCHTIME</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> on Friday. The test was happening tomorrow. I, Horikita Suzune, thought back on what happened last night. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Just as I’d been thinking it was time to go to bed, I’d received a text message. I remember feeling like my heart was going to jump out of my chest when I saw the sender’s name. It was a message from my brother. He had written one single sentence.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you have any regrets?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He’d sent me just that one question. I read his message over and over, thinking deeply, thrown completely for a loop.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">What should I do? This was a once-in-a-lifetime opportunity that had just fallen into my lap. If I let this chance slip away… Then the next time I’d be able to hear my brother’s voice would be his graduation ceremony. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After making up my mind, I wrote a message back to him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“Would you be willing to speak with me?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though all I had to do after typing that message was hit send, my fingers felt heavy. I couldn’t press the button.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I took a deep breath and hit the send button. All that was remained was to wait for my brother’s response. Just as I began to be consumed by anxiety, wondering if he’d actually respond, I heard from him. In the form of a phone call. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If anything, I felt relieved. I was glad that he called. It saved me from having to watch my hands shake while I texted back.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…It’s me. Suzune,” I said aloud.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You said you wanted to talk?” he asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What did you want to talk about?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Um, well, why did you send me a message like that…?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is that really important right now? Is that what you wanted to talk to me about on the phone?” he asked in return.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“N-No, that’s not it.” I had a feeling that he was about to end the call, so I frantically denied it, trying to stop him from hanging up. “Actually, if you’re okay with it, would you…mind meeting me, in person?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In person?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Y-Yes.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“When you enrolled in this school and rejected my proposal that you drop out, our relationship was over. You do understand that, don’t you?” he asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was the harsh reality of the situation. I couldn’t help but think that he had called me like this only on a whim. That was how distant my brother and I were now. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Honestly, I wanted to talk to my brother about lots of things. About everything that had happened. About everything that was going to happen. But…my brother would never ask me about those things.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s something I want to ask you in person,” I told him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">My brother went silent. I slowly continued speaking.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This will be the last time… I will not involve myself with you ever again,” I told him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was the only thing I could offer him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. Very well.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was the conversation that I had last night.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was now on my way to meet with my brother. We had decided to meet in the special building to avoid being seen by anyone else, since no one usually went there. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When I arrived at my destination, I saw he was already there.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">6.2</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">“S</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">ORRY TO HAVE</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> kept you waiting…” said Suzune.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Manabu just stood there quietly. From Suzune’s point of view, her brother hadn’t changed at all. He was still the goal that she’d been chasing all this time.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wonder how long it’s been since we’ve talked like this. Just the two of us.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…If we’re not counting that time right after enrollment, then it’s been about three years,” said Suzune.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That sounds about right. So, it’s been that long, hm?” said Manabu.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He thought back to when Suzune was in her first year of junior high. When Suzune had decided that she would enroll in the Tokyo Metropolitan Advanced Nurturing School, he’d pushed her away. At the time, he’d never thought his little sister would follow the </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">same path as him. But now here she was, standing before him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You said you wanted to talk to me. I’m listening.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If she came right out and said she wanted to reconcile with her brother, then the conversation would be over right now. Manabu would walk away without a moment’s hesitation. The old Suzune might well have answered that way.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s about the supplementary special exam. I’m sure you already know what the first-years are dealing with,” said Suzune.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. Classes are being forced to expel students in this exam.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s correct.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And?” Manabu urged her to keep speaking. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Suzune, who had been speaking relatively eloquently and easily thus far, stammered a bit.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you’re asking about my personal balance of private points, then I need to tell you that I used nearly all of them during the school camp. If that’s what you’re after, then you’re wasting both our time.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s not it. I’m not asking you…for that kind of support,” said Suzune.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She steeled herself, as if trying to cut through the hesitation she was feeling.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, what I wanted to talk to you about today is… I… Please, give me courage.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Having managed to get that out, she continued speaking.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I want to face this exam head-on. Other people are forming groups and trying to control the vote in order to keep themselves from getting expelled. But I’m sure that they’ll come to regret that down the road. That’s why, I… I want to stand up to them,” said Suzune.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Manabu just looked silently back at her, meeting her eyes as she spoke. As he listened, he thought back to what Ayanokouji had said yesterday. What his little sister was trying to do was not the easy path. But she was trying to do something with her own two hands—something that others couldn’t do. And so, to bolster her resolve, she’d come to meet with her brother.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you have time now?” he asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I didn’t really have any plans after this…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Suzune was a little taken aback by her brother’s unexpected question.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Then, before we get into the specifics of your situation, I’d like to ask you something. How’s school been for you?” he asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Has it been fun?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Uh, well… O-Okay,” said Suzune, obviously thrown off. She hadn’t been expecting to be asked a question like that. “I-I’m sorry, but well, uh…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though she seemed unable to answer the question, Manabu didn’t scold her for it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, if the question is…whether or not I’m having fun, then, I honestly don’t know. But it’s not boring,” said Suzune.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s just that there’s nothing I can really do about this, is all. I’m freaking out on the inside.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t worry. Thanks to Horikita, things don’t seem too bad. If anything, it’s like she saved you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If it weren’t for what Horikita said, a lot of students would’ve shown up on the day of the vote oblivious to the true goings-on. And they would’ve put my name on their ballots without thinking too deeply about it, concerned only with saving themselves. It was pretty easy to imagine such a scenario.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But… Where did Horikita-san hear about Yamauchi-kun’s betrayal, I wonder?” said Airi, the question suddenly spilling from her lips. “We’re all close friends with Kiyotaka-kun, so it makes sense that word never reached us, right? I would’ve thought that Horikita-san would’ve been in a similar position, but…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, you’ve got a good point there… Horikita didn’t really seem like she tried to form a group herself.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yamauchi was probably pretty pissed about that right now too, thinking someone in the group he’d worked to put together had betrayed him to Horikita. Not that he had the time or composure to realize that and point it out back then, of course.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t know who, but I’m guessing this means there’s someone out there who doesn’t want Kiyopon to get expelled, right?” said Haruka.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. Can’t all be bad apples in our class, then.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">They didn’t notice that the </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">someone</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> they were talking about was, in fact, Kei and me.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">6.5</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">O</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">N OUR WAY BACK</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> we saw Hirata sitting on a bench, a totally blank expression on his face. If anyone else saw him looking like this, they’d probably hesitate to approach him—because no one had ever seen him in such a state before.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“He seems so defeated.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. He doesn’t even look like himself.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Both Haruka and Akito understood the abnormality of the situation immediately. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I think I’m going to try talking to him a little,” I said aloud.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’d think twice about that, Kiyotaka. Don’t you think it’s probably best if we just leave him alone right now?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Maybe. But there’s something that’s been bothering me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Something bothering you?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry. You guys head on back. I have a feeling he won’t be too receptive right now if we try to talk to him as a big group. On the off chance he does get angry, I’d rather it just be at me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…All right. But remember, the vote’s tomorrow. It’s probably a good idea not to get him riled up. To be honest, Hirata’s the hardest for me to get a read on right now. I have no clue who he’s going to be using his criticism votes on,” said Akito.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I nodded in response to Akito’s warning and broke away from the group, grateful that everyone in the group understood what I was getting at. They headed on back, but I didn’t go over to Hirata right away. Instead, I took a picture of him looking completely depressed and dispirited from a distance, then sent the picture to Kei along with a short text. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hirata.” Not letting this chance slip by, I called out to Hirata right after.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Ayanokouji-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You got a minute?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, I do have time now. Well, um, actually, I wanted to talk to you too anyway.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata might have been waiting for me. If not, there’d be no point in him sitting out in the cold in a place like this. He wasn’t sitting in the middle of the bench, but near the edge, which could be taken as a sign that he was leaving a space open for someone. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I sat down in the open space beside him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The spring thaw will be here soon,” said Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I…was sure we’d all make it to spring together. Well, no. Even now, I still believe that deep in my heart,” said Hirata, even though what happened today had nearly brought the whole class down.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Despite exposing the most foolish and unsightly parts of himself in class earlier, he remained unchanged at his&#160;core. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I hate having to lose someone,” said Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s a problem we can’t solve, though. Whether it’s me, Yamauchi, or someone else, a student has to be sacrificed,” I told him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There wasn’t any emotion on Hirata’s profile.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Can I leave it to you?” he said. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Leave what?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Class C. I want you to lead the class from here on out instead of me,” said Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t be absurd. I can’t do something that big. If you want to protect the people in our class, do it yourself, Hirata.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s impossible. I just… It’s impossible,” he replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was guessing he hated himself for not being able to make a decision. That was almost certainly what was going through his head right now, but it wasn’t all there was.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I made the same mistake again. I was so remorseful, spent so much time reflecting on what happened back then, but still…” said Hirata, his voice trailing off.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I could see his regret in the tears welling up in his eyes. How much was this exam hurting him?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I feel like I can rest easy and entrust everything to someone as amazing as you,” said Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He let out a sigh, his white breath visible in the cold air. Looking at him now, I saw no trace of the dazzling and enviable leader of our class.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, for this special exam, it’s okay if you use one vote for me, one for Yamauchi, and then one for Horikita,” I&#160;told him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So you’re saying to leave the decision to the other students?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There was no need for Hirata to choose one of the three of us. The other 39 students would do that themselves.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You really are amazing, Ayanokouji-kun,” said Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Not really.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“As I was sitting here, I was approached by both Horikita-san and Yamauchi-kun. Horikita-san told me to vote for Yamauchi-kun. Yamauchi-kun told me to vote for you. They each had their own way of arguing their case. But you’re the only one not trying to throw someone else to the wolves. That’s not something just anyone can do,” said Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was precisely what my strategy was. I’d simply come to the decision that wasn’t a good idea to force Hirata to vote for someone. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m glad we were able to talk. I feel like I might be able to find my answer soon.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is that so?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata stood up. Perhaps he’d found his own way to get through this exam—not that I was going to allow it, though.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Should we head back?” he suggested.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The two of us headed back to the dormitory, without saying another word.</span></p>
