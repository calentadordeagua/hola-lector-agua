<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 3:<br/>In-Class Voting</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HE FOLLOWING DAY</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> was Tuesday, March 2nd. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira strode into morning homeroom shortly after the bell rang. It was a sight we saw every single day, and my classmates were all relaxed. She’d announced our test results yesterday, informing us that we had all successfully passed the year-end exam. And there were still a few days left before the final special exam of the year began on March 8th. There was no reason for anyone to be nervous, so their relaxed state was only natural.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, Chabashira had a remarkably grim look on her face as she stood at the podium. She projected a sense of tension and anxiety, and those feelings spread to the students, too.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Um, did something happen?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata, the person always putting the harmony of the class first, took the initiative to speak up. She didn’t answer him right away, but remained silent, almost like she was reluctant to speak at all. In the past, she’d always mercilessly launched into an explanation of whatever test we were facing, no matter how harsh it was. Given that, it didn’t take long at all for the students to understand that something was off about this situation.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“—There is something that I have to tell you all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She finally opened her mouth to speak. Her expression was as stern as always, but the strain in her voice gave me the impression she was struggling as hard as she could to get the words out. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Just as I told you yesterday, the final special exam for your first year will begin on March 8th. After you complete this special exam, you will be promoted to second-year students. That is typical of how things have worked in past years.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira turned around, picked up a piece of chalk, and extended her hand toward the blackboard. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“However, this year, things will be slightly different from previous years.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“Different?”</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> Hirata asked, sensing that something was off.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even after taking your year-end final exams, not a single person has been expelled this year. Getting to this point without a single expulsion has never happened before in the history of this school.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That just means we’re really good, doesn’t it?” said Ike, cutting in. I didn’t think he was supposed to be celebrating, though. If Chabashira were acting like her usual self, she would probably have sternly reminded him not to get carried away.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s right. And the school recognizes that, too. Normally, you could say this would be something to celebrate. Even we, the faculty and staff, hope to have as many students as possible make it to graduation. However, it must be said that problems arise when things don’t go according to plan.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The way she phrased that was strange. Hirata and my neighbor, Horikita, also sensed something off about her choice of words.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You sound like something’s bothering you. Like you’re bothered about the fact that no one’s been expelled so far.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s not true. But sometimes things happen that I didn’t expect,” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She’d said this would normally be cause for celebration, but her tone was grave. Horikita spoke up, as if trying to banish those feelings.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What are you trying to say? That there’s something wrong with us?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But nothing we said would change whatever Chabashira was about to tell us. She wasn’t free to do as she pleased. She was an employee of the school. Her only role was to convey its instructions to us.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The school has decided that, in consideration of the fact that no one has been expelled from the first-year class—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The words seemed to get stuck in Chabashira’s throat. She struggled to get them out.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“—as a ‘measure for exceptional circumstances,’ a supplementary special exam will be held immediately, starting today.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira wrote today’s date—Tuesday, March 2nd—on the blackboard, as well as the words “supplementary special exam.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait, what? What is this all about?! Dude, </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">another</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> special exam? That’s the </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">worst thing ever</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">! The school’s acting like a whiny little kid! They didn’t get their way since no one got expelled, and so they’re adding this other test!” shouted Ike.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira just ignored everything he said. Students had no right to object, and in fact…maybe she was the one being forced to give us this test against her wishes. She looked much tenser than usual. She wasn’t trying to scare us—it likely that the school really had decided on this test in a hurry.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Something tells me things are a little different from the way they’ve been so far,” Horikita muttered quietly, having realized that there was no point in fighting back. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Only the students who pass this special exam will be able to go on to take the special exam on March 8th,” said Chabashira, pausing for a moment after that brief explanation.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Come on, I don’t get this at all! I can’t believe they’re only giving </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">us</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> this extra special exam now!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Your frustration is completely understandable. This special exam is being administered although it was never previously planned for. Even though it’s just one more test than what students in the past have taken, the fact that it places a burden on all of you cannot be denied. This is something that the other teachers and I are taking very seriously,” said Chabashira.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The teachers were taking it seriously, huh? Which meant it was specifically the </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">instructors</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> who were taking it seriously, while the school administrators might not feel the same way. At least, that was one way to interpret her phrasing. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was certainly true that adding special exams would be tough on the students. If it was a written exam that tested academic skill, then students would have to study harder. If it was a physical exam, they’d have to train for it in a similar fashion. Regardless of the test’s contents, forcing the students to take it was harsh. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That being said, it wasn’t going to go away, no matter how much we complained. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira continued speaking.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The contents of this special exam are exceedingly simple. The dropout rate isn’t high at all, either, at less than three percent per class.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">A dropout rate of less than three percent. That certainly sounded low. But this supplementary special exam would certainly be unlike any written exams we’d had so far. There was no need to specifically mention the dropout rate. In fact, such an expression had never been used in any of the previous exams.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The students who noticed this felt even more suspicious. I looked over at my neighbor, who coincidentally looked over at me at the same time, causing our eyes to meet.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What is it, Ayanokouji-kun?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh. Nothing.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you look at me for no reason at all, that comes off as creepy, you know?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Yeah, I guess so.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I decided to just look out the window. The classroom was small enough that I could hear everything being said no matter where I was looking.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Just what kind of test will this </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">be</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">? And what will it be testing?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You seem anxious, but really, there’s nothing to be worried about. The supplementary special exam has absolutely nothing at all to do with your intellectual or physical ability. It’s something so simple that, when the time comes, anyone can do it. That’s right. It’s as simple as writing your own name on your test paper. And there’s only a three percent chance you’ll get expelled as a result. You would agree that that’s a low chance, right?” said Chabashira.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She still hadn’t touched on the core issue—the actual contents of the exam.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…The difficulty level doesn’t matter. From our point of view, even three percent is scary.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s true. You have a point, Hirata. I do understand how that three percent scares you. However, whether or not you can reduce that risk depends on what you do in the time you have before the actual test. As I’m sure you’ve probably already guessed,” said Chabashira.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How was that number derived? Based on what you’re saying, it sounds like it’s a simple lottery. Is that it?” asked Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The odds that someone from our class would be expelled were high. Chabashira had casually thrown out the three percent figure, but the burden this exam would place on the students was greater than they could imagine. Hirata, understanding this right away, pressed Chabashira on that point.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Please tell us. Just what kind of test will this be?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The title of this special exam is: ‘In-Class Voting.’” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In-Class… Voting?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira wrote out the title of the exam on the blackboard.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll now explain the rules behind this special exam. Starting today, you will have four days to evaluate your classmates. You will choose three students who you think deserve praise, and three that you think deserve criticism, and cast your votes on Saturday. That is all,” she explained.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So the students would be evaluating one another? If you thought about it in simple terms, then students like Hirata and Kushida would get a lot of votes and come out on top. On the other hand, students who were considered annoying or thought to be dragging the class down would sink to the bottom. The urgency of the situation was evinced by the fact that the test was being held on a Saturday, which was originally supposed to be a day off. However, based on what Chabashira said—</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Th-That’s it? That’s all there is to this exam?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s it. That’s all there is to it. I told you, didn’t I? It’s a simple test.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait, how do you determine pass or fail—a good score or a bad score—with a test like that?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I will explain that part now.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Tightening her grip on the chalk further, Chabashira continued to write.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The core of this special exam is the number of praise votes and criticism votes each student gets as a result of the polling. The top student—in other words, the student who gets the most praise votes—will receive a special reward. This special reward will not be private points, but instead, one ‘Protection Point.’ This is a new system.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We hadn’t heard of that kind of point before. Of course, everyone’s attention was piqued. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In the unlikely event that you are up for expulsion in the future, Protection Points will allow you to reverse that decision. Even if you fail a test, Protection Points will allow you to nullify questions you got wrong, based on the number of such points you have. However, these points cannot be transferred to others.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was no exaggeration to say that every student in the classroom was more shocked than they’d ever been before.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sure you all understand exactly how powerful these points are. In fact, they are worth, comparatively speaking, about twenty million private points. Of course, if you are an excellent student with no fear of expulsion, then they might not be worth that much,” she explained.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That probably wasn’t true. No matter who you were, you would absolutely want the ability to nullify expulsion. There wasn’t a student out there who wouldn’t welcome having that power. This was an extravagant reward. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In fact…it was far </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">too</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> extravagant. Depending on how you used them, these Protection Points could become an incredibly dangerous weapon. And, given how extravagant the reward was, it was clear that the penalty suffered by whoever came in last would also be significant.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, does that mean something bad will happen to the bottom three students…?” asked an anxious Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Not necessarily. In this case, only one student—the one who received the most criticism votes in the entire class—will be given the penalty. Other students will not be penalized, no matter how many criticism votes they receive. After all, the purpose of this supplementary special exam is to elect one student to be in the lead and one student to come in last.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What kind of penalty is it?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This supplementary special exam is not like the ones you’ve had thus far. One aspect, in particular, is different—and it is that this test is being held to resolve the problem of a lack of dropouts. That is the purpose for which it was designed,” explained Chabashira.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yep. What the students should be concerned about was the reason why this supplementary special exam was being held in the first place. If this exam was being held because no one had been expelled so far, then—</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This special exam is, as I explained, quite easy. This is not a difficult test, irrespective of whether you have poor grades or are bad at sports. But why, then, did the school prepare such an extraordinary reward as Protection Points? Because this is an exam where it is probably impossible to advance without someone being expelled.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira turned and looked at all of us, one by one.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s right. The student who places at the bottom will…be expelled from this school.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If there was a vote, there would be results. If there were results, someone would place first and someone last. And the person who came last would be expelled. The outcome was inevitable. No matter how exceptional the class, or how mediocre, the results would be the same. The only difference would be the question of who. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">So that’s what kind of exam this is, huh?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The school, frustrated by the lack of students getting expelled, had decided to hold this supplementary exam. It was an exam that had to result in someone being kicked out—if not, there would be no point in holding it in the first place. But the person who popped up in my mind was none other than Sakayanagi’s father, the board chairman. You couldn’t assess everything there was to know about a person just from meeting them once, but he didn’t seem like the kind of person who’d implement such an outrageous exam.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“U-Uh, I don’t really get it, sensei. S-So, you’re seriously saying that if someone comes in last place, then…that person is gonna get expelled?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s correct. That person will be on the chopping block, so to speak. But don’t worry. The class as a whole will not be penalized even if someone is expelled this time around. That’s the nature of this exam,” replied Chabashira.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This was clearly different from the special exams we’d seen thus far. Although the probability of expulsion varied from individual to individual, there was usually an equal chance for everyone to avoid expulsion together. This time, however, the exam was designed to ensure someone would be sacrificed. This was the “measure for exceptional circumstances” that the school had prepared. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was precisely because they were pressing for a forced expulsion that they were dangling something like Protection Points before us. But even so, the risk we were being made to bear was disproportionately high. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re probably thinking that this is outrageous. As your teacher, I think so, too. But it’s been decided, and there’s nothing we can do to fight it. We have no choice but to follow the rules and take this special exam,” said Chabashira.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Seriously? Really…?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">A dark cloud loomed over our class, which had only just managed to survive the year-end final exams. This weekend, one of us would be gone.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The time you have until voting day is limited, so please allow me to continue explaining the rules. The number of praise votes and criticism votes for each student will be made public at the end of the exam, meaning the entire class’s results will, too. However, information about who specifically voted for who will not be disclosed. The voting will be conducted anonymously,” said Chabashira.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">They had to make the voting anonymous if they were going to administer this exam. Praise votes aside, the matter of who gave who criticism votes would continue to be an issue for a long time afterward.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Continuing on. One praise vote and one criticism vote will cancel each other out. If someone received ten criticism votes and thirty praise votes, they would effectively end up with a positive value of twenty. Regardless of whether it is praise or criticism, you cannot vote for yourself. You are also not allowed to vote for the same person more than once.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What if we abstain…? Could we, for example, only submit praise votes?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No. Naturally, you cannot. You must fill in three names for praise votes and three for criticism votes. Also, even if you miss school on the day of the special exam because you are feeling ill, you will still have to cast your votes,” replied Chabashira.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That meant we couldn’t leave our ballots blank or abstain from voting. Several students looked deeply troubled. This exam certainly posed a serious threat for people who anticipated getting a lot of criticism votes. The students who’d made it this far by piggybacking on others’ efforts would be feeling all the more pressure right now. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…No, it’s too soon to be giving in to despair,” said Hirata, offering words of comfort to Ike and the others, to try and calm them down. “Sensei said that it was ‘probably’ impossible. That means there must be some kind of loophole.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In the tests we’d taken so far, Chabashira had chosen her words to give us hints about how to make it through. But how would that work this time? The ‘probably’ in her statement seemed to imply there were a limited number of methods we could use.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s not easy, but there most certainly is a method you can use to avoid expulsion,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wh-What do you mean, Horikita?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If we choose three people to receive praise votes and three people to receive criticism votes, and as long as we can control the voting by having everyone in class band together on this, then we’d essentially cancel everything out, so the students getting only praise and the students getting only criticism would all end up with zero. If we did that, then no one would end up in last place. Am I wrong?” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I-I see, that makes sense! Wow, way to go, Suzune!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was possible, if all our classmates cooperated. But if even just one person turned out to be a traitor, then the student targeted for betrayal would be sent down the path of expulsion. Also, there was a tempting reward waiting for whoever claimed the top spot, in the form of Protection Points. People like Kushida, who hated Horikita, might be a problem. Was it possible to compensate for that by making adjustments to the plan?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If Kushida was assigned to cast a criticism vote for Horikita, that might avert the danger, to some extent. If&#160;the final vote totals were made known to us afterward, it would probably help identify who’d turned traitor. We couldn’t afford to backstab each other on a whim.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What Horikita said just now about controlling the vote would be meaningless,” said Chabashira.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do you mean?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If no one is selected to be in first place and last place in this exam, the results will be rejected. Whether intentionally or accidentally, if the results end up showing that everyone has received a total of zero, then another vote will be held. In other words, the exam will be repeated endlessly until a decision is made on whom to expel.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The escape route that the students were desperately scrambling for had just been cut off.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait, isn’t that…kind of a strange rule? If we genuinely happened to end up with a total of zero after choosing who to cast our praise and criticism votes for, then we’d cast our votes the same way the second time, producing the same result. If you overrule the results by force, then you can’t say those results were based on a fair evaluation,” replied Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Horikita, your reasoning is correct. If you wound up with a total of zero by coincidence, then it’s true that making you cast your votes again would be contradictory. But think about this realistically. In a test where you’re being asked to rank someone first and someone last, it should be </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">extremely unlikely</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> that you’d just so happen to wind up with a zero-vote total for every single person.&#160;No?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira made a very good point in her response, too. A zero-vote result across the board wasn’t happening unless the vote was intentionally set up to be that way.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Then, what happens if two or more people tie for first or last place?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That outcome was quite likely.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There will be a deciding vote. If the vote remains divided even then, and a decision cannot be reached, the school will use a special method that they’ve devised in order to break the tie. I cannot explain what that method is at this current time,” said Chabashira.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So she’d only reveal it to us in the event the vote ended in a tie? The chances of such a deadlock were pretty low.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s no need to worry about that. In truth, the chances of actually getting to a tiebreaker are next to zero,” added Chabashira, sharing my opinion.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why is that? I think it’s probably enough.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, that’s because…where praise votes are concerned, we’ll be asking you to vote for students outside your class, too.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Outside our class?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You will be asked to choose one student that you deem worthy of praise from the three other classes. Naturally, each of those votes will also count as a praise vote. In other words, in the unlikely event that a student is disliked by their entire class but is liked by everyone in the other classes, it’s entirely possible that they would come out with about eighty praise votes, even after subtracting the criticism votes they would get from their own class,” she explained. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was quite irregular. It meant you could theoretically secure over one hundred praise votes, which did certainly make the chances of a tie much, much lower. I supposed the big picture for this supplementary exam was now visible.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.00em;">Supplementary Exam – In-Class Voting</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">EXAM CONTENTS:</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The exam consists of a poll, wherein students in each class will each cast three praise votes and three criticism votes.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">RULE 1:</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Praise votes and criticism votes effectively cancel each other out. Praise Votes minus Criticism Votes = Final Result.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">RULE 2:</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Students cannot cast votes for themselves, regardless of whether they are praise or criticism votes.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">RULE 3:</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Voting multiple times for the same person, leaving the ballot blank and/or abstaining from voting, etc., are not permitted under any circumstances.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">RULE 4: </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The exam will be conducted repeatedly until a first place and last place have been decided. The student in last place will be expelled.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">RULE 5:</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Each student will also be required to cast a separate praise vote specifically for a student in one of the other three classes. This vote is mandatory.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was all. Those were the details of the supplementary exam. There was no doubt that it was simple and straightforward, but it was also the cruelest exam we’d had so far. Someone from our class, and from each of the other classes, was going to disappear, this weekend.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sensei, why did you say that it was ‘probably’ impossible? From what I’m hearing, it doesn’t sound like there are any loopholes.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s right. There are none. However, there </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">are</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> variables at play. I’m sure this thought has crossed your minds, but things change when you use private points,” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re saying that we can resolve an expulsion with our points?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Twenty million points. If you can come up with that amount, then the school will have no choice but to rescind the expulsion.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Which was precisely why she’d said “probably” before, huh? Not restricting the transfer of private points meant negotiation was a viable tactic. In other words, the ability to buy praise votes with money </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">was</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> recognized as an ability in and of itself, and you were free to go ahead and do so.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The school was basically telling us to employ what abilities we wished. The skills we’d demonstrated to those around us over the past year. The financial power that we’d accumulated through our exams. Perhaps even what you might call the power of teamwork, through our friendships.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“P-Please wait a minute. Twenty million points, that’s…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s impossible, even if you pooled all of Class C’s points together. However, you could scrape together points from the other classes, or you ask for the upperclassmen’s charity. If you do that, then it’s not an impossible amount.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was true that we could realistically accumulate that much if we transferred points between classes or grade levels. As for gathering enough to protect one person in Class C, however—that would be difficult. The odds were high that not even Class A or Class B could gather enough points for that, even if they collected points from their peers. And even if they did manage to gather that many, it was doubtful that they’d actually use those points to protect a single student. Sacrificing all the wealth you’d accumulated so far was a considerably risky&#160;move. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That is the only method you can use to defend yourselves. Let me just say this: it is absolutely impossible poke holes in the rules set by the school in any other way. Now, the rest is for you to assess and decide,” said Chabashira, finishing her speech just as homeroom came to an end. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As our teacher disappeared from view, the students succumbed to feelings of dread.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do we do?! What do we do?! Dude, this is seriously the </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">worst</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> exam ever, isn’t it?!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Will you boys shut up?!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The hell are you telling us to shut up for?! You’re probably thinking about how you’re going to give me criticism votes, aren’t you?!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The guys and girls clashed with shouts and jeers, quickly turning into a jumbled mob with both sides on their guard.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How unsightly,” sneered one male student, watching the guys and girls fighting. It was none other than Kouenji Rokusuke, the single most peculiar student in our class. “Really now, there’s no use kicking and screaming now, is there?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And how the hell are </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">you</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">, of all people, so calm about this? Are you even aware of how much trouble you’ve caused the rest of the class so far?” said Sudou, pressing Kouenji for a response.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was certainly true that Kouenji’s capricious nature had complicated things for the class many times so far. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You selfishly withdrew from both the uninhabited island test and the sports festival, remember?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The class’s eyes fell on them. The more weak-willed of them, not wanting to be expelled themselves, were currently looking for someone to be the scapegoat. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It would appear the one who doesn’t understand this situation is </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">you</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">, Red Hair-kun.” Kouenji crossed his legs and laid them on top of his desk. “You seem to think that what you’ve developed over this past year is the key to this particular special exam.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s because it is!” snapped Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, it is not. This special exam is focused on the next two years.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kouenji outright rejected Sudou’s opinion. No—the whole class’s opinion.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? What are you even talking about…?” said Sudou uncomprehendingly. He probably thought this was one of Kouenji’s usual ridiculous antics.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t you see? This special test is, quite literally, a special case. It’s customary that a class that has a student expelled is severely penalized, no? However, no such penalty exists this time. In other words, this is an excellent opportunity to remove an unnecessary student, you see,” said Kouenji.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, that’s all the more reason </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">you’d</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> be a target, Mr. Troublemaker!” snapped Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No. I will not be a target.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? …And what exactly makes you so sure of that?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, that’s because I’m quite superior,” declared Kouenji with unapologetic, overwhelming boldness. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sudou flinched in the face of his unwavering confidence. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ve </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">always</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> placed near the top of the class in written examinations. Near the top of my entire grade level, actually. In fact, I came in second by a slim margin on the year-end final exam. Of course, if I were to really put my mind to it, getting first place would be no trouble at all. You understand that I surpass you when it comes to physical ability as well, don’t you?” added Kouenji, flaunting the depth of his potential.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Y-Yeah, and so what? It’s pointless if you don’t take anything seriously!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I agree. In that case, I will, as they say, ‘turn over a new leaf.’ Beginning with this exam, I will become a useful student, one who contributes to the class through the various exams and such. Don’t you think that would be a rather significant boon for our class?” said Kouenji.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wh-Wha-… Who would even believe something like that?! I’m way more useful to the class than you are!” shouted Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He was completely justified to say so. I had no reason to believe what Kouenji had just said, and neither did any of the other students. In fact, we couldn’t imagine him taking things seriously, starting with this exam. Honestly, it was likely nothing would change. If he made it through this exam, it was obvious that he’d return to leading a carefree life, doing as he pleased.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><img alt="" height="100%" id="INTERIORIMAGES_2" src="/hola-lector-agua/images/books/v10/OEBPS/Images/INTERIORIMAGES_2.jpg" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style=" top: 0px; vertical-align: top; height: 100%;"/></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well then, allow me to turn the question around. What you just said, that you are more useful than I. Is that something everyone here believes?” asked Kouenji, not addressing just Sudou, but the entire class. “Actually, I suppose it’s not just Red Hair-kun. There’s really no guarantee that a student who hasn’t been useful thus far might become useful in the future, is there? You can make whatever claims you want, as I just did. But what you really need is hidden skill. If you don’t have that, then you won’t be able to convince anyone.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The notion that students without ability had to work hard to turn over a new leaf. The notion that students with ability had to work hard to turn over a new leaf. Kouenji was saying that, though those two statements sounded similar, they were actually quite different. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He seemed certain that he wasn’t going to collect criticism votes and end up in last place. If anything, he seemed to be welcoming this supplementary exam with open arms. But it wasn’t like he wasn’t in any danger at all. Depending on how things went, he was at real risk of accruing criticism votes. For better or for worse, he was being much too blunt. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But if I were being totally honest, I agreed with Kouenji. If you considered the class as a whole, then we needed to come to a clear decision about this supplementary exam. It wasn’t a popularity contest—it was a chance to single out and eliminate an unnecessary student for the whole class’s sake. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The exams we’d taken thus far had probably had past instances where students who possessed some exceptional strengths also had some weaknesses, and got expelled because of those weaknesses. To put it more plainly, all we had to do was look at Sudou, the person engaged in argument with Kouenji. Though blessed with great physical prowess, his academic skills were near the bottom of the class. In fact, he’d almost gotten expelled from school once before because of his academic shortcomings.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But, with Horikita’s help, Sudou had started to gradually make up for those shortcomings. As a result, he’d begun to demonstrate his value as an asset to the class. </span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Stop starin’ at him so much,” said Akito.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okaaay,” replied Haruka, responding to Akito’s warning by tossing her hands up in the air.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Anyway, back to talking about Class C. How do you think we should take what Kouenji said?” said Akito, directing his question at Keisei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Keisei must have been thinking about that too, because he replied almost immediately.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You mean the stuff about people being skilled enough to stick around? He has a point, I suppose. But even so, I think that Kouenji, in particular, is an unnecessary student. He’d always getting the class all riled up. To be honest, he’s terrifying.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Keisei disliked taking risks. If you looked at things from his perspective, I supposed Kouenji was precisely the kind of person you couldn’t plan for.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Besides… Well, this might come off as a little cruel, but my heart doesn’t exactly ache for Kouenji. He’s probably going to be one of the easiest people to write in a criticism vote for. What about you guys?” he asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I suppose you might be right. If we do have to pick a name, then I guess it’s better to have someone in mind that we won’t hesitate to vote for when the time comes.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Um… But even though Kouenji-kun is a rather strange person, he always gets amazing test scores, doesn’t he? I think he contributes to the class far more than I do,” said Airi, speaking up in defense of Kouenji while clearly still feeling anxious herself. “Whenever the test results get announced, I always think, ‘Wow, Keisei-kun and Kouenji-kun are really amazing…’”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Come on, that’s not good, Airi. If you don’t come to a decision at a time like this, you’re only going to make yourself suffer later, y’know?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I know that, but…” Even so, Airi seemed to feel a strong resistance to the idea of just voting someone out.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, at any rate, I think that Kouenji-kun is our pick. Can we all agree on that?” said Haruka.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I have no objections,” replied Akito.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Haruka looked over towards Keisei, asking him, “Is this a good plan?” with her eyes.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“For the time being, yeah. Since we have to pick three people anyway, we can just change our votes depending on the situation,” said Keisei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So, the members of the Ayanokouji Group had, at least tentatively, had decided to use their criticism votes on Kouenji. Our opinions on the matter varied—some thought we needed Kouenji, and some disagreed. In my opinion, Kouenji was risky. His capricious whims could have a major negative impact on the class.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But… there was no doubt that he was talented enough to overcome that issue. If Kouenji ever tackled a test or assignment in full seriousness, he could achieve almost anything. Even though I hadn’t seen the full extent of his abilities yet, I was certain he had the skills to do so. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I don’t hate him or anything but… Well, for better or worse, Kouenji is an unknown quantity.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That seemed to be partly why Akito was convinced he should use his criticism vote on Kouenji. Kouenji just stuck out, compared to everyone else. Or rather, he was someone you just couldn’t get the measure of, even with all the talk surrounding him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Other than that… there’s Ike-kun, Yamauchi-kun, and Sudou-kun. Those three seem like the main targets for criticism votes, don’t they?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. I’d say that those three and Kouenji are very likely candidates for expulsion right now. But I can’t imagine they’ll sit around quietly and wait for exam day. I’m sure they’ll probably try to form a large group to collect praise votes and avoid as many criticism votes as possible.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s not like we’re in the clear ourselves by any means.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was right. The test had already begun. This was a battle to make allies while simultaneously establishing a common enemy.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wow. With the discussion we’re having right now…it’s hard to imagine everyone in class was all buddy-buddy just until this morning.” Akito let out a frustrated sigh as he thought about what laid ahead. “I’m just sick of it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Haruka must have thought of something, because she looked over at Ryuuen once again. “There are still several candidates for expulsion left. Maybe it would be best if everyone had a chance to avoid getting expelled.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was precisely because Haruka understood Class C’s current state that she knew how difficult of a situation that Ryuuen, in Class D, was in. It didn’t matter what kind of person you were. If everyone was out to get you, there was nothing you could do.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, Miyacchi, Yukimuu. If you were in Ryuuen-kun’s position, what would you do?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It probably wouldn’t matter. I mean, if the entire class is against you, there’s no point in even struggling. If it were me, I’d just give up,” said Akito, expressing that he’d just throw in the towel right away.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Keisei gave Haruka’s question some serious thought, but after a while, he simply shook his head.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nothing.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nothing, huh? What if he, like, threatened everyone in class or something?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’d just have the opposite effect.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If anything, there were probably several students who were expecting Ryuuen to do just that. Anyone Ryuuen threatened would probably have no reservations about using their criticism votes on him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, in that case, what about getting praise votes by yielding to the other classes?” asked Haruka.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If Ryuuen asked you, would you use your praise votes on him?” asked Keisei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? No, I doubt it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There you have it,” answered Keisei with a nod. “I’m sure most people would come to the same conclusion, since they know what Ryuuen’s like. There can’t be a lot of oddballs out there who’d want to help a guy like that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay, what if he like, bribed his classmates to buy votes or something?” asked Haruka.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even if Ryuuen’s saved up quite a few points, I can’t imagine he’ll have enough to buy a lot of votes. He hasn’t just made a lot of enemies—he’s earned the reputation of being a really troublesome opponent. I don’t see his classmates selling him their votes for cheap,” said Keisei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But doesn’t he have a chance to get praise votes from the other classes then?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t think so. As someone from another class, don’t you feel like it’d be easier to fight Class D with Ryuuen gone?” asked Keisei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah… Yeah, you might be right about that. It was scary, not knowing what he’d do next.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was exactly why Ryuuen was in this predicament. He’d become nothing but a burden, dragging down the rest of Class D. He might have dared to try collect praise votes and prevent his expulsion, but since even the other classes saw him as a hostile and troublesome foe, many people wanted him gone. There was little benefit to anyone, whether in or outside his class, in deliberately keeping someone who might present a future threat around. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Some students might be wondering what the future would hold. Some might even believe Ryuuen would become their class’s savior. But it was safe to say, based on what we could see, that such students were few and far between. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even if he did get multiple students to make deals with him to use their praise votes on each other, it would be very difficult to verify if they’d actually held up their end of the bargain. As long as the votes were anonymous, and at least one praise vote actually went through, anyone could lie and say they’d kept their word. In the unlikely event that Ryuuen wanted to dispute the matter, claiming he’d been wrong, it would be too late—he’d already have been expelled. Of course, before you could even get that far, the question still remained of who would even actually want to form a contract with Ryuuen by choice.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, he’s totally screwed then, huh?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“My guess is that he’s doing his best to stay calm. I don’t think he wants to get expelled, but getting all flustered and desperate would just look lame.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, I suppose that’s true… I guess that would be disgraceful for someone who used to be a king.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was a shame, but Ryuuen’s expulsion was a certainty. Of course, if he intended to fight for his life, then the story might turn out differently. But…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Well, no matter how much we argued, we probably weren’t going to come up with any answers. Ryuuen’s thoughts on this situation were something only he knew.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, why don’t we see for ourselves?” said a voice close to my ear. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was Horikita. She had a plastic bag in hand, from which peeked the sandwich she was having for lunch.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“See? See what?” asked Akito, confused by her choice of words, or perhaps sensing something was off.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What Ryuuen-kun is thinking about right now. What’s going through his head. The only way to know for sure is to talk to him,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wouldn’t. It’d be like kicking a hornet’s nest.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Nobody wanted to approach Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, all right then. Never mind,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s no point in getting involved with Ryuuen now. He’s got nothing to do with us in this exam.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I suppose so. It’s true that he has nothing to do with us. But he might be useful to me,” said Horikita. She paused for a brief moment, then, perhaps because I wasn’t getting up to join her, walked off by herself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What did she mean? Might be useful to her…?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Keisei and Akito shook their heads, unable to understand.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, uh, isn’t this kinda bad? Don’t you think Horikita-san might be in danger?” asked Haruka.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I think so too… Kiyotaka-kun,” said Airi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Yeah. I’ll check it out.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t think anything would happen, but it would probably be best if she had someone with her, just in case. For better or for worse, Horikita was the sort of person not to mince words. Akito got up to join me, but I stopped him from coming along, then followed after Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What are you going to talk with Ryuuen about?” I&#160;asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I thought he might be able to give me a useful hint.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">A useful hint? I couldn’t see what Horikita was expecting to get from Ryuuen. But seeing her take action like this, I guessed she had something in mind.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Did Sakura-san and the others ask you to watch out for me?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I knew it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita’s pace didn’t slow as we had that brief exchange. Soon after, we arrived at where Ryuuen sat. He had to have noticed us, but his eyes stayed focused on the book in his hands. Based on what I could see from the pages it was opened to, it was some kind of literary novel. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re awfully relaxed, Ryuuen-kun,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, look who it is. Suzune, eh? And you got your little tagalong with you, it looks like.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He suddenly closed the book with a </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">klop</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">. I could tell by the sticker that it was from the library. It went without saying of course, that when he said ‘little tagalong’, he meant me. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ryuuen briefly glanced at me, but then turned away. He focused his gaze in Horikita’s direction.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And what do you want with me?” he asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wondered why Horikita was willing to take the risk of making contact with Ryuuen at all.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll cut to the chase. What do you intend to do in this special exam?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s nothing I really can do, so I’m not going to do anything,” he replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you’re saying that…you’re prepared to accept expulsion?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If he left things as they were right now, Ryuuen’s expulsion was, needless to say, inevitable.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m a good target for my class. In an exam where you gotta kick someone out, no one wants to deal with the resentment of the person getting kicked to the curb. But I’m a special case,” said Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He reopened his book and looked back down, perhaps because he felt that this conversation wasn’t worth it. Apparently, he was serious about leaving school. About accepting being kicked out. </span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><img alt="" height="100%" id="INTERIORIMAGES_3" src="/hola-lector-agua/images/books/v10/OEBPS/Images/INTERIORIMAGES_3.jpg" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style=" top: 0px; vertical-align: top; height: 100%;"/></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sure people will be casting criticism votes for you. If you do intend to accept your expulsion, then I won’t say anything…and I suppose it won’t just be me. I’m sure there are many people in Class B and Class A who wish to see you gone. For better or for worse, you’ve gone too far, and there’s no one who will offer their hand to you,” said Horikita, hitting Ryuuen with the truth. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The truth hits harder when you’re already aware of it. But the truth wouldn’t hurt Ryuuen at all. He understood and accepted it wholeheartedly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re probably right. Class D doesn’t stand a chance of winning after I’m gone. As my enemy, the best and most reasonable decision would be for you to crush me right here and now,” said Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Rather than taking it in a negative way though, he seemed to react positively.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You have quite a high opinion of yourself. That is very like you, Ryuuen-kun. But even so, you got demoted to Class D because you lacked skill as a leader. Isn’t that right?” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Heh heh. Certainly.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Class D had been a dictatorship ruled by Ryuuen’s iron fist. Now that the system had collapsed and they’d been demoted to into last place, they were losing their chance to bounce back. However, Ryuuen’s plans had never been bound by class rankings in the first place. Whether you were in Class D or in Class A, if you had enough private points, you could turn things around and win. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Which was exactly why he wasn’t upset by being criticized for Class D’s demotion. Being in Class A might have its advantages, but they weren’t worth much. Ryuuen’s strategy went beyond that. It was an interesting battle tactic, but it also had a few flaws. He was keeping his classmates in check with force, rather than seeking their understanding. He’d been looking so far ahead that he lost sight of his own two feet. That was what led to his defeat and current situation.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It seems like you and I will never understand one another, no matter how much we talk,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Seems that way. Happy?” said Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Despite listening to the whole conversation, I still couldn’t see what Horikita wanted to learn.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, today might be the last time I’ll get to speak with you, so may I ask you just one more question?” asked Horikita. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Apparently, she was getting to the point now. What was she hoping to hear that could serve as a useful hint?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re in a more hopeless situation than anyone than now. If you were to try and tackle this exam seriously…would you be able to make it through without getting expelled?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita gave him a sharp look as she asked him that question. It was like she was telling him to look her in the eye and give her an answer. So that was why she’d wanted to talk to Ryuuen, despite having no need to get involved with him. She wanted to ask him how he could survive this almost totally inescapable situation, where there was a ninety-nine percent chance he’d get expelled.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s a stupid question. Obviously, I could,” replied Ryuuen, without a moment’s delay. He seemed to be confident in his belief that he could survive if he wanted to. As he stared back at Horikita, I saw no hesitation in his eyes at all.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even if you’re bluffing, that’s still impressive. I can sense nothing but confidence coming from you,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Satisfied now? Or perhaps you want me to teach you my secret plan to survive?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No need. You and I are in different positions.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, I suppose so.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thank you. I feel that my resolve has been strengthened a little, thanks to you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Your resolve?” asked Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita nodded.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Someone will most certainly be expelled in this supplementary exam. That is a fate that we cannot escape. In that case, we need to make the right decision about whom to expel. Do you understand the weight of the words I’m saying to you right now?” she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ryuuen smiled, not responding with a yes or no. “Your efforts might make the rest of your class push you away,” he said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If that happens, I suppose it just means that I’m not that qualified after all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wow, that’s weak. All I’m hearin’ is bluffing.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Tch…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though Horikita had remained calm, Ryuuen saw through her composed façade. No—rather than seeing through it, I should say he’d reached out and broken&#160;it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re trying to gain some self-confidence by talking to me… But your confidence, your resolve, it’s all still just for show.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita was getting a bit agitated at his words, beginning to heat up.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s the cutting someone out that’s the only hard part of this, you know,” Ryuuen added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…I can do it. I’ve shown no mercy to any student who has held the class back, ever since I enrolled here,” replied Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nah, you can’t.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do you… What do you even know about me?” snapped Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ve had plenty of time to observe you over the past year. I know practically everything about you. Besides, I can see the weakness hiding behind the words coming out of your mouth,” said Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita didn’t stand a chance of winning this game of words. The way she’d so carelessly, sloppily said, “I feel that my resolve has been strengthened a little, thanks to you.” The brief silence before she opened her mouth to say, “I can do it.” Ryuuen was quick to notice the things that others would pay no attention to. Horikita had shown him her weakness without even realizing it. Ryuuen was the one controlling the flow of the conversation.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re already trapped in dull, uneventful complacency. It’s your class. There’s absolutely no way you could be cold enough to make a hard choice now. The people who can are the people like me, who have no lingering affection for their class, or like Sakayanagi, who see their classmates as nothing more than pawns,” said Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The kinds of relationships you now had with your classmates, after making friends in class, were entirely unlike the ones you’d had before you made any friends. Certainly, Horikita wouldn’t have hesitated to do something of the sort when she first started at this school. She’d even been okay with expelling Sudou, who’d failed a test. But could she kick Sudou out now? If you asked that question in earnest, the answer was that she absolutely could not. Relationships were always changing. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You talk a big game, but you really don’t have a plan to get out of this after all, don’t you?” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why do you think that?” asked Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Did you really lose against your classmates? Or did you get beaten by someone outside your class…?” said Horikita, briefly glancing at me for just a minute before immediately looking back at Ryuuen. “At any rate, you’re just going to silently accept that you’ve lost and let yourself get expelled, huh?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She was trying to provoke him. But Ryuuen just quietly listened.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s like a reward for Ishizaki, the guy who beat me. That’s why I’m just gonna quietly accept it. It’s an opportunity that the rest of Class D shouldn’t miss out on. And of course, that includes you, too. You shouldn’t let this chance go,” he said with a smile, before looking back down at his book once again.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…I suppose so. Well then, I’ll have to keep an eye on my friends in Class C to make sure that they don’t waste their praise votes on you, even if it’s just by mistake. Of course, even if I didn’t do anything, it’s not like they’d vote for you anyway,” said Horikita. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She left and I followed after her. Ryuuen’s gaze remained directed at his book, not bothering to look back at us. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita seemed both calm and angry as she walked away.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“He is just absolutely, completely full of hot air. A total liar. There’s nothing he can do at all, but even so, he’s just trying to make himself look good, like a show-off. No matter how much he struggles, it’s not going to stop him from getting expelled,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I dunno about that. Maybe he really does have some way to get out of it,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s impossible. No matter how you look at the situation, there’s no way Ryuuen-kun can avoid expulsion. Even if he apologized right now and tried acting like an actual, decent human being, it wouldn’t change the number of criticism votes he’ll undoubtedly be getting. And it won’t give him more praise votes, either.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, I guess a straightforward strategy like that wouldn’t work.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But it would be pointless if he tried bribing others or resorting to threats, either. You were saying the same thing earlier, weren’t you?” answered Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yeah, that’s true. I guess she’d heard us.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Or perhaps you can see what Ryuuen-kun’s thinking? About how he can get out of being expelled?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nope, I can’t at all,” I answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I tried crunching the numbers and running the variables in my head, but still couldn’t see any reliable method for Ryuuen to survive in his current situation. There was a necessary part of the puzzle that he was lacking, which he’d need if he was going to survive.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Then there you have it,” said Horikita. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She left the café, still looking like she was in a sour mood. I turned around and looked over at Ryuuen for a brief moment. What would have happened if Ryuuen and I had crossed paths earlier…?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, that’s just pointless speculation. Especially at this point in time.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There wasn’t any point in giving any further thought to a student who was going to be leaving. I decided to stop thinking about it and return to my group.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">3.2</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HAT NIGHT,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> I got a phone call from Kei. It was mostly about the special exam.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, so, for this exam, what should I be doing, anyway?” asked Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’ve already started to form a group, right? With the people around you?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, I’ve got a few people. There’s seven girls in my group.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kei gave the names of the six other girls in her group. They were the girls that she was usually hanging around with.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Everyone’s pretty scared of getting expelled, after all. And well… to be completely honest with you, I have no idea how many people hate me,” said Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I guess it wouldn’t be strange if you got some criticism votes,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, uh, shouldn’t you </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">not</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> be saying something like that? Even if it’s just a lie?” snapped Kei. She sounded angry.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s best to keep a low profile for now, so you don’t draw any negative attention to yourself. If you stand out too much in a bad way, it could make you a candidate for expulsion,” I reasoned.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Got it. I’ll make sure I don’t do anything to make people mad,” replied Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s good. Actually, the fact that you’ve broken up with Hirata might actually work in your favor, Kei.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hirata is really popular with the girls. If you were still going out with him, then some of the students in class might have been planning…to force you out of the picture and break you up themselves, by getting you expelled,” I reasoned.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Whoa, </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">that’s</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> a scary thought. But it makes sense…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was precisely because the vote was anonymous that some people could make some pretty bold moves.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…You should be good though, right? I mean, you don’t really stand out. And your grades are pretty average, too,” said Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In the eyes of many of my classmates, there wasn’t really anything praiseworthy about me. Nor was there anything to criticize, either.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sometimes not standing out is a good thing,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But don’t you think that you might get a vote from Sudou-kun? I mean in the sense that he’d be eliminating a rival, as someone vying for Horikita-san. Though that might just be a selfish assumption on Sudou-kun’s part, I guess,” said Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You might be right.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As long as we each had to write down the names of three people, everyone was probably going to get a criticism vote or two. It wasn’t really worth worrying about now.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But those three idiots and Kouenji-kun are probably the ones in the most trouble in our class right now, right?” asked Kei. Apparently, her group had had a similar discussion to ours.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“They are definitely the most likely picks, but even so, we don’t really know what’s going to happen. Still, Kouenji isn’t really in a good place.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“He isn’t the kind of person who would try to form a group to control votes, I suppose,” she reasoned.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ike, Yamauchi, and Sudou were clearly going to form a group to try and support one another. Kouenji, on the other hand, was completely isolated and helpless. He also made enemies quite easily, thanks to his bullish attitude. And he’d had an explosive argument with Sudou right in front of everyone on the day the exam was announced. That had to have hurt his chances.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, what are you going to do? Who are you going to use your criticism votes on?” asked Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Still haven’t really thought about it. But basically, I plan on picking the people that won’t be useful to the class in the future.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s a level-headed approach. That’s very you, Kiyotaka.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Considering someone would be getting expelled, it was the only way I could make a decision.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, but… you’re not thinking about people like me, are you?” said Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You are an important part of the class. There’s no way I would,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I-I see. Well, that’s obvious.” She sounded a little bit embarrassed and surprised.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you do happen to notice anything—students being ostracized, or anyone starting to sound like a solid candidate for expulsion—contact me. It’s hard for me to get that kind of information,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I ended the call with Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I’d said that I’d be picking people that weren’t useful to the class moving forward, but that was, at best, just my personal opinion. As long as I wasn’t actively involved in the class, I had no plans to get deeply invested in manipulating the vote. In the end, a lot of groups were going to be clashing with one another in this test, and I intended to calmly accept whatever the results of that might be. Of course, it’d be a different story if I was the one under fire. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Still, as Kei had said earlier, the likelihood of Ike, Yamauchi, or Sudou being expelled was high. Then there was Kouenji. And if you turned your attention to the girls, you could probably say that the people with poor grades, like Inogashira, Satou, and Airi, weren’t in the clear.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, the fact that groups were still continuing to form meant that votes were probably going to be thrown around for reasons other than grades. Loners like Kouenji and timid people with few friends like Airi would probably be easy targets.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wonder what will happen?” I asked myself aloud.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I decided to keep an eye on how the votes were being allocated and prepare for unforeseen circumstances, while continuing to gather what information I could.</span></p>
