      <p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 8:<br/>All the Tricks</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"> </p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HIS PART OF THE STORY</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> went back to Friday, February 11th. The day when those letters stating Ichinose was
            a criminal were dropped in our mailboxes. After seeing Ichinose be affected by them,
            and having Kamuro get in touch with me to talk about her past as a shoplifter, I decided
            to lay out my pieces in advance so I’d be prepared for Sakayanagi’s strategy. </span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">To execute that plan, I called a certain female student on her phone and asked her
            to come and meet me in my room.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The time of our meeting arrived, and I heard the sound of light knocking on my door,
            rather than the doorbell. Since the door was already unlocked, I simply opened it.
            The faint scent of flowers tickled my nose at the same time that I felt the cold air
            rush in from the hallway.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Good evening, Ayanokouji-kun.” Since it was around midnight, Kushida spoke in a lowered
            tone.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry for calling you at such a late hour. If you don’t mind, please come on in.”
            </span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you sure?”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’d be cold if we stayed out by the front door, wouldn’t it?” I replied.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, that’s true. Thank you.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Going into a boy’s room in the middle of the night. And being alone together, on top
            of that. Anyone would find the idea fishy, but Kushida had come to my room without
            any hesitation.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s a little early, but this is for you, Ayanokouji-kun.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She took out a box of chocolates that had been wrapped with a pink ribbon. She must
            have been keeping it inside her jacket. </span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re okay with giving me this?” I asked.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I have quite a few to give out on the 14th, so if there’s anyone I can give them
            to early, I’ve been doing just that.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Since that was the case, I accepted them graciously. There was no reason for me to
            refuse.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So then, what did you want to talk to me about? It’s rather unusual for you to call
            at this time of night.” If this were a casual conversation, I could’ve approached
            her in the morning or afternoon. It was natural for her to be suspicious.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s something I want to discuss with you.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hm…” Kushida sounded surprised. “I thought you hated me, Ayanokouji-kun. And that
            you wouldn’t want to discuss anything with me.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t really hate you. Actually, if anything, I thought you’d prefer to avoid me.
            Right?”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah ha ha ha! I see, well, I suppose you’re right about that.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The laugh she released wasn’t that of the persona she showed everyone else, but it
            wasn’t a true reflection of the self she hid underneath either. It was something in
            between.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But don’t you have Horikita-san? Isn’t she far more reliable than someone like me?”
            she asked.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I can’t rely on anyone but you for this, Kushida.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I don’t really know if I can be of much help, but I don’t mind hearing you
            out, at least. But what do you mean when you say I’m the only one who can help you?”
            she asked, tilting her head to the side in apparent confusion. </span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I want personal information about some first-year students that would embarrass them
            if it got out. In other words, I want you to tell me their secrets.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…What do you mean?” Kushida still had a smile on her face, but it no longer reached
            her eyes.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You said it yourself before, didn’t you? That you already have enough information
            to bring a whole class to ruin. And you didn’t just mean our own class either. You
            have information on the other classes too.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida always worked hard to keep up appearances as a popular person, a person of
            strong character. People told her all kinds of things, all the time. She might not
            have quite as much information about the other classes as she did about Class C, but
            I was betting she knew something useful.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And why do you want to know something like that, Ayanokouji-kun?” she asked.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you know that Ichinose is suffering because of some rumors doing the rounds right
            now?”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, I know about that. There were those awful letters that went out today too…”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m doing this for the sake of putting a stop to it,” I told her.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hm. I don’t really understand. Is that really </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">your</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> intention, Ayanokouji-kun? Or is it—”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It has nothing to do with Horikita.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hm? Wow, you’re quite compassionate then, Ayanokouji-kun. You did save Sudou-kun
            back then too, I suppose.” Naturally, Kushida knew about the actions I had taken in
            order to prevent Sudou’s expulsion shortly after we started school here. “So you’re
            saying that knowing other people’s personal information is somehow related to stopping
            the rumors?”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t understand. If you start rumors that’ll hurt more people, won’t the situation
            just get worse? Or are you saying that that’s okay, as long as it diverts attention
            from Ichinose-san?”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She might have assumed my strategy was to save one person by sacrificing many others.
            While it was certainly a logical strategy, she was wrong about it being what I had
            in mind. </span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m pretty close to Ichinose-san,” Kushida continued. “So if there’s anything that
            I can do to help, then I want to help. It’s certainly true that I may know more secrets
            than the average person. But that doesn’t mean I can simply reveal them to you. Especially
            since they were only confided in me after I promised I wouldn’t share them.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Another natural response. You’d be hard-pressed to find someone who’d react well to
            having their closely guarded secrets made public. One might think it was safest to
            simply not tell anyone your secrets at all, but human beings weren’t that simple.
            Everyone laid bare their secrets to their families, to close friends, to lovers. Everyone
            wanted to share their feelings with another.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I can’t do anything to betray my friends. Besides, even if I do cooperate with you
            for Ichinose-san’s sake, wouldn’t I get found out as the person who spread the rumors?”
            she asked.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We’d have to take precautions to make sure that doesn’t happen, of course.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We couldn’t use secrets that were so serious that they’d </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">only</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> been confided in Kushida. Conversely, secrets so trivial as to be shared with all
            of someone’s friends didn’t work either. We needed to pick secrets that were the perfect
            balance of known by some people, but not very many. </span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you really think I’ll help you enact a plan that I don’t entirely understand?
            One that asks me to betray my friends like that?” she asked.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Convincing you won’t be easy, I suppose.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If I didn’t know about Kushida’s hidden side, then our negotiations would be dead
            in the water. There was no way that Kushida, who went to such trouble to play the
            part of an angel, would help do something that would throw others into turmoil. </span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But I </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">did</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> know about her true, hidden nature. Which meant we had room to talk.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you give me adequate information, I’m prepared to compensate you appropriately,”
            I said.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Compensate?”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I intend to work on giving you what you wish for, Kushida, to the best of my ability.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So you’re saying that you’ll get me what I want?”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, in a manner of speaking. That’s exactly what I’ll do.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s no guarantee that you’ll keep your word. You’re Horikita-san’s ally, Ayanokouji-kun.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Then consider this conversation your insurance.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do you mean?”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You know what I mean. You don’t need me to actually come out and say it, do you?”
            I briefly lowered my gaze, shooting a glance at Kushida’s pocket.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hm?” </span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She was still playing dumb, so I decided to push a step further. “Even if I don’t
            say it out loud, you should understand what I mean. A cell phone or a voice recorder.
            Or maybe both?”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you knew, huh? That I’ve been recording this.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I thought you’d at least do that much to buy yourself some insurance.” It was a given
            that she’d try to make use of our conversation. “I mean, I expected as much from you,
            Kushida.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But you were absolutely certain of it, weren’t you?” She was still trying to talk
            her way out of it, probably because she thought I was trying to bait her into something.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“A recording would significantly lose credibility if you cut it up to remove the parts
            that are inconvenient to you,” I said. “You’d want to use the data in its unedited
            form if possible. And to be able to do that, you have to be cautious with what you
            say and do.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Since coming to my room today, Kushida had been carefully choosing her words to be
            as polite as possible. She was making sure there were no faults in her behavior during
            our conversation, just in case something did happen.</span></p>

      <div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hm, for you to be so sure of it from just that… Not bad.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida took out her cell phone and showed me the screen to prove that she’d stopped
            recording.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay. I’m finished recording. Ugh, that was so uncomfortable,” she said. The graceful,
            mild-mannered vibe she’d been giving off had completely vanished. “Well, you may have
            expected as much, coming from me, but I knew that you were the one helping Horikita-san
            after all.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I admit that I gave Horikita some ideas.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, whatever, it’s no big deal. I can always ask you about that in the future,”
            said Kushida, getting to the crux of the matter. “So, how do you plan to use other
            people’s personal information to stop the rumors about Ichinose-san?” </span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She leaned in forward, intent on hearing what I had to say.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“By getting the school, which has been quietly monitoring the situation, involved.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Getting the school involved…?”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“As of this moment, Ichinose has taken no action regarding the rumors. So, naturally,
            the school hasn’t done anything either.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is it safe to assume that, though? That the school will take action for Ichinose-san’s
            sake?”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In a manner of speaking, yes. Even if the homeroom teacher heard about what was going
            on, the reason nothing is being done is because Ichinose herself has yet to request
            the school’s help. Which is why we need to escalate the situation to the point where
            the school can no longer ignore it.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">No matter how isolated you were from the rest of the world, the world was no longer
            a place where information could be simply swept under the rug. If this school became
            a place where rumors and defamation ran rampant, which in turn led to students dropping
            out—or, in the worst-case scenario, committing suicide—then its status and prestige
            would be reduced to ruins.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The school wouldn’t let something that could escalate into full-fledged bullying go
            unchecked. Naturally, Sakayanagi was keeping her tactics just barely on the acceptable
            side of the line. In that case, I would go behind her back and push her over the line.
            By doing so, I would force matters to come to a head, and to eventually die down.
            </span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was my goal.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Not everyone is capable of remaining silent, like Ichinose-san has. So they’ll go
            crying to the school. Is that what you’re saying?”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yep. And even if no one does go to the school, the year-end exams are coming up.
            That, coupled with these rumors going around, should create a tense atmosphere. There
            might be arguments, perhaps even almost fights.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And when that happens, the school will be forced to take action… Right?”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We’d disperse information that contained a mixture of truth and lies about a few students
            from each class. Most likely, more than half of the students targeted by the rumors
            would claim that they’re just lies. Perhaps even all of them would refuse to admit
            to anything. However, the fact that some of the rumors contained elements of truth
            would naturally be exposed. </span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If more rumors crop up right now, Class A will be the first to come under suspicion.
            That works to our advantage.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakayanagi’s faction, who’d started spreading rumors to mess with Ichinose, would
            realize these new ones were coming from a third party. But even if they noticed, it’s
            not like they could do anything about it. Try as they might to deny that they had
            anything to do with the new rumors, they couldn’t deny the fact that they’d helped
            spread the rumors about Ichinose. As long as that remained true, there was no avoiding
            the fact that people would suspect them before anyone else.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Now that Kushida realized how things might go, she seemed to have figured out my plan.
            “But how are you going to spread so many rumors? That won’t be easy.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How? We’ll use the school bulletin boards.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait, bulletin boards? You mean the ones in the school app? You know that no one
            uses those, right? And besides, if the school does take action, won’t they also punish
            the person who posted the rumors? Even if you can post anonymously on the bulletin
            boards, won’t the school immediately be able to track down who the posts came from?”
            She peppered me with question after question.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Naturally, I’ve already taken those risks into consideration.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re saying you’re prepared for what might happen in the worst-case scenario, Ayanokouji-kun?
            That you might be found out?”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. And of course, if that does happen, then I won’t say a word about your involvement.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I already had countermeasures in mind, of course, but I couldn’t say anything was
            certain at this stage. Regardless, I had no intention of posting anything on the bulletin
            boards that could point back to me.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This still poses a danger to me,” said Kushida.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I suppose that’s true. It would be completely unnatural for me to know so much about
            other people’s private affairs. If this did get traced back to me, people might think
            I’d gotten information from someone else,” I replied.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was important that I didn’t handle myself </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">too</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> perfectly before Kushida. I needed to make her think there were some things I’d missed.
            </span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, for our own safety, we need to be extremely selective about what the rumors say.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Okay. I understand what your goal is, Ayanokouji-kun. I’ll consider cooperating
            with you.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In other words, she hadn’t yet decided if she actually would.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So you’re saying you’ll cooperate if I accept your terms then. Is that it?”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s exactly it.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It would be difficult to execute this strategy without Kushida. I could easily come
            up with a bunch of lies, but that wouldn’t be enough to really get under people’s
            skin. It would be precisely the fact that the rumors had truth woven in with the lies
            that would cause people to panic. And that panic, like fire, would spread.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay, then, what are your conditions?” I asked. If they were unacceptable, then our
            negotiations would break down.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Horikita Suzune’s expulsion.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Unacceptable.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I suppose so, hm?” That was Kushida’s dearest wish. She knew I wouldn’t grant it,
            but she’d asked anyway, just in case. “I suppose that asking for you to drop out would
            be unacceptable too, Ayanokouji-kun?”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That would be even more unacceptable than having Horikita expelled.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah ha ha.” Kushida must have found my answers somewhat amusing, because she laughed
            out loud. “But there isn’t anything else that I want.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, can I make a suggestion?” I decided to make her an offer myself.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“All right. What?”</span></p>

      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Right here and now. Immediately.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If I carelessly gave him time to delay, there was a risk he might post the message
            using someone else’s phone. I wouldn’t necessarily mind it if he did that, of course,
            but I wanted to avoid any chance, no matter how minute, of my plans going awry later.
            More importantly, I needed to keep in mind the possibility that Kiriyama might leak
            the information to a third party too.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“All right. But you’re going to owe me a big one for this.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thank you very much.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I showed Kiriyama my phone screen, which displayed the rumors I’d prepared for each
            class. I then had him write them all down by hand. After about ten minutes of work,
            the entire process had been completed. I doubted any students would notice the posts
            right away, but I’d make sure they’d be the talk of the town tomorrow. </span></p>
      
      <div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
      
      <p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">8.2</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"> </p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">A</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">ND SO,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> the groundwork had been laid. All that remained were the finishing touches to my
            plans. Crushing Ichinose Honami’s spirit was the last item on my to-do list…since
            I knew Sakayanagi would crush it herself before long, anyway. </span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakayanagi’s scheme had succeeded beautifully. Ichinose continued to be absent from
            class even after it was thought she had recovered from her illness. It was February
            18th the day that Class A and Class D clashed. It had been five days since she had
            gotten sick, and Ichinose still wasn’t coming to class.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was sure she’d physically recovered, but the same might not be true of her emotional
            scars. Knowing she was absent from class yet again, I decided to get in touch with
            her. But if I tried to go see her after class or during a day off, chances were high
            that someone would spot me. Instead, I opted to visit her on a weekday afternoon,
            when nearly all the dorms were empty.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t contact her by phone ahead of time. I didn’t plan on giving her a way out.
            I just showed up at her door and rang the doorbell.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, I want to talk to you a bit. Can you come out?” I asked.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">A little while after, a response came from inside the room.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sorry, Ayanokouji-kun. I’m sorry to turn you away since you came out of your
            way to visit me, but could you please come back some time later?” she replied.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Judging by the sound of her voice, she was still in low spirits. But it seemed safe
            to assume that she’d completely recovered from her cold by now.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Were those letters really such a big deal to you, Ichinose?” I asked. She didn’t
            respond.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I sat down, leaning back against her door.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Will you be coming to class on Monday?” I asked.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…I’m sorry. I don’t know.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It seemed like she was willing to answer questions that didn’t touch on the heart
            of the matter, for now.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I have some time until lunch break is over,” I said. “So I think I’ll hang out here
            for a little bit.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I just proceeded to sit quietly there until the last possible minute before lunch
            break ended.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“All right,” I said. “Guess I’ll be heading back to class.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I…just want a little more time is all. When I’m feeling a little more put together,
            then I’ll definitely be coming back to class. So please don’t come here anymore, okay…?”
            I heard Ichinose say in a strained voice.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I returned to class.</span></p>
      
      <div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
      
      <p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">8.3</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"> </p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HE WEEKEND HAD PASSED,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> and it was now the 21st. It was now Monday, and the year-end exams would start on
            Friday, but Ichinose was still nowhere to be seen. Kanzaki, Shibata, and some other
            girls who Ichinose was close to were trying to call, text, and email her, doing everything
            in their power to get in contact. But I didn’t see them showing up at Ichinose’s room
            after class, which I could only assume was because Ichinose had asked them not to
            come anymore, just like she’d asked me.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">During lunch break, I slipped out of the school building and went over to Ichinose’s
            room. I knocked lightly on her door and called out to her without waiting for an answer.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I heard you’re taking today off too?” I asked.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was a reckless move on my part. She had told me not to come anymore, and I was
            ignoring her wishes. </span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This time, Ichinose didn’t say anything back to me from the other side of the door.
            I didn’t say anything to her either. I just sat in front of her door until the last
            possible minute, just like I had done the previous week.</span></p>
      
      <div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
      
      <p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">8.4</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"> </p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HE SAME THING HAPPENED</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> on Tuesday too. After confirming that she was absent from class once more, I went
            to Ichinose’s room. I don’t need to reiterate it all again.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If I were one of her classmates, she wouldn’t have been able to bring herself to be
            really mad at me. But I wasn’t. And yet, it was precisely because I belonged to another
            class that it wouldn’t inconvenience me that much if Ichinose decided to cut all ties
            with me. That was the main reason why I was being so assertive.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There wasn’t much time left until the year-end exam. If things continued this way,
            it was possible that Ichinose might be absent even on the day of the exam. Well, even
            if she did show up to class that day, the students of Class B were currently undergoing
            a great deal of mental anguish. It was possible that their test scores would be negatively
            impacted by these unforeseen troubles. Even if no one wound up expelled, it would
            still have a big impact on their class points. </span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ichinose needed to come to class on Thursday, and the students of Class B needed to
            feel reassured. The window in which that could be made to happen would end the next
            day, Wednesday.</span></p>
      
      <div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
      
      <p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">8.5</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"> </p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HE END OF THAT WINDOW</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> approached in a flash. Before I knew it, it was Wednesday. </span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I held a can of coffee that I bought from the convenience store in my hand. I let
            out a sigh, my breath visible in the air. </span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wasn’t going to press Ichinose that day, even though we were out of time. Because
            there was no way Ichinose, herself, didn’t understand that today was her last chance.
            She would definitely take action. I was sure of it. </span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“February’s already almost over. If we can get through the special exam next month,
            we’ll have officially made it to our second year. They say, ‘once on shore, we pray
            no more,’ but I have to wonder if there’s really any truth to that,” I said aloud.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The Uninhabited Island Exam. The Cruise Ship Exam. Paper Shuffle. Time and time again,
            we’d been subjected to a series of bizarre exams.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Once we’re second-year students, I wonder if the special exams will get even stranger
            than they are now?” I added.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Hey. Can I… ask you something kind of strange…?” asked Ichinose, mumbling in a quiet
            voice, as if she were talking to herself. It was the first time in a while she’d responded
            to me.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sure. As long as you’re okay with talking to me through the door, you can ask me
            anything,” I replied, welcoming her with open arms. But she didn’t respond immediately.
            </span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why haven’t you said anything to me, Ayanokouji-kun? Asked me anything?”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Like what?”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“My classmates and my friends from other classes have all come by, trying to convince
            me to come back to school. They all say they want me to talk to them about anything
            that might be bothering me. And yet, Ayanokouji-kun, you’ve been coming here every
            single day without saying anything like that at all… Why?”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She probably didn’t want me to worry about her, just as she hoped the other students
            wouldn’t. She couldn’t understand why I was slipping away from school every day and
            wasting my lunch break just to come here.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, that’s because those other students are really, really worried about you, Ichinose.
            Their attempts to convince you, again and again…that’s something I could never do.
            My ability to connect with other people is so limited that if I tried to use my emotions
            to appeal to your emotions, I can’t imagine it would really resonate.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I heard the faint sound of footsteps within her room. I had a feeling she was sitting
            on the other side of the door now. The door was the only thing separating us.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Maybe I’ve been coming here every day because I’m waiting for you to just unload
            everything and tell me,” I said.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Waiting for me… to unload everything and tell you?”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I decided to break down the walls of Ichinose’s heart for the first time. </span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I know what crime you committed.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…!”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I say that, but I don’t actually know the background details or anything. But
            the fact that Sakayanagi dug so deeply into this, and spread it around so much that
            it’s caused you to take time off from school, tells me how much this is weighing on
            you, Ichinose. But I guess there’s no point in me saying that to you.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How…do you know that?”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s not really important right now. I don’t intend to get any deeper into it.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If Ichinose didn’t want to talk about it, then our conversation would be over right
            here and now. </span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re probably not very good at opening up to other people about your own troubles,
            Ichinose. Even though you can save others, you can’t save yourself. That’s the kind
            of person you are. That’s why I’m here now.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The feelings I wanted to convey had to be reaching Ichinose, little by little. </span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There was a brief silence. It was hard, wanting to let your emotions out, but having
            no one you could let them out </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">to. </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I’d seen countless children suffer through the same thing back in the White Room.
            Eventually, they broke down and disappeared. Became broken people with no hope of
            ever recovering.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m your door right now. I can’t see your face and I can’t reach out and touch you.
            I’m just a door. No one will laugh at you if you reveal your weakness to a door.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There was a light </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">clack</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> as I set my coffee down on the ground.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So what are you going to do, Ichinose? This moment, right now, is your moment of
            truth.” </span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">All of Ichinose Honami’s friends were reserved, gentle people. It wasn’t hard to imagine
            them offering a deluge of kind words to their dependable leader. However, that wasn’t
            going to work. It might seem like the correct approach to the people supporting Ichinose,
            but you couldn’t handle this situation like it was a problem to be fixed. You just
            had to apply enough pressure to make her crack.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even someone so pathetic like me… Can I really…?”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Who has the right to deny you that?” I asked.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Can a criminal like me…ever really be forgiven…?”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Everyone has the right to be forgiven.”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I’d knocked on the door to her heart. All that was left now was to see how Ichinose
            would respond. </span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">From the other side of the door, Ichinose slowly opened her mouth to speak.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I… I shoplifted. And then I missed out on half a year of school during my third-year
            of junior high, because I was in so much pain. I couldn’t talk to anyone about it.
            I just kept blaming myself. I holed up in a small room, just like I’m doing now…”</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ichinose started speaking, finally pulling her hands away from the wound on her heart
            that she’d been so desperately trying to cover for so long. She talked about what
            she had done. About her weakness, about how she’d retreated inward and hid herself
            away. </span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">About how she told this story to Nagumo, and only him. About how Sakayanagi had approached
            her, asking for advice on a classmate, and told her that there was a shoplifter at
            our school. Ichinose had known then that it couldn’t be a coincidence. She’d known
            Sakayanagi had probably found out about her past because she heard it from Nagumo.
            With no room to lie about it, all she could do was confess it all.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ichinose had been putting on a brave face, unable to let herself be weak. Do you know
            how hard and scary it is to admit to your own crimes? Many immature young people have
            shoplifted… No, have committed </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">some</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> kind of “crime” at least once in their lives. But if you said that to a large crowd
            of people, they’d all probably say something like “I’d never do such a thing.” </span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">And that was only natural. Admitting to your own crimes was terrifying, as was talking
            about them in public. Our society is one that deals out harsh punishments in the name
            of justice, after all. Such is the tragic fate of a criminal. That’s why they hide.
            That’s why they never speak of their crimes, burying them deep within themselves instead.
            That’s why they try to live on under the guise of being good people.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ichinose, overcome by her guilty conscience, spent half a year completely alone. And
            then, finally, she was released from her shackles…no, she broke free of them. But
            her guilt would continue to follow her, no matter where she went. It would be with
            her until the day she died. </span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In fact, her guilty conscience was standing in her way right now, attacking her heart.
            Which was why she had no choice but to confront it. </span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">By the time she was done telling me her whole story, lunch break was already over,
            but that didn’t matter. Even though afternoon classes had already started, I just
            sat there and listened to what Ichinose had to say, without trying to console her
            or reprimand her.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ichinose was softly crying on the other side of the door, trying to hold back her
            tears. I offered her no words of comfort, because that would have been meaningless
            right now. It had been clear from the very beginning who her real opponent in this
            battle was: herself.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It all came down to whether or not she could put an end to this herself by coming
            to terms with what she’d done. Very, very few people are capable of honestly facing
            their crimes in the true sense of the word. But when we do face our crimes…then we’re
            able to grow and take one step forward.</span></p>
      
      <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">And that was the conversation Ichinose and I had, before she opened up and told her
            friends about everything she was going through. Everything.</span></p>
