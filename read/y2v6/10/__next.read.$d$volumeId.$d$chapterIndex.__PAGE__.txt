<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 4:<br/>Still, We Gotta Do It!</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HE LAST TIME</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> I saw Kushida-san was last week, during the special exam. It was now the end of class on the following Friday, and she hadn’t shown up once during this entire week. She wasn’t the only one: Wang-san and Hasebe-san hadn’t come to school either. They were absent from Monday to Friday, and it had already been five days. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In the meantime, though, the world continued to move around us; day-to-day life wouldn’t stand still and wait for us to catch up. Structured meetings and research for the Sports Festival. Student council work. Everyday studies. I felt like if I kept facing these oncoming waves head-on, my knees might give out and I’d fall over backward. But I couldn’t allow myself to fall right now. I had no right to wallow in self-pity when I declared that I would absolutely bring those students back to class. But despite repeated attempts, I hadn’t had any success.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had thought about contacting Ayanokouji-kun several times but stopped myself from doing so. If I went to him for help, the chances were that he’d accept, and there was a possibility that he’d give me the answers that I was looking for. But, in this case at least, this was something that I had to solve on my own.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And thus concludes homeroom for today,” announced Chabashira-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As soon as Chabashira-sensei left the classroom after the final homeroom session for the day, I followed after&#160;her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sensei, I’m sorry, but might I have a moment of your time?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t mind… Sure, that’s all right,” she said. “How about we walk while we talk?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Many students would leave their seats to go to the restroom during this time, so I knew we’d stand out if we stayed in the hallway. Perhaps Chabashira-sensei understood my intentions, since she suggested we walk as we chatted.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Kushida-san, Wang-san, and Hasebe-san have been absent for five days now,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Indeed. Wang and Hasebe are, ostensibly, sick. However, even though they called in to the school to say they’re sick, they apparently haven’t gone to the clinic for a check-up like they were supposed to. As for Kushida, she has only said that she’d be resting. I haven’t heard any other details.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There was no way she was simply recuperating. Her extreme absence felt like some kind of punishment aimed at me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is it possible that there could be severe penalties if this situation continues?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I assumed the teacher wouldn’t be able to provide me with a concrete answer, but I thought I should try asking anyway.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t worry so much about it,” Chabashira-sensei told me. “The rules are designed to provide a long grace period, especially for honors students like Wang and Kushida. As for Hasebe, she’s not exactly a troublemaker, so it won’t be a big deal for now. If they had no accomplishments under their belts, or they were students who typically had bad behavior, then it would be a different story.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, are you saying that…thanks to how they usually act, they’re okay?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s exactly what I’m saying. Besides, there are healthy, cheerful students who can play hooky quite cleverly, and there are students who have hurt feelings who could carelessly just skip an entire week. It’s difficult to tell the difference. The only way we can judge them is to look at their past behavior and performance in school.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I could feel the burden on my heart begin to lighten just from hearing that.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And besides, it’s not like school officials are monsters,” added Chabashira-sensei, in a soft tone. “They wouldn’t want to break a child’s heart by forcing them to go to school. At any rate, those three students have never been late before, and they’ve always been on their best behavior in class. They are more than qualified for a grace period.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She seemed so different now, almost like she was a different person entirely. I wondered if there was something behind it. Rumors had been going around among my classmates that she had changed because of the special exam, and perhaps it was really true. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“More importantly though, the school officials understand very well that we conduct strict special exams,” she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Because of that, they felt that it wasn’t strange for students to say that they just needed to rest or take time off. That was why they were allowing this situation to go on right now, then… </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After confirming that there was no one else around though, Chabashira-sensei stopped and said more. “However, the time limit is almost up. If their absences continue into next week, then those 100 points that you fought so desperately to earn will be snatched away without mercy.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Her statement had a hidden message. She was telling me to do something this weekend. But would I really be able to do something about this? I thought of asking her about the current situation, but little by little, my own weakness began to show its face.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thank you very much,” I said. “You’ve been a big help.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait, Horikita,” said Chabashira-sensei. “Is there anything else you wanted to say?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…No. I couldn’t possibly bother you any further, sensei,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I won’t know whether it’s really a bother unless you ask me. We still have a little time. Just talking to someone about it can make it a little easier, don’t you think?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira-sensei must be able to see right through me.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> I’d be lying if I said I didn’t hesitate at all, but I decided to gather my courage and say something.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I earned Class Points by having Sakura-san expelled. Was that the right course of action?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you regret your decision?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I thought it was right at the time. But…to be honest, I’m feeling shaken right now.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wish I could show you the answer,” Chabashira-sensei sighed, “but I can’t help you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I understand,” I replied. “As a teacher, you’re not able to answer that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s not it. All I can say at this point in time is that I can’t show you any evidence that you made the right choice. It’s certainly true that your decision was somewhat dictatorial and self-serving, and I’m sure a few students might have seen it as such. Your reputation among the other students has taken a hit, and you’re beginning to feel that you chose incorrectly.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That hurt to hear. I couldn’t say anything in return.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“However, that’s just because it was that important of a decision, right?” Chabashira-sensei continued. “Besides, no one is perfect right from the get-go. Even if we’re talking about problems of simple addition and multiplication, people make mistakes. But they learn and improve. Even I’ve gone through life making lots and lots of mistakes.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You too…?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, and even when I took that special exam. Forget about whether I made the right or wrong decision—I wasn’t even able to cast my vote in time. On that point, you showed me one answer, and I think that you did well. No one can get full marks on something without knowledge or experience. At the time of the special exam, you were recognized as the leader, and you were empowered. You were prepared to get rid of someone, and you protected Kushida. It’s not up to you to make your classmates recognize that it was the right thing to have done.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Her words were typical of a teacher. I was a little puzzled, though, since she never really said such things before.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You didn’t have to go for the 100 points at this current stage,” she added. “You had two choices. You could have either rationally cut the lowest-ranked person in OAA, or you could’ve accepted the inconvenience that came with favoring your promise.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, that’s true…” I knew that. I knew that, but even so, I still felt doubt.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But still… I think that I might have been blind to what was going on around me,” I admitted. “I can’t help but think that if I just listened more, then I might’ve found an answer that was better, that was even more correct.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We sometimes lose sight of our surroundings,” said Chabashira-sensei. “And later, when things cool down, we agonize over whether we’ve made the correct decision.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I hadn’t had any experience with that kind of thing before. Frustrated, I unconsciously clenched my fist tightly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Up until now, to put it nicely, you’ve just done things the tried-and-true way,” Chabashira-sensei told me. “Or, if I were to put it a little less nicely, you’ve simply been taking the easy way out, right? But that’s normal. It’s just that the peculiarities of this school made you seek new alternatives for the very first time.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes…” I conceded.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was powerful advice, but even so, I still couldn’t come up with an appropriate answer. I was sure I must’ve looked pitiful, but there was no dismay on Chabashira-sensei’s face. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Instead, she looked at me gently. “You fought within the rules that the school has set, right?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, but I broke my promise not to expel anyone but the traitor,” I answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Did you intend to protect Kushida from the very beginning? Was the promise you made a lie in order to get the class to vote in favor?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No!” I replied. “I really was prepared to do it, at the time… I was.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, there’s no problem,” said Chabashira-sensei. “Yes, it’s important to honor your promises, but even adults sometimes make mistakes when they promise something. You changed your mind, sure, because I know that you acted after realizing keeping Kushida was the correct decision. You’re free to disrespect or ignore anyone who mocks you now. Some will follow you, others won’t. Bringing a class of nearly forty students together as one is something that not even the likes of Ryuuen, Sakayanagi, or Ichinose can do. Other students might be yes-men on the surface, but you never know what they’re thinking deep down.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She gently placed her hand on my shoulder. “Don’t be afraid of failure. I don’t want to be the kind of adult who can’t accept or forgive a child’s failures.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But sensei, I haven’t failed yet.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…You’re right, you haven’t. I’m just saying that you need to see the choices you made all the way through until the end.”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira-sensei had a somewhat sad look on her face, but then she looked me in the eye once more. Her words had been stern, yet polite and loving. They almost made me choke on what I was about to say a little.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’ve changed, Chabashira-sensei,” I remarked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I hadn’t intended to actually say that, but the words just came out. I suppose that was because it was just how I truly felt.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is it strange that I’m playing the part of a teacher now, when I’ve been so cold and dismissive up until this point?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m a little surprised, but no, it’s not strange,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is that so? That’s good.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Perhaps she felt she had said too much, because she cleared her throat, and changed the subject.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Anyway, has Ayanokouji done anything about Kushida?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ayanokouji-kun…? He hasn’t really done anything,” I&#160;replied. “If I had to guess, I get the feeling that he’s waiting to see what I’ll do.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see,” said Chabashira-sensei. “So he thinks that you’re the one who should fix this, then…?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Maybe he just can’t deal with what was simply selfishness on my part,” I answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not so sure about that. It was Ayanokouji who took a bold action on the matter of Kushida. I can’t imagine that he’d leave you alone to handle it if he didn’t trust you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You seem to have quite a high opinion of him,” I said. “I recall you once saying that he was the most defective student there was.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You have a good memory, recalling things I said so long ago.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“He’s even better than his OAA ranking indicates,” I&#160;told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It sounds like he’s earned a lot of trust and appreciation from you then,” replied Chabashira-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You had said that he had some character issues, but they’re not limited to him… What exactly did you mean by that, anyway? Or were you mistaken, by any chance?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He was undeniably brilliant, and he was much calmer and more collected than I was. I couldn’t even begin to imagine ridiculing him with a label like “defective.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You know, you don’t need to take every single one of your teachers’ comments absolutely seriously,” huffed Chabashira-sensei. “After all, you’ve spent far, far more time with him than I or others have, right?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even so, I just wanted to ask.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…All right. My evaluation hasn’t changed since then. Well, no, actually, I feel like my evaluation has become even </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">more</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> credible since then,” Chabashira-sensei said. So, she </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">did </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">think he was defective. But no matter what she said, that didn’t change the truth. “It’s far too early to be dwelling on that right now though. There are other problems that you need to solve as soon as possible,” Chabashira-sensei reminded me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, I suppose you’re right,” I agreed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was true that I was concerned about it, but it was also true that I could put it off until later. What I needed to do now was to get Kushida-san, Wang-san, and Hasebe-san to come back to school.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Kushida is a tough nut to crack, huh?” said Chabashira-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“To be honest, right now, it feels like a waste of effort,” I said. “No matter how many times I go to her, and no matter how long I wait, she doesn’t open the door.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s rough.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Weekends aside, Kushida-san could just go to the convenience store and buy supplies as many times as she needed while I was in class. It would be pointless for me to try starvation tactics. I tried to reach her on her phone, but she was keeping it turned off.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I get the feeling she’s enjoying this though, whenever she senses me pacing about back and forth all confused on the other side of her door.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I suppose I can’t say with certainty that she isn’t,” Chabashira-sensei commented. “But even so, if you don’t do something, the situation won’t change, and things will just gradually get worse.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, I understand…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you can’t do something on your own, you can always ask someone else for help,” said Chabashira-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But which of my classmates could possibly be willing to help me persuade Kushida-san…?” I wondered aloud. “Hirata-kun’s the only one who comes to mind, and right now, he doesn’t seem to have the time for it either.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He was providing support for the situation with Wang-san, as well as working on Shinohara-san and her circle.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s true that knowing Hirata, he’d be a… Well, actually, I’m not so sure about that, when it comes to Kushida,” mused Chabashira-sensei. “He tackles things head-on, is mindful of others, and is a good person. I can’t imagine it would be easy to get her to open her door by bringing someone like him over once she shut herself off.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I think I understand what it is you’re trying to say, sensei. It’s because Kushida-san isn’t being honest with how she feels, right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Unfortunately, I can’t think of anyone who would be right for the job at the moment, but it might not be a bad idea for you to look beyond your own classmates,” suggested Chabashira-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But persuading Kushida-san would mean getting her to address her true feelings,” I said. “It would be a considerable disadvantage for us to let an outsider know.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I suppose you need to weigh the advantages and disadvantages. However, it’s not as if you’re forbidden from telling anyone about it. For example, a few teachers already knew about Kushida’s past. And some of the other teachers would probably choose to keep it a secret. Personally, I think that secrets aren’t particularly useful.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">If only there was someone who could move Kushida-san’s heart… No, even if they couldn’t move her heart, if they could even just help with some kind of breakthrough, then…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s about time I head off</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">” said Chabashira-sensei. “But let me say just one last thing, even if it might just sound like I’m being a meddlesome busybody. The most important thing is what </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">you</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> want to do with Kushida. Think about that long and hard.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">What I wanted to do with Kushida-san… Hmm.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thank you very much, sensei,” I told her. “I feel like my resolve is a little stronger, thanks to you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I still hadn’t found any answers yet, but I could feel the energy to make another desperate attempt surging within me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t worry about it. As a teacher, it’s the least I… I&#160;mean, it’s what I naturally should be doing.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">With that, Chabashira-sensei headed back to the faculty office. I continued to watch her from the stairs as she walked away, until she was no longer in view.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">W</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HEN I RETURNED</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> to the dormitory building after finishing my shopping at Keyaki Mall, I found Ibuki-san standing near the elevator door, scowling at me. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">M</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">Y DAILY ROUTINE</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> had started to change significantly since last week. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">First, she was going to pour her efforts into rebuilding the class. The matter of her reputation would come after that, on its own.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you satisfied, now that you’ve gotten more Class Points from that special exam?” asked Haruka.</span></p>

And besides, the ideal situation would have been for us to reach Class A without losing anyone. That’s what I wanted, but… Well, there’s no point in talking about that anymore, not at this point.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">No one wanted others to be expelled. And yet we fought on, and we had nominated Airi for very compelling reasons. There wasn’t anything more to it. We had already reached that conclusion.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“My best friend was sacrificed as a result of your selfish decision, Horikita-san. Are you aware of that?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">For the first time today, Haruka came out and said what she really wanted to say.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, I am,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita had still been battling with her own feelings more than a week after the special exam had ended. You didn’t have to ask her about that; you could tell that just from looking at her face every day. But still, that had nothing to do with Haruka. She wasn’t going to forgive Horikita just because she was doing her best. And she wouldn’t simply forgive Horikita if she produced favorable results either.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wow, you’re </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">such</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> a great leader. You’ll do </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">anything</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> to make sure our class wins,” said Haruka.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"></div>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span><img alt="" height="100%" id="INTERIORIMAGES_5" src="/hola-lector-agua/images/books/y2v6/OEBPS/Images/INTERIORIMAGES_5.jpg" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style=" top: 0px; vertical-align: top; height: 100%;"/></span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"></div>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m still a ways off from being a great leader,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You know I was being sarcastic, right?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course I do.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Didn’t you promise that you’d only go after the traitor? The one student that kept voting in favor the&#160;whole time?” asked Haruka.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“On that point, I think that my perspective was overly naïve,” Horikita admitted. “But even so, I can’t just act like that special exam never happened. All I can do is make the most of what I’ve learned for next time.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Some mistakes cannot be forgiven,” Haruka told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I won’t deny that. You’re right.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you really think that keeping Kyou-cha… I mean, that keeping Kushida-san in class was the correct decision?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It was because I decided that it was the correct decision that I moved to keep her in class. I was fully prepared for the students to antagonize me over it,” Horikita replied. “I feel like I’m going to be having this conversation over and over.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, I guess you will,” snapped Haruka, her tone intensifying slightly after she saw that Horikita wasn’t being apologetic or modest.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I have no intention of making some half-hearted apology,” Horikita went on. “No matter how many carefully constructed speeches I give, the fact remains that I changed my opinion about having her expelled, and I decided that we should keep her. You have every right to hold a grudge, and I know that one day, I might suffer painful payback for this. But I decided that Kushida-san can still be an asset to our class. And I am gradually becoming more and more convinced of that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But even if Kushida-san is so brilliant, there are other kids in the class who are worthless,” insisted Haruka. “It&#160;didn’t have to be Airi.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Haruka was arguing that someone else should have been cut, but Horikita had not come to that conclusion herself. Haruka then continued speaking.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t accept you. No matter how many people can accept you in the future, I never, ever will, Horikita-san.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Haruka was still keeping her emotions bottled up as much as she could but chose to show there was no sign of forgiving Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Then I’ll just have to do my best so that you can accept me,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I literally just told you I never will,” said Haruka.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I am responsible for Sakura-san’s expulsion. I won’t deny that. I </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">cannot</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> deny that. But even so, what am I supposed to do? Do you want me to say that I’ll drop out of school right now?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That wouldn’t bring back Airi, of course. The 100 points that we gained thanks to Airi sacrificing herself for the class’s sake would be meaningless if Horikita did something like that.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Or do you want me to get down on my knees and beg? Would that make you feel better?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It might have looked like Horikita was being aggressive and heavy-handed, but that wasn’t true. Horikita was suffering. But even though she was, she was trying her hardest to put on a tough front and face Haruka. As I sat beside her, I could see the true meaning in Horikita’s quivering gaze.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Give me Airi back,” said Haruka.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I can’t fulfill an impossible demand,” said Horikita after a pause.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s all I want. I don’t care about the class. I don’t care about anything.” Haruka grabbed a few stands of her hair and tugged as hard as she could, yanking them out. “You made the wrong decision back then.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you’re so upset about it, then maybe you should have fought,” said Horikita. Immediately after saying something that sounded close to a provocation, she kept talking without giving Haruka a chance to respond. “But that would’ve been pointless, right? Even if you had fought, there was no way that you could’ve pushed back on what was happening.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re right,” agreed Haruka. “I don’t think I could’ve done anything either. Kiyopon, you took advantage of Airi’s feelings and mercilessly pushed her into a corner. A&#160;normal person absolutely never would’ve been able to do something like that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Haruka addressed me for the first time since this conversation started and was looking at me with contempt. However, it seemed she had no intention of actually discussing it with me because she turned her attention back to Horikita right away.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you really think that Kushida-san is going to work for the class from now on?” she asked her. “She’s probably just going to betray you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If and when Kushida-san drags the class down, then yes, I’d regret my decision.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There was truly no guarantee that Kushida would necessarily be useful to the class. And if Horikita made a mistake in the way she guided the class forward, there could come a day where she regretted the choice of having Airi expelled. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But even so, if I were to go back in time to that moment with what I know now, I’m sure that I would essentially do the same thing,” Horikita went on, reiterating definitively that she would not change her conclusion. “I would still decide to save Kushida-san and expel Sakura-san. The only thing I’d do differently is that I wouldn’t have made that careless promise. That’s it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why? Why Airi…?” muttered Haruka.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was sure that Horikita would have answered the question even if I stayed silent, but I decided to speak up and offer my own thoughts.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s an issue of perspective,” I said. “That exam provided a strong stimulus for the students who were at the bottom of the rankings in OAA. If those students continue to hover near the bottom, then it’s possible they could be expelled next. I think that the fact that they’re now more acutely aware of the danger is a positive thing.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was why I had taken on the role of naming Airi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That sounds just like how Ryuuen’s class works,” snapped Haruka. “So what, if someone’s not good enough, they get cut?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s right,” I replied. “I don’t know what policy that Ryuuen has in place right now, but it’s a fact that it’s something like a reign of terror. So far, our class’s policies have been vague and far too lax.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That kind of reminds me of when we first started at this school,” Haruka said. “No one could come together on anything, and everyone was just selfishly doing their own thing.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If someone asked if the situation in our class now and the situation back then were similar, then sure, you could say that. But even though the situations </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">looked </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">the same, they weren’t actually.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But it’s different now,” I told her. “It’s inevitable that you’d want to prevent damage that doesn’t need to be done. In this case, we just minimized the damage that </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">had</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> to happen.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But—!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was the first time that Haruka raised her voice in this conversation.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Horikita came to the conclusion she did because she sensed that Kushida would bring much, much more to the class than Airi would if Horikita made Kushida into an ally,” I continued. “And it was because I could see that possible future as well that I decided to respect Horikita’s opinion and offer her my help.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Generally speaking, there were no certain futures. You could only imagine and then grasp a future that you could see. People aren’t all-powerful.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even though Airi’s gone, when I look around, it’s like the class has gone back to its usual routine,” said Haruka.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I understand your frustration, but I wonder… Did you feel the same way when Yamauchi-kun was expelled?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“He brought that on himself. This was different,” said Haruka.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s the same thing,” Horikita said. “You’re just angry that it’s someone you know who was lost this time.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And what’s wrong with that?” asked Haruka.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There was no clear goal to this discussion. Strictly speaking, there was no other solution to be found here other than getting Haruka to back down.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I won’t accept a reality like that,” Haruka insisted. “I&#160;can’t.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If Haruka didn’t back down, then that would mean there was a big problem ahead of us.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s true that Kushida-san might have been a threat before,” Haruka continued. “Sure, she’s reformed now, at least on the surface. And she might be contributing to the class from here on out. But do you seriously think I’d accept that and cooperate with you?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I suppose you have a point. While you were out for the week, I had a feeling that your issue was going to be more long-term than anyone else’s,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">While Kushida needed to be dealt with immediately, Horikita knew that Haruka was prepared to fight a long, drawn-out battle. Since Haruka had lost her friend Airi in the exam, she had nothing to fear now.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But even so, you came back to school,” Horikita added. “If you just wanted to talk to us, you could have done that while remaining absent. Right?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita was feeling hopeful. If, somehow, Haruka was holding onto that same hope herself and that was why she returned to school, it would have been a development to be thankful for. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, reality was not so sweet.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I only came because I haven’t found the answer yet,” said Haruka.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do you mean?” Horikita asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I came to school to look for an answer that I couldn’t find while I was shut away in my room.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When Akito heard those words, he cast his gaze downward.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m looking for an answer as to how I can get revenge on you, Horikita-san. And you too, Kiyopon,” said Haruka.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Those were the coldest words that she had said to us during this conversation. The very nature of the words that left her somewhat dry lips were different from a simple threat or bluff. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…You’re serious, aren’t you?” said Horikita. She had also realized the weight of what Haruka said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I just wanted to tell you that today,” Haruka said. “I&#160;will absolutely make you regret expelling Airi.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Haruka then stood up and left, leaving her drink completely untouched. Akito followed Haruka, looking more like he was chasing after her. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita wasn’t the only one who watched them go in exasperation—Keisei did too.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Personally, I don’t think you or Haruka are wrong,” he told us. “I know that’s probably an overly diplomatic thing to say, but it’s how I really feel. After all, in the end, the idea that ‘as long as you save yourself, that’s all that matters,’ is at the root of all of this.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Though Keisei seemed like he was ashamed of himself, he told us what he thought without trying to hide it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Everyone feels that way,” said Horikita. “It’s not strange to want to save yourself.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Which is exactly why I can’t understand how Haruka feels right now,” Keisei replied. “But I guess that’s why I don’t think I have any right to tell her to stop either. Even if it means causing trouble for the class.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Keisei weakly pressed his fist against the table and then got up from his seat.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Our friend group has already been broken in half, more or less. But even so, I’m going to be useful to the class in my own way. I can’t contribute to the Sports Festival, so I’m going to study even harder to compensate so I can contribute to the class that way. But if I don’t…the possibility I could get expelled won’t be zero.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though Keisei excelled in academics, he lagged behind in athletics and in societal contributions. It was also clear that he had a particularly strong disadvantage when it came to the number of friends he kept.</span></p>
