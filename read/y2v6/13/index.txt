<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 7:<br/>The Guest</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">T WAS AROUND</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> eleven o’clock in the morning, and I could just faintly hear the cheers from the other side of my closed windows. It sounded like the Sports Festival was in full swing. I didn’t want to assume everything was going smoothly, of course, but even so, the class was certainly putting in the effort to win it. Horikita’s class could hold their own well against the other classes and other grade levels too. It was that conclusion that had allowed me to choose, without hesitation, not to attend the Sports Festival myself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had taken care of all of the necessary arrangements on my end, so I just had to leave the rest to Chairman Sakayanagi. Although the wfact that he was the chairman didn’t necessarily mean that I could fully trust him, it was easy to decide whether I could or couldn’t trust him at all—because of course, it would be virtually impossible for me to remain at this school if he were to betray me. All that was left now was to wonder what kinds of battles the second-year students would face at the Sports Festival and what the results would be…and how greatly Sakayanagi’s presence or absence would affect the outcome.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I looked at the door. I had come up with a strategy to contain Sakayanagi, but…it was a little too late to see what the effects would be. I was concerned about a number of things, but I supposed that I’d just have to wait and see. And that included matters with the Sports Festival too.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">I think it’s about time I started getting lunch ready,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> I thought to myself. Just as that thought crossed my mind though, my doorbell finally rang. Now then, was this visitor going to be a welcome presence or not? I wouldn’t know for sure until I tried dealing with the situation. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As I kept my distance from the door and watched to see what would happen, a voice came from the other side of the door, as if whoever was standing there had anticipated my cautiousness. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hello, Ayanokouji-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I lowered my guard somewhat and reached out. I&#160;placed my hand on the door. I tried to imagine various scenarios, but from the moment she entered the dormitory it was as though I had lost. The person standing on the other side of my door was none other than Sakayanagi, dressed in her casual clothes.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She looked up at me with a broad smile on her face. “I’m terribly sorry, but would you mind letting me in for a moment?” she said. “Though I am only forbidden from leaving the dormitory building proper, I’m afraid that visiting a male student’s rooms during the Sports Festival could also be somewhat problematic.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Coming inside my room might be even </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">more</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> problematic,” I pointed out.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Still, I decided not to turn Sakayanagi away and welcomed her inside.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Please pardon the intrusion,” she said. Sakayanagi took off her shoes and came into my room, her physical limitations making her movements slow.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Come to think of it, this is the first time you’ve come to my room, Sakayanagi.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s because I don’t often visit anyone’s room,” she replied. “Have you already had lunch?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I was just thinking of making something,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. That’s good then. I brought this with me to say thank you for the invitation,” said Sakayanagi as she handed me a small plastic bag. “I bought these at the convenience store early this morning. It’s apparently a new product, and since I had the opportunity, I thought I’d like to eat it with you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When I took a peek inside the plastic bag, I saw two small Mont Blanc cakes inside. I figured it’d be a good idea to brew some coffee if we were going to have sweets like those.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’d be better to sit on the bed rather than the floor,” I&#160;said. “Sit down anywhere you like.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thank you very much for your thoughtfulness,” replied Sakayanagi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After directing Sakayanagi to sit on the bed, I went over to the kitchen, turned on the faucet, and started filling the teapot with water.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It doesn’t seem like you came here to visit me on a simple whim,” I remarked. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I said it with a straight face, but Sakayanagi chuckled softly like she found something amusing about it. “People don’t normally know who’s in the dormitory. People would not expect that I, the leader of Class A, would be visiting your room all alone, Ayanokouji-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Anyone, no matter who you asked, would be surprised if they saw Sakayanagi come to my room. They’d suspect that something was up, and that was exactly why Sakayanagi never made contact with me in the dormitory. Until today, that is.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You really are a bad person, aren’t you, Ayanokouji-kun? This was your strategy, wasn’t it?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Strategy? What do you mean?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Fu fu</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">. No need to put on an act. You were almost certain that I’d be coming here today, Ayanokouji-kun,” she said. “Actually, no, let me amend that statement. You were absolutely certain of it. Weren’t you?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It seemed as though Sakayanagi had completely seen&#160;through my trap without needing to think very much.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We, Class A, have fewer students,” she said. “We have an immediate disadvantage in this Sports Festival right from the starting line. Moreover, although we do have capable students like Kitou-kun and Hashimoto-kun, on average, Class A falls short of Horikita-san’s class. As such, if we were to win, we would need to determine what person should be participating in what competition and manage our schedules down to the second while simultaneously keeping an eye on which of our rivals are participating in what events.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I turned the teapot on, and it started to quietly bring the water to a boil. I then took a can of coffee out from the cupboard along with cups and a coffee filter.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You did this because you didn’t know how the situation might turn out if I participated,” she added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You have a high opinion of yourself, I see, same as always,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The best way to ensure that the other classes beat Class A in the Sports Festival was to make sure I didn’t take part.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The Sports Festival went along according to a very precise schedule. Knowing Sakayanagi, she could direct her classmates and organize her players well and get them into the appropriate placements. Moreover, she could have coordinated event participation using students from other grade levels too.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Last night, my father told me that he wished for you to be absent from the Sports Festival,” she went on. “He said it was to possibly prevent someone from the White Room attending as a guest from coming into contact with you. He said that he was going to assign someone to watch the dormitory too.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s true that Chairman Sakayanagi asked me not to participate, but I never thought that he’d tell his daughter about it,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Surely you must be joking,” she said. “You’re the one who instructed my father to tell me, Ayanokouji-kun, aren’t you?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She had seen right through me, as though what I had done had been simply a matter of course. Even though she was his daughter, Chairman Sakayanagi would never mix his personal and professional lives. That was why I had asked him to tell his daughter what was going on himself, rather than having me explain to her. I had requested that he explain the situation to his daughter beforehand, as she was someone who could have likely missed the Sports Festival anyway due to health concerns anyway, telling her that it was possible she could get wrapped up in whatever trouble came as a result of me and the White Room. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As the leader of Class A, Sakayanagi had been surprisingly willing to participate in the Sports Festival, but I couldn’t imagine that the chairman knew about that. And even if he had known, he would also decide it would be safer to just have her take the day off from the Sports Festival regardless. Since she was his own daughter, he would have known that if something happened, there was a risk that she might stick her neck out to help me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, there were some things that not even the chairman could predict. He didn’t realize that his daughter’s instincts and her curiosity were not easily suppressed. If I was going to be absent, it was natural that she would think of it as a good opportunity for the two of us to have a nice, long, undisturbed conversation. She had even showed up outside the door to my room, which was considered to be the most dangerous place of all, without any fear.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Did you choose to come here before noon just to make me nervous?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wanted to tease you a little bit, Ayanokouji-kun,” she admitted. “And I wanted to make you wonder if I had perhaps ignored your strategy and decided to participate in the Sports Festival anyway.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Incidentally, you and I are the only two students who are absent from the event, Ayanokouji-kun. Everyone else is attending.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">From the sound of it, Sakayanagi’s information network had kept her abreast of who was participating from each class. She must have gotten reports via phone before the Sports Festival began. There had apparently been no oversights on that point either.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Anyway, even though I did end up being mean to you by arriving just now, I honestly did plan on visiting you a little earlier in the day,” Sakayanagi said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The water just started coming to a boil in the pot, shaking about inside.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I went down to the lobby a few moments ago, to check on the situation outside,” she added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was, at least ostensibly, on sick leave, so I was strictly forbidden from leaving my room. Sakayanagi, on the other hand, wasn’t able to leave the dormitory building, but she wasn’t out on actual sick leave. Even though she’d get a warning in the unlikely event that she stepped outside the building, it wasn’t like she’d be violating the reason for her absence if she did.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, how were things down on the first floor?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There were three people there who I assumed to be security guards. It appears that they aren’t only stationed to stand watch here in this dormitory, but rather they’re meant to patrol the entire school so that their presence doesn’t appear unnatural.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was likely because while they were here to protect me, they were mostly security professionals meant to protect government officials.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The MVP in today’s Sports Festival and the one who made victory a certainty is neither Horikita-san, who proposed a partnership with Ryuuen-kun, nor Ryuuen-kun, who accepted her offer,” she then said. “Ayanokouji-kun, you’re the one who had the final word to guarantee my absence from the event. That alone was what it took to decide the winner. Bravo. I expected as much from you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But we still don’t necessarily know how it’ll turn out in the end,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s true that the tables could be turned unexpectedly, but it’s unlikely. I would think that by now, Class&#160;A is completely at the mercy of Horikita-san’s class, who is fighting this battle head-on, and Ryuuen-kun’s class, who is employing every trick imaginable. Even if a body has excellent arms and legs, it can’t do anything without a head. That’s the sort of class I’ve been building, after&#160;all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Similar things could be said for Ryuuen’s class, but generally, the issue in Sakayanagi’s class was that it was too strong at the top. The fact that the leader solved all problems meant that, on the flip side, nothing could be solved if the leader wasn’t around.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, it’s all right,” she said. “In exchange for losing 150 points in this event, I will be enjoying my time together with you, Ayanokouji-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She didn’t seem to care even the slightest bit about any damage being done to Class A.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re not afraid of a drop in Class Points,” I remarked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This school’s systems are a game to me,” she replied. “As long as I maintain Class A’s position to some extent, there’s no problem.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">At any rate, since she had gone through the trouble of bringing Mont Blanc cakes to enjoy, I took them out of their packaging and brought them to the table on two plates. Then, I poured hot water from the pot into the filter with coffee grounds in it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It seems like you’re used to doing this,” observed Sakayanagi as she watched my movements.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This isn’t really that big a deal for me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I suppose that these kinds of things, like preparing coffee and so on, are new and fun for you. Isn’t that right, Ayanokouji-kun?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakayanagi knew as well as I that these were the sort of things we never did in the White Room.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The same goes for everything at this school, really,” I&#160;said. “I just wanted to do normal things is all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">At any rate, I was curious about what Sakayanagi had said moments earlier.</span></p>

If that were the case though, whoever it was, they would have likely barged into my room without ringing my bell and waiting around like this. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">My doorbell rang once more.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It would probably raise suspicion if I ignored it any longer since this person was operating on the assumption that I was resting in my room. It was also possible, though unlikely, that this person was a school official.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Who’s there?” I asked, without moving from my position on the bed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Stay where you are and listen,” responded the visitor.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Whoever it was, he must have been able to tell from the sound of my voice that I was sitting far away from the door. It was the voice of a young man. Not an adult, but someone my own age.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You sound familiar,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, the visitor’s identity wasn’t coming to my mind. Judging from the voice, he sounded like he was a student. And while I couldn’t place exactly who it was, he was definitely familiar. Of course, if you were living on campus, you would inevitably hear a large number of different voices all the time. Even so, I immediately recognized that I had definitely heard this voice somewhere.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You called me once,” I added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When I made that remark, the person standing on the other side of the door remained silent for a short while.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Impressive,” he said. “You remembered my voice after only hearing it once.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The fact that this was coming after my father paid a visit to this school also made an impression on me. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You didn’t say anything that sounded like you wanted something from me back then,” I pointed out.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It was good that I didn’t,” said the visitor. “Something unfortunate happened afterward. I haven’t made contact with you since then, but… Anyway, I’m sure you’re probably curious who I am, but it doesn’t matter. I am neither friend nor foe to you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, why’d you come?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sure you’re thinking that once you’ve eliminated Tsukishiro and any students from the White Room, peace will return. I came here to advise you that such thinking is a mistake.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“Fu fu,”</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> Sakayanagi chuckled. “This sounds like quite an interesting conversation. Would you mind if I joined&#160;in?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sakayanagi Arisu, huh…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The young man on the other side of the door showed no sign whatsoever that he was perturbed by Sakayanagi’s unexpected comment. In fact, he was able to identify her immediately just after hearing her. Had he figured that out by narrowing down the list of everyone absent today? Or did he recognize her voice?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“At any rate, if you want to stay at this school until graduation, do be on alert,” said the visitor.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You say you’re neutral, but you’re giving me support,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Your presence is causing a negative impact. I just want to prevent anything further.” His voice then trailed off. Apparently, this person must not have intended to stay for very long as it sounded as if he had walked away already.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That voice…” Sakayanagi mused. “Where have I…?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you have any idea who that was?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Like you, I’m afraid I don’t have a clear answer to that question, Ayanokouji-kun. However, I just had a feeling that I’ve heard that voice from the other side of the door somewhere.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So, in other words, I could take what Sakayanagi said to mean that she remembered hearing that voice someplace else, but someplace different from where I heard it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It wasn’t recent,” she added. “Five, maybe ten years… It’s a fairly old memory.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If that’s the case, then the chances of that person being a White Room student are slim to none,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. If I met whoever that person was when I was small, that’s true,” she agreed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was understandable, then, how he reacted when he learned that Sakayanagi was here in my room. Not only was he not surprised, but he reacted as though she were someone he was acquainted with. But whether I was dealing with Amasawa or that man, this occurrence wasn’t something I cared about. As long as there wasn’t any harm being done to me at the moment, then I wasn’t inclined to bother.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"></div>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">7.2</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HE SPORTS FESTIVAL</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> which I wasn’t present for, ended in almost an ideal manner. The class too was in total excitement over the final results, the likes of which would’ve been unthinkable at any other time in the past year and a half. The gap between Horikita’s class and Class A had narrowed, and Horikita’s class now had undeniably significant assets thanks to a growth in Class Points through the Uninhabited Island Exam, the Unanimous Special Exam, and the Sports Festival. Several days had passed since then, and it was now just after the middle of October.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The Sports Festival rankings were as follows: Horikita’s class came in first place, Ryuuen’s class came in second place, Ichinose’s class ended in third place, and Sakayanagi’s class was in fourth place. Of course, it wasn’t as though any one person was responsible for these rankings; it was the will and the strength of the class as a whole. Furthermore, Sudou and Onodera each took first place respectively for individual scores. Kouenji also placed first in all ten competitions he took part in, but since all of his were individual competitions, he took second overall.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kouenji seemed satisfied with that though, and no problems had arisen. Sudou and Onodera were each presented with the option of transferring classes, but they chose the Private Points rewards instead without hesitation. While there were still some points of concern with Horikita’s class, they were most certainly heading toward Class A. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kei apparently had plans to meet up with a friend today, so I decided to stop by Keyaki Mall and head back to my dorm room after that. As I walked back alone though, Horikita came up to me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’d like to speak to you for a moment, if you don’t mind,” she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“As long as we can walk and talk, then sure,” I replied. “I’m heading back to the dorms.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s fine.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Since she had gone out of her way to come talk to me when I was on my way back to the dormitory, it must not’ve been something she wanted too many people to overhear. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I learned a great deal from the Unanimous Special Exam,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And what would that be?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The Sports Festival was over, but her problems hadn’t all been solved. While there were some instabilities remaining, the class was starting to move forward again. In the midst of all of that, Horikita was troubled but was apparently learning from it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wasn’t wrong,” she said. “In making the choice to keep Kushida-san, I mean. I can see once again that I made the correct decision.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita demanded results from her, and Kushida responded by contributing to the class, even earning points in the Sports Festival. She was once again being a diligent honors student in her daily school life, and although her Societal Contribution score in OAA had fallen slightly from the top in the beginning of October, it was probably only a matter of time before she regained her position. If I were to make an unforgiving comparison, Kushida was contributing far, far more to the class than Airi could. Of course, it wasn’t though the decision was one that only came with benefits.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I know, you know,” Horikita continued. “And I know that there are still some matters of concern, especially the issue of Hasebe-san. I honestly don’t know what to do about her. But, if there is another special exam like that one, I think that I’ll be able to handle it better when the time comes.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And your basis for saying that is?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I made an ill-advised promise to the class during that test in order to reach a unanimous decision,” she said. “I&#160;said that the traitor would be expelled, and then I went back on my word. Making that promise was an easy shortcut to getting a unanimous decision, but I didn’t understand the magnitude of that risk. I already knew deep down that Kushida-san was the traitor. Also, I made the decision to make that promise even though I hadn’t made up my mind about letting Kushida-san be expelled. That was a mistake.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If there was a possibility that you were going to keep Kushida from the start, then it’s definitely true that making a careless promise like that would only end up coming back to bite you,” I agreed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita’s decision back then was a last resort and made as time was about to run out. But even so, it was true that if the class had come to a unanimous decision at that time, with Horikita leaving the possibility that Airi or other dead weight students similar to her in terms of ability could be the ones expelled, the aftereffects wouldn’t be as significant as they were now. What had been thrown away, and what had been gained? </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We gained Class Points,” Horikita said. “But we also lost more than a few things. That special exam taught me a lot. It showed me both success and failure.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I imagine that you’d rather not fail, though,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita closed her eyes, suddenly took a deep breath, and then opened her eyes once more. “I’m still only in my second year of high school. I’m a child. Failing is a good thing.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’ve reconsidered things,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s not like me to agonize over things, to be stuck on the fence. I…I’m going to be like myself. I may not be able to do things as well as the other leaders. But Hirata-kun is here, and Karuizawa-san is here, and so are Sudou-kun and Onodera-san, and Kushida-san and Kouenji-kun too. With their support, I’ll move forward. I’ve decided to think of it like Class A is there, waiting ahead.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And I count you among those with me, of course. I&#160;don’t know what you’re thinking, and you’re totally uncooperative in many ways, but…you are indispensable to the class, and to me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">My presence was somewhat like training wheels on a bicycle. Even if it was indispensable at first, you’d take them off, fall down a bit, and then be a bit wobbly as you tried to ride forward. Then, eventually, you’d be able to ride without difficulty. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">You’re not supported by only one person as you’re pedaling that bicycle, Horikita. You’re supported by your classmates. And after I make sure you mature a little more—</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">I’ll leave your class.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wasn’t going to say anything right now, but eventually, Horikita would learn the reason why. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">And…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She’d definitely understand.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The time would come when she’d have a class that she was absolutely convinced will win, and she’d be faced with the reality that she couldn’t.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I would teach her that, for her own sake, and no one&#160;else’s.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As long as I was winning, that’s all that would matter.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If I decided to become her enemy and defeat Horikita, then it was a done deal.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, I planned on leaving</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">precisely</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> because I want to be defeated. I </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">wanted</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> there to be uncertainties in the future. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There was a contradiction there: I had an answer, but I wanted to be wrong.</span></p>
