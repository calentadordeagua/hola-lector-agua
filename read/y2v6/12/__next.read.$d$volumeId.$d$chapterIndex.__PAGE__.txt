<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HE NEXT MORNING</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> I watched from the faculty side as all of the students gathered on the field. Student Council President Nagumo was standing near a podium that had been set up, giving his opening address. The guests that had been invited from outside campus were watching over the students as well. There weren’t that many guests though, just a few dozen or so. Even so, the students seemed uncomfortable at the sight of unfamiliar outsiders. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The students were still in a state of restless excitement; they were about to thrust themselves onto the stage, so to speak, about to dive headfirst into the Sports Festival. The student council had told me in advance about the guests that were going to be invited, but the people present were much more imposing than I had expected. They&#160;were from political circles and similar who were involved in creating this school.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There weren’t any politicians present that I had seen on TV before, but I was sure that these people weren’t very far off from being on television themselves. They were dressed in suits and wore stern expressions on their faces as they watched. It was almost as though they were monitoring prisoners. But even in a situation like this, President Nagumo remained unperturbed, making a dignified speech. He was fulfilling his role in a manner comparable to my older brother, giving the students the kind of wonderful performance my brother had in the&#160;past. After President Nagumo’s speech was over and the students applauded, the baton was passed to the teachers, and we were once again reminded of things to note during the Sports Festival.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Now, the time had come for the main event to begin. From this point onward, we were free to do as we pleased. As long as they abided by the rules, students could participate in any competition they were currently registered for. Alternatively, they could abstain from an event and choose to enter another competition instead if they decided as such after seeing what kind of opponents they’d be up against and that they’d be at a disadvantage—although they would need two points in order to do so. And we couldn’t forget that students who had completed all of their competitions and were not planning on participating in any more had to go cheer in the designated area. If you were seen aimlessly chatting with people, resting, or slacking off in an unrelated area, you would be disqualified from the event and your points would be forfeited. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">My class had formed a cooperative partnership with Ryuuen-kun’s class. We had made adjustments to spread students out as much as possible in individual competitions in order to avoid clashing with his students. As for team competitions, we had hand-picked students from both of our classes who could win easily. We made sure there were equal numbers of students from each class, and we also made sure that the same number of points would be distributed to our two classes regardless of whether those teams won or lost. However, no matter how exceptional a student was, the maximum number of people you could enter in a team competition was fixed. There was a measure to prevent either side from using outstanding talents like Sudou-kun and Yamada Albert-kun for extended periods of time. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Additionally, we had a contract in place that allowed for each person to enter into a maximum of three events so they could lend a hand in group competitions if needed. This arrangement was limited to events that could be registered for in advance, however, and we had made sure to include that in the contract. It would’ve been nonsensical for us to have disputes on the day of the Sports Festival and have students yelling and screaming at each other to help them out with this or that. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Furthermore, we didn’t have any firm restrictions against working together with students from Ichinose-san’s or Sakayanagi-san’s classes. If there were any competitions where we could make use of their students, then for the sake of convenience, we were willing to allow some cooperation. I wasn’t worried about there being any problems because Katsuragi-kun and I had bounced ideas off of each other back and forth quite a few times.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There was less to worry about at the start, since at this point, most students would just be participating in competitions that they had registered for in advance. Having said that, I needed to remember to check in with my classmates every hour and make adjustments as necessary if any problems arose. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The first event I was going to participate in myself was the 100-meter dash. The start time was fifteen minutes after the event began, so there wasn’t any need to rush. It was still a good idea to arrive early though and check out the competition that was already—</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“All right, Horikita! You and me are gonna have a showdown!!” shouted Ibuki. She came running at me at full speed immediately after the crowd disbanded and the students were free to go about the grounds. She glared at me while stopping to catch her breath.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you an idiot?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wha-?! What the hell was that for? What, you scared you’re gonna lose to me? Is that it?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No.” I rejected her in a split second. “What competition are you about to go to? Take a deep breath and then answer me that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? The 100-meter dash, obviously. That was the deal we made, and I wouldn’t forget that,” said Ibuki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, exactly. The 100-meter dash. And our agreement was that we registered for the first race. That means that we’re going to start running right away. And if that’s the case, why were you recklessly running here at top speed a moment ago? You know that we’re going to have our competition soon, so you should be waiting patiently in the designated place. There shouldn’t be a need to explain any of this to you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She must have realized it now that I had explained.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Damn it,” she mumbled under her breath. “A-anyway, you still better race me!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Relax. I was planning on it, without you even having to tell me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ibuki-san wasn’t an easy opponent to go up against. I won the 100-meter dash last year, but only by a slim margin. I would’ve normally wanted to avoid challenging her, but I was also greatly indebted to her. If Ibuki-san hadn’t helped me recently, then it was possible Kushida-san still might not have come back to school. Even so, I couldn’t lose to her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I knew she wouldn’t want me to just let her win either, so I was going to give her a real race and we’d compete fair and square. Ibuki-san didn’t seem to like walking next to me, so she put some distance between us as we headed over toward registration for the first event. I could feel a pleasant kind of tension building, and this would be a battle just between second-year girls. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Not much had changed from when the advanced reservations were made, and my only potential rival was Ibuki-san, but it would’ve been foolish of me to interpret that as a stroke of good luck. If I had an easy battle ahead of me, that just meant that there would be stronger rivals to face in other competitions.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HE 100-METER DASH</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> was held immediately after the opening ceremonies, and in that first race, I had my showdown with Ibuki-san. It ended in a narrow victory for me. Oddly enough, I won by about the same slim margin that I did last year. After we crossed the finish line, Ibuki-san kicked the dirt in apparent frustration and made excuses for losing, saying it was because she stupidly ran over to me as fast as she could before the race even began.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">My next battle with her would be in my fourth event of the day, the running long jump. The two contests before that were our own individual pursuits. My second event was a steeplechase, in which I placed first. The third was a team-based tug-of-war contest, and my team won third place in that. So far, I personally had accumulated a total of twenty-one points: I had gained five points at the start of the Sports Festival, ten points for placing first place twice in individual competitions, three points for placing third in the team tug-of-war competition, and another three points for general participation. I could confidently say that I was off to a great start.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Then, at around ten o’clock, it was time for my second showdown with Ibuki-san: the running long jump. I&#160;finished my turn with an impressive leap, and my jump&#160;was recorded as 5.79 meters. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Not bad.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> Setting aside the fact that this was a situation where there was no room for failure, I thought I might have just set a personal best. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ibuki-san was three spots behind me and was breathing heavily as she looked at my record. In all, there were three jumpers left. I felt that now that I was, at least tentatively, in first place for the event, I was one step closer to scoring points.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As I watched the next contestant, I heard someone call for me from behind.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Suzune! Found ya!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When I turned around, I saw Sudou-kun running up to me with Onodera-san behind him. I had high expectations that the pair of them would be top scorers in this Sports Festival.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“From the looks of it, you’re doin’ pretty good!” said Sudou-kun.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’ve won three competitions in a row since the day began, Sudou-kun,” I replied. “And on top of that, you’re looking totally relaxed. I’m impressed.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, y’know, I’m tryin’. Anyhow, Onodera competed in two events and got first place in both of</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">’em too. Right, Onodera?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I just happened to get lucky in a few ways,” she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Onodera had no equal when it came to swimming, but she had made a good show of her talents in track-and-field events as well.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You know, when we first started school, I didn’t have the impression that you were that fast. When did&#160;that happen?” I asked. I was particularly curious about that&#160;because I had seen her in gym class. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, to be honest, I don’t like running all that much,” she said. “I’ve never really been interested in anything besides swimming. I guess it’s just like, before, I just kinda </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">did</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> it without really putting in an effort.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“She said she’d never do long distances,” said Sudou-kun.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, it’s super exhausting. I can’t run that far, and it’s not really good for you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Apparently, the two of them had been practicing together day after day ever since they decided to team up. It seemed they made an even more natural pair than I had imagined they would.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Still, to tell ya the truth, I’d love to go up against Kouenji if I got the chance,” said Sudou-kun. “He’s won three events in a row and got first place in all of ’em too. It looks like he’s gonna keep the wins comin’.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Absolutely not,” I told him. “It’s not a good idea for our classmates to try and destroy each other. You understand that, right?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sudou-kun and Kouenji-kun both had the potential to get first place. I understood that Sudou-kun wanted to compete against him, but we had to prioritize the overall&#160;class.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I-I know, I’m just jokin’,” he said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t worry,” said Onodera-san. “I’ll keep an eye on him and make sure he doesn’t get out of line, so you can relax.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thank you,” I said. “The more I can leave to you, Onodera-san, the less I’ll have to worry about.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s like you don’t got any trust in me at all. Man…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sudou looked disgruntled, but when I turned to look him in the eye, he averted his gaze awkwardly. That was a sign he was reflecting on how he had behaved in the past.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I’m sure that you two are planning on competing in more competitions still, Sudou-kun, Onodera-san. Good luck to you both.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. I’m gonna keep up this winnin’ streak,” said Sudou-kun.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was encouraging to hear. Just then, I noticed that the final competitor in my event had taken the starting line. I stopped the conversation there and turned my attention toward Ibuki-san.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Let’s not bother her anymore,” Sudou-kun said to Onodera-san. “How about we go check out the next event?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, let’s. See you later, Horikita-san,” said Onodera-san.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I casually watched them go from the corner of my eye, but my gaze was mostly focused on Ibuki-san since she had started her run. I understood very well that her abilities were close to my own. In other words, it was conceivable that she could surpass my own record. I&#160;was&#160;feeling two emotions at that moment: I wanted her to fail, but I also wanted her to give it her best and make this a good fight. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was sure that she must’ve been feeling intensely pressured, but her movements were nimble and graceful. She leaped forward and landed on her feet, but she ended up pitching forward and falling. Even though she had a face full of dirt, she quickly looked up and turned her eyes to the scorekeeper. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">5.81 meters. It was just two centimeters different, but even so, those two centimeters made her jump better than mine. I had lost.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah!!!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ibuki-san pumped her fist in the air, looking as gleeful as a child. If she had lost this competition, she would’ve lost our little best two-out-of-three showdown already. Instead, she had made a brilliant leap and won.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You see that?!” she yelled. “I won! You lost!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I could tell that she was happy, almost to the point of being obnoxious. It was actually making me feel a little irritated.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I have to wonder if you had an advantage over me because you have slightly less air resistance than I do…” I mused. If our abilities were about the same, then that would be the only difference…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh?” she blinked. “Air resistance?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Never mind.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t make weird excuses. Just admit you lost.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t get carried away,” I told her. “This just means we each have one win and one loss. We’re back to being even. That’s all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though I warned Ibuki-san not to get overly excited, she still had a huge grin on her face that wasn’t going away any time soon. I suppose I should have regretted the fact that I missed out on getting first, but seeing her look so happy, I suppose I just couldn’t help but…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I won! I won! I won!” she exclaimed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">…Yeah, no. I </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">did</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> regret not getting first. If anything, the amount of mental and emotional stress I was feeling suddenly increased. I now had one win and one loss. I&#160;would have loved to get to our third match right away, but there were several team competitions worth a lot of points coming up after this. I had to wait until the balance beam event later that afternoon to settle the score with her.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HE SPORTS FESTIVAL</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> had started without Ayanokouji-kun. An electronic scoreboard was set up on the field so that we could see how each class was scoring and confirm their respective results at any time. Although Ryuuen-san’s class started off at the top of the rankings, we, now Class B, overtook them in short order and took first place for ourselves. We had been holding onto it ever since. Our ideal final ranking would be Class D in second, Class C in third, and Class&#160;A in fourth.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was hoping that things would continue on like this all the way until the end without any trouble. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Great work out there, Horikita-senpai,” said Yagami-kun.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But you’re in first place in your grade, aren’t you, senpai? I can’t believe that you started in Class D last year.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Was that a compliment?” I asked. “Or was there some sarcasm mixed in?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Heavens, no. I genuinely respect you. Not as much as Student Council President Nagumo, but still.” Out of the corner of his eye, Yagami-kun saw the instant that Student Council President Nagumo cleared the finish line. “I heard some third-year students talking earlier. Apparently, that’s his fifth first-place finish in a row.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Some girls cheered for him, and several of the guests turned their attention to President Nagumo as well. However, Nagumo walked away with a blank expression on his face without so much as a word to any of the cheering girls. He put some distance between himself and others, making it clear that he wanted to be alone.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Knowing President Nagumo, I expected him to say something, but he doesn’t look even the least bit happy,” I remarked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, win or lose, he’s already guaranteed to graduate from Class A, I suppose,” said Yagami-kun. “Perhaps he’s just not feeling too enthusiastic?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was certainly true that the Student Council President had a solid position. From his perspective, the Sports Festival rankings really must’ve meant nothing. I&#160;wondered if he was aiming for first place in this event simply because he couldn’t afford to be lax in front of the current students and the guests.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I think I’ll have a little chat with the President,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see,” said Yagami-kun. “Well, my next event is coming up, so if you’ll please excuse me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">With that exchange behind me, I decided to approach the Student Council President. Another third-year girl was standing next to him and seemed to be talking to him. It was Kiryuuin-senpai from Class 3-B. I heard rumors about her from time to time from my interactions with other third-year students. I also knew that she had incredible scores in OAA. Not wanting to interrupt their conversation, I decided to just give them a slight bow and wait.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Congrats on your fifth win in a row, Nagumo,” said Kiryuuin-senpai.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What’d you come here for?” he asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No need to be so unkind, is there? I was just concerned for you. You don’t seem to be very happy, even though you won. And it looks like more than a couple people are cheering you on too…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t make me laugh,” he scoffed. “How could I call winning a contest like this an accomplishment?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, it would have been easy for you to just gather up weak opponents to face so you could steal first place by force. But looking at the people you were racing against just now, it didn’t seem to me like that’s what you did.” She was pointing out that he didn’t appear to be cutting any corners in the event.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I heard through the grapevine that Ayanokouji is absent today,” she added. “Is that perhaps the reason for the long face?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Ayanokouji</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">. His name was popping up again, even in a place like this. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The Student Council President sighed quietly, without so much as looking at Kiryuuin-senpai. “I thought he’d satisfy me, but I guess I was wrong.”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What are you planning, Kushida-san?” I wondered aloud. “It doesn’t look to me like you’ve had a change of heart and you want to work together with me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I honestly told her what I thought, and Kushida-san didn’t deny it. However, what I really found curious was that Kushida-san’s intense gaze was focused not on me, but on Amasawa-san.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait, you and her…?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You too?” said Ibuki-san.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not going to tell you the reason why, but I’d be more than happy to help you out if it means I can give her some payback,” said Kushida-san.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As they say, the enemy of my enemy is my friend. Unexpected allies were falling into my lap.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But she is most definitely a formidable opponent,” I&#160;added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You got that right,” agreed Ibuki-san.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ibuki-san immediately started warming up with some stretches, psyching herself up. Amasawa-san looked back at us from afar and smiled, apparently finding something about this funny. Amasawa-san’s abilities were incredible—Ibuki-san and I had experienced them firsthand—but the other people on her team were an unknown quantity. Going just by the OAA values that I could remember, I recalled that Nanase-san had a relatively high score in physical ability, but I didn’t have any impressions of the other students. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was sure that I remembered all the names of students with scores around A, so I reasoned that they could have Bs going by the highest estimate, or perhaps somewhere below that, but… At any rate, the problem was that I was still three people short. Analyzing my opponents when I didn’t meet the requirements to even sign up would be counting my chickens before they hatched.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What are your conditions for the remaining three players? Just that they’re not from Ryuuen-kun’s class?” said Kushida-san, asking me about my requirements for the team.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, exactly,” I answered. “I’d like to have as many people from our class on the team as possible. But our priority is winning and making sure we have good players.”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">And with that, Kushida-san walked off.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“She said, ‘Got it,’ but what is she gonna do?” said Ibuki-san, puzzled. “There’s no way people are gonna help us that easily.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I traced Ibuki-san’s curious gaze as she watched what Kushida-san was doing. Kushida-san had gone to talk with Rokkaku-san, a student in Sakayanagi-san’s class. After chatting for a while, the two of them went on over to see Fukuyama-san, also from Class A. After that, they went over to talk to a student from another class who was cheering for her classmates in the gymnasium.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s Himeno-san from Ichinose-san’s class, isn’t it?” I muttered to myself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida-san, the two students from Class A, and the one student from Class C talked for less than a minute. Then, Kushida-san came back over with those three students in tow.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“These girls said that they’d play with us,” she said. “Volleyball isn’t really Himeno-san’s thing, but she agreed to play as long as the five of us back her up. I told her that if she left the hard part to us, it’d be okay.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida-san had apparently spoken to Himeno-san using her usual “Kushida-san Mode” that she didn’t ever use with me. I especially couldn’t hide my surprise over the fact that she had convinced two students from Class&#160;A to lend us a helping hand with no questions asked. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We’re in panic mode right now ourselves since we’re probably about to lose the Sports Festival,” said one of the girls from class A. “But even so, we want to at least contribute something to our class, even if the worst is happening, right?” She turned to her classmate, who nodded in response.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">These two girls wanted to achieve something precisely because they were in Class A, which was currently dead last in the Sports Festival. Kushida-san recognized that mindset, and at the same time, instantly picked out which students were talented players. Even if she didn’t exactly remember what their scores were in OAA, being Fukuyama-san’s and Rokkaku-san’s friend, Kushida-san had a solid understanding of how physically capable they were.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“She pulled off a feat you could never achieve in your entire life, Ibuki-san,” I teased.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Shut up,” she snapped. “You weren’t able to find anyone either.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, there are still about five or six people in the gym that we could probably talk to about joining the team, but…this is probably the best possible team we could make right now,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">At any rate, while it was doubtful whether we’d actually be able to register still, I had managed to get a full team together for volleyball. Sure, one person on the team was from Ryuuen-kun’s class, but that was okay. Winning the volleyball competition and earning ten points from it was overwhelmingly more valuable than competing in the shuttle run, which would only net me two or three points. And even if we lost this competition, the gap between my class and Ryuuen-kun’s class wouldn’t change either, which was another benefit.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ibuki-san and I were positioned at the front as the top players on the team, and we had Kushida-san, Rokkaku-san, and Fukuyama-san as capable players backing us up. Himeno-san rounded out the team, and although she might drag us down a little bit, we had more than enough skill to make up for it.</span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">6.5</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">W</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">E TOOK ON OUR FIRST MATCH</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> without any difficulty. Right now, we were watching Amasawa-san’s team play. Nanase-san was the one who really took control of the match. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This Nanase girl pretty much flew right under my radar, but don’t you think that </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">she</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> isn’t really that big a deal as we thought she’d be?” Ibuki-san was, of course, referring to Amasawa-san.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re right, I don’t get the feeling Amasawa-san is so good that we need to be wary of her,” I agreed. “I assumed she was joking about her lack of volleyball experience, but now I’m not so sure…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was possible she had been deliberately holding back so far, but I didn’t get that impression from what I saw. The students that they were playing against weren’t all that good, and Amasawa-san was still better than them both on offense and defense. I didn’t see Amasawa-san as that much of a threat. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, after the midpoint of their match, the situation gradually started to change.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ibuki-san had been watching the game somewhat listlessly up until that point, but she started to pay attention more closely as well. In less than ten minutes since the match had begun, Amasawa-san was visibly showing signs of improvement. She had incredible adaptability and intuition, the likes of which could not be explained away as mere innate physical ability. But just as Amasawa-san was beginning to show us a glimpse of her talents, Nanase-san spiked the ball, ending the game.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“They’ll be playing us after our next game,” I remarked. “She might be even better by then.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Just a couple matches worth of experience is nothing,” said Ibuki-san. “We can totally beat her.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was dangerous to be overly optimistic, but it was true that their team had really won the game without Amasawa-san even touching the ball that much thanks to Nanase-san taking the lead. When our turn came, we won our game too, seizing a decisive victory at around 3:40. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In this Sports Festival, there were many differences in the rules compared to a normal competition, and this volleyball tournament was no exception to that. There was no rotation in who served, and you could have any player of your choosing serve the ball. The winning team was either the first to score ten points overall or the team who scored the most points by the ten minute mark. If&#160;the score was tied when time ran out, the game would go into overtime. If that happened, the side that scored last and tied things up had the serve, and the first to score a point won.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Will settling things with me on the volleyball court really make you happy, Ibuki-senpai?” replied Amasawa-san.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Aha ha ha! You know, I don’t hate your way of thinking,” chuckled Amasawa-san.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">They weren’t wishing each other good luck in their upcoming match, saying that they hoped it’d be a good game or anything like that. Instead, the sparks were flying between them as they waited for the signal for the match to begin. Amasawa-san’s presence was unsettling for sure, but Nanase-san was the one that we really needed to be wary of.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m gonna be the attacker, just like in our last game,” Ibuki-san declared confidently. She sounded more fired up than before. “I’m gonna slam the ball into their side of the court with everything I’ve got.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Though she had some difficulty controlling the ball, I certainly couldn’t complain about Ibuki-san’ spikes. Their destructive power was second-to-none. When the match began, Ibuki-san served the ball, and we quickly scored a point. I thought we’d carry that momentum forward, but Nanase-san quickly spiked the ball back, and her team scored a point on us. After that, I was expecting it to be an even closer game at the start, but we held a slight advantage and established a small lead in the first part of the game with a score of 4 to 2. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As I anticipated from watching her play, Nanase-san was an even match for Ibuki-san and me, but apart from that, my team had a slight advantage. The situation changed in the middle of the game though, when there were five minutes remaining. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ibuki-san took three steps in her run-up, leaping up into the air, and spiked the ball. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Up until this point, Ibuki-san’s spikes had sailed past the net and earned us sure points. But this time, Amasawa-san appeared and blocked the shot. Actually, no, that wasn’t quite right. Amasawa-san slammed the ball back down on our side of the court, with the same amount of momentum. The ball hit our side and the first-years’ team earned one point.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Too bad, huh, Ibuki-senpai?” gloated Amasawa-san. “Hey, Nanase-san. What do you call that kind of play again?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I believe that’s called a ‘roof,’” Nanase-san replied. “I’m not too familiar with the terminology though.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, senpai, since I can see what your attack pattern is like, I’m afraid you’re not going to be scoring anymore,” Amasawa-san taunted. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Like hell!” Ibuki-san yelled. “Next time I’m gonna score against you for sure!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Calm down,” I told her. “She only happened to block you this one time.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Shut it. Pass the ball to me again.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Now, the score was 5 to 3 and it was our serve. This whole thing would’ve been much easier on us if we could finish the game now, but… The rules stated that if a player went out of bounds, one point was immediately awarded to the opposing team, so players couldn’t go about the court recklessly. If you served the ball from a standard position, it was only natural that the other team would return it. At any rate, we had defended well until now. I&#160;gave the ball over to Ibuki-san.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This time for sure!” she shouted. “You’re goin’ down!!!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ibuki-san changed up her rhythm, leaping high into the air after two steps for her run up. She then made the best spike of the day. The two first-years who jumped into the air to block weren’t able to get a hand on the ball, and it headed straight for the ground on their side of the court. However, Amasawa-san stopped it from landing. It was almost as though she knew exactly where the ball would land; she received it beautifully and completely stopped it, pushing the ball back up into the air on their side of the court, in enemy territory.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Nanase-san jumped high into the air with her golden hair flowing behind her. She spiked the ball, sending it straight towards Himeno-san. Unable to move, Himeno-san froze, and Kushida-san quickly raced over to try and receive the ball. Unfortunately, she was unable to control its momentum. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The first-year team were starting to catch up with us, slowly but surely, and when we reached the final stage of the match, we were neck and neck.</span></p>

I instructed our teammates to get the ball to Ibuki-san, and the game resumed. After both sides exchanged the ball back and forth, Amasawa-san was poised to spike for the first time.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No way am I gonna let </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">you</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> of all people score,” hollered Ibuki-san, jumping to block.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Too bad for you!” teased Amasawa-san, smiling.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She had faked us out with a decoy. They had been planning to have Nanase-san spike from the very beginning.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ibuki-san was caught completely off guard. She tried to reach for the ball, but she couldn’t get a handle on it. The ball was heading straight for the ground on our side of the court, traveling at a sharp angle…but Kushida-san slid over in the nick of time, making a risky move to receive the ball.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ibuki-san!” she shouted, setting the ball up for her.</span></p>

It slipped right past Amasawa-san as she attempted to block and went straight for Nanase-san, who was ready and waiting.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, Nanase-san was too exhausted to scoop the ball back up, and she ended up causing it to sail out of bounds. If Nanase-san had been at full strength, she might have stopped it beautifully. At any rate, the score was now 7 to 6. We secured a one-point lead when time was nearly out. And whether their team liked it or not, there was only a minute or so until time ran out, and we had the next serve.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay, now I think it’s time I take this seriously,” said Amasawa-san.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was almost as if she was suggesting she was just playing around until that point. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Nanase-san deftly blocked Ibuki-san’s next serve. The ball was robbed of its forward momentum and was sent sailing high up in the air, and we all stared at a single point where it was likely to go.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m going for it!” I shouted.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The ball was rocketing toward me with intense speed. Despite my efforts to focus my reflexes, my reaction was delayed, and the moment I tried to reach for the ball, it was already too far away for me to reach. The sound of the ball hitting the floor echoed violently through the&#160;gym. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Out!” shouted the referee.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I supposed that my delayed reaction was a blessing in disguise, so to speak. The ball had partially landed on the white line that indicated what was in bounds on the court.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Aw,” said Amasawa-san. “Sorry, Nanase-san, it went out. It sure is hard to control the ball perfectly, isn’t it?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Whew, that was a close call,” I muttered. “Still though, we definitely shouldn’t underestimate her potential after all…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though I had to tip my hat to Amasawa-san’s unfathomable ability and intuition, we had essentially narrowly escaped from certain death just then. The one-point gap between us widened to a two-point gap. Shortly afterward, their team scored another point on us, but just then, the whistle blew. Nanase-san was just tossing the ball into the air when she heard it and looked suddenly startled. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Aw, time ran out,” she said. “And things were just starting to get interesting too.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There wasn’t the slightest hint of regret or frustration in her voice. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, like…you just count to ten whenever you get mad?” Sudou blinked. “That’s it?” </span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Okay.” Though Sudou was skeptical, he committed what Onodera told him to memory.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wanted to team up with you </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">because</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> I admire you, Sudou-kun,” she added. “Don’t betray the faith I have in you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Onodera…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After they finished wrapping Onodera’s ankle, she tested how it felt and then stood up.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s all right,” she said. “Anyway, this tournament all comes down to this one last game whether we like it or not. If we lose this game, we lose the tennis tournament. But if we win it, the tournament’s ours.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Yeah,” said Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The third game would be the deciding factor. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Housen continued targeting Onodera relentlessly now that the injury to her left leg had dulled her movements. Even in cases when Housen actually ended up getting scored on because he took it too far, he still showed no sign of letting up his assault on her. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Despite that, Team Sudou was leading now, with the score 3 (40) to 1 (15). Housen knew that if Team Sudou scored on him just one more time, the game would be over. Even so, he targeted Onodera once again, sending the ball hurling her way at high speed. This time, Onodera was unable to avoid it, and it smacked her directly in her right upper arm. Onodera crouched down, in pain.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">not </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">how you’re s’posed to play,” Sudou snarled. “Enough of this—!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sudou was so angry that he felt like his blood was coming to boil. But then he remembered the magic spell that Onodera had taught him moments earlier. While glaring at Housen, the source of his repeated frustrations, Sudou let out silent shouts of anger in his mind. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Ten seconds of anger.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> He just had to hold it for ten seconds. He began counting, </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">1, 2, 3,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> and so on, and then took a deep breath to help get his emotions in check.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">8… 9… 10…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sudou took those insults that he wanted to hurl at Housen and kept them inside, swallowing his worlds back down. Although his irritation hadn’t completely disappeared, he succeeded in taking a step back, calming down, and looking objectively at the situation. He took stock of everything: the judges’ suspicious eyes, Onodera’s gaze, the fact that this was a game that needed to be won, and their remaining time. Sudou knew that if he got into it with Housen again, he’d naturally be put in check himself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Onodera, do you believe in my strength?” he asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course I do,” she replied after a moment. “I’m playing together with you because I believe.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sudou took a deep breath and centered himself again. Then, he tossed the ball up into the air and made the best serve of the entire day. Without missing a beat, Housen returned Sudou’s ball, and from that point on, Sudou and Housen began knocking it back and forth, back and forth, like something out of a sports manga. Both players kept hitting the ball with incredible intensity, not letting up one bit. But one time, Housen was a little lacking in his return, and Sudou didn’t miss his chance. He smashed the ball back into Housen’s side of the court.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeaaaaaahhhhh!!!” Sudou, still holding onto his racket, let out a victorious war cry that carried all throughout the gymnasium.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We did it!” shouted Onodera.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Having been dominating for so long only to slip up and lose in the endgame because of his carelessness, Housen was immensely frustrated. He slammed his racket onto the court hard, snapping it in two.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We won, Onodera! It’s all thanks to you!” Sudou, still in a total adrenaline rush, excitedly ran over to Onodera and hugged her intensely, sharing his enthusiasm.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"></div>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span><img alt="" height="100%" id="INTERIORIMAGES_8" src="/hola-lector-agua/images/books/y2v6/OEBPS/Images/INTERIORIMAGES_8.jpg" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style=" top: 0px; vertical-align: top; height: 100%;"/></span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"></div>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wh-wh-wh-whah?!” Onodera didn’t understand what had happened and was flustered. “Wai—ouch! That hurts, Sudou-kun!!!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When Sudou heard Onodera shout out in pain after he hugged her tightly with his thick, burly arms, he quickly regained his composure.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“S-sorry! My bad!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Perhaps Sudou was just so happy that he had been able to control his anger in addition to achieving victory, but he had the biggest smile of the day on his face at that moment.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Congratulations on the victory, Sudou-kun,” said Onodera.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thanks, Onodera. We definitely would have lost if it weren’t for you, though.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s not true. If anything, I probably held you back…” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t wanna say it was a good thing that you got hurt and all, but when it happened and I lost my temper, I really thought we were gonna lose then,” Sudou admitted. “But you brought me back from that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see,” said Onodera. “In that case, I guess…that means we’re good partners, huh?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. You’re super easy to play with, and you’re dependable. You’re, like, the best partner, Onodera, for real! Oh, I sure hope that Suzune’s around here somewhere and she saw our win just now…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There were quite a few guests and students around, though, and Sudou wasn’t able to find Horikita right away.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Suzune, huh…” murmured Onodera.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? Where?” exclaimed Sudou. “Do you see where she’s at?!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, um, um, sorry. Wrong person, I guess.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Damn. Welp, maybe she’s out on the field or somethin’…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, um… How about we get dinner together sometime? Like after we’re done with club stuff and heading back to the dorms?” asked Onodera.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh?” said Sudou. “Oh, sure, that’s fine with me. Anyway, though, help me find Suzune. Where the heck are ya? Suzune!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Aha ha ha, sorry, but I’ll pass on that,” said Onodera.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Despite the fact the game was over, Housen must not have been satisfied, because he strolled up to Sudou. “’Sup Sudou. Don’t get carried away after winnin’ a game like this, got it? You do realize that if I had actually taken this seriously, I would’ve beaten you into the ground, right?” he said. “I think I wanna settle this with you outside, so follow me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, hold on just a—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Onodera was about to step in and say something to Housen now that he was here trying to pick a fight, but Sudou quietly held her back.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Truth is, I got some beef with this guy from a little while back,” Sudou said. “I ain’t surprised he’s trying to get in my face.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“B-but!” protested Onodera.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Onodera wanted to protect Sudou and keep him from getting into trouble, but Sudou just smiled back at her. He then turned to Housen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry, but I don’t feel like goin’ along with whatever this is,” he told him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? Who said anything about you goin’ along with it or not?” Housen scoffed. “Startin’ now, you’re gonna be my punching bag.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry, not interested, dude.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After Sudou rejected his demands for a fight, Housen shoulder-checked Sudou and drove his clenched fist right into Sudou’s abdomen. Struck by Housen’s powerful punch, even though Housen hadn’t even wound up his arm for it, Sudou fell to his knees.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sudou-kun!” exclaimed Onodera, worried.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, Sudou gestured to her to stay back with a wave of his hand and slowly got back onto his feet. A teacher rushed over to see what the matter was, but Sudou simply said that nothing had happened, and the teacher left.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ow. Ah… Dude, I already know you’re good in a fight,” Sudou said. “I can’t complain too much because I was in the wrong back then too. But y’know, if you go any further with this, I’m really gonna have to let the teachers step in.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“God, you’re pathetic,” sneered Housen. “So what? You were way more fun when you came at me before, y’know?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Maybe, I dunno,” replied Sudou. “Let’s go, Onodera.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Y-yeah,” said Onodera.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ugh, you’re such a boring little brat. Don’t you ever get in my face again,” snapped Housen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If anything, Housen telling him that actually came as a relief to Sudou. It meant that if he didn’t do anything to bother Housen himself, there wasn’t going to be any more trouble. Sudou learned that by not giving in to his anger, he had been able to turn the situation around into something much more positive.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Y’know, I guess I ought to be grateful to Housen too,” he said. “When I see him like that, tryin’ to pulverize anyone and everyone around him, I realize how seriously lame I must’ve looked. It makes me cringe so hard, it hurts. I can’t really put this into words too well, but… When I tried that trick you taught me, it’s like somethin’ inside me just suddenly…stopped. It’s like, why </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">was</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> I so angry all the time? It’s almost like there was some evil spirit that was in me and now it’s gone.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">While Sudou was grateful for winning all ten competitions he had been in consecutively, he was just as appreciative of the Sports Festival overall. And, of course, of Onodera too.</span></p>
