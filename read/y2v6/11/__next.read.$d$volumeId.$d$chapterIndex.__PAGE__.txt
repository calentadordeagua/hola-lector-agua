<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 5:<br/>An Arrangement</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;"> </span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">WAS IN THE KARAOKE PLACE</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> inside Keyaki Mall, having come here to listen to what Horikita had mentioned to me yesterday. This was one of the best places to make sure that you had a private space aside from a dorm room. When I had set foot inside one of the rooms, I noticed that there wasn’t anyone inside except for Horikita and me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If we’re just going to talk, then it wasn’t really necessary to come all the way to this karaoke place, was it?” I&#160;asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Considering the fact that Horikita and I had visited each other’s rooms in the past, it shouldn’t have been a problem having this conversation in either one of those places. The fact that she had chosen to meet here meant that someone else would be coming. I decided not to press that matter too deeply and instead let Horikita take the lead.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We have a little bit of time before we’re scheduled to begin… Want to sing something?” she asked. She picked up the microphone that had been lying on the table and held it out to me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No thanks, I’ll pass,” I said. “Why don’t you sing, Horikita? If you do, I’ll clap along and stuff.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Absolutely not,” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">An immediate rejection. Did that mean she recommended someone else to do something that she hated to&#160;do…?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s because I’m going to study,” she explained, silently pulling her notebook out of her bag.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">With that, she began to review her notes. Tablets and other devices were provided in many of our classes at school, but when one was engaged in independent study, it was probably easier to learn by looking at your books and notes directly. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Since no one was singing, the room was rather quiet. Despite how weird this bizarre arrangement felt, I decided to just sit quietly on the sofa and let time go by.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Before long, our meeting time had passed, and it was now 5:10 p.m. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita had been checking the time on her phone every few minutes since before the start of the hour. She&#160;looked up and let out a sigh.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sorry,” she said. “It seems like this might be a longer ordeal than I expected.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t ask who we were going to be meeting with, but I could safely assume now that whoever it was, they were late now because the meeting time was supposed to be five o’clock. The fact that the other party hadn’t contacted Horikita suggested that there might have been some unavoidable circumstances, or perhaps that this person was a bit fast and loose—or it was possible that this person was </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">intentionally</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> late. As we waited another fifteen minutes or so, I went down a mental list of various students it could be and dismissed them in my mind one by one. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">At last someone outside the room slowly opened the door that hadn’t budged an inch all this time. The person I saw on the other side of it was…not someone I had expected—Katsuragi Kouhei from Class 2-D. From what I had seen, I had taken him to be someone that was pretty particular about time, so this was a shock.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I apologize for being late,” he said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s all right, don’t worry about it,” Horikita told him. “I’m sure you must have had your own share of struggles to deal with, right, Katsuragi-kun?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…More or less, yes,” he muttered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Then, Katsuragi urged a person who was looming behind him to enter the room as well, and that second person came into view.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Y’know, Suzune, it’s fine if you wanna go on a date with me, but there’s a bunch of extra people here,” he said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Katsuragi, a former leader of Class A, was with the man who had pulled him from his old class: Ryuuen Kakeru. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“They’re here because it would have been difficult to have a constructive conversation if you and I were to meet alone,” Horikita pointed out.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Despite the wry smile on Ryuuen’s face, he didn’t let his guard down in his sharp observations of Horikita. Now that the matter of Kushida had been settled and several distractions had been cleared away, Horikita had regained her usual composure. And since Horikita and Ryuuen had shared little in the way of direct interactions since we entered our second year of school, it wouldn’t have been surprising if Ryuuen had sensed some change within Horikita at this point.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wonder, were you late on purpose to try and rattle me? Perhaps to get an edge over me?” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Who can say?” replied Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Before they could come together and join forces, the two started feeling each other out, each trying to make a show of force and be hostile to the other. It was probably safe to assume that even Ryuuen and Katsuragi didn’t know the exact reason why they had been called here.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You said you had something you wanted to talk to us about… So, come on. Let’s hear the details,” Ryuuen said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Could you sit down?” Horikita asked. “If this was something that could be wrapped up in one or two minutes, I wouldn’t have gone through the trouble of asking you to come here.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ryuuen took a glance over at me and then sat down defiantly on the sofa. He proceeded to grab the tablet that was plugged into the wall charger and entered his order. His hands moved in such a way that showed this was a familiar process for him. He then roughly chuckled the tablet at the table. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Seeing that, Horikita reached over and picked it up. “Katsuragi-kun, would you care for anything?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll have an oolong tea,” he replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After Horikita entered Katsuragi’s order, she carefully returned the tablet to its original position on the charger.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The reason I’ve called you here is to talk to you about—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita immediately tried to launch into the discussion, but Ryuuen motioned for her to stop with a wave of his hand, like he was trying to take the wind out of her sails.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Before that, there’s somethin’ I wanted to ask. How’d it feel to scrape off that dead weight in your class and get some Class Points for it? Felt pretty damn good, I bet.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ryuuen calmly and matter-of-factly asked Horikita something that, for her, might have been damaging. It was probably also a way for him to try and gain the upper hand in a situation where he didn’t know what we were going to talk about yet. It was a given that Ryuuen had used his allies to get that information for him. He was making this move based on the assumption that our internal affairs were still left unresolved.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita, sitting beside me, wasn’t fazed. “It’s not as if problems haven’t sprung up as a result, of course,” she said. “But, unfortunately for you, things won’t turn out the way you’re hoping. Most of the major problems have already been resolved.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was a lie, because at the very least, the problem of Haruka hadn’t been dealt with. It was unclear just when that bomb would go off. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wow, you’re sure spewing those lies pretty confidently, huh?” Ryuuen had also determined that what Horikita said was untrue, which was why he made such a leading statement. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita didn’t care. “You can go ahead and think that I’m lying. I don’t care. Besides, you’re not the sort of person who would simply believe anything I said in the first place anyway. Isn’t that right?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Who knows? Maybe I actually trust you more than you think. Ever think about that?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even if you meant that seriously, no, even if you’re joking, it’s not funny either way.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita sidestepped his provocations. Katsuragi stared at her as though he were analyzing her, and slowly crossed his arms.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And what about you?” she asked. “I thought for sure that you would’ve expelled someone.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Aren’t you worried that you’d lose comrades?” Katsuragi replied. “You’re the only one who made the wrong choice.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Three out of the four classes had opted to protect their classmates. Katsuragi was trying to give Horikita the impression that she was the only one to have chosen unjustly, who’d made a mistake.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s unfortunate that we were the only ones who could make the correct choice,” said Horikita. “You didn’t even take one step forward in the race toward Class A.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s enough on that subject for the time being,” said Katsuragi, bringing that part of the conversation to a close.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As he did so, there was a soft knock on the door. One of the staff members had arrived, delivering the oolong tea that Katsuragi ordered along with an orange juice. The juice was placed in front of Ryuuen, but it really didn’t seem like the kind of thing he’d order. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita and Katsuragi both unintentionally found themselves staring at the bizarre pairing of Ryuuen and an orange juice. Incidentally, I couldn’t help but stare too. Ryuuen and orange juice? That just didn’t seem to fit. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">While everyone was wondering what was going on, Katsuragi spoke up.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, now that we have our drinks, let’s get down to business. What is the purpose of this meeting?” asked Katsuragi, urging Horikita to speak.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita nodded, and as she looked between Ryuuen and Katsuragi once again, she started to speak.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m proposing a cooperative partnership for the upcoming Sports Festival in order to defeat Sakayanagi-san’s class,” she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Katsuragi’s shoulders twitched slightly, indicating that he was surprised. Immediately afterward, he returned to his usual calm demeanor, and after a pause, he asked her a question in return.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do you mean exactly, by cooperative partnership?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Cooperation” could imply a wide variety of things, and the exact degree of cooperation required could vary greatly depending on how each person interpreted it. It made sense that Katsuragi would want to hear more details, but at the same time, he wasn’t going to dismiss the proposal immediately. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ryuuen, on the other hand, looked unsurprised and unimpressed. He simply watched quietly with a smug smirk on his face.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In the upcoming Sports Festival, there’s both competition between the grade levels and competition within each grade level,” Horikita said. “I wish to make the most of a system wherein we can win points and share them equally if we win team-based competitions, where multiple students are involved.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">our</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> class, though?” asked Katsuragi. “Would you mind telling me the reason?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The leader of that class, Ryuuen, didn’t interject, not even once. He simply listened.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“First of all, it goes without saying that teaming up with Class A is out of the question. Helping that class earn points while we’re trying to catch up to them would be completely backward. Therefore, that leaves us with two choices: either Ichinose-san’s class, or your class. According to my analysis, while Ichinose-san </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">is</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> the most trustworthy of anyone, I’d be hard-pressed to say that she has many gifted athletes in her class.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So you’re saying that you chose us by process of elimination?” replied Katsuragi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If I made this decision simply by process of elimination, I wouldn’t have chosen to team up with anyone in the first place, Katsuragi-kun,” said Horikita. “The only class that I trust even less than Sakayanagi-san’s class is the one that Ryuuen-kun, your leader, is in charge of.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was certainly true that Ryuuen wasn’t an easy person to team up with. Katsuragi nodded deeply, in a show of sympathy.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re right about that,” he said. “Even I think so, and I’ve become one of his classmates. There’s no one as dangerous as Ryuuen when it comes to having someone watching your back. In that case, I have to ask why you are even proposing a cooperating partnership, and going so far as to shoulder such a big risk for the sake of it?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“To win, of course. We can’t arrive at the top if we don’t stop Class A’s unchallenged rise in the rankings.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“However, what would be the point if Ryuuen ends up betraying your expectations in the end?” Katsuragi pushed. “He is a man who will do whatever he feels is necessary. That’s just what he is like. Having experienced it first-hand, I understand it all too well myself. I cannot recommend you do this.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Katsuragi’s opinion of his class’s leader was so scathing that it was hard to imagine that he was the chief strategist on Ryuuen’s side. He was warning Horikita of the danger here: if she entered this partnership carelessly, she could forget about rising to the top. She could instead be swallowed up by Ryuuen’s class.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I actually had no intention of immediately getting down to business in today’s discussion, you know,” said Horikita. “I hadn’t talked with Ryuuen-kun in quite some time, and I can’t trust someone who would be late without a care in the world. However, </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">you</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> apologized for being late, Katsuragi-kun. When that happened, I changed my mind. At the very least, I can trust you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s quite simpleminded. Do you not think my behavior was just another one of Ryuuen’s ploys?” said&#160;Katsuragi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If I can’t figure out whether I can trust someone or not, then I’ll simply be swallowed up sooner or later,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Her trust was likely a bet on Horikita’s part. If you placed Ryuuen and Katsuragi side by side, Katsuragi would appear to be a good, sensible person by comparison. However, if Horikita showed that she was prepared for what might come, then even Katsuragi would have no choice but to believe her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re a little different from how you were before, Horikita. You seem to be maturing as well.” Katsuragi sensed the change in Horikita and interpreted it as growth. He once again demonstrated a willingness to sit down and engage in a dialogue. “I understand what you’re saying. I will now offer you my own personal perspective, from where I’m sitting.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Katsuragi made a point of saying “personal” there, which meant it was safe to assume that Ryuuen’s intentions and thoughts were not being taken into account. At least, I supposed that was what Katsuragi was indicating to us.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I too envisioned a plan wherein your class and ours joined forces to defeat Class A,” he said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You did too…?” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s right. Your class has talented individuals, such as Sudou and Kouenji, whose abilities go well beyond their grade level. Out of the four classes in our grade, yours rests at the top in terms of physical ability and the available players. We would have no need to worry about being dragged down if we partnered with you. You’re not unconditionally trustworthy, but the fact that you’re not the sort of class that would casually betray us means that you’re not a bad choice.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">While Katsuragi was speaking, Ryuuen turned to look at me but remained silent. Up until now, there hadn’t been anyone else in Ryuuen’s class who could handle negotiations, so Ryuuen had always taken the initiative in these sorts of talks. However, with the addition of Katsuragi to the class, the need for Ryuuen to step in had diminished and he now had the option of being able to wait and see how things went. One could say that this was an extremely significant positive for him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was unsettling, not knowing what Ryuuen was thinking—not knowing what he was going to propose, or when he’d do it. And while it was easier to talk with Katsuragi, Horikita was probably starting to become aware of that scary side of it too. Even so, it was something that couldn’t be avoided if discussions with that class were going to be happening on a regular basis for the next year and a half.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In reality, however, I would say that the chances of Ryuuen accepting or rejecting your proposal are about fifty-fifty,” said Katsuragi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It had already been more than a week since we had heard the details for the Sports Festival. That meant that if Katsuragi and Ryuuen’s class had really been thinking of cooperating with someone, Horikita might have already heard talk about it. In other words, in Katsuragi’s mind, their priority was not joining forces, but rather was something else.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If we were to enter into a cooperative partnership with someone else, then we would naturally secure first and second places between our classes,” he continued. “In that case, it inevitably would be the overall strengths of our respective classes that would determine the winner. If we were to look at simple probabilities, we would have to accept and be content with the possibility that it’d likely be your class that takes first, Horikita, and that ours would take second.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">By working together, Horikita and Ryuuen’s classes would get a jump on Sakayanagi’s class and Ichinose’s class, and effectively create a situation where it would just be Horikita’s class versus Ryuuen’s class for the top spot. That was probably the reason why Katsuragi said there was a fifty-fifty chance of Ryuuen accepting her proposal. Even though Katsuragi was entertaining the conversation here, it wasn’t like he was showing that he’d immediately agree to a cooperative partnership. Negotiations with Ryuuen would not begin until the hurdle in front of us was crossed… </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">What is Horikita going to do in this situation?</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> I couldn’t help but wonder.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In other words, you’re saying that you see our class as a threat,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course we do. The situation now is very different than it was a year ago. Unlike before, when you were ridiculed as a collection of defectives, you’re now ranked as Class B. And you achieved that after dropping down to zero Class Points, no less. More recently, Kouenji single-handedly achieved victory in the Uninhabited Island Special Exam, and you made the difficult choice of abandoning a classmate in exchange for 100 points in the Unanimous Special Exam. There is no doubt that your class is formidable.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even though those accomplishments aren’t mine, it certainly doesn’t feel bad being praised like that,” Horikita admitted. “At any rate, if we don’t join forces and instead try to tackle the Sports Festival on our own, we could create a worst-case scenario where Sakayanagi-san’s class takes first place. The important thing here is defeating Sakayanagi-san’s class. Am I wrong?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re right about that,” Katsuragi agreed. “That is also true… Ryuuen, what do you think?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Katsuragi then turned to Ryuuen, asking for his opinion for the first time in this conversation.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you’re askin’ me to lend you a hand, that means you’re gonna give us something equally valuable in return, right?” Ryuuen asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You seem to be misunderstanding something here,” Horikita said. “While it’s true that I’m the one approaching you with this proposal, that doesn’t mean that you’re in a position to come to a compromise with us. Instead, I would rather you understand that you’re in a position where you can form a cooperative partnership with the class that is going to get first place.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t make me laugh. I’m in a position where I can win </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">without</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> your help, but if you’re gonna beg me for help, I guess I could lend a hand. And if you don’t like it, we can leave. Got it?” </span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh?”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though we had already reached an agreement about the Sports Festival, since I was the one putting forward this idea, there was a chance he’d turn me down. That’s what it sounded like from the way he phrased his answer.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I understand,” I replied. “Leave that to me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had an idea for containing Sakayanagi, and it was something that neither Ryuuen, nor Katsuragi, nor even Horikita could do.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">T WAS JUST BEFORE</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> seven o’clock at night, and three students from Class 2-A—Sakayanagi, Kamuro, and Hashimoto—were gathered at the café within Keyaki Mall.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">O</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">CTOBER WAS FINALLY HERE</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> and the Sports Festival was fast approaching. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sure.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Secrets, huh.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> What was she referring to? My family? The White Room? What I was trying to do at school? Friendships? Romantic feelings? If she was talking about any one of those things, then I hadn’t done anything </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">but </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">keep secrets. I hadn’t told Kei the truth about anything.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As Kei and I were wandering around the store, looking around this way and that, we happened to bump into Satou. She seemed to be by herself. As soon as we ran into each other, her eyes focused on where my and Kei’s arms were linked together.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“W-wow, you guys sure look lovey-dovey,” she blurted out. “W-well, I’ll get outta your way then. See ya!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah, wai—h-hold on?!” sputtered Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kei tried to keep her from going, but Satou ran away as fast as her legs could carry her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Aw, jeez…” Kei put a hand to her forehead, looking defeated.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re still worried about Satou?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s not like that, it’s just… Well, it doesn’t feel good, I&#160;guess…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, we’ll just have to refrain from going out arm-in-arm in public from here on out.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t wanna stop,” huffed Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though she felt bad for her best friend, she apparently wasn’t willing to compromise on this. As we walked through the rice cooker and kettle section, we bumped into Ishizaki and Albert. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh? Hey, ’sup, Ayanokouji!” said Ishizaki, beaming.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In that same instant, I felt Kei gripping onto my arm a little more tightly than before.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, hey, you’re on a date with Karuizawa, huh? And whoa, you guys’re arm-in-arm, too… It’s like you’re one o’ those guys with a real life…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ishizaki looked at me with envy, but my attention was more focused on Albert beside him. He was holding a large brand-name pot in his hands. It was a little strange to me that it didn’t look that big, but maybe it was because Albert was so big himself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, you’re wonderin’ about this thing?” said Ishizaki. “Ryuuen-san’s birthday’s on the twentieth, dude. We picked this out for ’im.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? The twentieth… He’s got the same birthday?” asked Kei, surprised.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She looked up at me with alert eyes, seeming a little on edge.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s the first I’ve heard about it,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Who’s got the same birthday as Ryuuen-san?” asked Ishizaki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When Ishizaki directed his gaze over in Kei’s direction, she glared back at him and hid behind me a little.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Come on, dude, tell me—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In that instant, Albert lightly placed his hand on Ishizaki’s shoulder, interrupting him. Ishizaki finally seemed to grasp the reason why Kei was so wary of him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Oh! Ohhhh… That’s it… Dude…” muttered Ishizaki sadly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though Ishizaki had been acting on Ryuuen’s orders, he still had been complicit in what happened, in calling Kei up onto the roof and engaging in what could be called bullying. It was natural that Kei wouldn’t take kindly to someone like Ishizaki now. Perhaps Ishizaki was angry at himself for being so slow on the uptake. After clicking his tongue, he lightly smacked his own head with a clenched fist.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry… I mean, man, I shoulda said that to you before, and… About what happened to you on the roof, I, uh—” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t talk about that here,” hissed Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Though Ishizaki tried to apologize, the fact was that he was still quite lacking in sensitivity. We were in the mall right now, and people we knew could show up at any moment. Kei probably wouldn’t be too happy if someone tried to bring up what happened on the roof at a time like this. Just letting Ishizaki and Albert walk away would solve the problem for now, but as long as Kei and I were going to continue having a relationship, there’d be more than a few opportunities to run into Ishizaki like this.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why don’t we change locations?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even in a place like Keyaki Mall where people were often coming and going, there was no shortage of blind spots. Kei looked unhappy but she didn’t say anything. She simply walked along with me, still arm-in-arm. Albert put the item he was holding back on the shelf and followed Ishizaki in similar fashion. They must have been willing to apologize precisely because they felt bad about what they had done.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When we neared the emergency exit, we were far enough away from the shops so students would be able to see us, but they wouldn’t be able to hear us. If a familiar face appeared, we could stop the conversation and there wouldn’t be a problem.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m real sorry!” said Ishizaki. “I mean, for real, I went all this time without givin’ ya an apology! I’m sorry!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Whatever,” huffed Kei. “I’m still annoyed even if you’re saying you’re sorry. If anything, it makes me feel even </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">more</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> irritated.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Uh…?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You guys got the crap beaten out of you by Kiyotaka. You’re only apologizing to me because you lost.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“W-well, I, uh…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If Kiyotaka hadn’t saved me back there on the rooftop… Or if he lost against you and Ryuuen, you wouldn’t be apologizing to me now. Am I wrong? Ugh, this is so annoying.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kei certainly had a point that what they had done was terrible and troublesome. I was on speaking terms with Ishizaki and Albert now myself, but that all started because of the rooftop incident. It made sense that Kei was saying there were big “ifs” at play here.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Look, I know, and like, I can’t argue with you blaming me for what I did, but…” stammered Ishizaki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t really blame you,” Kei corrected him. “It’s natural that the strong be on top. I don’t like being beneath people, so I’ve been trying to stay on top myself. I’ve acted all high and mighty to people below me too. I&#160;know how it goes.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Despite the differences in the degree of their actions, Kei and Ishizaki were essentially the same in character. Their value systems were basically, “If you can’t beat ’em, join ’em.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Kei, I know what you’re trying to say, but I’ve come to know a little more about Ishizaki now that I’ve come&#160;into contact with him myself</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">” I cut in. “He’s definitely had some positive growth since before.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do you mean, ‘positive growth’?” she replied. “It doesn’t look to me like he’s changed at all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This is just how I feel, but I think that if Ryuuen tried to do to someone else what he did to you that time, I can’t imagine Ishizaki would go along with it that easily,” I answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh really? It doesn’t look to me like he can stand against Ryuuen, though.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She was right on the mark. Ishizaki audibly gulped. Unable to say anything back to that, he felt overwhelmed by regret and bitterness and slapped his own knee hard with his palm. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kei sighed. “Enough already,” she said. “You’re friends with Kiyotaka now, right? I’m not going to forgive you, but I’m done blaming you for it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“R-really?” said Ishizaki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I literally just said so. It’s over and done with. Okay?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“O-okay!” Ishizaki looked up, a happy expression on his face. He decided to pose his question to Kei again. “Hey, uh…um, yeah. So, uh, about the whole birthday thing earlier, who else has the same birthday?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Though Kei still didn’t trust him, she pointed to me with her index finger.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? Wait, for real? Ayanokouji, your birthday’s October twentieth?!” Ishizaki seemed almost unbelievably shocked. “Whoa, dude! It’s like, destiny or somethin’, don’tcha think?!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Whaddaya mean, destiny?” Kei scoffed. “There are over four hundred people in our school. It’s not weird that some people have the same birthday.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But, I mean, jus’ the fact Ayanokouji and Ryuuen-san do is, like, crazy, dude! Ain’t it?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He was overjoyed by a simple coincidence. Just as Kei had said, there was nothing strange about it, but for some reason, even Albert seemed a little happy about it too.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Can we go back to the store now?” asked Kei, turning back to me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh! Hey, that reminds me! Hold on a sec!!!” Ishizaki belted those words out in a particularly loud voice, causing Kei to cover her ears, irritated. “I got a little proposition for ya. So, if you want, how about we celebrate both your birthdays together and all on the twentieth? Ryuuen-san and Ayanokouji’s Double B-Day party! Dude, wouldn’t that be the best thing ever?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Well, no, it wouldn’t. From the second I heard that suggestion pass his lips, I thought it was absolutely </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">not </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">the best thing ever… Honestly, even though I tried to imagine what that might be like, I couldn’t picture it very well.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If he apologizes to me, then I’m fine with it,” said Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh?” sputtered Ishizaki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I mean if </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">he</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">, and by that I mean </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Ryuuen</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">, bows down and apologizes to me, I’ll agree to it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Her words were nothing more than a pretense; Kei meant to refuse his offer. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ishizaki’s mouth hung open. Then, when he realized how difficult it would be to convince Ryuuen to do that, he clamped his mouth shut in a frown.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ryuuen wouldn’t apologize to me though, would he?” said Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? Uh, well, yeah, that’s probably never gonna happen…” mumbled Ishizaki. It’d be impossible for Ishizaki to even </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">suggest</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> to Ryuuen that he apologize. Ishizaki froze for a moment, but then it seemed like he found some determination because he forced his mouth to open again. “If you two say that you wanna do it though, then I’ll talk to Ryuuen!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Maybe you shouldn’t bother?” said Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If Ishizaki did try to talk to Ryuuen, he might find a beating waiting for him. Ryuuen was so well-known in our grade level that it was easy to imagine such a scenario.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll try somethin’!” Ishizaki insisted. “If I can get him to promise to say he’s sorry, then we’ll have a birthday party!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well… If you can really get him to actually do it, then I suppose I’ll consider it…” said Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ishizaki was positively bursting with enthusiasm, but at the same time, he was promising something without due consideration that would likely lead to his own downfall. I thought that I should clearly tell him that I was against the idea. It was true that Ishizaki had been demonstrating his strong willpower lately. It was also true that some changes were beginning to take shape in Ryuuen’s way of thinking: he hadn’t tried to get anyone expelled in the Unanimous Special Exam, after all. However, that couldn’t be interpreted as a shift in his instincts or true feelings.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">People didn’t change that easily, not even if they wanted to. Ryuuen wasn’t trying to change—he was trying to </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">evolve</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">. Up until now, he was a man who had fought using evil as his only weapon. Now, he had begun to wield good as well. He was beginning to control both sides of the same coin at will. And if Ishizaki was misreading that fact, then he’d—</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I think it’s time you stop,” said Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But Ishizaki’s resolve remained firm. “If I can get Ryuuen-san to say he’s sorry, will you be okay with the birthday idea thingy?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ve got it! Let me formally ’pologize to you again when we have the party. I’ll getcha a little somethin’ that’s got even </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">more</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> thought put into it than my present to Ryuuen-san as my way of sayin’ sorry!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kei reluctantly admitted defeat in the face of Ishizaki’s intense enthusiasm, telling him, “All right, whatever.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, dude! It’s settled! Anyway, I’m gonna get a move on now and pick out Ryuuen-san’s b-day present!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Albert nodded in response, and then he and Ishizaki went on ahead, back to the store. They seemed to understand that they couldn’t walk around together with Kei and me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why did you agree to Ishizaki’s request?” I asked Kei. “I thought for sure you’d turn him down.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though I expected her to openly listen to what Ishizaki had to say and accept his apology, I had to admit that I honestly hadn’t thought that she’d accept Ishizaki’s offer about the birthday party.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, I mean, it’s true I would prefer to spend your birthday with just the two of us, Kiyotaka, but…well…”</span></p>

And even </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">you</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> need friends, Kiyotaka.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I understood immediately that Kei was referring to the dissolution of the Ayanokouji Group. When she realized I guessed as much, she blushed and averted her eyes.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And besides, Ishizaki-kun said that he wanted to get me something as his way of saying sorry. I thought it would be okay to accept it is all,” she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Somehow, that dishonest part of Kei was very much like her too. Anyway, it’d be best to take Ishizaki’s proposal with a grain of salt and assume this birthday thing probably wasn’t going to happen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">And so, the days leading up to the Sports Festival continued to go by, just like that.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">A</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">FTER SATOU FLEW</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> out of the electronics store at full speed, she stopped to catch her breath in front of the girls’ restroom.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">I’m sure that Kei-chan is holding herself back from doing that kind of thing with Ayanokouji-kun when they’re in class and stuff, after all,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> she thought. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">And I’m sure that Kei-chan wants to be all alone with Ayanokouji-kun more, with just the two of them.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> </span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Who I am isn’t important right now,” the probable first-year said. “Truthfully, there’s something I want to tell you as soon as possible. If it’s not a problem, could I have a minute of your time?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“H-huh? What is this all about?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The stranger was telling Satou that she wanted to tell her something, and Satou felt perplexed—on top&#160;of how unsettled she was still feeling about the image of&#160;Ayanokouji and Karuizawa clinging together, which she still couldn’t get out of her mind.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Information about Ayanokouji-senpai.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Satou stopped moving.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…About Ayanokouji-kun?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. About him and his girlfriend, Karuizawa Kei-senpai.”</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"></div>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span><img alt="" height="100%" id="INTERIORIMAGES_6" src="/hola-lector-agua/images/books/y2v6/OEBPS/Images/INTERIORIMAGES_6.jpg" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style=" top: 0px; vertical-align: top; height: 100%;"/></span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"></div>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hearing the names of the two people who were taking up approximately 99 percent of her headspace right now, Satou couldn’t help but look up. As the stranger slowly but surely closed in on her, Satou felt a slight twinge of nervousness.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’d like to talk to you about this in detail—but someplace we can be alone, if at all possible. Would you be able to?” </span></p>
