<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 3:<br/>Two Teachers and A Fated Special Exam</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HE NEXT MORNING</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> I was assigned to be the manager (?) of the maid café. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But then, when we saw Chabashira’s grim expression as she strode into our classroom, many students immediately noticed that something was off. However, this time, unlike the usual for situations like this, the words “special exam” weren’t the first to spring to mind for most of us. That was because they probably assumed the next exam would be the sports festival itself. Furthermore, the cultural festival was coming up right after that.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Before the sports festival in October, you’ll be taking a new special exam,” announced Chabashira.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Her statement caused a bit of an uproar amongst the students. There hadn’t been any special exams around this time last year, not while we were getting ready for the sports festival. But it sounded like this year was going to be different.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ugh, come on, are you kidding me?” Ike groaned. “We </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">just</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> got through that brutal uninhabited island exam, and now we have to take another one…?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Though it had already become customary for him to complain about these things, I heard Ike mutter discontented grumblings before anyone else did. I supposed that for Ike, a new special exam was a grim prospect. He had just managed to overcome the uninhabited island when his back was against the wall and expulsion seemed imminent. On top of that, he had just officially become Shinohara Satsuki’s boyfriend as well. No matter how much Ike and Shinohara deepened their relationship and grew closer, suddenly finding themselves expelled from school now was most certainly possible, depending on the type of special exam. There was no doubt that students with particularly low levels of overall ability felt a sense of urgency.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, bring it on.” Sudou, who had absolute confidence in his athletic prowess, smacked his fists together. “I say we clear this special exam quickly before we completely dominate the sports festival.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t get carried away,” snapped Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Sorry.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita’s immediate warning made Sudou feel a little dejected. He went silent. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">What a wonderful master-slave relationship they’ve… Er, well, I suppose I could say that they’re nurturing a friendship.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If I’m being honest here, there have been very few instances of special exams being given at this time of year in the past,” Chabashira told us. “In fact, a special exam will not be held for the first-year and third-year students.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Satou had been leaning against the back of her chair before, but now she pitched forward. “You mean that our grade is the only one getting a special exam before the sports festival?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira didn’t deny it at all. She simply nodded.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Because you second-years have been so exceptional, the school is giving you an appropriate level of recognition for your worth,” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh?” said Satou. “Wait, a special exam because they recognize our worth…? Isn’t that a little strange?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It is certainly true that special exams come with a level of risk you ought to be wary of</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">” said Chabashira. “Class Points or Private Points could be lost. Some students could even be expelled from school. However, on the other hand, it could be said that these give you even more opportunities to lead a more fulfilling life here at school. The more special exams that are held, the better your chances are for being promoted to Class A. And that is considered to be the most important thing of all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was true that if you wanted to earn a large number of Class Points, it was extremely difficult to do so through ordinary, everyday activities. If anything, the periods of time when there weren’t any special exams were largely about keeping our Class Points from falling any lower. Whether it was the uninhabited island special exam or any other, opportunities to move up to higher-level classes only came when a special exam was being&#160;held. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Happiness and unhappiness are two sides of the same coin. So, there are benefits to those risks, right?” said Horikita, having calmly accepted the news and taken a position close to Chabashira’s.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Exactly,” said Chabashira.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That being the case, there’s nothing for us to fear,” Horikita declared. “We are most definitely closing in on Class A now. There’s a chance we can break out of the three-way tie between B, C, and D. This just means that our chance is already here.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The more opportunities we had, the better. That was the commonly held belief for everyone aiming for the top.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s true, you’re right about that… And it’s not like complaining about it will make special exams go away or anything,” replied Satou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita’s words seemed to have convinced even Satou and our other lower-level classmates, judging from their expressions. Though you could say it was still a work in progress, Horikita’s growth as a pillar of support seemed to be having a clear, positive effect on her classmates. I&#160;suspected that Chabashira was probably inwardly pleased by this as well, even if she didn’t let it show on her face at all. She wasn’t the sort of teacher to show her sweet side in the first place, but lately, I felt like it was coming through a bit more than usual.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You will be taking what is referred to as the ‘Unanimous Special Exam’ this time.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The monitor lit up and Chabashira began explaining the exam. It was accompanied with various visual aids that had become customary at this point.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This special exam is exceedingly simple. Hence, I will be accepting questions at any time if there is anything you are curious about. This exam will be administered tomorrow, and as you can probably surmise from the name, it consists of a series of multiple-choice questions which you will answer repeatedly until the entire class comes to a unanimous decision.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Tomorrow?” repeated Horikita. “That’s…rather sudden.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We certainly weren’t being provided with adequate prep time for this. Of course, there weren’t any advantages or disadvantages because it was essentially a fair contest, but our class that had just settled down was now beginning to stir once again.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“As I just said, this special exam is simple,” Chabashira replied. “The school believes that there will not be any problems in going ahead with the exam tomorrow. There isn’t a need to spend time discussing things in advance.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We would vote over and over again in class until we came to a unanimous decision. Just from hearing about that part alone, I certainly couldn’t see anything particularly complex about this exam. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That means we’re not fighting against another class this time then, right?” asked Yousuke, immediately hoping for an answer on that point, considering it more important than anything else.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s right,” said Chabashira. “Since this special exam only takes place within your class, you will not be competing against any rivals. On the day of the exam, the school will give you five ‘issues’ to complete. These issues are the same for all classes, with no differences between them.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If the issues were different, that would mean the difficulty level would be different from class to class. I&#160;supposed that made sense.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This might seem sudden, but I’m going to jump right into it. I will provide you with an example to help you understand.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.00em; text-transform: uppercase;">EXAMPLE ISSUE:</span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.00em;"> Lose 5 Class Points, but all classmates gain 10,000 Private Points.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: normal; font-size: 0.80em;">CHOICES:</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> For, Against</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The sample issue was shown on the monitor. Just like Chabashira had said, it was simple and easy to understand.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hm? Wait, what’s this?” said Shinohara. “Um… We’d go down by five Class Points, but in exchange, we can get 10,000 Private Points… This is an issue? Would that be a gain or a loss?” She counted on her fingers, trying to calculate the actual gains and losses in her head.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It wasn’t surprising that many unexpected questions would come to mind for people. Still, even though this was only an example, I had expected there to have been more choices to agonize over. We earned 100 Private Points each month for every Class Point. That meant that five Class Points were worth 500 Private Points. If you thought about the issue presented to us, the latter option was worth more in terms of Private Points, and by an overwhelming amount. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, Class Points would continue to hold value. In one month, five Class Points was worth nothing more than 500 Private Points. However, if you calculated their worth over a longer time span, like say, over a year, then those mere five Class Points would be worth 6,000 Private Points. Considering the time we had remaining until graduation, we had eighteen opportunities left to receive Private Points, meaning from October of our second year to the March of our third year. In other words, we could consider the value of those Class Points to be worth 9,000 Private Points.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But things weren’t actually so simple. Supposing the loss of five Class Points here in this exam carried through, and in the end we missed on reaching Class A by that same margin, we would look back on this decision as&#160;the worst possible choice we could have made. Of course, the&#160;probability that five points would make all the difference between winning and losing probably wasn’t that high. In that case, it was quite possible that there could be cases in which it’d be more beneficial for us to gain the 10,000 Private Points. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Whichever point of view we took, there were advantages and disadvantages in the end.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“All thirty-nine of you will vote anonymously on each issue presented, choosing from the options presented to&#160;you,” Chabashira explained. “You’ll probably understand how this works the quickest by simply trying it for yourself, so let’s do just that right now. I’m sure that many of you have quite a lot of questions, but I’d like you to try and enter your votes without any time for discussion. Please enter your vote, for or against, on your tablets.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After Chabashira tapped some things on the device in hand, all of the students’ tablets’ screens, mine included, now displayed something new. Our tablets displayed the issue, and we could either select </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">For</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> or </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Against</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> on the&#160;screen. This truly was an unusual special exam unlike any we’d seen before. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I decided to consider this issue seriously for a moment.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Private Points had no direct effect on our Class Points. The fact that all of our classmates would get 10,000 Private Points by voting in favor was a simple benefit. And, by voting in favor, we were losing only five Class Points. But still, five Class Points was five Class Points. In that case, I supposed I needed to consider how someone would think about this, in terms of human nature. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It wasn’t a question of whether gaining 10,000 Private Points or not losing five Class Points was the better option. The real question was actually the opposite: could we make either of these choices without regretting it later? I decided to press </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">For</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">, which was likely going to be the less popular option, to see what the results would be. I figured it wasn’t the best idea to be unanimous the first time around. The tallying must have been complete shortly afterward because Chabashira looked up from the tablet in her hands.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“All right. Now that you’ve all finished voting, I’d like to go ahead and show you the results immediately.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">And with that, the tally was displayed up on the monitor.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.00em;">Round 1 Voting Results:</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> For: 3 Votes, Against: 36 Votes</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I knew that there would be more votes against, but it was an even wider margin than I had expected.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“H-hey, um? Wouldn’t we get more going with the 10,000 Private Points than we’d get from five Class Points?” said Ike, puzzled. He looked around at his classmates. “We’d only get a few Private Points from those, right? Or did I mess up my math? Why’d so many people vote against?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Judging from that comment, I could assume that Ike had also voted in favor.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If we’re only talking about the number of Private Points we’re getting, then yes, it’s true that we’d get more Private Points by going with the 10,000 option,” said Horikita. “But Class Points are essential if we’re going to aim for Class A. If it’s only a difference of a thousand points, then why would we deliberately reduce our precious Class Points? There’d be no need to do that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She must have voted against, and she offered a theoretical explanation for her choice.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In the unlikely event that those five Class Points end up spelling the difference between winning and losing, then I certainly couldn’t help but regret our choice if we gave them up,” she added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Just as I thought, many students were naturally worried about the risks in the unlikely event something happened. I also couldn’t forget that the other three classes were also going to be voting on these same issues. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, in other words, if this exam isn’t going to be that easy, that means it’s highly likely that there are going to be issues that people will be divided on,” Yousuke concluded.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">His thinking was right on the mark. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I can’t really come up with anything right on the spot,” Yousuke replied, “but…I think there would definitely be those kinds of issues…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">What kinds of questions would my classmates agonize over? It seemed like Yousuke wasn’t able to come up with any ideas right away. I thought I’d speak up with one easy-to-understand issue.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Until we graduate, we can only eat either rice or bread starting now. Choose,” I told them.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? What’s with those choices?” said Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Choosing between rice and bread is something that kind of makes me want to chuckle at its simplicity, but it’s actually a hard choice,” said Yousuke.</span></p>

Absolutely not, no way.”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If I had to choose, I suppose I’d pick rice too,” I said. “And, well, as you can see, we three have our own opinions on this, right? <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No way! I mean, not being able to have any at all until graduation? I’d still vote for bread,” said Kei.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t know… Difficult choices will obviously have to be made, but losing 300 Class Points would mean giving up our ticket to Class A,” said Yousuke. “Our first priority should be clearing this exam.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kei sighed, “I’m starting to think that this is going to be really tough…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is that why you called us here?” asked Yousuke.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re right,” said Yousuke. “But in that case, shouldn’t Horikita-san be included in this discussion we’re having now too? She took on the role of class leader for this exam, after all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yousuke’s point was obvious. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This time, we will be supporting Horikita from the shadows,” I told them. “What we’re discussing here is strictly confidential.”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You can’t always count on me to be able to act,” I said. “You should be prepared for unforeseen situations.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Unforeseen situations?” she repeated.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I could suddenly fall ill, or I could unexpectedly get expelled. That’s conceivable, isn’t it?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s… Well, I… Well, expulsion, like, no way! I can’t picture that happening,” she replied. “But it is true you could suddenly get sick, that might happen.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I couldn’t back them up at any given time forever. If they couldn’t act in anticipation of situations like that, they couldn’t expect to see the class make a great leap forward.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I decided to tell Yousuke that now so he could prepare himself. So he wouldn’t go out of control if that time came.</span></p>

Anyone would want to avoid a heavy penalty, after all, even if it meant voting for an option that they didn’t like.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yagami reached for the steaming cup of tea that sat in front of Kushida, taking it into his hands.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Now then, onto the heart of the matter,” he said. “You are already in the middle of your second year. However, while you do want those two expelled, you still haven’t had the perfect opportunity fall into your lap yet.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“As far as I’m concerned, you’re a little bit responsible for that yourself</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">” snapped Kushida. “But whatever, it doesn’t matter.” She held herself back from going further, understanding that there wasn’t anything to be gained by lashing out at Yagami right here and now.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Have you told Horikita-senpai?” asked Yagami.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh… You mean about being the leader? Yes, more or less. I think she would’ve gone ahead and done it on her own anyway, though, even if I didn’t say anything.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, no, it isn’t good to leave things in a state of ambiguity. It is important for you to make sure that Horikita-senpai is entrusted with the role and to get her to make a firm declaration that she would handle it, Kushida-senpai.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, what now? Do you think I can get her expelled in this upcoming special exam?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">At that question, Yagami let out a little chuckle before taking a sip of tea from his cup.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, certainly,” he said. “I listened to your recording to make sure that there was nothing I missed hearing or misinterpreted, just in case. After hearing what you’ve provided, things have been made clear. It is…quite possible that you could do that in this upcoming special exam.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…How could you possibly know that?” Kushida snorted. “The only instance where expulsion is given as a penalty is if someone runs over time. Do you think that Horikita would make a mistake like that? No, not just Horikita; </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">no one</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> would make that kind of mistake.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Certainly not. It’s unlikely anyone would be stupid enough to allow themselves to run over time and be expelled for it. But from what I can tell, I think that there are other ways someone could be expelled.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida blinked. “Huh?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Expel Horikita-senpai, or, depending on the situation, expel Ayanokouji-senpai. There might be a possibility that you can crush the people you wish to crush. When that time comes, you shouldn’t hesitate to guide the conversation toward targeting both of them.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yagami then proceeded to elaborate on what he meant by using an example: an issue that was expected to be given in this upcoming special exam.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is that really true…?” asked Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I don’t think it’ll necessarily be exactly the same, not word for word,” Yagami said. “However. I do think there is a significant possibility that there will be an issue on the exam like the one I just told you about.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yagami hadn’t actually been told about this special exam by Tsukishiro, but after listening to the teacher’s explanation, he figured that issues like the one he had mentioned would be on the exam.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, when an issue like that does appear, there is only one course of action for you to take, Kushida-senpai.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yagami went on to explain precisely how she could drive Horikita and Ayanokouji into a corner, should that issue come up.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, what do you think?” he said. “Doesn’t this make the possibility of getting them expelled look much more attainable? Of course, you’ll be reducing your entire class to tears by doing this, but that would be a small matter for you, wouldn’t it?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you… Do you really think I could do something like that?” Kushida asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In my view, you certainly have the capability. Or am I wrong in my assessment of you?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You certainly seem to have a high opinion of me.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I had to test my senpai when we first met, to see if you were someone I could use.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…What do you mean by that?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you remember when I approached you that time? ‘It’s me. Do you not recognize me?’” asked Yagami.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I was flustered,” she said. “What about it?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What about it? Well, normally, anyone would have had their doubts in a situation like that. After all, we were complete strangers then, Kushida-senpai. We hadn’t met before, not even once. Despite that, you immediately navigated the conversation, deftly managing to make your way through by improvising. That was exactly how I came to understand that you, senpai, are someone capable.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But what if I had said, ‘Who are you?’ back then, when we met?” said Kushida. “I might have simply forgotten if we met before.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t think you would have. Because if you didn’t know where we met before, it could have turned out that we went to the same junior high. If that was the case, there’d be a possibility that I knew about your past. I’m sure it would’ve been a big problem for you in the unlikely event I were to say something like, ‘I know about </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">that</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> incident’ to you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yagami was implying that in order to reject the possibility of something like that happening, Kushida immediately improvised back then to go along with the flow of conversation.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If I wasn’t someone who attended your junior high, I could, for example, have gone to the same cram school. Or I could have been someone younger than you who just happened to live in your neighborhood. If you found out that it was one of those things later, then the risk of me being someone who knew about your past would be greatly reduced and you could laugh things off as a simple misunderstanding. Your first priority with any new person is to make sure they didn’t go to your junior high school, right? That way, even if someone were to bring up a topic even only tangentially related to your past, it would be easier for you to divert the conversation away from it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Having finished his explanation, Yagami drank about a quarter of the tea in the cup and set it down on the table.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Who even are you, anyway?” asked Kushida quizzically. “How do you know about my past if we didn’t even go to the same school…?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I understand why you are so guarded, but please, just consider me a guest in a rather unique position,” Yagami said. “Yes, just so. My purpose is to play with Ayanokouji-senpai.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? To play with him?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, well, I don’t think he knows about me at all. Right now, what I’m most preoccupied with is testing out various things without Ayanokouji-senpai noticing.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What if I got upset back then, when we first met? If I didn’t give you the answers you wanted?” asked Kushida, curious about what Yagami would have said at the time.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I would’ve found it interesting,” he replied. “I am sure that Ayanokouji-senpai would have noticed your discomfort, saw that something was off, and he would have eyed me with suspicion. I probably could have </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">greeted</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> him at a much earlier stage, I suppose.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Did you and Ayanokouji go to the same junior high, by any chance?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, who can say? Anyway, that’s of little concern to you, Kushida-senpai. Let’s turn our attention to the special exam for now, shall we?” said Yagami, redirecting the conversation.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I know. If that issue that you’re hoping for does come up, then…at that time, I’ll try to make a move.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You will </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">try</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> to make a move? That’s not good enough.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Not good enough?” Kushida spat back. “What the heck do you mean, not good enough?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yagami got up and moved in close to Kushida, grabbing her by the shoulders. The young woman reflexively tried to flee.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey! What are you doing?!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida tried to break free from his grasp, but Yagami was stronger than she imagined he’d be despite his slender build. She was utterly unable to move.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Please listen very carefully,” he told her. “You are in far more dire straits than you think you are, Kushida-senpai. Not only do you have people near you like Ayanokouji-senpai and Horikita-senpai to worry about, but you have other causes for concern as well. There are others whose very presence continues to pose a threat to your safe daily life, such as myself and Amasawa-san… Isn’t that right?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s…true…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As Yagami looked straight into her eyes, Kushida glared directly back at him without an ounce of fear.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It goes without saying, but it’s no easy feat to get one of your classmates kicked out of this school,” he went&#160;on. “It’s also quite a challenge to drive someone toward expulsion in the course of ordinary daily routine. If you have the chance to expel them through a special exam like this one, then, without a doubt, a golden opportunity has fallen into your lap,.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I understand that,” Kushida said. “But if I push too far, I’ll be putting myself in danger too.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That means you just need to be prepared for that,” Yagami said. “Either you remove them, or you’ll be removed yourself.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He was putting Kushida under great pressure. It was essentially kill or be killed. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course, the decision is yours to make, Kushida-senpai. Anyway, if I were to say something like, ‘If&#160;you don’t want everybody to find out about the way you caused your old class to fall apart, then get either Horikita-senpai or Ayanokouji-senpai expelled…’ Well, that would be nothing short of a threat, and thus a violation of the rules of the exam.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’d say what you just said right now sounds like a threat,” muttered Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Please pardon my rudeness,” said Yagami. “I didn’t intend to threaten you. I just think the truth is that you’re not prepared enough for this, Kushida-senpai. Be prepared to eliminate them no matter the sacrifice. If you can’t do at least that much, you will never, ever be able to drive them to expulsion.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Then, removing his hands from her shoulders, Yagami stepped away and sat back down.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Let me ask you once more,” he said. “You wish to have those two expelled. Is that right?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When he looked into Kushida’s eyes once again, she looked back at him with a mixture of anger and frustration. She didn’t even have to speak her answer aloud. She had been wishing for that every single day for the past year and a half, almost like she were trying to place a curse on them.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes,” she replied at last. “I want to have Horikita and Ayanokouji expelled. I </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">will</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> definitely get them expelled…!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wonderful. I can feel it,” said Yagami. “I’m finally able to confirm that your convictions are indeed genuine, Kushida-senpai.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida had made up her mind: in order to prevent her wound from opening up any further, she needed to have Horikita and Ayanokouji expelled from school as soon as possible. And as for Yagami, who had been running his mouth freely…she would have to get him expelled as well.</span></p>
