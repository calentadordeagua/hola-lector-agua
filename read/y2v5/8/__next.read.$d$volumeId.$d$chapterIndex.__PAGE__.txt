<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Physical ability was very necessary in this battle that was shortly to come, just as Chabashira herself said moments ago. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Following that, the Advanced Nurturing High School will hold its first-ever cultural festival in November,” Chabashira went on. “As with the sports festival, we will provide you with detailed information about the event shortly. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That meant, most likely, that September would mostly be spent preparing for the sports festival, probably in the form of a few more hours spent in physical education classes each week. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In addition, of course, you will have a mid-term test and a final test in between these events,” added Chabashira.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.00em;">Cultural Festival Overview</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Each second-year class will be given 5,000 Private Points per student to be used solely for festival preparations. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Start-up funds and additional funds will not be reflected in final sales totals. Any funds that are not used must be returned in the end.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">100 Class Points will be awarded to classes in first through fourth place. 50 Class Points will be awarded to classes in fifth through eighth place. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Looking at the rewards, we had a chance to get a decent number of Class Points for placing high, and there weren’t any penalties for ranking low. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It is entirely possible that there could be conflicts with other classes and grade levels over stall locations, but only one class is allowed to use a space in each location,” Chabashira explained. “In that case, there will be a bidding process, and the class with the higher bid will earn the right to use that stall space.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Meaning that if we were forced to use more of our points thanks to a bidding war over securing the rights to a prime location, the budget we could allocate toward the actual program or goods for our stall would be greatly reduced. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The school will neither publicly disclose what each class will be doing nor where their stalls will be located. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You may also find that there are additional things you’ll need, from time to time. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s all for the cultural festival and its rules,” Chabashira said. “Major preparations and the set-up period will come after the sports festival concludes, but starting today, please begin to discuss what you’ll be doing and how you’ll allocate your budget on your own time.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The more time that we devoted to the cultural festival, the more prepared we’d be.</span></p>

I found it likely that a number of students here had experienced a cultural festival before in their junior high days. Since I didn’t have any information in particular about it, I decided to just be a listener, as usual.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yousuke had gotten permission to use the monitor in the classroom. “First, let’s start by making a brief list of options that come to mind easily,” he said, typing on his tablet.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“When people think of cultural festivals, the things they typically expect to see are food stalls and haunted houses, that sort of thing,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yousuke started adding these most obvious examples to a list, one by one: food-related options, a haunted house, a maze, a café, live music, a play, and so on.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The event will be held from ten o’clock in the morning to three o’clock in the afternoon,” he said. “As for food and beverages, adults will be coming as well, so we’re anticipating that we’ll have to be prepared for that. However, if we do so, we might see more competition in that regard…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The rest is just a matter of budgeting things well,” said Horikita. “Compared to a haunted house or a maze where we can keep costs down after we’ve simply constructed what we need, the cost of offering food will inevitably be higher.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Some items, such as music equipment, could be rented for a fee. We could use those for whatever we decided to do, like if we did a live musical performance. However, they were limited in number, so they were first come, first served. There was also the question of how many students were even skilled enough to actually turn a profit.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We have thirty-nine people in our class,” Yousuke continued. “Meaning, our budget as it stands now would be 195,000 points. To be perfectly honest, that’s not enough. With that kind of money, even if we said we were going to make food, I don’t feel we can simply snap our fingers and decide that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I have a suggestion. May I?” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course. Your opinion is welcome, Horikita-san,” replied Yousuke.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“As you’ve said, Hirata-kun, our budget for the cultural festival is limited. However, no matter how much we argue about the options listed, there are many things that we still don’t know. If we decided to cook takoyaki at a food stall, we would need answers to a lot of questions, like what ingredients to use, how good our classmates are at cooking, and so on. In that case, I think that the first thing we should do is repeatedly present our ideas to the class and test them, even if we have to use our own Private Points to do so.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Many students nodded in agreement with Horikita’s suggestion. It was honestly important that we tested things out no matter what we decided to do, whether it was a food stall or something else. That was certainly true. Of course, there was a risk that we’d end up having to pay out of our own pockets to do that, but it was simple to just dismiss that as a necessary upfront investment.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But… Well, I’m not trying to say that I think it’s a bad proposal,” Matsushita started, “but don’t you think some people might be reluctant to try anything if they’re going to have to pay themselves?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Matsushita feared that in that case, some students might just decide to leave the planning to the rest of the class and not help with the cultural festival.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s fine,” said Horikita. “I don’t want to waste my time with just any random idea. But that being said, we mustn’t disregard anyone trying their best to contribute. If anyone thinks of an idea that sounds like it could be the right one, they should actively try and present it. What if we pay out a reward to whoever proposes an idea that we end up using?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, that’s a great idea,” agreed Matsushita. “There’s nothing wrong with rewarding people for their hard work and giving something back.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We can work out the specifics of the rewards later, but to give an example, if we’re awarded 100 Class Points via the cultural festival, that would mean our entire class will get 390,000 Private Points per month. We can divide that by the number of people who proposed an idea and give it out later. That way, there shouldn’t be any complaints.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hypothetically, if we had five activities or events, that would mean 78,000 points per person. If the number of people who proposed ideas and the number of collaborators was too large for it to be profitable for us to divide the total amount between them, I figured it would still be acceptable even if the total amount was spread out over two or three months. That way, students who had actively contributed to the cultural festival would gain something, and those who cut corners would get some benefit later on too. Ultimately, if our Class Points were going to be increased, there was no reason to object.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Also, we need to be very thorough in withholding information so that no one steals our ideas,” added Horikita. “Be very careful about what you say, wherever you say it, whether you’re in school, in the dorms, or at Keyaki Mall.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Strict confidentiality. Over these next two months of preparations, that would be of the utmost importance. From here onward, these discussions would continue, and the class’s first step would be for people to present their ideas to either Horikita or Yousuke. If there was a possibility that the ideas presented could be adopted, we could proceed to the next steps.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"></div>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">2.3</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">F</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">OR THE FOLLOWING</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> two weeks or so, our lives at school carried on as usual. We spent every day working hard in class while preparing for both the cultural festival and sports festival in parallel. It was a precious time when we could say that we were just going through normal, routine, everyday lives at an ordinary high school. Surprisingly enough, news about my relationship with Kei didn’t spread past Sudou, and there weren’t any signs that anyone else had found out about us.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Then, in mid-September, on the Wednesday of the third week of the month, something happened after class. As I was sitting in the back of the room, someone came into view. It was unusual for this certain someone to reach out to Horikita, but that’s exactly what was happening. She came up to her as she sat in the middle of the front row.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Um, excuse me, Horikita-san?” Satou called to her, sounding a little shy and reserved. “If you don’t mind, could I have a minute of your time after class?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Satou was one of the girls who had </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">never</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> interacted with Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I need to go to the student council in about an hour to take care of some business,” Horikita replied, “but as long it doesn’t conflict with that, I don’t mind. What is&#160;it?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She seemed puzzled, but she also had never been approached by Satou before. After Horikita’s slightly perplexed response, Satou spoke up once more, somewhat meekly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, about ideas for the cultural festival, we’ve been thinking a lot about it, and um… Well, you said before, um, that you wanted us to come talk to you if we came up with anything, right?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. I did say that we’re open to presentations…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, that, exactly, let me make a presentation,” said Satou. “I’ve come up with an idea that can seriously win us this cultural festival.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Satou’s confidence was showing, but Horikita wasn’t going to be so easily impressed. It was little wonder why, though; over the past ten days or so, more than a few students had brought their ideas to Horikita. Both guys and girls had made proposals to her because of the rewards they would receive if their ideas were adopted. The ideas presented were varied and ran the gamut from the mundane to the bizarre, but what they all shared in common was one thing: Horikita wasn’t going to take anything you said seriously if you just randomly threw out the name of an idea and nothing more.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">On the day that Horikita made a statement about offering rewards to anyone whose idea was accepted, Hondou immediately proposed that we sell karaage fried chicken because it was delicious. However, Horikita turned him away, telling him to go write up a detailed proposal first. She was unwilling to accept a vague idea thrown at her with no additional information. Though Hondou was discouraged after being told off, he submitted a detailed proposal document the next day. Unfortunately, all it contained was a recipe he probably just pulled from the internet and an impassioned statement about how much it would sell and how delicious it would be.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Upon seeing Hondou’s low-level proposal, Horikita reiterated the importance of the documentation. For example, if we were going to operate a karaage fried chicken stand, how much would it cost? What location should we use? How many people would be needed to run it? How much would we sell the chicken for, how many customers could we expect, and what was the basis for those estimations? Horikita also rather bluntly declared that she would only listen to those who had gotten their ideas in proper order. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After that, it felt like the number of people who approached Horikita casually with hasty proposals ought to have decreased dramatically, but surprisingly enough, the number of students who came to her with proposal documents actually </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">increased</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> day by day. Several ideas had actually made their way onto Horikita’s list of things to consider for the festival. However, none of the proposals thus far had been particularly decisive, so nothing had been officially adopted yet.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well then, let’s see your proposal,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, um, yes. I do have a presentation prepared, but… I can’t quite show you here,” Satou said. “If possible, could I have a bit of your time, just a little while from now?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is that so? Well, all right. Where do you want me to&#160;go?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, um, right… There’s an empty classroom in the special building, please be there in thirty minutes. I got permission from the teacher in advance,” said Satou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“An empty classroom?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Though Horikita sounded perplexed, Satou simply said, “That’s right, see you there!” before she turned and walked away. Then, Satou met my gaze as I was watching this happen, and she walked over to approach me as well.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, um, Ayanokouji-kun, do you have some free time today?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Me? I don’t have any plans in particular, no,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You heard what I was talking about with Horikita-san earlier, right? I was wondering if you could come with her. Thirty minutes from now.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why me, though?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s a secret for now. If you come, you’ll understand.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Just like when she was speaking to Horikita earlier, Satou’s confidence was visible on her face. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay, I’ll be waiting!” she said to me. Then, after checking the time on her phone, Satou hurried out of the classroom.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What is up with her, I wonder? She certainly seemed pretty confident,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Maybe that just means she’s come up with something special?” I mused.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even so, I’m not sure why we’re going through all this hassle.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wasn’t sure what it was all supposed to mean either, but we would find out in another thirty minutes. Horikita and I decided to kill some time in the classroom before heading over to the special building.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"></div>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">2.4</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">S</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">INCE HORIKITA AND</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;"> I</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> were heading to the same place, we walked over to the special building together. When we arrived outside the classroom that Satou had told us to go to, Maezono was there for some reason.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah, I’m standing watch,” she said. “We didn’t think that anyone would come here after class, but still, figured we’d do this just in case…you know.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Standing watch…?” Horikita repeated. “This is far more elaborate than I thought.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita seemed surprised that they had gone so far as adopting security measures, even though that was something of a prerequisite if we wanted to keep our ideas hidden until the day of the festival. People wanted to keep others from finding out what classes from which grades were doing what. I was surprised too, though. Not only had they asked the teacher to borrow a room in the special building, but they even assigned someone to stand watch to prevent interference from third parties. Moreover, they had even gone as far as sealing up the windows, albeit somewhat simply. You could not see into the room from outside.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well then, let’s hurry on inside and have a look,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, please wait a minute,” said Maezono. “From here onward, it’s going to be a trial run, as if it were really operating. That way, you can experience it for yourselves as if you were customers, Horikita-san, Ayanokouji-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So that’s what’s going on,” said Horikita. “All right. I&#160;suppose this is much easier to understand than reading through some hastily-thrown-together proposals.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After seeing how elaborate this whole production was so far, Horikita’s expectations had undeniably been raised, whether she liked it or not. Whether she would end up adopting this idea for the cultural festival was another matter, but at this point, it was clear that they were putting in a serious effort to win this. I was sure that must have made Horikita happy. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After the two of us once again made sure that there wasn’t anyone else in the vicinity, we slowly opened the door. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The first things that caught my eye were the unexpectedly vibrant colors inside. The decorations that they had put up were so bright and colorful that it made it hard to believe this was just another sterile, nondescript classroom.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Th-this is…” stammered Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Welcome to Maid Café Maimai!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Three girls greeted us in unison, each of them in a unique costume. Satou, the one who had called us here, and Matsushita, standing next to her, were both dressed in maid costumes. Mii-chan, though, averted her eyes from us shyly and was dressed in a cheongsam. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Although monitors were typically installed in the classrooms, it seemed like the classrooms in the rarely used special building still came equipped with whiteboards. It seemed like they had put it to good use and the name of their café was written out adorably in marker. We were shown to our seats and handed a menu of homemade items.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What would you care to order, Master? Mistress?” asked Satou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Please hold on a minute. May I ask you something before we order?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? What is it?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Surely it must have taken quite a bit of time and money for you to put this all together?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It certainly looked like it would’ve been difficult to arrange all this in just a day if you had to, that was for sure. Even if they worked hard to get all the decorations up, what about their costumes?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Satou turned to Matsushita. “How long did it take us again, Matsushita-san?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“About four days of preparation time,” Matsushita replied. “I think the costs were surprisingly reasonable, actually. All in all, we spent about 13,200 Private Points. The four of us planned it together—us three here and Maezono-san outside. We divided the costs amongst the four of us, so it was roughly 3,300 points per person. As&#160;for what specifically we spent those points on, it was our three rental outfits, and then the items we used to put together the decorations, like origami paper and markers. We picked those up from the general store. All of the tableware here actually comes from our own personal belongings, so there was no cost for that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Interesting.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> I supposed that was the reason why there was a lack of unifying cohesiveness to the tableware. Of&#160;course, since we were only in the planning stages now, that wasn’t a negative factor. In fact, I was genuinely impressed by how well they had prepared for the event while keeping their costs to a minimum. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The impact was perfect. Better than any other proposal I’ve seen thus far. But…” Horikita trailed off.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She offered high praise for how thoroughly they had prepared for this, seeing as they had generally prepared everything. However, Horikita wasn’t so simple as to decide just from that.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The main question I have is, have you prepared an overall budget for this? I’d like to see what specific processes you have in mind,” said Horikita sharply.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Satou didn’t panic in the slightest, however, and turned to look at Mii-chan.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Um, well, we’ve tried to put as much as we possibly could into our proposal documentation,” Mii-chan said, taking a clear file folder from her bag and handing it over to Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wondered if Mii-chan had been the one to write it out. There were three pages in the folder, packed with details written in beautiful handwriting. Matsushita had mentioned that the costumes they had were rented, but it looked like they received quotes from three different companies and rented one outfit from each. There were comparisons in price, quality, and selection. The proposal also contained the costs for tableware that they would use during the cultural festival, including lower and higher cost options. There were also details on the approximate number of guests expected as a criterion, differences between that number and maximum guest capacity, and so on.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This is far and away more complete than any other proposal I’ve seen so far,” said Horikita. “Well done.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hearing Horikita’s genuine compliments, Satou and Matsushita turned to Mii-chan, poking her in the side and telling her that she was being praised. Mii-chan, though looking as embarrassed as she’d likely be on the day of the festival, gently made a grateful bow to Horikita. You could say that this proposal from Satou and the other girls had earned a perfect score. However…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It certainly is an interesting idea,” Horikita said. “Though you could hardly say such things are uncommon, I feel that it has potential, especially if the experience is well put together. However, there are some downsides. The costume rental fee is 4,000 points per outfit. If we follow your proposal, it will cost 40,000 points for ten outfits. Aside from that, the estimated cost of preparing beverages and snacks comes to 50,000 points. That’s a total of 90,000… And then there’s the decorations put up in the room, which come to 5,000 points, and then there’s the location cost on top of that, which means… This is by no means an inexpensive plan.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even if we could afford to implement everything without difficulty, especially since we wouldn’t need to pay wages to the people working the café, we’d </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">still</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> be spending half of our current budget on one single idea.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Y-yes, that’s true…b-but I think that we could raise the costs of the items we offer. To compensate!” said Satou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As an example, I saw that on the menu that Satou and the other girls put together, one cup of tea cost 800 points. That was more expensive than getting a drink at the café in Keyaki Mall. Of course, it was conceivable that they could reduce their prices significantly depending on adjustments they made in the future, but it still seemed fair to judge that what they offered had a good chance of selling. Horikita looked quite serious as she reread the three-page proposal documentation. With Satou and the other girls standing around her dressed like something out of a fairy tale, something struck me as strangely out of place or unrealistic about the whole situation. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita finally looked up, having perhaps reached a conclusion.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Just double-checking, but…no one else has seen this presentation yet, have they?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course,” replied Matsushita with a nod, her confidence showing. “We haven’t let anything slip.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Satou and Mii-chan followed suit, nodding as well. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Very well. I will be taking serious steps to help ensure that this maid café proposal passes then. Would you be willing to further scrutinize your proposal, including a thorough cost-reduction?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Really?! Yay!” shouted Satou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The three girls gleefully exchanged high fives, each of them overjoyed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s too early to celebrate. Don’t forget that we’re only taking it under positive consideration for now,” warned Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Though that’s what Horikita said, the fact the girls had gotten Horikita to pledge that she’d take care of things to help get the idea approved was a huge gain for them. Once Horikita and I stepped back out into the hallway, Maezono was still there standing watch and waved to us happily. I was sure she was looking so pleased because she had heard the commotion from inside the room.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You sure seem to have a high opinion of what you just saw,” I said to Horikita. “I never expected you to come out and say that you’d take care of things for them.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wouldn’t just casually accept something if I thought we had no chance of winning with it,” she replied. “To tell you the truth, the majority of proposals that had been brought to me were rejected right on the spot, or simply put on hold at best. The fact I said what I did is just a reflection of how strong their idea is.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I figured that the idea of a maid café in itself was likely not that unusual. However, it seemed that Horikita was willing to cooperate with them because she saw the potential to fully demonstrate the strengths of our class and to do something that would impress visitors. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Supposing for the sake of argument that another class were to run a maid café,” I began, “do you think we’d still be able to win?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, I do. You don’t think so?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I don’t know.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If we just thoughtlessly opened up a food stand, we’d likely have several rivals. On the other hand, if we ran a maid café, even if we had one or two competitors, it was possible we could outdo them by using the assets we had at our disposal. Aside from those three girls back there wearing sample outfits, there might very well be some other hidden talents lying dormant in our class.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What are you talking about? I’m going to need your help too in order to make sure their idea becomes a solid success,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“My help?” I blinked. “Wait, you’re not asking </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">me</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> to dress up too, are you?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What kind of stupid nonsense is that? If I’m doing this, then I’m going to make sure we give it our all. So, in that case, we need to have the best people for the job, right? So, with that in mind, I think that you, a guy, should be the one to help in that regard.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait, that’s… Well, I don’t really understand what that’s supposed to mean exactly, but…I would think there are others who are better qualified for this.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I suppose you have a point. People like Ike-kun and Hondou-kun might be connoisseurs, of a sort, when it comes to this sort of thing. But I fear that if we told them about this idea, that could lead to information getting leaked. They seem to have loose lips.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I…can’t deny that,” I admitted.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">They were the kind of people who might very well end up carelessly revealing information by mistake. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t want to recklessly increase the number of students in the know here,” Horikita said. “Understand?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, I do.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Maybe it was just sheer bad luck that Satou had asked me to come along. Or maybe things were just destined to turn out this way.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That being said, I’ll be leaving the matter of choosing people to you first,” she went on. “You can tell anyone that you want to bring about this project, of course, but please remember to keep this strictly confidential. If&#160;things go&#160;wrong, this proposal will be off the table.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was just how important it was that we kept this a secret.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Actually, come to think of it… Yes. With that in mind, I’d like to keep information sharing to the absolute minimum. Can I leave this matter entirely to you? We’ll settle on a formal budget at a later date, but I’ll need you to scout for people, handle all preparations, and manage things.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait, wait, hold on,” I protested. “This is a huge jump all of a sudden. You’re </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">really</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> planning on having me handle all of this, alone?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No one is saying that we’re just going to have just one thing for this cultural festival,” she pointed out. “We will definitely have more than one offering, considering our balance of human resources, both men and women. Figuring out a way to increase our profits on a low budget is going to take a lot of work, so </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">I’d</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> like to focus on that aspect.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sure, I wanted to let her concentrate on doing that part of the job, I understood that. But I also had to wonder, why </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">me</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I trust this means you’re accepting my formal offer?” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t remember showing any suggestion at all of having accepted any offer, but the decision had apparently been made for me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Fine, I’ll do it…” I huffed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wondered if it was even possible for me to operate the ideal maid café. I wasn’t very confident. Considering Satou, Matsushita, and Mii-chan were already confirmed to be involved… How many more waitresses did we need? Though the cultural festival was still a way off, it was looking likely that I was going to need to get people together soon.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m going to head over to the student council office,” Horikita announced. “See you later.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Y-yeah…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Having been made to accept a job that made me want to hold my head in my hands in defeat. As I went to leave the building, I happened to cross paths with Chabashira. Considering the location, it seemed unlikely that she was there through sheer coincidence. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You went to see Satou and the others?” she asked. “I&#160;heard about their proposal and what they’re thinking of doing. Not a bad idea.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, I did, and I agree. I figured they needed to make sure their application to be here was approved before they could have even started preparing for a showcase for us, after all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It wouldn’t have been a laughing matter if they had gone through such thorough preparations without knowing whether they had permission to use the building.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I was curious about how it was shaping up myself so I thought I’d come over personally to take a look,” said Chabashira. “How was it?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Good,” I replied. “Horikita is positive about it too. She must have thought we have a chance of winning with it. She’s working out the details now.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. Well then, I suppose that means I don’t need to go out of my way to go see it,” said Chabashira.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I kind of got dragged into this whole thing myself.” I sighed. “It’s turned into a bit of a bother.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Meaning?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Horikita’s instructions were that I was to supervise this project.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You, Ayanokouji? Wow, that’s…” Chabashira trailed off. Then, she smirked at me like she found this whole thing comical. But at the same time, she was giving me a look that seemed to both sympathetic and pitying. “That’s great,” she finished her thought. “Horikita has some rather interesting ideas herself.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I think that people like Ike and the Professor would be far, far better suited for this kind of work though,” I&#160;said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though I had heard of maid cafés, I didn’t know a single thing about what they were supposed to be like.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“When it comes to understanding otaku culture, then sure, you might be right about that,” Chabashira said. “However, what’s important at the cultural festival is sales. Those two might be capable of improving the quality of the attraction, but they likely wouldn’t be good at finer calculations and turning a profit. That is exactly why it makes sense for you to supervise it instead. It’s a problem that can be solved by asking them for their opinions, if necessary.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was easy for her to say. For your opinions to have meaning, you needed to have at least a bare minimum amount of knowledge about the topic at hand. If you just listened to people’s advice while you were in total ignorance on a subject, there was no guarantee that you’d arrive at the correct answer. At the same time, it would be difficult for you to point out anything that might be wrong.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Just brace yourself and think of it as an opportunity to learn about something outside of typical academic fields, Mr. Maid Café Manager,” Chabashira teased.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I suppose…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Just as I was leaving, Chabashira called out to me once more from behind.</span></p>
