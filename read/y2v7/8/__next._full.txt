<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 2:<br/>Getting Ready for the Cultural Festival</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">T WAS MONDAY</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> November 1</span><span style=" font-size: 0.75em; vertical-align: super;">st</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">, the start of chilly fall weather. The days and months had gone by quickly, and in just two more months, we’d be on winter vacation. I&#160;supposed that meant that I wouldn’t have this seat or this view from it for too much longer. The fact that I felt a sense of reluctance to part with it was proof to me that the change in seating was a good system. I didn’t technically know at that moment if there would be another change in seats next year like this, but whatever happened, the view would likely be very different from what I had before.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Seconds after the bell rang, Chabashira-sensei appeared in the classroom.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Good morning. Everyone here?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The students’ chitchatting ceased. They immediately went quiet and faced forward, their eyes and ears focused on the teacher, just like always. This school’s unique systems, where even students’ behavior and attitude outside of class factored into the evaluation of the entire class, resulted in serious and disciplined attitudes among the student body. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Not that anything had changed that much compared to last week, of course. But even so, I could sense that they’d improved even further since then. Seeing her students, who continued to grow and mature day by day, Chabashira-sensei nodded deeply.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sure that your preparations for the Cultural Festival are already well underway,” she told us, “but I have some additional notes to explain. First, I will once again show you an overview of the Cultural Festival as a refresher for anyone who needs to check it over.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.00em;">Cultural Festival Overview</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Each second-year class will be given 5,000 Private Points per student to be used solely for festival preparations. Students will be allowed to spend those available points freely. First-year students will be provided with 5,500 points each for start-up costs, and third-year students will be provided with 4,500 points.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Additional funding may be provided for community service activities, such as student council activities, contributions via club activities, and so on. Further details will be announced to each class after the rules have been finalized.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Start-up funds and additional funds will not be reflected in final sales totals. Any funds that are not used must be returned in the end.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">100 Class Points will be awarded to classes in first through fourth place. 50 Class Points will be awarded to classes in fifth through eighth place. No Class Points will be awarded to classes in ninth through twelfth place. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This is what you have seen before. You should have no problems remembering these guidelines,” said Chabashira-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Since there weren’t any questions from the students, she went on with the explanation. “The details about additional funding have been finalized, so I’d like to announce those now.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Additional funding.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> According to the overview, additional funding could be earned for community service activities and the like. That meant student council activities, club activities, and so on. Apparently, the time had come for us to hear more about that. If your budget wasn’t finalized, it’d be impossible to determine how many activities you could run during the festival, as well as the exact nature and scope of those activities. Despite this inconvenience, it wasn’t much of a problem as long as all classes in all grade levels were faced with the same conditions.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“First, I’ll show you the total additional funding to be given to this class, along with an itemized breakdown of your totals,” announced Chabashira-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She fiddled with her tablet and pulled up a spreadsheet with lists of data. The file showed that a total of twelve people were eligible to receive additional funding.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita Suzune: Student Council Service Bonus – 10,000 Points</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sudou Ken: Club Activity Bonus – 10,000 Points</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Onodera Kayano: Club Activity Bonus – 10,000 Points</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had guessed that 10,000 points was likely the maximum as only three students had been able to gain that degree of additional funding. The nine other students received other sums, ranging from several hundred to several thousand points in recognition of their contributions. For example, Yousuke was going to receive 3,000 points as his club activity bonus, while Akito would receive 1,000. The list mainly showed students in the class who were active in clubs. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In total, this class was going to receive 39,400 points in additional funding.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If we were to contextualize the value of those three big bonuses another way, they would be worth more than six people’s shares of start-up points. This funding was going to be essential in putting together our plans for the Cultural Festival.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I cannot give you an itemized breakdown of what the other classes are receiving, but Sakayanagi’s Class A received 18,800 points, Ryuuen’s Class C received 17,000 points, and Ichinose’s Class D received 26,600 points in additional funding,” Chabashira-sensei said. “In other words, you have the most additional funding out of all the second-year classes.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That also meant that, in terms of funding, Ichinose’s class was in second, and Sakayanagi’s class just barely beat out Ryuuen’s class for third place. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">That puts him last, huh.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> These were unexpected results, to be sure, but I supposed that the student council service bonus might’ve been a contributing factor. It was, simply put, a big deal that both Horikita and Ichinose received 10,000 points each. Other students like Sudou and Onodera were considered to be outstanding contributors to their respective clubs—head and shoulders above the rest of the school, even. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Since we couldn’t use our own personal Private Points for the Cultural Festival, Horikita’s class needed to stay within a total budget of 229,400 points if we added up the value of all students’ start-up plus the additional funding. Every single point counted.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, Horikita’s class couldn’t be too proud just from looking at this. Those additional funds would be an advantage in the preparatory stage before the start of the Cultural Festival, but if those funds weren’t reflected in the final sales totals, they’d end up being a waste if they weren’t utilized well. What Chabashira-sensei was showing us was apparently the explanation for additional funding, but that shouldn’t have been all there was to know. Several pieces of information we needed about the Cultural Festival still hadn’t been disclosed to us.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Now then… Next, I’ll share details about guests with you all, which is going to be important as that’s how you’ll be making your sales.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">How many guests would be coming to the Cultural Festival? What kind of people were they? How much money would those guests have on hand? We didn’t have that kind of information yet. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The guests we’ve invited include people who have some connections to the operation of this school, as well as their family members,” explained Chabashira-sensei. “Naturally, this means guests will represent a wide range of age groups—from the elderly, to toddlers, to elementary school students, and so on. It’s also been decided that people who regularly work at Keyaki Mall, the convenience stores, and so on will be invited to attend as guests as well.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Our tablet screens now showed us a graph indicating the number of guests by age. People in their thirties and forties accounted for the greatest percentage of attendees, followed by those under twenty, and then those in their fifties.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Each adult guest will be provided with 10,000 points. Minors will be provided with 5,000 points. There will be 283 adults and 202 minors. There are 485 attendees in all, meaning that the total amount they will have to spend comes out to 3,840,000 points,” explained Chabashira-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This meant that the ranking of classes across all grade levels would depend on how much they could take in sales from that total.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I should also mention that the number of participants includes us as well, meaning your teachers,” Chabashira-sensei added. “Homeroom teachers are restricted from using their points on anything run by classes in the grade level they teach in, but apart from that, they’re treated no differently than any other guest.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I figured it was essential that teachers were prevented from spending their points within the grade level they taught in. After all, as homeroom teachers, they’d inevitably want to spend their money on their own class if they were able to.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is it possible for any of the guests to use more than the 10,000 points of spending money?” asked Ike.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira immediately shook her head. It was a rash question from Ike, like usual, but she didn’t really admonish him in any way when she answered. If anything, she seemed to enjoy the fact that Ike hadn’t changed. “No,” she said. “Guests can only spend the amount they’ve been given. That limit is set in stone.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Guests weren’t provided with unlimited funds, so it wouldn’t be possible to try to hook individual big spenders. Instead, it would be a contest over who could snatch up the most guests. There would inevitably be a battle over customers.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Now, to bring up something important: the payment method,” Chabashira said. “The school will be using a system where payments are handled via a dedicated phone app that allows the school to track sales in real time. Please keep in mind that the app will be disabled the moment that the Cultural Festival ends at 4:00 p.m. You’re free to choose when exactly you’ll have customers check out, but we recommend that you have them pay in advance before you provide any goods or services.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If you decided, for example, to have customers pay </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">after</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> eating their meal, then it was possible that 4:00 p.m. could come before they paid. In cases like that, you’d risk not being able to collect those points.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Now that I’ve explained all of that, please raise your hands if you have any questions,” said Chabashira-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita’s hand shot up straight away. “How will the rankings be determined in the event that multiple classes make the same amount in sales? I understand that this is an extreme example, but what would happen if every class got the same amount in sales—320,000 points—and there was a tie?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Since the total amount of available points was neatly divisible, you couldn’t be sure that Horikita’s example wouldn’t happen. If everything proceeded normally, the probability that every class would get the same amount in sales was astronomically small, but it wasn’t impossible that classes would engage in collusive price-fixing. If everyone tied for first was treated equally and granted the same privileges, then everyone could raise their Class Points equally. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I would have assumed that the school would have considered some kind of countermeasure for that, but…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In the event that multiple classes earn the same amount in sales, they will be treated as being tied for the same ranking,” Chabashira-sensei replied. “So, if all twelve classes achieved equal sales numbers like you suggested, Horikita, then all twelve classes would be treated as though they’ve won first place and would all receive 100 Class Points.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Was the school being lax here because classes wouldn’t lose Class Points even if they lost? No, perhaps they determined in the very beginning that it was unlikely that a large number of classes would be tied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“However, total sales will only be confirmed after this ‘exam’ has concluded. Sales manipulation by any third parties are also not allowed,” added Chabashira-sensei. “It is impossible for classes to come together, discuss, and make plans to engage in price-fixing and share sales prior to the festival. Also, it’ll be impossible for classes to arrange to divide their sales up equally after the Cultural Festival concludes. You understand what this means, I&#160;trust?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If sales numbers couldn’t be manipulated after the fact, it was even more unlikely that all the classes would tie for first place. More importantly, though, it was hard to imagine that all classes would come together in a friendly manner at all, giving up a precious opportunity to compete with each other.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But we probably </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">won’t</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> get the same amount in sales, right?” Maezono voiced her doubts, clearly not having understood the reason Horikita had asked that question. “Like, thinking normally? I don’t think we need to worry about it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, if we were fighting normally, then no, we wouldn’t need to worry about that, Maezono-san,” Horikita replied. “But it’s not impractical to find out the rule for such a situation.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita’s statement was also valid. It wasn’t a bad idea to find out, just in case. As for whether or not there would be any sort of price-fixing agreements, that was unclear at the moment. It was possible that certain grades or classes might collude to create a situation where they would get an equal number of sales. There were several possible ways it could be done, but if the classes worked it out in advance so that their final sales numbers of all goods and services added up in such a way that the classes evened out, it wouldn’t be that difficult to create a scenario in which all products being sold equaled the same.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, we also needed to be prepared for acts of betrayal, unforeseen circumstances, and unforeseen trouble. It would be no laughing matter if, even after you prioritized selling out your product, your sales results put you at the bottom of the rankings. The hurdles that you had to overcome in order to intentionally create a tie were much, much higher than one might&#160;have imagined.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Does anyone else have questions?” asked Chabashira-sensei. No one else raised their hands to ask anything further about the Cultural Festival. “Well then, that’s all there is to say about the Cultural Festival. Next, I’ll announce the results of the second semester midterm exams. This time, there was a student whose results surprised even me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She moved on to announcing the results of the written exam. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Miyabi has… I don’t think he’s ever stumbled,” Asahina replied. “Not even once. At least, not as far as I know.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Nagumo’s classmates had a great deal of confidence in his ability to win.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s definitely a fact that Student Council President Nagumo is an outstanding person,” I said. “Even if he were faking the kind of abilities he has, it would be impossible for him to misrepresent his scores in OAA or to become the student council president.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There were more than a few things that couldn’t be managed through political maneuvers alone.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“He likes being number one,” Asahina said. “That’s why he’s been fighting to be on top, even here at this school. He became student council president in the end, so I guess that makes him a man of his word.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But, if you were to ask me if Nagumo was number one, then I’d immediately say no, he’s not,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How could you say that…? He hasn’t really lost to anyone in particular this whole time.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I think it’s because he’s been very fortunate with who he’s been up against.” Nagumo wasn’t weak, not at all. But I didn’t have any doubt that his opponents had been. “I think that perhaps his greatest misfortune was that he didn’t have anyone in his grade level who was his equal or better who was also willing to compete with him.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“A worthy opponent… You mean he didn’t have a rival?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Exactly.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Unfortunately, by only going up against lesser opponents, Nagumo had been able to take and hold onto the number-one spot without much effort. Of course, it was possible he might’ve started at number two or three right from the start, but he soon overtook his opponents and then was far and away in the lead. So, when he turned back to see where his opponents were after finishing the race, he saw that there was no one on his tail. Everyone had given up and either started walking the rest of the way or just stopped completely because they knew they couldn’t beat Nagumo.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There might have been times where people with talent, like Kiryuuin, were lurking nearby, but if those people didn’t try to catch up to and overtake Nagumo, then to him, they wouldn’t be any different from weeds and pebbles on the side of the road. The fact that Nagumo hadn’t experienced the harshness and difficulty of competition or the frustration of losing while he was still young could be the reason his thinking had become so warped. Even in planning and executing this bizarre revenge plot against me, there wasn’t anything like a sense of defeat in it, or an inferiority complex. He was simply fixated on dragging me out into the open. When he told me that he wished to have a one-on-one showdown with me&#160;during the Sports Festival, he never even considered that he could lose.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Of course, there was no arguing with the fact that he didn’t know everything about me, so it was a given he’d think that way. But hypothetically, even if he </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">had</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> seen my full ability in action, he still probably wouldn’t think he could lose. He was a man who had never truly known defeat. That was the downside of continuously experiencing winning streak after winning streak.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wish we could just stop the fighting in this school already,” said Asahina.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I have to wonder about that,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“At least, I hope that nothing happens now…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t think that was likely. Nagumo’s way of thinking had been made clear to everyone, albeit indirectly, via the Cultural Festival. It had blatantly changed. To the casual observer, it would appear that Nagumo’s belligerent side, and his curiosity, had simply died down. However, that wasn’t really the case—this was the calm before the storm. After this, Nagumo was going to come after me… No, not just me. He was going to do something to people aside from me, too, to hold them accountable. Getting one or two people expelled might not be enough for him. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I supposed that this was the bill that had come due—the cost of the disrespect that I had shown Nagumo so far. If I let this bomb go unchecked after it had swelled this much, then those worries would come to pass. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I recalled what Manabu had said.</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;"> “Nagumo’s way of doing things would make a great number of people unhappy.”</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">What he said then was half-true. I wasn’t going to deny that I had played a part in what was happening, but Nagumo’s choice had already been made, and he was smoldering in his feelings and thought processes. But what Manabu said was half wrong too. That was because, through Nagumo’s methods, some of the students who originally wouldn’t have been able to graduate from Class A were indeed getting the chance to do just that. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It wasn’t just the third-year students either. The first-years and second-years were getting the chance too, albeit on a limited basis, with the Class Transfer Tickets. Though there were restrictions in their usage, they didn’t exist during Manabu’s time. If I had been the old me, the version of me that existed through last year, I probably would’ve just stood by and watched what Nagumo was doing.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m starting to get a little interested in Student Council President Nagumo,” I remarked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You heard what I said before, right?” asked Asahina.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I did.” I felt a curiosity welling up from deep inside me, which I hadn’t felt before.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I knew you were strange,” Asahina teased, smiling. Lowering her gaze to her bandaged leg, she let out a small chuckle. “I’m sure that your meeting was a coincidence, but maybe that’s why Miyabi wants to fight you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Thinking back, that “coincidence,” or rather, the result of it, had been a major factor in my meeting Asahina. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Coincidence, huh.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> Just now, in this very conversation, I had come to one logical realization. The coincidence that we had just talked about was something that wasn’t controlled. However, that didn’t necessarily mean that coincidences </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">couldn’t</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> be controlled. They could change form greatly depending on your point of view, your perspective. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Asahina Nazuna and her charm, the existence of coincidence, and Nagumo Miyabi. This wasn’t bad as a single test case. Just like an experiment was a series of failures followed by success.</span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">2.5</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">A</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">FTER LEAVING ASAHINA</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> in the nurse’s office, I returned to the gymnasium. I wanted to check in on Kanzaki since I had been wondering about him—and Ichinose too, who I had expected to have returned. If I drew attention to myself, though, it would probably just be a repeat of what happened before, so I stayed further away from the entrance when I went back there. The fact that I couldn’t see Kanzaki in line made me wonder if he was somewhere inside the attraction, or if he had already&#160;left. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, considering how he had been acting earlier, he would definitely have waited. I couldn’t imagine that Kanzaki, who had been waiting for Ichinose to come back and waiting for me to leave, would have missed what happened earlier. There had been a bit of a commotion when I escorted the injured Asahina out, after all. I had then taken her to the nurse’s office and come back here, so I had been gone for roughly fifteen minutes. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Unless Ichinose had returned immediately after I had left, I expected she’d be inside the gym somewhere, if she were still in the nearby area. As I was making general observations on the situation, I decided to take a closer look at the faces of the students as they were coming out. A few minutes passed, and then Kanzaki slowly emerged from the exit. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">So, he was in the gym after all,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> I thought to myself. </span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I thought for sure that Ichinose would’ve been beside him, but he was alone. He eyed me suspiciously for a few seconds and then approached.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you came back after all,” he said. “I guess her injuries weren’t too serious.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I supposed he was right. If her injuries had been serious, it’d be difficult to imagine that I’d just be standing here casually like this. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m guessing you find it strange that Ichinose isn’t here with me?” he continued.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“To be honest, a little, yeah.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I didn’t call her over because I was concerned about the possibility we’d bump into you as you came back from the nurse’s office,” he explained. “Besides, there are still a few days left for the pre-opening, anyway.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Did that mean that Ichinose would have time to inspect the pre-opening, even if she didn’t rush now? It seemed like what Ichinose’s class would be doing for the festival had been decided then, at least to some extent. If they were still looking for ideas as to what to do, then they would have needed to go ahead and experience the pre-opening as soon as possible, without bothering with anything like engaging with me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’d like to continue our conversation from earlier,” said Kanzaki. “It seems like your class is doing very well.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was clear that he was referring to the series of events around the same times as the Uninhabited Island Special Exam and the Unanimous Special Exam—and if we were going back even a little further still, what happened at the start of our second year too.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s not like we’ve emerged from it all entirely unscathed or anything,” I replied. “Unlike your class, Kanzaki, we’ve lost people. There are negatives that aren’t reflected in Class Points alone.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Your class isn’t the only one facing unseen risks. And taking into account the things that can be seen as a positive, I’d say that there’s a sizable difference between you and us.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t take what he said as envy. Rather, it sounded like Kanzaki’s honest, candid opinion.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“A class like yours will eventually contend with Sakayanagi’s class,” he added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">One thing that caught my attention was Kanzaki’s somewhat farsighted view of his own class. It was like he had taken a step back and observed the big picture.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Have you already given up, then? On trying to make it to Class A?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Maybe so.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">His response was more like an admission than saying no. It wasn’t difficult to guess what was going through his mind. It wasn’t like Ichinose’s class was performing disastrously; they hardly ever lost Class Points from things like tardiness, absence, or behavioral problems because their class was so serious. They also weren’t at much risk of losing a great number of points anywhere because they rarely made big mistakes in the special exams. However, on the other hand, they weren’t blessed with opportunities to make big jumps forward in special exams either.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No one has noticed, but our class is a slowly sinking ship,” he said. “It would be cute if they were simply pretending not to see it, but everyone actually thinks that we aren’t, sincerely.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You alone seem different in that regard, Kanzaki,” I observed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Only until a little while ago. There’s no point in trying to raise the banner of rebellion alone.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Meaning that you’ve given up?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“My class can’t make it to A,” he said, clearly and definitively. “If the possibility of us getting to Class A has been reduced to zero, then the only thing left to do is find another way. If we’re going to sink, we should give as&#160;many people as possible a chance to escape.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Saving up twenty million points and transferring classes?” I guessed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, because Student Council President Nagumo has actually pulled it off and it’s been proven effective. Ichinose has been storing Private Points for some time now. If we increase the percentage saved to the limit, we could transfer at least two or three students over to Class A. In addition, we first learned of the existence of Class&#160;Transfer Tickets at the Sports Festival. It won’t be easy for us to get them, of course, but it’s a genuinely heartening factor to have more options to plan for.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why bother telling me the state of affairs in your class?” I asked. “I can’t imagine that you’re trying to stir up confusion.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why indeed… I don’t really understand what I’m doing myself.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was an uncharacteristic response for him. Even though he gave me that answer, he paused for a moment and began searching for the reason why he told me those things. Perhaps he felt like his previous answer didn’t seem quite right.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I just didn’t have anyone to vent to,” he concluded. “That might be why.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If you had worries about your daily life, then you would share them with those close to you, regardless of whether they were in your class or not. By doing that, you could work toward a resolution. However, when it came to worries about your class, you inevitably had to seek solutions from outside. If you said something like “We&#160;have no other choice but to give up on Class A and transfer classes” to your classmates, it would obviously cause unavoidable discord. It would be impossible to reach a consensus on that point in Ichinose’s class.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You were the only person who came to mind who could understand what I had to say, and who wouldn’t carelessly let anything slip,” he said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It looked like he thought I’d be a good outlet to vent to. That probably wasn’t the only reason, of course. It&#160;seemed like he also bore some resentment toward me since I had a strong influence on Ichinose.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t care about what’s happened between you and Ichinose, nor do I care what kind of relationship you two have,” he said. “Even the fact that you’re such a bad influence on her that we can’t even conduct satisfactory information gathering here is trivial.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s a scathing way of putting it,” I remarked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’ll have to forgive me for that much. I’ve had my fair share of frustration.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He then gave me a gentle wave, signaling that he was leaving. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">The class strategist who had given up on winning…</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> As I looked at his back as he left, I noticed he looked one size smaller than usual. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It might’ve been a little uncouth for me to call over to him and stop him right now, but I couldn’t let Kanzaki walk away as he was right now.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Could I get a minute of your time sometime soon?” I asked him. “I want to talk to you a little more. About the future.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Can we just talk now? You can take as much time as you need if you want to discuss the future.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry, but I want to do some research on what the third-years are doing for the festival at the moment.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Besides, even if we did start our discussion now, I wouldn’t be able to move things forward in any way. In order to discuss what lay ahead, I would need another piece of the puzzle, with which I could get them to take that step toward the future.</span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">2.6</span></span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">O</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">N FRIDAY</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> of the following week, I went to a place I didn’t normally go to in order to meet with a certain student. </span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">My arrival was probably unexpected, even for Nagumo. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“A clear lie.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It could be true.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was engaging in word games a little bit, but Nagumo seemed to realize that pressing the matter any further was pointless.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I got the impression his irritable attitude, like he was tired of dealing with me, was sincere. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If Nagumo couldn’t be bothered to have a conversation with me as an individual, I couldn’t blame him. However, that probably wasn’t what was happening here. If his interest in me had genuinely disappeared completely, then sure, that would be a different story…but I could still see that the spark was there somewhere. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In other words, he was absolutely not going to refuse&#160;me. It was precisely because he had taken some precious time out of his day to meet me here that I could state that so definitively. After a few more moments of silence, Nagumo instructed me to sit. I shifted my chair so we could face each other directly sitting down.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry, I don’t have anything to drink,” said Nagumo.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t mind.”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So…it’s not just Karuizawa who’s an exception, but even Honami?” he asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“As I told you before, it’s all the same, no matter who we’re talking about. Whether you try to expel Kei, or toy with Ichinose, or Horikita, or anyone else for that matter. Even so, I think it would be unwise for you to think that you can rile me up.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Nagumo snickered at that, but then immediately switched to a serious expression.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I take back what I said earlier, about it being a game,” he said. “You’re the only person that Horikita-senpai acknowledged. I’m sure of that now.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m glad. Well then, if you’ll please excuse me,” I said, preparing to leave.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey,” said Nagumo.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is there something else?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I admit that you’ve got a hell of a poker face. I also understand that you negotiated pretty hard to draw me out. So, for once, let me hear what you really think. If&#160;Karuizawa really were to be expelled, would you truly just stand by and watch?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wouldn’t want Kei, or, well, any of my classmates to go away, no matter who we’re talking about. I would resist that happening as much as possible.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s not an answer. What you said just now was about if people left the class. I didn’t sense any worry at all from you when you told me what you’d do if Karuizawa, someone special to you, disappeared.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I turned to look at him. Normally, the answer here would be obvious. I would make a statement like </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“I was just bluffing to keep you from realizing my true feelings,”</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> or something along those lines. But that wouldn’t be the best answer for dealing with Nagumo right now.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If she disappears, she disappears. That’d be the end of her,” I said. “Nothing more, nothing less. In fact, it would make sorting things out easier, so it would be a great help to me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…You have seriously got a screw loose,” muttered Nagumo, and I saw for the first time that he was shaken—or rather, that he couldn’t understand me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll contact you again in due time,” I said. I then exited the student council office, quietly closed the door behind me, and walked off. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Nagumo described me as having a screw loose, but that wasn’t true. From my point of view, a person who made the wrong decision based on temporary feelings was one who didn’t have their head screwed on tight instead. It&#160;was the same whether that person was a stranger, a lover, or a family member. If the time came when that person failed and dropped out, that was the end of it. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Your utmost priority was to protect yourself. That was the undeniable solution.</span></p>
