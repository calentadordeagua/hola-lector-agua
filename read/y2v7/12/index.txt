<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 6:<br/>The Cultural Festival</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">A</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">FTER A LONG PERIOD</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> of preparation, the Cultural Festival was finally here. The festival proper began at nine in the morning and students were required to arrive at school by eight thirty. Furthermore, the school gates were actually open starting at six in the morning, so, if necessary, we could make early morning preparations as&#160;well. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita and I met up in the dormitory lobby bright and early at 6 a.m. so we could head to school together. We were planning to do some final checks before the festival started so there wouldn’t be any issues during the&#160;day. As soon as Horikita and I met up, she turned her attention to a box that I was holding in my hands.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Morning. Could that be the cardboard box that you told me about before?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry that I made you work out a budget for something you hadn’t planned for,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It wasn’t a large sum, so there wasn’t much of an impact. Besides, each second-year student was originally entitled to 5,000 points anyway, so we should be able to use those points freely.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We passed by other students who had the same idea to come early. They ranged from first-years to third-years, though there weren’t that many people overall. We briefly stopped by the classroom to drop off the box I was carrying, and from there, we went over to the maid café.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Did you see the message from Matsushita-san?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, I did,” I replied. “I’m sure it’s got to be especially hard for her, since she was one of the people who worked the hardest getting the maid café up and running to this point.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Early that morning, Matsushita sent us a message and reported that she had to take the day off due to illness.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even so, it was the right decision on her part,” Horikita said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If she only had a slight fever, she might have been able to push through it and force herself to work, but it sounded like she had developed other symptoms, like a cough. Being in that condition, she couldn’t be entrusted with a job that would require her to interact with customers. Even if we tried to reassign Matsushita now, she wasn’t feeling well, so she couldn’t be entrusted with a heavy workload. And on top of that, if her illness spread, it could affect the class even beyond the Cultural Festival.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Besides, we made preparations ahead of time for such situations,” Horikita added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It wasn’t enough just to reassign personnel; you needed to understand where to pull people from to fill in the gaps.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That reminds me,” Horikita said. “Have you heard that rumor going around that it was Hasebe-san and Miyake-kun who let information slip about the maid café?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, more or less. But I suppose we could have predicted from an early stage that something like that might happen.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kei had frequent interactions with the other girls, so I already heard about that from her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, I suppose so… I wonder if I made the right choice in leaving them alone.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Rumors are just rumors,” I told Horikita. “In truth, Haruka and Akito didn’t actually do it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita’s feelings of self-loathing over not being able to help Haruka and Akito were clear in her expression.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You shouldn’t show weakness on your face so easily,” I told her. “It’ll only give your opponents an opening to take advantage of you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even at times like this, you’re so calm. You talk like this is someone else’s issue, even though you’re involved too.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I noticed that Horikita was looking at me, as though she were examining my own face. This period of observation continued for about five or ten seconds, and then I noticed that her expression changed. She now looked puzzled and had a crease between her eyebrows.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s something I want to ask you,” she said. “Do you interact with any first-year students regularly?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“First-years? No, not really. I talk to Nanase and Amasawa every once in a while, but that’s about it.” Since I technically hardly ever approached them myself, I didn’t think it was right to say I interacted with them much. “That’s all you wanted to ask?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, I guess…” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Speaking of, what about you? You talk with some first-years on the student council, right?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well… Yes, I do. I’ve gotten more chances to interact with my juniors now and then.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">About three people had been brought onto the student council this year. For a long time, the only second-year student on the council had been Ichinose. The quality of the members aside, there weren’t enough people serving. The most recent addition to the student council was Horikita, but that was likely a measure to make up for the small number of members. Though there was no limit to the number of people who could be on the student council, generally, there were anywhere from eight to twelve students at any time. Currently, there were three third-year students, two second-year students, and three first-year students. So, I suppose you could say that they were in keeping with how the council had been structured in the past.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“At first, I thought it was a waste of time,” Horikita went on. “With the kind of work I was doing on the student council, I felt like I would rather be in my room, studying, for my own sake. To be honest, even now, that feeling hasn’t changed.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It wasn’t just student council work that could be thought of like that. Whether you were talking about club activities or a social life, those kinds of things were, fundamentally, a waste of time. While some people might go from being a club member to a professional, and others might turn their social connections into job opportunities, for many people, those kinds of things would become nothing more than memories of bygone&#160;days. On the other hand, though, if you dedicated yourself to your studies, that was likely to lead you to a great future. It was the safest and most reliable option that a student could take. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But even when it comes to things like that, there are lots of things that you can learn,” she said. “I’m beginning to understand that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Your older brother was the student council president too,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“My brother’s situation was different from mine though. He was able to perform his student council duties flawlessly, while getting impeccable grades in school at the same time. I don’t think that he ever felt that the student council was a burden, nor did he ever agonize over whether he was lacking in his studies.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">While I didn’t know what the truth was, Horikita Manabu certainly was calm and composed at all times. I&#160;had to assume he must have put a significant amount of effort into what he did, but he didn’t let it show.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I suppose I am grateful to you, though, if we just consider the outcome,” Horikita said. “Joining the student council has helped me to see things that I couldn’t before.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had thought that she was going to thank me for it, but then she continued.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ve once again been reminded of my brother’s greatness. I have a lot of extra work to do if I want to catch up to that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It would’ve been nice if you just honestly said thank you,” I answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You need to accept at least </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">some</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> grumbling from me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I agree and sympathize with you, though, that it must be a challenge to follow in Manabu’s footsteps.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I knew I was in no way inferior to Manabu in terms of pure academic and physical ability. It was just a hypothetical situation, of course, but if Manabu were in my same grade, taking into consideration the rules here at this school, then there was no telling what kind of fight there might’ve been between us. His abilities certainly gave me that impression. </span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">6.1</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">A</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">S NINE O’CLOCK</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> rolled around in the maid café in the special building, an announcement was made to all of the students throughout the school: the guests had entered through the main gate and the Cultural Festival was officially beginning. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I overheard a conversation between Shinohara and Ike, who were standing shoulder-to-shoulder.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh jeez,” Shinohara said, “I’m so nervous…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, this is the first time we’ve had any contact with people outside the school since we started here,” said Ike.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I suppose that the longer you spent in a closed-off environment, the more tense you might feel about it being opened up. As for Satou and the other maids, they were still discussing the shift changes needed due to Matsushita’s absence. Although each of the girls was going to have more responsibilities now, they made the appropriate adjustments to their schedules. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Dressed in her maid outfit, Satou held her hands together anxiously, but then proceeded to smack both of her cheeks with her palms to regain her confidence.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Let’s give it our best… You can do it, me!” she told herself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kei, who was going to be helping out behind the scenes, encouraged her cheerfully. “You’ll be fine, Maya-chan. I’ll&#160;be sure to back you up too.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, I’ll give it my best!” replied Satou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ever since that big turning point, Kei and Satou really had gotten much closer. Nothing in the coming days was likely to challenge their relationship as best friends. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The other people I needed to worry about, though, were…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I looked around to observe some of the other students. Sudou and some of the guys on the boys’ team were having their final meeting with Yousuke and weren’t really bothering to listen to the announcements. They needed to all be on the same page about what to do in the event that the café got too crowded, or if there was trouble. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After Horikita gave out some general instructions to the class, we noticed that we were short two people. Immediately after she finished talking, the two of us exchanged looks. We were likely thinking the same thing. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She came up to me and whispered in a hushed voice. “Hasebe-san and Miyake-kun don’t seem to be here.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I doubt they just went to the bathroom either,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Perhaps it was because the other students were too preoccupied with what they were doing themselves, but no one else seemed to have noticed yet.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I expected something would happen today,” Horikita said, “but…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If they’re just skipping, then that actually might be something to be grateful for,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita hadn’t counted on them being involved from the very beginning. So, from where she sat, there was no need for her to get worked up about those two simply not helping out. However, if they were going to try to sabotage us, that would be a different story. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Them being absent will add fuel to the fire of that rumor…” she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sure, if they leaked that information and then skipped out on the Cultural Festival afterward, I suppose they’d take a fair amount of heat for it,” I agreed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ve been keeping an eye on the situation so far, thinking that time would tell, but…I still think I should take action sooner rather than later. We should at least dispel the rumor.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I understand what you’re trying to say, but you should focus on the Cultural Festival today.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Will that really be okay?” she asked me in return.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even if you did make that rumor go away, that doesn’t change the fact that those two aren’t here. Besides, there’s still the possibility they could embarrass the class in some other way during the festival.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If Horikita carelessly acted and tried to rush to their defense while there were still causes for concern, she’d risk stirring up more animosity. Taking Haruka’s side would only be possible when it could be clearly determined that she and Akito weren’t enemies.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…I suppose you’re right,” Horikita conceded, with a look of painful reluctance on her face. She cleared her throat, as though trying to dispel her concerns. “I’m going to trust that you’ll be able to handle the matter of Hasebe-san and Miyake-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I answered her with a silent glance before turning to greet our guests.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The flyers that Horikita’s class and Ryuuen’s class had made would quickly circulate throughout the school. Now, the entire school would know that Class 2-B and Class 2-C were engaged in a head-to-head contest. This&#160;way, people would inevitably assume that both sides were making significant wagers. If guests knew about the competition between us, they would also be under the illusion that both classes were facing similar risks. There wasn’t any need for me to deliberately threaten my classmates with expulsion.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Now, go ahead and call all the girls who aren’t busy and have them pass these flyers out,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“G-got it! We’ll let them know!” said Hondou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Now, the plan was that Hondou and the other guys would run over and pass the information on to our other classmates directly. Then, in addition to handing out flyers that I had arranged in advance, they were also to notify the boys running the food stalls so that they would know what was going on too.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, did you hear? They say that Horikita’s class&#160;and Ryuuen’s class are having some kind of showdown, and&#160;they’ve playing for major points.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I heard that the leader of the losing class will even get expelled, yeah?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It seemed that news had even reached the ears of ordinary students who had nothing to do with this showdown. They were starting to hear about this competition we had going on. Speculation led to rumors, and rumors led to speculation.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m going to head back now,” I declared. “Let me know if you hear anything else.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ike and the other guys were in the sort of position where they could always keep an eye out for changes in the situation, since they were the ones delivering food. They nodded, a dependable air about them, and I left the matter in their hands as I headed back to the special building. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">On my way there though, I spotted a girl clad in Japanese-style clothing standing in the corner of the mostly empty corridor, with a stack of flyers in hand.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Come on over,” she said flatly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The way she passed out the flyers to adults that occasionally walked past her reminded me of the emotionally dead adults that I sometimes spotted at Keyaki Mall, mechanically handing out tissues to passersby. This girl was acting much the same way, simply handing out a predetermined number of flyers in a completely indifferent, uninterested manner.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Can I have one?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Here,” she answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wondered if she even noticed that it was me. She muttered what I </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">thought</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> sounded like a quiet thank-you (perhaps?) as she handed me the flyer. But when I took it from her, she finally took a look at me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ugh.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I never imagined you’d be handing out flyers like this, Ibuki,” I commented.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Shut up. Go away.” She shot me a glare and a disgusted look, as though she were being looked at by someone she absolutely did not want to see her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You know, I caught wind of the story, but wow, I guess you really are a woman of her word after all, huh.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I’d heard that she agreed to dress up in a traditional outfit after losing a bet with Ryuuen. It actually looked better on her than I had thought it would.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I guess it’s true what they say,” I added. “You know, that one proverb about clothes on a horse?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She glared at me furiously, but she didn’t seem to actually understand what I meant by that, which was a relief.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Never mind,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Trying to pass out all of your flyers wasn’t going to be easy if you were doing it in a place where there weren’t many people around.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Maybe you ought to head somewhere else?” I suggested. “I saw Yamashita and the others handing out flyers over there.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You must be joking,” she replied. “Why should I have to work with </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">them</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I figured Ibuki would say as much. She immediately rejected my proposal.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Can’t you just take all these from me?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s a pretty big ask,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’d rather just toss these damn things out, stuff ’em into a garbage bag or something…” Ibuki cursed as she stared down at the stack of flyers in her hands with clear disdain.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Despite saying that, she still wasn’t throwing them out—probably because she wanted to accept the punishment that came with her loss. If Ibuki kept holding her opponents to things whenever she won a contest but ran away whenever she lost, then there was no way that she’d ever be able to compete with Ryuuen or any other opponent in the future.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What kind of contest did you and Ryuuen have anyway?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I would’ve preferred a one-on-one fistfight, but he suggested we play a card game,” said Ibuki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“A card game? You mean like, poker or that sort of thing?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, something like that, I guess.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t really care about the specifics of how they competed, but the fact that it was proposed by Ryuuen gave me pause. Maybe Ryuuen successfully managed to entrap Ibuki by making that challenge. At any rate, I figured it’d be poor form for me to bother her any further.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll make sure to spread the word about you, that you’re working hard to promote here,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do NOT spread the word. I’ll kick your ass,” she hissed, and quickly thrust her leg out with a sharp kick, causing her costume to flutter. I hurriedly dodged it. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She clicked her tongue. “Tch.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That reminds me,” I said, “I’ve heard that the proper greeting at these sorts of cafés is ‘Welcome home, Master.’ How about you try it out on me?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll say it if you let me kick you in the face,” said Ibuki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Let’s just drop it then,” I conceded.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She raised her leg slightly to threaten to kick me, so I decided to leave, dejected. By the time that I returned to our maid café, the somewhat relaxed vibe from earlier was gone. Now, we were seeing the biggest crowd of the day. Customers had begun to crowd in and form a line. Horikita had also come inside to help with line management and was directing customers.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It seems like flyer distribution has gone off without incident,” she said to me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It has,” I replied. “Starting now, this class and Ryuuen’s class should be starting to leave the others in the dust.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Everything is going as you’ve planned.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wasn’t the one who added a unique flavor to this thing, though.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita and I nodded to one another, and we each headed back to our respective posts.</span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">6.5</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">M</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">AID CAFÉS ARE A SURE BET</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> an easy, tried-and-true idea. The fact that Ryuuen had made our plans known to the school might have worked in our favor rather than against us as there were no classes, except for Ryuuen’s—who opted to try that kind of thing. Also, we were able to attract customers in an effective manner. That in itself was a welcome development, but we were now seeing a problem that we hadn’t encountered during the test run. Thanks to the news about our showdown with Ryuuen’s class being spread, more and more people had been compelled to come here, and now we were finding that we had </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">too many</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> customers.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The space in the classroom was pushed to the limit. If&#160;we tried to cram any more seats into the room, it would only make the situation even more suffocating. Our only choice was to make customers wait, but due to their very nature, maid cafés didn’t exactly have a fast turnover rate. It was fundamentally important that the adults leisurely enjoyed their conversations with students dressed as maids. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We could have considered handing out numbered tickets or something, and asked customers to come back later, but that kind of option wasn’t necessarily a good idea for a festival like this. Suppose that you were a&#160;customer who had 3,000 points to spend. What would you do if you received a number and were asked to come back in an hour? While I’m sure that some guests would be conscientious and come back, I was also sure that most would just patronize another exhibit in the time they had. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Before they knew it, they’d likely end up spending close to all of their points, and since they wouldn’t have enough to spend at the maid café in that case, they’d leave. That was an entirely plausible outcome and was precisely why we wanted customers waiting in line to stay there until they were able to come inside and spend. And, if at all possible, we wanted to be able to take in the points they were thinking of spending elsewhere too.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This isn’t good,” I said. “Some people have gotten tired of waiting and are starting to leave the line.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">A warning light was going off, metaphorically speaking. The prospect of taking a big risk and getting a big return for it was looking dicey. I figured that starting now, we had no other choice but to stop new people from joining the line. However, just as I was about to head to the end of the line, Kushida came over to talk to me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ayanokouji-kun, can I step away from my waitressing duties for a little while?” she asked. “I have an idea.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She must have been concerned about the situation and came to check things out.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What are you planning?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The customers waiting in line are bored, but they’re really interested in the maid café. They’re probably hungry too, so it makes sense that they’d leave.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, I suppose it does.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was just around lunchtime. It was obvious just from a cursory glance at the adults who were in the classroom right now that many of them were here for food and drink. Kushida picked up a bag filled with homemade cookies she’d made which we had been selling as gifts in the maid café and walked out into the hallway. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida turned to the customers who were getting impatient and gave them a big smile. “I’m terribly sorry for the wait, everyone,” she said warmly.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"></div>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span><img alt="" height="100%" id="INTERIORIMAGES_4" src="/hola-lector-agua/images/books/y2v7/OEBPS/Images/INTERIORIMAGES_4.jpg" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style=" top: 0px; vertical-align: top; height: 100%;"/></span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"></div>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She then proceeded to pull cookies out of the bag, giving one to each of the customers waiting in line. One of her intentions here was likely to give the customers a little something to hold them over until they could eat properly, but that probably wasn’t the only reason. Now that the customers had received something from us, they would feel guilty if they were to leave.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Now, if Kushida were to walk away from the customers after that, it probably wouldn’t be too difficult for them to get out of line and leave while shouldering some small feelings of guilt. But she stayed and continued smiling and talking with the people in line. Between that and the cookies, it wouldn’t be so easy for them to leave, even if they were feeling impatient. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There were some disadvantages in having Kushida stay in the hall, but the people who were already sitting in the café were definitely going to be spending at least some money already. For the time being, it was more important for us to keep the money flowing beyond this point. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida could see what was going on better than anyone else, and she also knew how to make the most of her skills. What could she do to get as many people on her side as possible? Actions like being emotionally warm with adults of the opposite sex, carrying on conversations that made them feel happy, and at times, some degree of physical intimacy, like holding their hand. And she didn’t show the slightest apprehension or revulsion over doing those kinds of things.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The other girls in the café had been giving it their best the whole day too, of course, but Kushida was the only one who perfectly embodied all of the necessary elements of being a maid. Even when she had to occasionally take over cashier duties, Kushida barely made any mistakes, not even stumbling over calculations. She hadn’t taken part in any of the practice sessions, so I supposed this must have been what you could call natural talent.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Kushida’s really in her element here, huh,” observed Yousuke. He nodded as he watched Kushida go, seemingly as a sign of respect. “I guess it’s like this is a tailwind for her, since she’s been facing some serious headwinds for a while now. And it’s a boon for Horikita too, since she defended Kushida.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">With Kushida was doing so much today, her classmates had no other choice but to recognize her worth—at least to some extent.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“People tend to resent others easily,” I said, “but on the other hand, they can accept others easily too. People’s evaluations of each other swing back and forth all the time, especially when they’re young. It’s like they’re two sides of the same coin, going from front to back and then back to front again. Still, the more someone flip-flops, the more exhausting it will feel.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even so, I’m okay with that,” said Yousuke. “As long as Kushida-san can fight together with everyone in the class, then that’s fine with me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m genuinely impressed by what I’m seeing. Don’t you think it’s incredible that she can do all of that perfectly, right on the spot?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I think that it’s the cumulation of everything she’s done. Apparently, Kushida-san visited Horikita-san’s room rather late at night several times while we were preparing for the festival. I think they were practicing.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So, in addition to her own natural talents, she really had been hard at work practicing behind the scenes. If&#160;Yousuke’s reading of the situation was correct, then this was another reminder of how valuable Kushida was. It&#160;would also serve as justification for Horikita’s confidence in her. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I headed back to the waiting area, and then I proceeded to drag the camera around for about thirty minutes.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Excuse me, um, Ayanokouji-kun? Where is Kushida-san?” asked Mii-chan. She looked like she was in a bit of a hurry.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Kushida?” I replied. “She should still be out in the hallway, helping with line management.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I knew I told the maids that before, but…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s a customer who wants to take photos with Kushida-san, but we can’t find her.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Wait. Kushida was helping with managing the line, but now she’d disappeared? Yousuke and I immediately took a look in the hallway, and sure enough, Kushida wasn’t there anymore. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yousuke called out to one of the guests in line. “Excuse me, but did you happen to see the girl who was helping manage the line?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, you mean the girl who handed out cookies?” the guest replied. “Some other student came up to talk to her, and then she followed after her. That was about five minutes ago.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What did she look like?” I asked, interrupting the conversation.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Um, lemme think… She had her hair done up like this. Pigtails, I think.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It didn’t seem like Yousuke knew who the person was describing off the top of his head, but I had a pretty&#160;good&#160;idea.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sorry, but I’ll need you to handle things here for a little while,” I told Yousuke. “Ask another maid to take over what Kushida was doing earlier.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The girl the person described was exactly the sort of trouble that no one had expected would occur, and that was why I understood right away it was a problem that I needed to deal with.</span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">6.6</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">T WAS A DIFFICULT TASK</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> to find a specific person at the Cultural Festival, a place where many people of all ages and genders were present. And it was even more difficult to find someone when you couldn’t predict where they might have gone. As I tapped at my phone, I&#160;let out a sigh of admiration at the overwhelming information network I had access to through it. Its speed and precision filled me with awe; I had been able to find out their location within minutes of making the request.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida and the other girl weren’t in some corner of Keyaki Mall, nor were they by the dormitories. Instead, they were in the back of the indoor pool. When I arrived, I saw Kushida standing there, looking rather out of place dressed in her maid outfit. It seemed like she was having a heated conversation with the other girl. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida walked right up to her and raised her voice in anger, albeit in hushed tones. “Look, stop making me repeat mys—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When the other person noticed me standing there, she gestured to Kushida to stop talking.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh…? Why… Why are </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">you</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> here, Ayanokouji-kun…?” asked Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do you mean, why?” I replied. “The star player in our line management program went missing, so I’d obviously come looking for you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Although I assigned a substitute maid to take over line management duties following the example Kushida set, I didn’t know how well she would be able to keep customers interested.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I thought I managed to slip away undetected pretty well. Good work on finding us here, senpai,” said the other girl—Amasawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">From the way she phrased that, it sounded as though Amasawa had been waiting for the moment when she wouldn’t be under my surveillance.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Unfortunately for you, I’m working with someone rather dependable right now,” I told her. “No matter where you go, I’ll find you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Not even Amasawa seemed to have any idea who I was talking about, but she wasn’t asking me who it was either.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I was going to give her right back to you. Isn’t that right?” said Amasawa, turning to Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes,” said Kushida. “She’s exactly right. I feel bad for sneaking out without saying anything, but I wanted to talk to Amasawa-san for a minute too.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, you could have just stood there and chatted,” I said. “There was no reason for you to be gone for ten or twenty minutes,” I answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida knew full well that our top priority was managing the line and making sure customers went to our café. That was exactly why she was the one handling those duties, giving up her waitressing role to do so. She wouldn’t have left her position if it wasn’t something serious.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Whatever the story is between you two, we’re really busy with the Cultural Festival. Can’t you do this another time?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There was no need for them to choose today as the day for this conversation.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You don’t seem even the slightest bit surprised to see me with Kushida-senpai,” Amasawa observed. “Did you know?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No,” I replied. I really hadn’t known that they’d been in close contact with one another before now. “But now I understand everything, after seeing that you got in touch today.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I couldn’t help but come to a certain conclusion from what was happening here. Even the information in my head that felt unnecessary was pointing me in that direction. Why had Kushida been so stubbornly persistent in trying to get me expelled in the Unanimous Exam? Why had she taken such a reckless gamble? But if there was a student from the White Room behind it, forcing her to take that gamble, then it wouldn’t be so strange. It&#160;would also make sense that they were meeting like this now, since people could be traced rather easily during the festival. Kushida’s behavior, rejecting invitations from&#160;her classmates after class and heading off somewhere on her&#160;own, was also consistent with this theory of mine.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll make sure to give Kushida-senpai back to you after,” Amasawa promised. “Can you please give us just a little time? Pretty please?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She still didn’t seem to realize that I already made up my mind.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sorry, Ayanokouji-kun, but could you please let me stay for a bit?” Kushida said. “I really will come back as soon as I can. I need to talk to Amasawa-san.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I understand what you’re saying, but I can’t,” I replied. “This is as far as it goes, Amasawa.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You have such a naughty look in your eyes, senpai. It’s like you’re completely undressing me with your eyes.” Amasawa pressed the tip of her index finger against her lips in a seductive manner, but I knew she didn’t truly have anything sexual on her mind. It was an act to hide her wariness, to hide that she was on her guard now that I had seen through her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Kushida,” I said. “Amasawa and some other person found out about your past and were holding it over you. That was why you caused that incident during the Unanimous Special Exam and forced the whole class into it, just so you could get Horikita or me expelled. Or maybe you had been trying even before that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wh…?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida couldn’t confirm nor deny what I just said, but judging by the look of shock on her face, I was right on target.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Let’s not do this now, senpai,” Amasawa said. “This time is for me and Kushida-senpai, after all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry, but no. Even apart from her work as a maid, Kushida is necessary for the class.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do you mean by that?” asked Amasawa. “It’s not like I’m really doing anything bad.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You might be right about that. But I’m not so sure about the other person.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">At that, Amasawa’s demeanor changed for the first time in our conversation. An unsettling grin came to her face, and she went to Kushida and grabbed her wrist.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey?!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Then, with her right hand on Kushida’s wrist, Amasawa pulled her in close, stood behind her back, and covered Kushida’s mouth with her left hand.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Perhaps you have some idea of who this </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">other</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> person is, senpai?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida knew very well who the other White Room student was, of course. That was exactly why Amasawa had covered her mouth. It was a precaution to prevent her from interrupting and revealing it unexpectedly. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I think that you know, Kushida-senpai,” Amasawa added. “If you say anything, I’ll make sure you’re expelled, okay?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida’s face twisted in pain. Amasawa must have been holding onto her arm pretty tightly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This isn’t like you, Amasawa,” I remarked. “It seems to me like you’ve really been pushed into a corner.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hold on, senpai. I didn’t say anything yet, did I?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Your every action speaks for itself,” I answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida, who was enduring a lot of pain right now, probably wasn’t going to understand the true nature of our conversation. Amasawa herself didn’t know how much I understood either.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Anyway, why don’t we have a little conversation ourselves, hm? Just the two of us, another time?” offered Amasawa. “Please just pretend that you didn’t see anything and go away for now, Ayanokouji-senpai. We’ll be heading back in about ten minutes.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And if I don’t say yes?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Then I might make Kushida-senpai unable to carry out her duties,” said Amasawa, tightening her grip on Kushida’s right arm.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ngh?!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m a cute little girl, but I can easily break an arm or two,” said Amasawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay then, let’s put it to the test,” I said. “Let’s see if you can break Kushida’s arm first, or if I can stop you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The distance between me and her was roughly five meters.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you serious?” asked Amasawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, are you serious about breaking her arm? Or do you think that I can’t stop you?” I asked in return.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes to both.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, you’re wrong about both. Let’s do this.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Amasawa, smiling, loosened her grip on Kushida, albeit only ever so slightly. At that exact moment, I kicked off the ground and dove toward her. Just as she was about to adjust her grip again and start trying to break Kushida’s arm, I slipped in between them. My right hand slid down Kushida’s arm to her wrist, and then, as Amasawa pulled her left hand back from Kushida’s mouth and was bringing it behind her back, I grabbed hold of Amasawa’s right arm.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No wa—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Her defensive instincts must have kicked in. In a split second, she gave up on Kushida’s arm, shifted her attention to me, and clenched her left hand into a fist. However, I didn’t give her the chance to do anything. I&#160;quickly moved behind Amasawa and pinned her against the ground, holding her down the way Amasawa had been doing to Kushida moments earlier.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Gah!” she spat.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The momentum from being shoved down to the ground knocked the air out of Amasawa for a moment and she started gasping. Her labored breathing caused some dust particles to float in the air.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh… Wow… That was a little unexpected…” she managed to say.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Did you think there wasn’t much of a difference between you and me?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I could tell that she thought that just by looking at her. Amasawa normally acted nonchalantly, like she didn’t care about anything. But now, her pride had been deeply wounded.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re saying that I miscalculated…?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Maybe so.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Amasawa’s fighting abilities, which she had learned in the White Room, were the real deal. Even up against people with formal training like Horikita and Ibuki, or people who learned how to fight on their own out in the real world like Ryuuen, Amasawa would be the clear victor. However, as for the question of whether she could compete with me on equal terms, that wasn’t even up for discussion. If my opponent’s skill level jumped from five to twenty, or even to thirty, they still weren’t anywhere close to a hundred.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“When did you first figure you could beat me?” asked Amasawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Since the moment I met you,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If that came from anyone other than you, Ayanokouji-senpai, I would throw it back in their face and tell them that was a lame joke.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll tell you this right now: you and this other person seem to be thinking you might be able to drive me into a corner and get me expelled, but didn’t you ever wonder why I never once asked for that other person’s name?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The smile slowly faded from Amasawa’s face. Up until this point, I had never gone out searching for the White Room student myself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s because I never thought it’d even be a fight, Not from the very beginning,” I explained.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You… You’re serious, aren’t you, senpai?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You of all people understand, don’t you, Amasawa?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If Amasawa were someone who only casually dabbled in hand-to-hand combat, then she probably wouldn’t have gotten a sense of it yet. But Amasawa wasn’t like that. From the movements she witnessed me make in a period of time that measured less than ten seconds, my victory had been decided by a wide margin.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You and that other person should have challenged me earlier,” I went on. “And you shouldn’t have done things in such a roundabout way, involving other people in this business for your own amusement.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You…knew why I contacted Kushida-senpai, don’t you?” asked Amasawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I only connected all of the dots a few moments ago. And now, something that you didn’t foresee is about to happen.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Something I…didn’t foresee?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Just stand watch by the student council office after three o’clock today. But don’t let anyone see you. Do that, and you’ll have all the answers.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Amasawa gradually relaxed and went slack, so I released my grip on her. There probably wasn’t any further need for force.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We wasted a lot of time here,” I said, turning to Kushida. “Let’s head back to the maid café.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is that a good idea, with the way things are?” asked Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Amasawa wasn’t injured, but she didn’t seem like she was going to do anything. She now stood there, dazed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t worry,” I told Kushida. “You don’t have to worry about her exposing your past.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I started walking away, and Kushida hurried to catch up to me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How did you know all of that stuff, Ayanokouji-kun?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Who knows? But you can trust me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Who </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">are</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> you, Ayanokouji-kun? Really?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I supposed her question was inevitable since she heard my conversation with Amasawa and witnessed our fight. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t really know anything about fighting,” Kushida continued, “but…I know that what I saw just now wasn’t normal.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s not unusual for people in our grade to study martial arts,” I replied. “Horikita and Ibuki are trained martial artists, and even people who’ve picked up how to fight on their own like Ryuuen and Akito are good in a fight too. It’s just that there’s no real contest between a boy and girl.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I tried explaining to Kushida that the only reason I had won the fight was just because of the overwhelming difference between sexes. Whether she would accept that or not was a different matter, however.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We need to hurry on back and have you help manage the line again,” I added. “We’re counting on you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“O-oh, okay, yeah…” After saying that, Kushida looked like she had made up her mind to do something. She bowed to me. “Thank you for saving me…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">An unexpected gesture of gratitude. Of course, when Kushida was using her public persona, it was much easier for her to behave politely and modestly than the average person. She was the type of person who could express feelings of gratitude extremely easily.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sure you probably don’t think I’m sincerely grateful to you, and that’s fine,” she said. “It’s just…I wanted to tell you that, even if you thought it was a lie.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s no big deal. If anything, it was just the natural thing to do, as classmates.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Then it’s okay for me to assume this </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">isn’t</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> a favor I have to pay you back for?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She strongly emphasized that part of it. I thought about it for a moment, but I couldn’t back out now.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course it’s okay,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Besides, even if I did make this into something I wanted her to pay me back for, it wasn’t the kind of thing that Kushida would ever be able to repay.</span></p>
