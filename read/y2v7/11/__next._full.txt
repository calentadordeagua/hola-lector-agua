<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 5:<br/>Meeting the Day Before the Cultural Festival</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HE DAYS FLEW BY</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> quickly, and it was now Friday, November 12</span><span style=" font-size: 0.75em; vertical-align: super;">th</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">. The day before the Cultural Festival had come, and school had ended for the day. Every class was solemnly preparing for what was to come. Today after school was our trial run for the Cultural Festival, an initiative spearheaded by the student council. It was going to be an important test to determine how things would go tomorrow. All of our classmates, with a few exceptions, had begun moving about to start getting ready.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita’s class had a total of four offerings. The first was the maid café, which, suffice to say, was already well known. The focus of the café’s sales would be drinks like tea, coffee, and so on. Then, there were going to be photoshoots with the maids. The latter was particularly time-efficient and the set price per photo was high, so if there were a large number of customers who wanted to take part in that, we could take in a great deal of income.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The second and third offerings were going to be food stalls set up outside. One featured flour-based foods (such as takoyaki and okonomiyaki), and the other offered Western-style pasta and bread. These food stalls would generate sales on their own, but they would also accept orders made from the maid café. When an order was placed, a student tasked with transporting the food would fetch it from the stall and deliver it to the customer. In order to capitalize on the originality of the maid café, it also had a limited food menu, which was a slight variation on the existing menus already offered at the food stalls.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Then, the fourth and final offering, something we had hastily thrown together with what we had left in our budget, was a quiz game aimed at children. That would be held outside.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you sure it’s okay to let Hasebe-san and Akito-kun go like that?” said Maezono, just as Haruka and Akito had exited the classroom, their backs to everyone else.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We can’t force them to stay,” Horikita replied. “Let’s consider this an opportunity to test if we can run this smoothly with just thirty-five people—excluding Kouenji-kun, Hasebe-san, and Akito-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, those three weren’t the only students who appeared unwilling to cooperate. Kushida had hardly said a word regarding the Cultural Festival over the past few weeks, and she had also been heading back to the dorms immediately after class ended, without offering help. She was aware that she was going to be handling customer service duties as a maid during the festival, and she had gone to Horikita several times with ideas. Some of her ideas had been adopted, although they were minor things. However, Kushida hadn’t participated in any of the practice sessions with her fellow maids to see if they could keep in sync with each other. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Satou went over to Kushida, mustering up a fair amount of courage, trying as hard as she could not to let Kushida know that she was on alert.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’d like to do some final checks for the festival tomorrow, and also to practice some of the moves that we’ll be doing… Do you think you might have some time today?” asked Satou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sorry, Satou-san. There’s something I have to do after class today that I can’t miss,” said Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This actually wasn’t the first time that Kushida had used that exact wording.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You know, you’ve been turning me down like this for a long time now… Are you really going to help out?” Satou asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There was tension in the air, and just as Horikita was about to get up from her seat, Yousuke was beside her and stopped her, almost as though he had anticipated her actions. I didn’t know what the correct decision was, but I also knew it was impossible to create a harmonious class if you meddled in every little incident. Sometimes, things had to be resolved by the relevant parties involved. You could say that this was uncharacteristic behavior from Yousuke instead, as he normally spared a caring word for his classmates more than anyone else, but…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Perhaps it was because he felt that Horikita making an&#160;unnecessary display of special treatment toward Kushida in view of their fellow classmates would be a bad move. Horikita understood that too, of course. However, she also was faced with a dilemma: She just couldn’t let things be.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t worry,” Kushida said. “I’ve been keeping the festival in mind and I’m not planning on dragging anyone down.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But, Kushida-san, you haven’t practiced even one bit, have you?” Satou protested. “I’m sorry, but I don’t know if I can trust you with the important role of being a maid.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Today was the trial run, and thus the perfect time to practice. It seemed that Satou, who had accepted Kushida’s unwillingness to participate up until now, wasn’t going to back down today. However, Kushida wasn’t going to either.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, why don’t you try removing me from the roster? Even though I can’t really think of any other good replacements.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was a brutal statement, but she was correct. Even based on her physical appearance alone, there wasn’t anyone who could serve as Kushida’s substitute who wasn’t already assigned to the role of maid.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well then, I’ll see you at the Cultural Festival tomorrow,” Kushida said. “Bye-bye.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Although she spoke in the same tone of voice as the gentle Kushida from before, there was no denying the fact that her behavior was cold. She stubbornly refused Satou’s proposal and left the classroom. Did she simply not want to spend time with her classmates who now knew her true nature? Or did she really have something to do that she couldn’t miss? The mood in the classroom was clearly getting worse, but even so, there was nothing anyone could do. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Matsushita, unable to bear the sight of Satou looking so frustrated and down, turned to Horikita directly for advice.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, Horikita-san, I know the show is tomorrow…but don’t you think that maybe we should remove Kushida-san after all?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I understand what you’re trying to say. But I don’t intend to remove her at this time,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But she’s always saying that she has something to do, every single day. She’s obviously lying,” countered Matsushita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was certainly true that Kushida’s recent behavior was puzzling in many respects. The fact that she kept her distance from a lot of people ever since the Unanimous Special Exam was understandable, but even so, her especially uncooperative behavior was quite conspicuous. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You might be right,” Horikita acknowledged. “I don’t know why she isn’t taking part in practice either.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay then, in that case—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But don’t worry. She’s thinking about the Cultural Festival and the maid café in her own way.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You believe in Kushida-san, then.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I suppose you could say that if we don’t believe in her, nothing is going to change…” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Though Matsushita wasn’t entirely convinced, she nodded and then turned to Satou. She had been doing a lot for this, perhaps because Matsushita herself was one of the original proponents of the maid café. Although Kushida’s refusal to participate in practice sessions was certainly a cause for concern, Horikita’s demeanor showed that she wasn’t feeling flustered about it. In fact, she was exuding confidence, which must have been backed by something. That was probably why Matsushita decided to follow Horikita’s lead and bet on that confidence. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">They didn’t seem like they would be asking me for my help, so I figured I’d just keep an eye on things.</span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">5.1</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">O</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">N THE FIRST FLOOR</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> of the special building was the stall designated as “Special 02.” This location was normally just a spare classroom. Now, it was being decorated by students. The girls were doing most of the work. As for the boys, if I had to say anything…they were assisting. It was interesting to note that the girls were&#160;overwhelmingly better at this sort of decorating than any of the boys were. It was probably safe to leave the setup to them, with Horikita supervising.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Up on the second floor of the special building, in a classroom in the back, preparations for another concept café were steadily underway. Unlike our maid café, the concept Ryuuen’s class were using for their café was “Japanese-style clothing.” Even the food and beverages served were completely different from what we offered, including things such as Japanese sweets, tea, and the like. In the midst of their preparations, I happened to come across a particularly conspicuous certain someone. Even though she was dressed in a Japanese kimono in line with the concept of the café, she was sitting by herself in a chair, reading a book.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When Hiyori noticed me standing there, she lifted her book up, and for some reason, hid everything below her&#160;eyes.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Hello.”</span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span><img alt="" height="100%" id="INTERIORIMAGES_3" src="/hola-lector-agua/images/books/y2v7/OEBPS/Images/INTERIORIMAGES_3.jpg" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style=" top: 0px; vertical-align: top; height: 100%;"/></span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s been a while,” I said. “I hear you haven’t been to the library much lately?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, that’s not true. I’ve just, well, I’ve been going at a slightly different time.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had thought it was strange that a bookworm would’ve stopped visiting the library, but from the sound of it, she just started going at a different time of day.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see you’re working too, as a salesperson.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m handling checkout,” she said. “I’m not very good at interacting with people, so… Well, I’m not very good at moving about either. I tried practicing by carrying food around on a tray, but that didn’t go so well.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So, in short, she wasn’t very good at any of this in general. But as long as she could smoothly handle whatever came her way at the register, I supposed that was all that&#160;mattered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“By the way, Ibuki-san is part of the staff too,” added Hiyori.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ibuki is? I was under the impression she would never, ever wear that kind of outfit.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“She and Ryuuen-kun had a contest, with the stipulation that she’d be completely exempt from helping out in the Cultural Festival if she won.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And she lost,” I surmised.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hiyori had a somewhat amused smile on her face, perhaps because she was remembering what happened then.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, where is this Ibuki who lost?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“She isn’t participating today, apparently,” Hiyori replied. “She said that she absolutely hates the idea of wearing the outfit outside of the Cultural Festival.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It wasn’t as though I didn’t understand how Ibuki felt on that point, but I just hoped she’d be able to serve the customers well when the Cultural Festival came, and she had to perform without rehearsing. Well, I supposed Ryuuen would probably deal with any issues related to that as they arose. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wanted to check on Ryuuen as the owner of this café, but he was nowhere to be seen. I wondered if he left preparations for the day prior to the festival to the other students.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It seems like Ryuuen-kun went to see how Class A is doing,” said Hiyori.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Class A?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s because their class still hasn’t disclosed what they’ll be doing for the festival.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was true—even though the Cultural Festival was tomorrow, there were no details about what Sakayanagi’s class was doing. It wasn’t strange, then, to want to know what their class was going to do. As long as every class was participating in this pre-opening event, it was a given that they’d be getting their things ready by some point today.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll head out too then,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Now that my conversation with Hiyori was finished, I&#160;decided to go search for Sakayanagi’s class.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Um, excuse me, Ayanokouji-kun…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hm?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ryuuen-kun and the others went up to the third floor,” Hiyori said, “so Sakayanagi-san is probably there.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. Thanks, that saves me some time.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It seemed like she wanted to say something else, but then she immediately shook her head. Did this mean that three of the second-year classes were concentrated in the special building, albeit on different floors?</span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">5.3</span></span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HE FIRST-YEARS</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> and some of the third-year classes were putting on a number of exhibits that resembled those you saw at festivals. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m only joking,” said Horikita. “I wouldn’t force her to put on a uniform. Besides, if she wore it and looked unhappy, it would give our guests tomorrow a bad impression.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Here, take a look at this,” I said, hanging Horikita a notebook so she could look it over. “I made a few adjustments after seeing the trial run today.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thanks. There don’t seem to be any problems with the schedule that you’ve put together,” Horikita said, looking up from the notebook. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Participants in the Cultural Festival were required to take a one-hour break at some point before the end of the festival, and they had to notify their homeroom instructor before doing so. During this break period, students were forbidden from helping with any festival stalls or exhibits, and their work had to be coordinated accordingly, regardless of whether things were busy or not.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"></div>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">5.4</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">A</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;"> </span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">BOY AND A GIRL</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> stood facing one another on the roadside path leading to Keyaki Mall. The Cultural Festival test run had already begun by this point, and there were no other students in sight.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We can finally talk, Yagami-kun,” said Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I never would have imagined that you’d just barge in while we were preparing for the festival,” he replied. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wouldn’t have been able to catch you if I hadn’t. It’s&#160;like you’ve been avoiding me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After she had made contact with Yagami back in a classroom, he forced her to move locations because he didn’t want to discuss matters at that time or place.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The fact that we haven’t been able to meet up was just a coincidence,” he said. “Come to think of it, it seems you came by my room a number of times. I apologize for being out when you dropped by.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Both of them were smiling as they spoke. If someone were to see the two of them talking, it would look like a friendly chat.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Were you really out? Or were you just pretending to be out to mess with me?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Pretending to be out? Why on earth would I do something like that?” replied Yagami. “There seems to be some kind of misunderstanding here.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s no misunderstanding.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yagami’s refusal to give her a straight answer made Kushida feel like she was trying to grab at a cloud. Frustrated, she took a step forward.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You threw me away because I’m no longer of any use to you. That’s all it is, isn’t it?” she demanded.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yagami had expected Kushida to get Horikita and Ayanokouji expelled in the Unanimous Special Exam. Since she had failed to carry out his wishes, and he had cut contact with her, it wasn’t surprising that she believed he had discarded her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you remember when I contacted you on the night of the Unanimous Special Exam, after the exam ended?” asked Yagami.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. Of course I do.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When Yagami called her that night, he learned that Horikita and Ayanokouji had not been expelled at all. Kushida told him that directly. He hung up on her immediately, and Kushida hadn’t talked to him since.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll be honest with you,” Yagami said. “I thought that you hated me, Kushida-senpai. That’s why I haven’t had the courage to face you directly, and I might have been unconsciously trying to avoid you lately.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Stop it,” she snapped. “There’s really no point in trying to lie to me like that anymore.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Listening to him pretending to act like a junior who liked her only sent shivers down her spine now that she’d seen a glimpse of his true nature.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“My apologies. Well then, could you please tell me about what you went through that day?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida had already begun to understand that this first-year student she was talking to simply enjoyed toying with her. He already knew everything that happened during the Unanimous Special Exam; he was simply trying to play with her further.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not going to answer that,” she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why not? I know, at the very least, that you tried to get those two students expelled, Kushida-senpai. But the end result was that Sakura-senpai was expelled instead of you. What I would like to know are the </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">details</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> of how that happened.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I didn’t do anything in that special exam. Sakura-san had the lowest score in OAA and was inevitably thrown out. That’s all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Details of what happened during the Unanimous Special Exam hadn’t been shared with anyone outside of the class, and that was why Yagami wanted to know exactly what happened. Kushida tried to push the narrative that Sakura Airi was chosen on the basis of her lack of ability and nothing more. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yagami, still smiling, placed his hand gently on Kushida’s shoulder. “You mustn’t lie,” he said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Lie…?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Your routine has changed dramatically ever since the Unanimous Special Exam. You appear to be getting along with students from the other classes as usual, but I’ve done some investigating, and I know that you’ve become distant from your own classmates. In other words, a certain degree of your true nature must have been exposed during the Unanimous Special Exam.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">On the surface, Kushida still smiled at her classmates, just like she always did. But there were limits now, as there was more distance between her and her classmates than ever before. She used to hang out with a small group of girls twice or three times a week, but now that number had gone down to zero. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t know what you’re talking about,” Kushida insisted. “I get along with my classmates like I always have.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida tried to frame it like he might have gotten the wrong impression from what he’d seen, or that he was spouting off nonsense without any proof, but Yagami just kept smiling.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s no use trying to hide it,” he said. “Your classmates found out everything about your past, Kushida-senpai. And it was most definitely Ayanokouji-senpai who drove you into that corner. Am I right?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yagami spoke eloquently, as though he had been watching Kushida and the others fighting in class firsthand. The fact that he deliberately mentioned Ayanokouji’s name and not Horikita’s was also surprising.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s simply your imagination. It’s not really what happened,” said Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re free to try to dodge the truth, but, well… Anyway, what do you want from me if there’s nothing to say? I have to help with the Cultural Festival, so I’d like to get back as soon as possible.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m tired of spending time with you, Yagami-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re…tired of it?” he asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t bother me ever again. That’s all I wanted to say to you today.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida was suddenly telling Yagami that she wanted to end this.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you want to end our relationship,” he said. “I can understand your feelings on the matter. Now that your past and your true nature are known to your class, I&#160;suppose that it’d be pointless to try to drive Horikita-senpai and Ayanokouji-senpai to expulsion, wouldn’t it, Kushida-senpai?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not going to correct you on every little thing,” she huffed. “If you want to selfishly interpret the situation however you want, then go ahead.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re an interesting person, Kushida-senpai. What you said just now was the truth. And what’s more, you yourself are beginning to think that you can throw yourself into this environment. Which is why you want to end this uneasy relationship with me and look forward.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Look forward</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">. That phrase that came out of Yagami’s mouth stuck in her mind.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ayanokouji-senpai aside, have you reconciled with Horikita-senpai?” he asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not going to answer that either.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“From the sound of it, you were completely won over, huh? I’m a little disappointed, Kushida-senpai.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida resisted the urge to throw his words back in his face, but anger welled up within her. She hated Horikita as much as ever.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I—!” she began.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, it’s all right. Don’t say anything further. I can tell just by looking at you, you know,” Yagami said. His dismissive attitude lacked some of the politeness from earlier. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida couldn’t help but find that unsettling, but she couldn’t afford to show any weakness here. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If anything, Kushida was clearly able to tolerate more than the average student, perhaps because of her repeated run-ins with unusual people such as Ayanokouji, Ryuuen, and Amasawa. While she was surprised that she came to that realization herself, she continued to be tough.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We’re done, Yagami-kun. We won’t have anything to do with each other anymore. Okay?” she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Please relax. You’re worried that I might go around exposing your past, aren’t you, Kushida-senpai? That’s why you came to check on me, to remind me not to try anything. Right?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s right. If you were to expose me, Yagami-kun, then word would get around the school.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, would you please listen to what I have to say first?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You have something over me, but I have something over you too. I’ll tell them everything about you, Yagami-kun. I’ll tell everyone that you tried to use me to get Ayanokouji and Horikita expelled, and that despite the polite face you wear, you do fiendish things.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida didn’t know if that would be enough of a threat. Even so, this was the only weapon at her disposal that she could use to defend herself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So you’re threatening me in return…” said Yagami. “I’ll keep what you said in mind. Well then, are we finished here?” Whether what Kushida said worked or not, Yagami stopped the conversation there and started walking away. “I’m the leader of Class 1-B, so I’m rather busy with all sorts of things for the Cultural Festival,” he said. “If you’ll please excuse me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t forget, Yagami-kun. As long as you keep your word, I’ll keep mine.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yagami eventually disappeared from view, still smiling all the while, his footsteps light and nimble.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…I really hope this is the end of it…” muttered Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But at the same time, she sensed that this wasn’t over yet. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">In that case, what should I do?</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> she wondered. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Should I just leave things as they are, and wait, and do nothing? Or&#160;should I make the first strike?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s no use. I can’t stop Yagami.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida had challenged and lost to various opponents up until this point, including Horikita. She had to abandon naïve lines of thinking, like the idea that she could handle him on her own, but she was painfully aware that she was all alone. Even so, the situation had changed dramatically. Her opponent was definitely looking down on her. He wasn’t just underestimating her on the surface either. Deep down, in his heart of hearts, he was putting her down. Kushida prided herself on being good at reading into that kind of thing.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There are things I need to do before I fight him,” she decided.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida knew that Yagami wasn’t the only problem that needed solving. She had no intention whatsoever of going back to being that kind, gentle honor student, but she had to make solid contributions in order to maintain a stable position in her class. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida Kikyou knew very well how to survive.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s unusual of you to call me, Sakayanagi,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I could hear her chuckle softly on the other end of the&#160;line.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“You might be right about that, certainly. Would you happen to have a few moments for a chat?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wouldn’t have answered if it was an inconvenience.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">I see. In that case, allow me to get straight to business. I’m sure that you’re participating in the Cultural Festival, of course, Ayanokouji-kun. My father seems to be doing a great deal of fretting about the possibility that someone from the outside might try to take you back with them.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The Chancellor called me a little while ago, actually. He said that I should consider being absent from the festival too, but I had politely declined.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I probably would have been a part of the Sports Festival in the end, if not for the fact that my own absence ensured Sakayanagi wouldn’t attend. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“Aren’t you afraid? …No, that was a foolish question. Let me rephrase. Are you assuming that the people involved will not try to capture you?”</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She was basically saying that, if I wasn’t making that assumption, she didn’t see the point of deliberately putting myself in harm’s way.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s a simple matter of comparing actual harm versus potential harm,” I replied. “If I could resolve everything by simply bowing out of both the Sports Festival and Cultural Festival, then that would be fine. But there’s also a school trip coming up as well. And there’s no guarantee that next year’s Sports Festival or Cultural Festival events won’t also have visitors. It would be simple enough for me to remain hidden in my shell, but I think losing opportunities because of that would be more of a problem.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“So essentially, you would like to enjoy the time you have remaining at this school and experience it as a normal student would, as much as possible.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She must have understood what I was trying to say because she more or less summed up my position. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Besides, I have other objectives as well,” I said. “I don’t want to waste my opportunities.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, I have nothing further to add. I think it would be best for you to do what you wish, Ayanokouji-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was curious about what she was doing for the Cultural Festival, but I was forbidden from asking her about it. Was she simply trying to win with whatever her class put on, or was she going to abandon the competition? Did she have some other goal in mind? It was possible that she </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">would</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> answer me if I asked, but that was another issue. Whatever choice she made was up to Class&#160;A to decide; a third party had no authority to determine what was right or wrong.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">However, you never know when unforeseen situations may arise. Even if the Cultural Festival is safe, we don’t know what will come next. If you have any trouble, please come talk to me at any time.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s really kind of you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“We can’t have you disappearing before our rematch after all, Ayanokouji-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll be careful.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“Well then, I’ll talk to you again soon. Goodnight.”</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> Sakayanagi ended the call right there, avoiding any pointless chatter.</span></p>
