<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 9:<br/>Those Working Behind the Scenes</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">F</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">OUR O’CLOCK</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> was here at last, and the hectic Cultural Festival finally came to an end. Just as the school explained to us beforehand, the accounting app forcibly shut down, and no further sales could be entered. The results would be available to be checked via our phones in two hours’ time, starting at six o’clock in the evening. But even though the festival was over, the fact remained that we were going to need to keep taking care of things until everything was done. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The guests who stayed all the way until the end of the festival began to leave their seats now that the café was closed. They each gave the students their impressions of the maid café, telling them what they thought. Just about all of the comments were positive, telling us that the café was interesting, fun, and so on. For the students who worked so hard, these warm words penetrated deep into their hearts and seemed to wash away their fatigue. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Incidentally, the second the clock struck four, Chabashira-sensei bolted out of the classroom as fast as her legs could carry her. I assumed running in an outfit like that would make her even more conspicuous, but I decided I’d just leave that issue alone.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was just around 5:30 p.m. when the last of the customers had finally left, and all of our classmates (with the exception of Kouenji) were gathered in the maid café. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Great work, everyone,” said Horikita. “A lot of things happened today, but we finished the Cultural Festival strong and in an ideal way. I can’t imagine that we could’ve had a better turnout than we did.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ike and the other guys had just finished cleaning up and packing away the outdoor food stalls and were also in attendance. Some café guests were still eating at the time the festival ended, so they had left a little bit later, and there were still some things left to be cleaned up, but Horikita went ahead and started summing up what happened at the festival.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The results will be announced later, but there’s something I wish to talk to you about first,” she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">That’s right.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> There were thirty-seven people in class, as Akito and Haruka remained. Although Horikita hadn’t prompted her to say something, Haruka took a step forward as she was clearly the person on everyone’s minds right now. As the classroom sat in silence, Haruka opened her mouth to speak.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I think that there’s something I wanna say first, though,” she muttered. “I haven’t forgiven all of you here.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Some students had been expecting her to start things off with an apology, and they exchanged looks with one another, feeling more bewildered than angry. It didn’t seem as though they particularly blamed her for anything. Everyone could understand. They had matured enough to the point where they could feel the pain of what it was like to lose a friend—a close confidant.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She continued from there. “But the person that I can’t forgive the most is me. I assumed that everyone who got kicked out of school must be miserable. Like Yamauchi-kun who left last year, and now Airi.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">At the mention of Yamauchi’s name, Sudou, Ike, and some of the other guys looked distant, like they were thinking about him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I was convinced that it was in Airi’s best interest to remain at this school. I selfishly assumed that’s what would make her the happiest. That’s why I hated everyone… Why I wanted to get revenge.” Haruka then gripped her uniform’s skirt tightly, feelings of frustration welling up within her. “I was planning on dropping out at the end of the festival too.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was something that Haruka didn’t need to tell us, but still, she confessed the truth because she hated hiding it. I figured that some students had anticipated it, but most of them remained silent with their mouths clamped shut. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Then, Akito spoke up and revealed the truth as well, unable to stay quiet. “I was planning on dropping out too, together with Haruka.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you two had chosen to drop out of school, we likely would never reach Class A,” Horikita said. “That would’ve been the easiest, most potent method of taking your revenge.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There was no need for complicated schemes or petty tricks. Just by dropping out of school, they would cause us to lose a lot of Class Points.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But, if you’ll give me a chance, I’d like to stay in this class now,” pleaded Haruka.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’ve had a change of heart?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Airi’s out there in the world, spreading her wings, trying to do her thing,” Haruka said. “That’s what Kushida-san showed me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hearing Kushida’s name, everyone turned their eyes to her in unison. Kushida appended Haruka’s statement, as the majority of the class couldn’t understand what was going on.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It looks like Sakura-san is trying her best to become an idol,” she explained. “You can find her if you do some digging on social media. Maybe you can ask Hasebe-san to tell you about it later.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Some students seemed surprised, and others seemed to have already had some idea. But what all of those students shared between them was a common understanding that Airi had taken a step forward.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Airi is going to grow—a lot,” Haruka said. “I’m sure she’ll grow even more than I thought she would. That’s why I want to graduate from Class A and become someone who can go see her. I want to be able to show myself to her without feeling ashamed.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’ve made a good decision, Hasebe-san,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I plan to accept whatever punishment for all the trouble I caused,” said Haruka.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Me too,” added Akito. “I’m just as guilty. I didn’t help with the festival and caused a lot of trouble for the class.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita took a step forward to speak before any other student could chime in.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, skipping the festival in itself is problematic behavior, but fortunately, it doesn’t violate any school rules,” she said. “We’re only required to take a one-hour break at minimum; we didn’t necessarily have to work at the festival. Kouenji-kun hasn’t shown up at all since this morning, so he’s in the same situation.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Haruka looked stunned yet relieved as Horikita approached.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you are looking for a punishment, though, it can only be this: you’re to remain with me as my classmate from now onward. Can you face that reality?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wondered what Haruka thought—what she saw reflected in Horikita’s eyes in that moment.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll try,” Haruka said. “I’ll give it my absolute best.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay. So, does that mean from now on, I can assume you’ll be your usual self, Hasebe-san?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Don’t worry. I won’t cause any trouble.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita nodded, telling her that was plenty. “And you too, Miyake-kun,” she added. “You’ll be just like how you used to be. Okay?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, of course,” he answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, that’ll be all for today. Let’s all work together and finish up the last bit of cleaning quickly,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Keisei walked up to Haruka and Akito, albeit somewhat hesitantly. After Akito offered an apology, Keisei’s eyes welled up, a little red with tears, and he replied with words of relief. Then, with an apology from Haruka, the three of them were brought together for the first time in a long while, and they looked at each other with small smiles on their faces. Eventually, Akito and Keisei directed their gazes over at me, as though they had made up their minds about something.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The boys signaled to Haruka, and then, all three of them were looking at me, albeit with some hesitation and confusion. If I came forward and approached them right here and now, we might have been able to reform our old group, even if only in a formal sense. But that was no longer necessary. I turned my back to them and offered words of appreciation to Satou and the other girls. Our group, which used to be five, had now become three, but I hoped that they would be joined together by a stronger bond than before. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">My presence wasn’t needed there. I figured the three of them could sense that what I just did was my way of saying farewell, a sign that we were going our separate ways. They didn’t approach me or try to talk to me either. </span></p>

<div style="page-break-after: always;"></div>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span><img alt="" height="100%" id="INTERIORIMAGES_9" src="/hola-lector-agua/images/books/y2v7/OEBPS/Images/INTERIORIMAGES_9.jpg" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style=" top: 0px; vertical-align: top; height: 100%;"/></span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I didn’t extract information from her,” I said. “You were the one who repeatedly made contact with Satou and slowly but surely drove her into a corner. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The person standing before me, Tsubaki, had been the one who had contacted Satou, as someone that Satou didn’t know or have any connection with.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t know what you mean by that,” she said teasingly, making it clear she was kidding.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“It seems you’re as good as people say,”</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> remarked the caller.</span></p>
