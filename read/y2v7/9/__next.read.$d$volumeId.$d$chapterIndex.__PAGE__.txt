<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">W</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">E WERE SURPRISED</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> by Ryuuen’s announcement that his class would step into the ring with a concept café on Monday, November 8</span><span style=" font-size: 0.75em; vertical-align: super;">th</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">. As a result, we had to respond to a variety of issues, but my classmates were determined to fight, and what they had to do remained unchanged. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In response to Ryuuen’s proposed bet, Horikita made a presentation for the class and asked for their agreement to fight head-to-head against his class for a million Private Points. The arrangement was that the class with greater sales in the Cultural Festival would receive that million points from the other class. Many of our classmates had a positive attitude, which was a big plus. They weren’t letting themselves get flustered, and instead were determined to fight seriously and win.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When Chabashira-sensei left the room after class ended, I took out my cell phone. I noticed I’d received a response to my earlier message in chat, so I went ahead and read it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">I have time. I’m headed to the designated location.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Apparently, he was open to my request. I suppose that meant that the preface to this conversation that I&#160;had brought up to him the other day, about the future, had&#160;proven successful.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, Kiyotaka,” said Kei. “Let’s walk back to the dorms together.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry, but I have plans today,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? Really? Okay, then…” Kei quickly changed gears and turned to Satou, who was still in the classroom. “Hey, Maya-chan, let’s go, you and me!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I come after Ayanokouji-kun?!” she laughed. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, hey, don’t say it like that. Okay?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Her joking aside, Satou didn’t appear displeased by Kei’s request at all, and she actually accepted Kei’s offer to walk together with a big smile. Kei invited another group of girls to join them, and they all left the classroom happily. Among them was Shinohara, who, up until just a little while ago, had been on poor terms with the others. It looked like Kei had matured even more since she’d gotten closer with Satou. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">At any rate, I was grateful that the other girls could keep Kei company. I decided to exit the classroom and head over to the special building to meet with Kanzaki, the person who I’d asked to come out. We wouldn’t be able to simply discuss this matter over the phone, via chat, or out in public. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">On my way over, I spotted Mashima-sensei, the homeroom instructor for Class 2-A, standing in the hallway and chatting with some teachers from other grade levels. Although the unusual sight caught my attention, I didn’t stop. As I passed them by though, a part of the teachers’ conversation reached my ears.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Chabashira-sensei sure has changed lately, don’t you think?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s like she’s softened up,” another said. “Or rather, she seems to smile more.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Mashima-sensei, you were in the same grade as Chabashira-sensei when you were in school, right? I&#160;wanted to ask you a few things…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Apparently, they were talking about Chabashira-sensei. I figured that the teachers could’ve just hung out in the faculty lounge if they wanted to talk, but I supposed that if the topic of conversation was a specific teacher—and one of the opposite sex, no less—it might make more sense for them to have their conversation&#160;here. It went&#160;without saying that the change they were talking about had been triggered by the Unanimous Special Exam. Chabashira-sensei had come out of her shell, not only as a homeroom instructor, but as a teacher in general. There was no doubt that they perceived that difference. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Noticing my presence, Mashima-sensei brought the conversation to a stop. He must have decided it wouldn’t be a good idea to unintentionally let a student overhear what they were saying.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ayanokouji, what is your business here in the special building?” he asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was a fair question, since few students passed through these hallways after class for no reason.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I have a short meeting with someone,” I replied. “We&#160;have some things to talk about that we don’t want to be overheard.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hearing that answer, the teachers, with the exception of Mashima-sensei, looked somewhat embarrassed. Perhaps they decided to disperse, because they then started walking away. I could’ve left immediately too, but I had a little time to spare before my meeting.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Actually, this is perfect timing, Mashima-sensei. If&#160;you wouldn’t mind, I have a few questions that I’d like to ask you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I figured it must’ve meant something that Mashima-sensei stuck around even after all the others walked away.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Questions for me? What did you want to ask?” he replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s about the Cultural Festival,” I said. “Regarding the rules that aren’t explicitly stated.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He had a slightly dubious look on his face, but Mashima-sensei immediately responded appropriately and honestly to my request, as a teacher. This school was built on special rules that were very different from ones at a normal high school. School officials were well aware that each individual student had their own unique perspective on things and would focus on different aspects. However, there would inevitably be things that would catch people’s attention.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not sure what it is you want to ask me,” he began, “but I must ask you—are you sure you shouldn’t be checking with your own homeroom instructor, Chabashira-sensei, first?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He didn’t hesitate to make sure that I wasn’t mistaken in approaching him with my questions. Indeed, normally, it would’ve made more sense to go to your own homeroom instructor if you were looking for an explanation of the rules.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There are instances when, depending on the time and circumstances, it’s more expedient to ask someone other than Chabashira-sensei,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Teachers are supposed to be fair to the students,” said Mashima-sensei. “But even so, it’s not like no problems ever arise when teachers deal with students from other classes in the same grade level. I’m sure that you’re aware of that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He was warning me that sometimes it’s too late to be asking things.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ve determined that you’re not the sort of person who would betray my expectations, Mashima-sensei,” I&#160;told him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If that’s what you’ve decided, then let’s not do anything uncouth,” he said. He was basically telling me that, rather than worrying about whether he’d reciprocate my trust, if I was going to trust him, then I should just go ahead and say what I wanted. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So then, what was it you wanted to ask, exactly, about the unstated rules?” he pressed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Now that I had Mashima-sensei’s permission, I went ahead and asked him for advice on a special case. He didn’t seem at all surprised by my words, but I supposed that was to be expected. Even the school officials had a set of unstated, behind-the-scenes rules so that they could satisfy students’ various wishes. That was exactly why Mashima-sensei didn’t find it strange that there were students who thought like me. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Your line of thinking is exactly correct,” Mashima-sensei said. “It’s not impossible to do, if it’s necessary.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I knew it,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It wasn’t a crazy idea. There could be cases where it was called for, like if a class was placed in an unfortunate situation, or in the event of a major inconvenience. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“However, if you were to ask me whether it’s efficient…I&#160;have my doubts,” Mashima-sensei said. “As I’m sure you’re already aware, no problems would arise if it happened between students. Or, well, to be more precise, you would discuss it amongst yourselves to avoid any problems. You understand what I mean when I say that, yes?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. I was wondering if it was something that didn’t need to be explicitly stated in the rules and could just be done independently.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s right. Of course, the risks would be different for each, but why take that option into consideration?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I thought it was only natural to be prepared for any contingencies.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He nodded, thinking it over. “Putting aside whether you exercise it or not, then… Hm. Yes, I suppose that getting an understanding of it beforehand certainly wouldn’t be a bad thing,” he reasoned. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Although Mashima-sensei hadn’t mentioned it, he might have had a general idea about our sales strategy based on what I’d said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m glad to get confirmation on this,” I said. “Thank you very much.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re quite welcome,” said Mashima-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That made one less thing that I needed to check on for the Cultural Festival—an unexpected boon. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I gave Mashima-sensei a slight bow as thanks and was about to leave, but then he called out to me once more.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ayanokouji, I believe you had heard a little bit of our conversation earlier, about Chabashira-sensei, but… What happened in the Unanimous Special Exam?” he asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You haven’t heard anything about it from Chabashira-sensei?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even Mashima-sensei knew the results, obviously, but when it came to Chabashira-sensei’s change of heart, there were some things he apparently still didn’t understand.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It seems like, regardless of the fact that someone was expelled, she’s now able to look forward and smile,” he said. “Something significant must have happened during the course of the special exam to cause her change of heart. Am I right?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If I remembered correctly, Mashima-sensei and Chabashira-sensei had been in the same grade level when they attended the Advanced Nurturing High School. As&#160;he knew things about her past, it wasn’t shocking that her sudden change would come as a surprise to him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Forget what I said just now,” Mashima-sensei said quickly. “This isn’t the sort of thing I should be asking a student.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I understand. If you’ll please excuse me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I gave Mashima-sensei another courteous bow and then headed over to where I would be meeting Kanzaki in the special building.</span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">3.1</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HE CULTURAL FESTIVAL</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> was gradually approaching, but there were some other issues that also needed to be addressed in tandem with those preparations. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">One was to bring about a change in Ichinose’s class. The countdown clock to their collapse was ticking down even faster than I’d anticipated. I needed to take steps in order to avoid that, and this time, I wouldn’t be dealing with its leader, Ichinose.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">What I needed to do now was make changes in the people under her—her classmates. However, that was a measure that needed to be handled with care, and who else could be capable enough to fulfill that role except for </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">him</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry for calling you out here,” I began.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When I arrived at our designated meeting place after class, I saw that Kanzaki was already waiting for me with a grim look on his face. He certainly didn’t appear to be in the mood for a pleasant chit-chat.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What business do you have with me?” he asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I met Kanzaki shortly after I enrolled here at this school, but he was in another class, and we weren’t particularly close acquaintances. Lately, I felt like he distrusted me; if anything, I’d say he probably disliked me too. I didn’t necessarily expect him not to respond to my call because of that, though. It wouldn’t be any wonder to me if that very wariness of me was why he wanted to talk. And if it was the sort of rendezvous where he wouldn’t want to be seen, then it was all the more likely he’d go through it. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The time has come to talk about the future,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The future?” he repeated. “What in the world are you… Never mind. First, there’s something I want you to hear.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kanzaki adjusted his posture and stood up straight, wanting to speak before I could get to business. I was a little surprised by his unexpected first move, but I decided to listen to what he had to say.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ve been troubled all the time lately. I haven’t had anyone to talk to, so I’ve just been mulling things over by myself.” After that, he paused and corrected himself. “No, it would be an exaggeration to say that I was </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">troubled</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">, I suppose. Every day, I was thinking about my future path.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">His words were filled with emotion, which was unusual for the calm and collected Kanzaki. I decided&#160;to stand quietly and listen until he specifically asked me to&#160;answer back.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I thought about things like… What I should be looking for during the rest of my time at this school?” he said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I figured he wasn’t agonizing over things like stumbles in friendships or problems with the opposite sex. There was only one issue that would be preoccupying students at this school: being promoted to Class A.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sure that I probably don’t even need to tell you this, but our class cannot win,” said Kanzaki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">What exactly would they be unable to win? The Cultural Festival? The year-end final exam that was a little further down the road? </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">No, he wasn’t talking about such minor things. The reality was that Ichinose’s class couldn’t make it to Class A. And that fact was what Kanzaki was lamenting.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s not as though we’re lagging behind the other classes in terms of academic ability, physical ability, or leadership skills—not whatsoever. Actually, I feel we’ve been rather blessed in those regards. But I’ve learned that those things don’t necessarily lead to victory,” said Kanzaki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He had begun to think for himself, understand things by himself, worry over things by himself. Just as I had imagined, Kanzaki was the beginning. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I know what you’re trying to say,” I said. “So? What do you want from me, Kanzaki?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If he just needed someone to listen and show that they understood things well, that was something anyone could do.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I want…your advice, regarding Ichinose,” said Kanzaki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But why did it have to be me? The names of a few other people with whom common ground might’ve been shared came to mind immediately.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, actually, that’s not all,” he corrected himself. “I’d&#160;like to hear your opinion on what our class should do in the future.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s a ridiculously huge ask,” I told him. “And you’re looking for that from me, someone who isn’t your classmate, to boot?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Yes, you’re right about that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was easy to read Kanzaki’s state of mind from the pained expression on his face. He was not the type of person who could casually go to others for help. It was precisely because he had been driven to this point that he had no choice but to resort to such measures. Actually, at first, he couldn’t have even considered asking for help like this. He must have been suffering all alone for long enough that he had figured that the only future that awaited him was even more suffering.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“She doesn’t really listen to my opinions,” he said. “Actually, no, not just me. It’s the same for anyone else.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ve always seen Ichinose as the kind of student who would listen to anyone,” I countered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s only when you’re facing in the same direction as her. I’m sure I don’t need to explain that to you now.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I said that deliberately to test him, but it seemed that hadn’t been necessary. To put this in simpler terms, if you asked Ichinose to help save someone, she wouldn’t give a second thought to the risks. She wouldn’t betray you and would stay with you until the end to give a helping hand. However, on the flipside, if you were to ask Ichinose to help you put someone in harm’s way for no reason, she would never, ever help. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">You could describe her as someone who righted wrongs, someone who stood for justice, and those descriptions would be appropriate. Ichinose would remain steadfast and refuse to go against her nature, even if you were to offer her some kind of incentive, like money.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I won’t say that she’s facing the wrong direction,” Kanzaki said. “But idealism is just that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There are more than a few situations where that idealism is necessary, though,” I countered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I suppose so. And I’m prepared to go through hardships together, when things are going well.” In fact, Kanzaki and his fellow classmates had shared many joys and sorrows together with Ichinose up through this point. “But what about right now? We’ve lost Class&#160;Points by continuing to follow Ichinose’s policies. We’re at the bottom of the rankings, and we can’t find a way to break out of it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re being very frank and open. Are you sure it’s okay to tell me about what’s going on in your class like this?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s a stupid plan,” he muttered self-deprecatingly, as though he were laughing at himself. “But even if it is stupid, it’s still a plan. And right now, I have no other choice but to rely on you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He averted his somewhat resigned gaze from me and stared at the empty hallway floor.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“During the Unanimous Special Exam, I asserted that we should have expelled one of our classmates so that we could get Class Points,” he said, continuing. “I cast my vote in favor of doing so and struggled against the others, but even so, my attempts ended in failure.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">While I didn’t know anything about the state of affairs in their class, it was easy to imagine that situation. Kanzaki agreed with the idea of having someone expelled in order to elevate their class’s rank and make everyone better understand reality. He continued to vote in favor and tried to change the opinions of his classmates, but none of them, Ichinose included, agreed with him. However, his classmates didn’t mercilessly attack Kanzaki after he staged this revolt; they simply stated that they should all work hard, together. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t reply to that, and Kanzaki eventually broke the silence.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…It’s funny, isn’t it?” he muttered Kanzaki. “What would even come from me telling someone this kind of thing, whether they’re friend or foe?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He realized all on his own that there was no way he would be able to get any advice. What he was doing was truly nonsensical, something he had lost his mind over. Right now, he probably wanted to kick himself over it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ichinose holds you in great esteem. She’s devoted to you,” he said. “Only a unique person such as yourself would be able to bring about a change in her policies. She could see things clearly, if it came from you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see,” I replied.</span></p>

If she sided with Kanzaki, fighting alone and unassisted, it was inevitable she’d be given the cold shoulder by her other classmates. Kanzaki knew full well that was not something she wanted. Even so, someone had to do something.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I… If we can win…I want to,” said Himeno.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kanzaki revealed to Himeno the possibility that he would give up on leading and remove himself from the class. By doing so, he unlocked what she was feeling deep inside. Himeno hadn’t given up on the possibility of the class changing and coming out on top, and in no small measure. However, all that she did was unlock the door. It wasn’t open yet.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, we have to act now,” Kanzaki said. “Am&#160;I wrong?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If Himeno didn’t make a move soon, there really wouldn’t be anything else Kanzaki could do. And supposing if Kanzaki didn’t want to make a choice here, he would have no other path but to change course and try to win himself by transferring to another class. And&#160;then Himeno, who couldn’t say that something black was black without the majority agreeing to it, would be completely defeated.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I understand what you’re trying to say… But still—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kanzaki cut her off there. “I hope you’re not going to say that there’s still a chance we can win under Ichinose’s policies.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That struck a chord with Himeno, resonating with her deeply. Her lips closed shut tightly and she stopped speaking partway through her sentence.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Himeno, don’t you want to graduate from Class A?” asked Kanzaki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">His words pierced Himeno’s heart like a spear, making it bleed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If I could graduate from Class A, then yes. Of </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">course</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> I want to!!!” she screamed, the words bursting forth.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Himeno’s loud voice echoed down the hall. Kanzaki was so taken aback by the sheer impact of it that he was rendered speechless; it must have been several times louder than he was expecting. </span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"></div>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span><img alt="" height="100%" id="INTERIORIMAGES_2" src="/hola-lector-agua/images/books/y2v7/OEBPS/Images/INTERIORIMAGES_2.jpg" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style=" top: 0px; vertical-align: top; height: 100%;"/></span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"></div>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But the way things are now, no matter how hard I rack my brain, I can’t think of any way we could do it! It </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">can’t</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> be done!” she shouted, her emotions exploding. “Even you understand that, Kanzaki-kun, don’t you?!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course I know that! It’s </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">because</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> I understand that we have to do something now! I don’t want to lose to the other classes!!!” he shouted back.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Although his voice didn’t come out as loud as hers, she was just as taken aback by his impassioned shouting. When I saw Himeno flinch and realized how she looked amused and bewildered, I became even more convinced that Himeno had shown her true self for the first time. I was also convinced that Kanzaki had a childlike side. On top of that, I was convinced that there were probably more than a few other students in Ichinose’s class who only went along with things to go with the flow. Underneath the surface, things had to be different. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">A year and a half into being at this school, many people in Horikita’s class had exposed their weaknesses. There were those who presented themselves as honors students who didn’t care at all if others were expelled. There were those who couldn’t study or hold discussions well and immediately resorted to violence. There were also those who were parasites, clinging to powerful people in order to get to the top of the caste system. There were those who made plans to expel their fellow classmates in order to erase their own past. Those weak-minded students had fallen as far as they could, and then they were starting to climb back up. And, among them, some were now showing unbelievable growth.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…I didn’t know you felt like that, Kanzaki-kun,” Himeno said at last. “You’re always so calm and quiet. I’m really surprised.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Me too,” said Kanzaki. “I had no idea that you had those kinds of thoughts, Himeno.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ichinose’s class likely hadn’t faced the obvious hardships Horikita’s class had. When students saw that someone had fallen behind, they would be cared for, kindly and warmly. Students were supported and protected from all sides so they wouldn’t tumble again. Help would repeatedly be given to a student who had hurt their hand. Eventually, the students would understand that they had to be careful, because others worried about them. Why did they fall? Why did they hurt their hand? The truth was that there would be more pain ahead, but they held onto it silently so as not to cause worry. And the result of all of that was that Ichinose’s class consisted only of superficial relationships.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had been silent for a long time, but I spoke up again at last. “Now is the time to become friends, in the true sense.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But what should we do?” said Kanzaki. “How can&#160;we move forward? Even if Himeno is the one who can change people’s thinking, it’s meaningless if it doesn’t lead to the next step.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s no need to rush for an answer,” I replied. “Starting now, you both should try searching.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Searching…? For what?” asked Himeno.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“For students like you, those who keep their true feelings hidden deep down,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even if they couldn’t find other people on their own, as long as they discussed it with each other, I was sure they could broaden their view many times over. The addition of another person’s point of view would bring about a number of new discoveries.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And if…supposing we find one person…?” said Himeno.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s easy,” I replied. “Then the three of you search. Then three become four, and you repeat the process.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Eventually, this small spark would turn into a huge flame. And at that point, Ichinose would come to understand that her class was about to change.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s not too late. Get stronger. And try to defeat Horikita’s class in the year-end exam,” I added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If they did that, they would have a slight hope of making it to Class A when we entered our third year.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What are you going to do, Kanzaki-kun?” asked Himeno.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We have to be prepared to work,” he said. “More grueling work than we could imagine. But…it’s not impossible.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Now that he saw what Himeno was like, Kanzaki wouldn’t ever be able to say that there weren’t any likeminded people in his class. As for Himeno, she probably gained an understanding of Kanzaki’s strong will from seeing it up close.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We both want to graduate from Class A,” Himeno said. “Until now, I hadn’t been able to admit that to anyone…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Regardless of the circumstances it had taken to get here, Himeno had conveyed her feelings to Kanzaki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, you’re right about that,” he agreed. “Our goal hasn’t changed one bit since the beginning.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Starting now, these two were preparing to move forward, like little children taking their first steps. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You know…after hearing what Ayanokouji-kun said, there’s one kid in our class that I’m kind of curious about,” Himeno said. “If you want, after this, would you want to come with me to talk to them?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kanzaki nodded vigorously. From this point on, there was no point in me sticking around. I would just be a third wheel.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ayanokouji, I’ll repay my debt to you in the year-end exam,” said Kanzaki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I figured that repayment meant winning and earning the right to challenge the other classes for Class A.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Horikita’s class is formidable, Kanzaki,” I cautioned him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, I know…” he said. “Sorry, but I have to be going now. I don’t want to waste a single minute. No, not a single second.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Himeno nodded, took out her phone, and together with Kanzaki, she turned her back to me and walked away. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There was a side of me that was worried about just how much change those two would be able to bring about, but from the sounds of things, they could be even more successful than I had imagined. Maybe they really </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">would</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> win the year-end special exam, or maybe they really could beat Horikita’s class. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However things turned out, it wouldn’t interfere with my plans, but it was one more thing for me to look forward to.</span></p>
