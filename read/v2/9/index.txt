<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 3:<br/>Weak Point</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">he bad news just kept coming. During homeroom the following morning, as Chabashira-sensei was about to leave, she assaulted us with one of her infamously brief and off-hand announcements. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I have an announcement for you all. There was a bit of trouble the other day, an incident between the student sitting there, Sudou, and some students from Class C. In short, there was a fight.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The classroom erupted. Depending on the degree of responsibility that Class C saddled him with, Sudou could face suspension, and we might see a reduction in our class points. Chabashira-sensei laid the whole situation bare for the class. Her face was so devoid of emotion or interest when she spoke that there was a certain serene beauty to it. She inserted no personal bias whatsoever when she addressed the class, and explained the situation from a position of neutrality.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Umm. So why hasn’t this issue been resolved already?” Hirata asked a rather reasonable question.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The complaint came from Class C. They claim that the fight was one-sided. However, when we spoke to the accused, Sudou said that their claims were false. He insists that the students Class C called him over and started the fight.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It wasn’t my fault! It was self-defense! Self-defense, I tell you!” shouted Sudou as he shouldered his classmates’ icy glares.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But there’s no evidence of that. Am I wrong?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What evidence? I don’t have any.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So in other words, we don’t yet know the truth. Therefore, we have put our decision on hold for now. Our response, and the punishment, will come when we find out who was at fault.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“All I know is that I’m innocent. If anything, I ought to receive a settlement for my trouble.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So speaks the accused, but I wouldn’t say that you have a high level of credibility right now. If there is an eyewitness, as Sudou seems to believe, then the situation may change. If anyone here witnessed the fight, please raise your hand.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira-sensei continued talking in a robotic, flat voice. No students raised their hands in response to her question.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Too bad, Sudou. It appears there are no witnesses in this class.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Looks that way,” he grumbled.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When Chabashira-sensei cast a doubtful look at Sudou, he glanced downward.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In order to check for witnesses, every teacher will inform their classes of the details of this incident.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh?! You’re telling everyone?!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The school probably had no say in the matter. Because Sudou had insisted it was a false charge and brought up a potential witness, the school had to be sure. For Sudou, who had hoped to hide the situation, none of this was good.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Damn it!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sudou’s plan had already fallen apart.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Anyway, that is all. We’ll make our final judgment next Tuesday, taking into account any eyewitnesses and evidence. With that, let’s end homeroom for the day.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira-sensei left, and Sudou followed right after her. He probably realized that if he stayed in the classroom, he’d lose his temper with someone.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Man, isn’t Sudou just the worst?” Ike was the first to talk.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If we lose points because of Sudou, will we be at zero again this month?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As uproar enveloped the classroom, and things started to get out of hand. If we ended up losing points because of this, Sudou would most likely become the sole target of our class’s frustrations. Naturally, Kushida didn’t want this to happen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Everyone, can you please listen to what I have to say?” Kushida stood and tried to silence the commotion.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What our teacher said seems to be true. Sudou-kun may have been in a fight. However, Sudou-kun was dragged into it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Kushida-chan, what do you mean? Do you believe Sudou?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida conveyed yesterday’s story to the whole class. She told them how Sudou had been chosen as a candidate to play regularly on the basketball team. She also described how some students in the basketball club were envious of Sudou, and how they’d called Sudou out and threatened him in order to make him leave the team. She also explained that Sudou, acting in self-defense, had punched their lights out. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Most of the class listened to Kushida’s heartfelt words in silence. If Sudou or I had tried to explain the situation in the same manner, we probably wouldn’t have been nearly as effective. However, not everyone in class easily believed the story. Sudou’s typically poor behavior made it difficult to swallow, no matter how reasonable it sounded.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’d like to ask you all again. If anyone knows someone who saw what happened, whether it’s someone in class, a friend, or an upperclassman, please tell me. You can contact me at any time. I would sincerely appreciate it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though she’d basically said the same thing as Chabashira-sensei, the class had a very different response. She had an innate ability to connect with people. Her presence glowed so bright that I could almost feel it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Instantly, silence enveloped the classroom. The first to speak wasn’t the eyewitness, but Yamauchi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, Kushida-chan. I just can’t believe what Sudou said. I think he lied so he could justify what he did. He talked about beating up kids all the time in junior high. He even told us how much fun it was to beat people up.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After Yamauchi voiced his concerns, the rest of the class murmured their dissatisfaction with Sudou in turn.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I saw him grab some kid by the collar just because they bumped into each other in the hallway.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I saw him cut in line in the cafeteria and lash out when someone tried to tell him that wasn’t okay.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida’s appeal for Sudou’s innocence didn’t seem to have reached anyone. They’d already hung Sudou out to dry, because he was probably going to lose them their hard-earned points.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I want to believe him.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata, the class hero, uttered those words as he stood in support of Kushida. His appearance was dashing, and he clearly wasn’t drunk on the same anti-Sudou Kool-Aid as everyone else.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If a student in another class were casting doubt on him, I could understand it,” Hirata said. “But I think it’s wrong to doubt a fellow classmate right away. Shouldn’t friends do everything within their power to help someone in need?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I agree!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Karuizawa, heroic Hirata’s girlfriend, called out in agreement, brushing her bangs aside with her hand as she spoke.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If it were a false charge, that’d be a problem, wouldn’t it? In any case, you’d feel bad for him if he were innocent, right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If Kushida lived by the softness of her heart, then Karuizawa lived by the strength of her will. Perhaps it was because of Kushida and Karuizawa’s influence that many of the girls started to express their support.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This was typical of Japanese people. They would follow suit when someone took the lead. Though they probably mocked him in their hearts, they would help Sudou a little bit. Sudou’s critics stopped, at least for the time being. Hirata, Kushida, and Karuizawa had attracted the rest of our class’s admiration.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll try asking my friends!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well then, I’ll try asking the upperclassmen I know in the soccer club!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll ask around, too.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Starting with those three, we launched our investigation to prove Sudou’s innocence. Well, I guessed I didn’t have to take a turn. I wouldn’t be any good at this, anyway. Better to just leave it to them and fade out quietly.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-before: always;"></div>
<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">3.1</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">“I</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> planned</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;"> </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">to fade out… But…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Lunch. For some reason, I’d gotten mixed up with our usual group in the cafeteria. Our group consisted of myself, Kushida, Horikita, Ike, Yamauchi, and Sudou. There was no getting around it. When lunchtime came, Kushida had invited me with a smile. She’d said, “Want to get lunch?” I’d said okay, of course. I mean, I didn’t have any choice in the matter.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You seem to get yourself into trouble time and time again, Sudou-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita gave an exasperated sigh. Naturally, we were discussing how to prove Sudou’s innocence.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, guess we don’t have much choice. As your friends, we’ll help you out, Sudou.” Although Ike had been against Sudou in the beginning, his attitude had completely changed. That was definitely because Kushida had appealed to him and asked him to help. Ignorant of Ike’s true feelings on the matter, Sudou still apologized.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sorry, Horikita. I’ve caused trouble for you again. But it really wasn’t my fault this time. All I did was blow up those Class C jerks’ plans.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sudou spoke to Horikita with indifference, almost as if he were describing someone else’s problems.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry, but I don’t feel like helping you this time.” Horikita flatly refused Sudou’s pleas for help. “For Class D to rise through the ranks, it’s important to recover the class points we lost as quickly as possible. However, we probably won’t get any points now, thanks to you. You hindered those plans.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait. You might be right about that, but I’m not the one at fault here! Those guys picked a fight with me! What part of that is my fault?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You keep focusing on who started the fight, but that’s a trivial detail. Did you never consider that?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What’s trivial about it? It makes all the difference! I&#160;didn’t do anything wrong!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is that so? Well, best of luck to you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita picked up her untouched tray and stood.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So you’re not gonna help? I thought we were friends!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t make me laugh. I’ve never once considered you a friend. Nothing makes me more uncomfortable than being around someone who doesn’t recognize his own stupidity. Goodbye.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita appeared more exasperated than angry. She let out a deep sigh, and left.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What the hell is her deal?! Goddamn it!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Unable to direct his rage anywhere else, Sudou slammed his fists on the cafeteria table, spilling a nearby student’s miso soup. The student glared, but when he saw how scary Sudou looked, he remained silent. Yep. I could understand that feeling pretty well.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I guess it’s just up to us.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I knew for sure you’d understand, Yamauchi. I’m really counting on you too, Ayanokouji.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Apparently I was second to Yamauchi, judging by the “too.” Well, I guess I didn’t find that particularly surprising.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even if you ask me to help, you do know that there’s not much I can do, right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">My self-deprecation was apparently ineffective.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’ve been like that since yesterday, Ayanokouji-kun. Won’t you say something, Ike-kun?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I…I mean, it’s certainly odd that Ayanokouji thinks he’s not going be useful. At least him being there is better than not, I guess? Probably?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Just as expected, Ike couldn’t come up with how I’d be useful. I looked over at Kushida smugly. It was like I was showing off a talentless person’s power.</span></p>

She wasn’t the type to stop for anything, so she probably headed straight towards the dorms. I made my way past students putting on their shoes, getting ready to go home. Most people strolled in groups of two or more as they headed back, but I glimpsed a lone figure walking with confidence. It was Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Horikita-san.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I hesitated, but Horikita responded at once.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What is it?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Apparently she hadn’t thought we’d chase after her. She turned, looking surprised.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I really want you to help us with Sudou’s case. Is that possible?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I thought I already declined? A few minutes ago, no less.” Horikita shrugged, as if expressing how idiotic she found us.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I know you did, but…but, I think this is necessary to reach Class A.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Necessary to reach Class A, hmm?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita looked unconvinced. She didn’t seem to be listening to Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re free to run about for Sudou-kun’s sake. I’ve no right to stop you. However, if you need help, try someone else. I’m busy,” Horikita said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Busy? But everyone else is busy with Sudou’s case right now,” I blurted out, earning a glare from Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Her eyes seemed to say, </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Why are you still talking?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I have an important daily routine, so I need my alone time. It’s unpleasant to have that time stolen from me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Just the answer I would expect from an unabashed misanthrope. If she’d simply said she didn’t like spending time with other people, that would’ve sounded like an excuse.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even if I step in and save him now, he’ll land himself in trouble again. It’s a vicious cycle, isn’t it? You seem to think that Sudou-kun is the victim here, but I think differently.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? But </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">isn’t</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> Sudou-kun the victim? Besides, it would be bad if he were lying.” Kushida didn’t seem to understand Horikita’s meaning.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Maybe the Class C students really did start this fight, but Sudou-kun was also one of the perpetrators.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“W-wait. What do you mean? Wasn’t Sudou-kun dragged into the fight?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita slowly turned her gaze towards me, her expression saying </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">good grief.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">No, I didn’t say anything. I averted my eyes as if trying to escape. After a silent moment, Horikita spoke in an exasperated voice.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why was he dragged into the fight? This problem will persist until we understand that fundamental question. Got it? I do not wish to help until that question is answered. Since you weren’t able to convince me, why don’t you ask the guy standing next to you? Even though he pretends not to understand what I’m thinking, he probably does.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Please stop saying that I understand you.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> Kushida looked at me, unable to hide her confusion. Her expression seemed to ask what I knew. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Come on, Horikita, don’t say anything unnecessary…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita began to walk off, indicating that the rest was up to me. Her words seemed to have finally made an impression on Kushida, who didn't pursue her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sudou-kun is a perpetrator? Is that…true?” she asked.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-before: always;"></div>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span><img alt="" height="100%" id="ClassroomElite-Vol2_INTERIORILLUSTRATIONS_5" src="/hola-lector-agua/images/books/v2/OEBPS/Images/ClassroomElite-Vol2_INTERIORILLUSTRATIONS_5.jpg" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style=" top: 0px; vertical-align: top; height: 100%;"/></span></p>
<div style="page-break-before: always;"></div>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida turned to me, as if asking me to save him again. Since Horikita had revealed that I was feigning ignorance, things were probably going to be a pain in the butt. Besides, I’d gladly give Kushida my bank </span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">PIN</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> if she asked for it with such a cute expression.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I understand a little of what Horikita meant. At the very least, Sudou does share some fault in this case. He’s&#160;the type who gets angry easily, right? Whenever he’s faced with someone he doesn’t like, he lashes out, and speaks and acts in an aggressive, domineering way. When I heard he was being considered for a regular spot on the basketball team, I was both surprised and impressed. No one disagrees that he’s an incredible player, but if he acts so arrogant and prideful, some people will come to hate him. Those who work incredibly hard for their spot would probably see Sudou as a rather disagreeable person. Then there are the rumors, right? People say that Sudou’s been fighting people since junior high. I haven’t met anyone who knew Sudou at his previous school, but considering how many people are talking about it, there might be something to the rumor.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">People didn’t hold a very good impression of Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This was bound to happen eventually. That’s why Horikita said Sudou was a perpetrator.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So…his typical behavior, plus his repeated actions, led to this situation, then?” Kushida asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. As long as he continues to antagonize those around him, trouble will inevitably follow. Also, if there’s no proof, then people will use his image against him. In other words, they’ll judge him based on their impressions. For example, let’s say there was a murder case. There are two suspects. One of them has committed murder in the past, while the other is a good, upstanding citizen. Based on that information, who would you believe?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If asked, nearly everyone would give the same response.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well…I’d pick the upstanding citizen, of course.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The truth might be different. However, the less information you have upon which to base your judgment, the more you have to rely on whatever meager information you have at hand. That’s what’s happening here. Horikita cannot overlook the fact that Sudou doesn’t recognize his flaws.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t think this was exactly a “you reap what you sow” situation, though.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. So that’s what she meant…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida gave a small nod.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So Horikita-san isn’t going to save Sudou-kun because she wants to teach him a lesson?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I guess so, yeah. By punishing him, she wants him to understand himself better.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida understood, but clearly didn’t agree. It seemed like she was a little angry, balling her fists in anger. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t agree with abandoning Sudou-kun just to punish him. If she’s dissatisfied with him, I think she should at least talk to him directly. That’s what friends would do.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t think that Horikita considered Sudou her friend, though. Besides, Horikita wasn’t the type to teach through kindness. She didn’t feel an obligation to others.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You should act according to your own principles, Kushida. I don’t think it’s wrong to want to help Sudou.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida nodded without hesitation. She would extend her hand to a friend in need as many times as it took. It sounds simple, but it was actually difficult. Only someone like Kushida could do such a thing.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“However, I think it would be better if we carefully considered whether or not to directly point out Sudou’s problems. If he doesn’t actually take the time to reflect, there’s no point. There are certain realizations you can only come to on your own.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay. I understand. I’ll follow your advice, Ayanokouji-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida arched her back and stretched; I’d changed her thinking.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay, let’s go hunt for the witness.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We returned to the classroom, and rejoined Ike and Yamauchi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? So, you couldn’t persuade Horikita?” Ike said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, I’m sorry. I failed,” Kushida replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, no. You don’t have to apologize, Kushida-chan. We should be fine with who we already have.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m counting on you, Ike-kun. Yamauchi-kun,” Kushida appealed with sparkling eyes. The two of them gazed back, love-struck.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay, so where should we start?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Randomly searching for a witness would be ineffective. It would’ve been better to come up with a plan before starting our search.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If no one has any objections, how about we start by asking around Class B?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why Class B?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Because that’s the class that would most want a witness.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry. I don’t quite understand, Ayanokouji-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Between D and C, which class most threatens Class B? Or to put it another way, which class is more likely to threaten B’s place in the ranking?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“C, of course. So we should ask C last, I suppose. But why not start with Class A?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We know too little about Class A. I don’t think they’d want to get involved in a troublesome affair that might negatively impact their points. It’s also possible that Class A students don’t care, as they feel no connection to what goes on between C and D.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Of course, I still didn’t know if we could trust Class B. If they had a particularly cunning person, he or she might have devised a plan to defeat not only C, but Class D as well. Even if that plan didn’t exist, I believed we ought to prepare countermeasures based on that idea.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well then, let’s go to Class B right away!” Kushida cried.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Stop.” I reflexively grabbed the back of Kushida’s collar.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nyaa!” Surprised, Kushida let out a cat-like yelp.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So cuuute!” After seeing Kushida’s adorable reaction, Yamauchi had hearts in his eyes. She was probably being adorable on purpose… Despite thinking that, my heart pounded like crazy.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It is certainly true that your excellent communication skills are indispensable. However, this isn’t the same as casually strolling into another class and trying to make friends.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You think so?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If the witness was willing to help Class D for nothing, or if they were friendly, then there’d be no need to worry. However, it the witness was a calculating person, then he or she might not agree to help. We wouldn’t know whether that person would assist Class D unless we tried asking, though. Even if we went to Class B to talk…how would things turn out?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you know anyone in Class B?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I do. I’ve only gotten to know a few people, though,” Kushida said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well then, let’s talk to those people first then.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We absolutely did not want word to get out that Class D was frantically searching for witnesses.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait, ask them one by one? Wouldn’t it be way easier to ask everyone at the same time?” said Ike. He didn’t seem to like this roundabout way of doing things.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I also think you’re being too negative. I do think it’s a good idea to ask Class B, but I also think we should ask several people at once. If not, we may not find the witness in time.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. You might be right about that. We should do what you think is best, Kushida.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sorry, Ayanokouji-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida clasped her hands together apologetically.  She hadn’t really done anything wrong, though. It was only natural that we’d have different opinions on the matter. Besides, in times like these the majority should decide what to do. Kushida’s plan had convinced me, so I withdrew my own. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Suddenly, I felt a sensation, like someone was watching me. I turned around. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">About one third of our class remained in the room. Nothing here seemed especially odd. Still, I couldn’t quite put my finger on exactly what was bothering me, or who’d given me the feeling of being watched.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-before: always;"></div>
<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">3.3</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">he first classroom we visited had a slightly different atmosphere. Although it looked fundamentally the same as ours, it felt as though we had come to an alien place. It was a bit like soccer; clearly the difference between a home and an away game in soccer was no trivial thing. We also didn’t know if the students around us were friend or foe. Even Ike and Yamauchi appeared to be shaking in their boots. They simply stood frozen in the classroom doorway, unable to move.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida was the only one who remained unfazed. In fact, she found her Class B friends and, with a smile on her face, waved to them and headed over. What an incredible attitude. I wanted to learn how to be like that. She chatted with people regardless of gender, exactly how she acted in Class D.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">No one was more jealous of this than Ike and Yamauchi. Kushida was happily chatting with people who were clearly more attractive than they.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“D-damn it! There are way too many guys after my Kushida-chan. This sucks!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">What was he talking about? </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">His</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> Kushida?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t panic, Ike. It’s okay. We’re in Kushida-chan’s class, so we’re one step ahead of them!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The pair, comrades in vexation, grasped each other’s arms. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Although there were only about 10 people left in the classroom, Kushida began explaining Sudou’s case. All things considered, the atmosphere in Class B wasn’t that different from Class D.  Certainly not what I’d expect from a class full of honor students. They didn’t seem especially formal. In fact, many of the students seemed to do as they pleased. Although they were free to act however they wanted within school regulations, I’d expected their hair and clothing to be a bit more reserved. Instead, however, some students had dyed hair, and…well…certain girls wore rather short skirts.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As the saying goes, you shouldn’t judge a book by its cover. Or perhaps they were superior to Class D in more than simple academics.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This school was far too mysterious. Thinking about these things was a pain in the butt. Anyway, I had only come here to accompany Kushida today, so I thought it best to leave things to her. I moved further away from the doorway to evade Ike and Yamauchi’s notice.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wanna go home.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t want them to hear me grumbling to myself. From outside the window, I could see the Track and Field Club running and working up a sweat. The air conditioning inside school was especially effective, so I didn’t really feel like going out.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wow, those guys in the sports club really work hard, huh?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ike, having scouted around Class B, joined me in looking out the window. He was an especially capricious person, so waiting probably bored him. “I think people who participate in clubs are stupid,” he said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why would you say that? You know such a statement will alienate about half of the students here, right?” I didn’t know the precise numbers, but I estimated at least 60 to 70 percent of the students in this school participated in clubs.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you like exercising, then what’s wrong with just doing it as a hobby? What benefit is there to undergoing such a harsh regimen?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I thought it odd to view club activities solely in terms of benefits or drawbacks. Besides, there were many benefits to participating in a club. You gained the skills necessary to build relationships with other people, and you also got to experience victory and defeat. These were things you couldn’t learn just by studying alone. Besides, someone who never participated in clubs and only went straight home after class could probably stand to learn that lesson.  </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re probably right,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I waited for a few minutes until I received Kushida’s report. I certainly didn’t expect what she told me.</span></p>
