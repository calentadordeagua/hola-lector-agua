<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 4:<br/>An Unexpected Witness</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">he next morning, students busily exchanged information with each other. Those from Hirata’s group and those with Kushida had spent yesterday searching for the witness. Ike and Yamauchi hated ladies’ men like Hirata, but seemed excited about all of the girls who hung around him. They happily tried to chat them up. As I listened in, though, I heard that Hirata and his group hadn’t obtained any valuable information. They’d recorded the names of the people to whom they’d spoken, and occasionally taken notes on their cell phones.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Meanwhile, I was alone, like always. I could talk to Kushida, but felt disadvantaged when it came to a big group. I couldn’t really talk, so I asked Kushida to fill in me later. Meanwhile, my neighbor—who continued to reject Kushida’s invitations, no matter what—sat with a nonchalant expression as she prepared for class. Sudou, the person of interest, still hadn’t arrived.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Man, can we even prove that those Class C guys were in the wrong?” Ike asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“As long as we can find a witness, it’s not impossible. Let’s keep trying our best, Ike-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Before we try our best, though, is there even a witness in the first place? Didn’t Sudou only say that he </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">thought</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> someone might’ve been there? Wasn’t that just a lie? I&#160;mean, he </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">is</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> violent, and he does provoke people.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If we keep doubting him, we won’t make any progress. Am I wrong?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I guess, you’re probably right about that, but…if Sudou is in the wrong, then our hard-earned points are all going be stripped away, right? We’ll be at zero. Zero! We’ll be back to having no pocket money at all. Our dreams of screwing around to our hearts’ content will remain unrealized!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Then it would be a good idea for everyone to start saving up again,” Hirata said. “It’s only been three months since we started here.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Our classroom hero didn’t waver as he delivered his magnificent speech. The girls immediately blushed in response. Karuizawa wore a particularly proud expression, perhaps because </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">she</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> was the one he’d chosen to be his girlfriend.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I do think our points are important. They’re tied to our motivation, right? So, I will defend our class points to my last breath. Even if it’s only 87 points.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I understand how you feel. However, it can be dangerous to cling to our points and lose sight of reality. The most important thing is to treasure our friends as much as possible.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ike, who considered Hirata a goody two shoes, eyed him suspiciously. “Even if Sudou was at fault?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Being punished when you’d done nothing wrong was horrible. That much was obvious. However, Hirata nodded without a moment’s hesitation. It was as though he believed self-sacrifice was unimportant. Ike immediately looked downward, as if pressed under the weight of Hirata’s noble intentions.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I think what you’re saying is reasonable, Hirata-kun, but I still want my points. The students in Class A get nearly 100,000 points every month. I’m super envious of them. There are girls in their class who buy lots of stylish clothes and accessories. Aren’t we just the bottom of the barrel compared to them?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Karuizawa’s legs dangled over her desk. People looked bitter when she pointed out the stark difference between our classes.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why couldn’t I have been in Class A from the start? If I were in Class A, I’d probably be loving every second of my student life.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wish I was in A, too. I’d be doing so much fun stuff with my friends.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Before I knew it, the meeting to save Sudou had transformed into a grievance session, with students pleading for a way out of the class. Horikita spontaneously broke into derisive laughter in response to Ike and Karuizawa’s delusions. As her neighbor, I was the only one who noticed. She seemed to be implying that they couldn’t have started in Class A if they’d wanted to. Horikita immediately took out a library book and began reading, almost as if she was trying not to be distracted by the noise. At a glance, I saw she was reading Dostoevsky’s </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Demons</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">. A good choice.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It would be awesome if there was some secret trick we could use to switch Class A in an instant. Saving up class points is way too hard.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There was a 1000-point difference between Class A and us. An absurdly huge difference.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Then cheer up, Ike, because there </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">is</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> one way to instantly reach Class A.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira-sensei spoke from the classroom entranceway. She’d arrived just five minutes before class started.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait. What did you say, sensei?” Ike nearly fell off his chair before recomposing himself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I said that there’s a way to reach Class A without class points.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita looked up from her book, probably trying to gauge whether Chabashira-sensei was lying.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Come on, now. Don’t tease us, Sae-chan-sensei!” Usually, Ike would’ve sunk his teeth into that information. This time, he laughed it off, as if saying he wouldn’t be fooled.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s true. At this school, there are certain special methods you can use,” answered Chabashira-sensei. Judging from her answer, she didn’t appear to be joking.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t think she’s saying that just to confuse us.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira-sensei sometimes withheld information, but she didn’t lie. Ike’s guffaws gradually stopped.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sensei, what </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">are</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> these special methods?” Ike inquired politely, as if not to offend her. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">All of the students fixed their eyes on Chabashira-sensei. Even those who didn’t see a substantial benefit to reaching Class A appeared curious.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I told you on the day you arrived. I said there’s nothing you cannot buy with your points at this school. In other words, you can change classes by using your personal points.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira-sensei shot a quick glance over at Horikita and me. We had tested out that very method ourselves by buying a test point from the school. That supported the truth of her claim. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Class points and private points were linked. If we didn’t have any class points, then we wouldn’t get the private points, either. However, it wasn’t a perfect one-to-one correlation. Based on what we’d heard, you didn’t necessarily have to lose out on points. Because students could transfer points, it was theoretically possible to collect private points even if your class points were at&#160;zero.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“S-seriously! How many points do we need to save up to do that?!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Twenty million. Do your best to conserve points. Do that, and you can enter whichever class you like.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After hearing that absurdly high number, Ike tumbled out of his chair.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Did you just say twenty million? That’s impossible, though!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Everyone in the class started booing. Their disappointment was to be expected. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Normally yes, it would be impossible. However, as it’s an avenue to Class A, it’s naturally expensive. If I were reduce the number by one digit, there would probably be over 100 people in Class A come graduation. Then there’d be no point to our system.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even by maintaining our monthly allotment of 100,000 points, that still wouldn’t be an easy number to reach.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Just out of curiosity, has anyone successfully changed their class like this before?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was an obvious question. The Advanced Nurturing High School had been in operation for about 10 years. A thousand and some students had struggled to make it through. If anyone had managed to achieve success by that method, there would’ve been talk about it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Unfortunately, no. No one has done it before. The reason why is as clear as day. Even if you perfectly maintain your class points upon starting school here, after three years you’d only have 3.6 million. Class A could raise points enough to reach four million. Normally, you simply wouldn’t be able to do it.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, doesn’t that just mean it’s impossible?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">almost</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> impossible. However, that does not mean it actually </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">is</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> impossible. There’s a big difference, Ike.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, around half the class had already lost interest in what she was saying. For students in Class D, who dreamed of a mere 100 or 200 private points, reaching 20 million was a faraway dream. It was beyond the scope of our imagination.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“May I ask one question, please?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The watchful Horikita raised her hand. She looked quite determined, considering this was a potential road to Class A.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What is the highest number of points that a student has saved since the opening of this school? I’m just asking for the sake of reference.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“A very good question, Horikita. About three years ago, one student from Class B saved nearly 12 million points before graduating.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“T-twelve million?! A student from Class B?!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“He was expelled before graduation, however, so he was unable to save those 20 million points in the end. He was engaged in a large-scale fraudulent operation to save up points.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Fraudulent?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“He took advantage of the newly admitted first-year students who didn’t yet understand the system. He went to them one by one and scammed points out of them, so he could reach the 20 million points necessary to move to Class A. There was no way the school could overlook such reckless actions, however. I don’t think that his goal was particularly bad, but people who break the rules must be punished.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was more than a mere anecdote. That story made the possibility of success sound truly impossible.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you’re saying that even if we were to use criminal methods, 12 million points is about the limit?” Horikita muttered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Give up on that method. Strive to save up your points responsibly.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita returned to reading, likely feeling idiotic for bothering to raise her hand at all. In this world, stories that seemed too good to be true were just that.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, that reminds me. None of you have gotten any points yet from club activities, have you?” remarked&#160;Chabashira-sensei, with the air of suddenly remembering.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do you mean?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There are cases where individuals may win points based on their efforts in club activities, or their degree of participation. For example, if someone in a calligraphy club were to win a contest, they could receive corresponding points for that award.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This new information took everyone aback.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Y-you mean we can get points for participating in clubs?!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. The other classes should have already been well informed of this.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wh-what the hell? That’s so mean! Why didn’t you tell us earlier?!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I simply forgot, sorry. However, extracurricular activities aren’t there just as a way to earn points. So it shouldn’t make any difference when you learned this information,” said Chabashira-sensei without a hint of timidity.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, no, no! That’s not true at all! If you’d told me before, I—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you saying you would have participated in extracurricular activities? Do you really think you would’ve been able to see any results after joining a club, results like winning prizes or being in games, with such shallow feelings?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well…you might be right about that, but… It was possible!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I understood both Chabashira-sensei and Ike’s viewpoints. If someone were to join a club simply for the sake of earning points, he or she probably wouldn’t see great results. If anything, joining a club for such a lazy, indifferent reason would probably make you a nuisance for the students who </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">were</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> serious about it. On the other hand, someone who joined the club for the sake of points might discover that they possessed a talent for it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">At any rate, I deduced that our homeroom teacher was being extremely and deliberately mean.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You know, if you think back on it now, it was obvious from the start.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do you mean, Hirata-kun?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Think back. Remember what our P.E. instructor Higashiyama-sensei said when we went to the pool? He said the student who got the best time during our first class would get 5000 points. That was a stepping stone to prepare us for other opportunities. It seems plausible, right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ike scratched his head and slumped. “I don’t remember,” he said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If I got points for it, I probably would’ve joined calligraphy club, or some other kind of art club.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It seemed like Ike could only see the positive side of things. I thought there naturally must be a downside.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There might be cases where if someone didn’t participate in their club seriously, he or she would be penalized. The easy route would probably destroy you. However, learning that our points would reflect the efforts we made in club activities was encouraging.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Horikita. Doesn’t this mean there’s some value in saving Sudou?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We should save him because he’s in a club?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sudou told us that he may be picked as a regular on the team even though he’s a first year, right?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita gave a small nod.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If he was telling the truth…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She sounded somewhat doubtful.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s better to have a lot of private points, right? He can supplement his own grade if he fails, and he can save others.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I have a hard time imagining that he’d use his points altruistically, though.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m saying that it’s better to have points saved up, just in case. Right?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Whether class points or private points, it was better to have a lot of them. There were absolutely no negatives to that. Also, we knew very little about how else to earn points at this stage. If our chances improved with Sudou in the class, then that was reason enough to make an effort. Horikita fell silent. Even she didn’t have the ability to create any points for us right now.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I won’t say that I’ll help, but I suppose I ought to acknowledge Sudou’s existence, at least a little.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita’s language was harsh, but she acknowledged the connection to her own interests. I considered it unnecessary to say more, so I stopped talking. Horikita pondered the issue in silence.</span></p>

<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">4.1</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">O</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">ur class became temporarily excited by that fairy tale, but we were soon pulled back to reality. After school we resumed the search for a witness. I hung behind Kushida, Ike, and the others, trailing them like a ghost. I was impressed, surprised, and in awe of their natural conversation skills. It was clear as day that I, someone who couldn’t even converse with my fellow classmates, was not well suited to this task. How could they talk with people they’d never met before and behave like old friends? Monsters.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In this investigation, we sought not just names, but also contact information. Perhaps they felt compelled to give Kushida their information because of her personality. What a wonderful talent…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Though Kushida and the others spent a good amount of time going to the second-year classrooms and talking to upperclassmen, they didn’t turn up any good leads. As time passed, the number of students who remained after class was dwindling. With a lack of new students to speak with, we decided to stop our investigation for the day.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Looks like we didn’t have any luck today, either.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Everyone went back to my room to reevaluate our strategy. Sudou came by shortly afterward and joined our discussion.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What happened today? Did you make any progress?” he asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“None at all. Sudou, are you sure there was a witness?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I understood Ike’s doubts. Even though the school had said that’s what happened, there wasn’t any new information.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? I never said there was someone there. I said that it </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">felt</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> like someone was there.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh. Is that so?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s certainly true that Sudou-kun didn’t say he saw anyone. He said that he had a feeling that someone was there.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Maybe Sudou was hallucinating? He must be taking some pretty potent drugs or something.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Man, that was going a bit too far. Sudou put Ike into a headlock.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Gyahh! I give, I give!” Ike cried.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">While the two of them messed around, Kushida and Yamauchi were wracking their brains. After discussing the matter for about 10 minutes, Kushida spoke up.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We might want to change our methods a little. For example, let’s look for someone who might have found a witness.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Look for someone who </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">found</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> a witness? I don’t get what you mean.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you going to look for the people who went to the special building on the day of the incident?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. What do you think?”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Professor, did you buy your laptop computer from the school, with your points?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, I most certainly did. It cost 80,000 points. But what of it?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m looking for something among the electrical appliances sold by the school."</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I explained the gist of what I wanted. Although many similar products sat on the displays in front of me, I&#160;didn’t know which one to choose. It probably would’ve been faster to just ask the store clerk, but I didn’t want to for various reasons.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ayanokouji. Though I am considerably well-versed in that particular electronic field…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s okay if you don’t know.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Please wait,” said the Professor as I was about to end the call. “I </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">do</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> know. In fact, I have two of them at my parents’ home.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No way! You’ve had them since junior high? Isn’t that bad?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do not misunderstand me. They were only for experimentation, for the sake of my language studies.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, could I trouble you to help me set it up?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Puh, leave it to me. I’m sure that someday I will have a favor to ask of you in return.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Clearly, he was the man for the job. When entering a subject that I didn’t understand, it was important to find an expert.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry to keep you waiting,” I said to the girls.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Have you already finished?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Today was just a preliminary inspection. I don’t have enough points to buy anything.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Suddenly, Kushida froze while glancing over at Sakura’s profile.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sakura-san, have we met somewhere before?” Kushida murmured.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? N-no. I don’t think so, but…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry. It’s just that when I look at you, I get this feeling that we’ve met before, Sakura-san. Hey, weird question, but could you try removing your glasses?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh?! B-but that’s… My eyesight is so bad, I wouldn’t be able to see a thing…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakura put her hand up and waved it, signaling to Kushida that she did not want to.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We should hang out together again, Sakura-san. Not just with me, but with my other friends, too.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakura looked like she wanted to say something, but she couldn’t find her way to the end of the thought. She said nothing. Kushida seemed to feel that it would make trouble if she pressed the issue, so she kept quiet. Or rather, she didn’t ask anything else. In the end, we returned to where we’d started.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Umm… Thank you for everything today. You really helped me out,” Sakura said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s okay, it’s okay. There’s no need to thank us. Actually, Sakura-san, you can talk to us normally, you know? If it’s okay with you. We’re in the same grade. It sounds a little weird when you speak so formally to us.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was certainly true that Sakura’s speech patterns weren’t exactly what you would expect from a peer. But changing that might be easier said than done for her; she was visibly perplexed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I didn’t mean to sound that way. I wasn’t aware of it… I sound weird?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s not really a bad thing! I mean, I’d be happy if you didn’t speak so formally to me, though.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah… O-okay… I…I got it. I’ll do my best.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I thought that Sakura would have rejected the notion, but she managed to squeak out a few words of agreement. It seemed like she wanted to accept Kushida’s proposal. Perhaps this was how people became friends, little by little. Even Sakura, who seemed to have hardly interacted with another person ever, was steadily growing closer to Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s okay, though. You don’t need to force yourself.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I-It’s okay. I…will.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakura kept her eyes down as she spoke. Partway through her sentence she began to sputter, and her words faded so much that we couldn’t hear them. However, it didn’t seem as though she felt uncomfortable. Kushida smiled in satisfaction, but didn’t try to coerce anything more from Sakura. That specific distance felt exactly right for where they were at the moment. If you tried to strong-arm people who weren’t good at socializing, it could backfire. Rather than be grateful, they’d probably find it off-putting. Rather than draw them nearer, being overbearing would probably end up pushing them further away.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well then, we’ll see you at school. Okay?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">With that, Kushida thought the conversation had ended. However, rather unexpectedly, Sakura didn’t move.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakura spoke in a small voice, but looked straight at us. When our gazes met, however, she immediately averted her eyes.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“About Sudou-kun… As my way of saying thanks for today, I… Well, that might be a little misleading, but if you like…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She paused, and started again more clearly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I-I may be able to help you with Sudou-kun’s case.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In her own words, Sakura told us that she was the witness. Kushida and I exchanged glances.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, does that mean that you saw Sudou-kun fight with those other students?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. I saw everything. It was a total coincidence, though… I’m sure you don’t believe me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, we do. Why did you decide to tell us now, though? I mean, I’m happy you did, but I don’t want you to force yourself. You don’t have to do this just because you’re grateful, you know?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakura couldn’t seem to get her words out. She lightly shook her head. The fact that Sakura had waited until now to talk proved that she was more concerned about Sudou’s case than anything else. I wondered if getting a foothold on friendship made her want to cooperate.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is that really true? You’re not forcing yourself?” Kushida asked. She must’ve been thinking the same thing as me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakura nodded sheepishly, as if she could feel that we were worrying about it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s okay… I think that if I kept quiet, I’d probably come to regret it. I…don’t want to cause trouble for my classmates. But, if I spoke up as a witness, then…I would definitely stand out. I hated the thought of that… I’m sorry.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">While Sakura apologized to us repeatedly, full of remorse, she also promised Kushida that she would testify.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thank you, Sakura-san. I’m sure Sudou-kun will be really happy.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida took Sakura’s hand, and Sakura looked at Kushida’s smiling face. I wondered if a new friendship had been born right here, right now. At any rate, we had Sudou’s witness.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-before: always;"></div>
<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">4.11</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">hat night, I clutched my phone tightly. My hand was sweating so much you’d think the air conditioning in my room wasn’t working.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We got closer to Sakura, but… Is it really okay for me to say that?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yesterday I would’ve said no, but our chances are better today. Ahh… I think we still have a way to go yet, though. You’re making yourself all frazzled.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I’d guessed that Sakura would probably grow closer to Kushida, specifically. But I had a feeling that Sakura had erected a rather high wall between herself and other people. Unless we could get her to climb over that wall, calling on Sakura as a witness would be difficult.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That reminds me, why did you try to get Sakura to take off her glasses?” I asked Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I mean… I thought saying so might be kind of cruel, but… I just feel like her glasses don’t really suit her, for some reason. It’s like she doesn’t really need them, or something. I don’t understand it myself. I also thought that we’d met somewhere before, but that was probably just a misunderstanding.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, perhaps that was just your imagination, Kushida? I mean, Sakura is pretty far from being stylish, right? I mean, so am I, but she even chooses clothes with plain colors so she stands out as little as possible.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, there’s that. I don’t think that she’s concerned with fashion or anything. But I wonder why?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Back when her camera fell and Sakura had bent down to pick it up, I’d seen her glasses from the side. Something about them had struck me as being out of place.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I felt like there’s something a little off, like she’s wearing fake glasses.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? Sakura-san wears fake glasses? But she said that her eyesight was really bad…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Although real glasses and fake ones appear similar at first glance, there is definitely a difference between them. Real glasses show some distortion on the lenses. There wasn’t any distortion on Sakura’s glasses. At first, I thought that there was definitely some link between those fake glasses and Sakura’s fashion sense, but then I found myself puzzled over something she said today.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Appearing fashionable with glasses? Hmm, that doesn’t sound normal.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If she wanted to liven up her appearance with decorative items, she should have bought other clothes or make-up.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Or maybe it’s to cover up some kind of complex? Like how someone thinks they’ll appear intelligent by putting on glasses?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s that. Wearing glasses </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">does </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">make you look smart.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In Sakura’s case, though, she probably wears them because she doesn’t want others to see her true self. She’s always slouching and won’t look people in the eye. I doubt it’s simply because she dislikes other people.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I felt like there was some hidden way to get over that wall. Something.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I knew it was right to bring you along, Ayanokouji-kun. I feel like you’re very observant of people.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was a little embarrassed. The best part of interacting with Kushida was how we were able to connect and converse naturally. People who didn’t know how to get closer to another person would bungle and compromise until they got to a point where they just gave up.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So then—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Just as Kushida was about to gently nudge me to keep the conversation going, my phone buzzed. I checked the caller </span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">ID</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> without letting her see. If it were Ike or Yamauchi, I’d call them back later. But if it were Horikita…I’d have to think about it. That’s what I was prepared for, but…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The name on the screen read “Sakura.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sorry, Kushida. Can I call you back in a bit?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, sure. Sorry for talking so long.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Though there was a heavy regret in those parting words, I didn’t have the time to address it. I answered Sakura’s call before it cut out. After pressing the call button, I waited several seconds, but the line remained silent.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Um… Hello. This is Sakura…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This is Ayanokouji.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though we’d exchanged contact information, I&#160;found it a little strange that she’d called me. Even when I formally exchanged contact information with someone, they would never call me nine times out of ten. If I did need to contact someone, I'd just have Kushida do it for me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thank you for coming out with me today,” Sakura said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, no problem. It wasn’t really a big deal. Don’t worry about it. You shouldn’t fuss so much and keep thanking me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Silence followed, but it wasn’t Sakura’s fault. I didn’t really know how to respond to her. I thought of how Kushida took the lead in our conversation. Still, I had to do my best on this call.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What’s the matter?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Umm…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">More silence. What should I do? </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Please, Hirata. Teach me.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What were you…thinking about?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakura asked me a rather ambiguous question. What was I thinking about? She likely didn’t want to know my thoughts on how cute Kushida looked in casual clothing, or how exceptionally interesting I found Sakura herself. I&#160;had no idea what Sakura was expecting.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Did something happen?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Something about the emotion behind her words made me uneasy, so I cast a verbal line to see if I could reel in anything else. However, the line tautened and snapped as soon as it touched water. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sorry, it’s nothing. Goodnight.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakura ended the call without even giving me a chance to reply. No “please wait” or “hold on.” I thought about calling her back, but I couldn’t understand why I’d failed in our conversation. I thought about it carefully while washing my face. I’d spent about 10 minutes talking with Kushida, but during that time, there weren’t any signs that Sakura had tried to call or left messages. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Perhaps Sakura had planned on calling Kushida after talking to me? I had a hard time imagining that. Normally when you had to call two people, the first person you’d call would be the one you knew better. In this case, I was the only person she could call and see, so I was the reasonable choice. Just to be sure though, I went ahead and sent a chat message to Kushida and asked her if she had heard from Sakura.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">A few minutes later, Kushida confirmed that she hadn’t heard back from Sakura. Just as I’d thought.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I was asked to invite you as well, Ayanokouji-kun. Did you talk to Sakura-san?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When I’d met Kushida that morning, she’d said something like that. Because Sakura got really nervous when she was alone with Kushida, I had thought she’d just invited someone else who was suitable for the task, but…was it not like that? Aside from a crazy dream like it being love at first sight, was there some reason </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> had been chosen to go? I remembered something I’d felt while talking with Sakura today.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakura and Kushida struck up most of the conversations, but I’d broached a topic. Namely, the clerk at the store who’d helped with the repair order. I hadn’t brought up anything else. What if that’s what she’d meant when she asked, “What were you thinking about?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">All of the puzzle pieces I’d collected were too small, and too few. I was able to conjure up several scenarios and speculations, but they all lacked credibility. I didn’t have enough information to come to a definitive decision.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Normally I’d have thought that asking around at school would be fine, but in Sakura’s case, things wouldn’t be so simple. If I just went up and started talking to Sakura, who normally didn’t talk to anyone, it would make her stand out. She wouldn’t like that. I prayed that the anxieties I’d developed over the phone call were unfounded, and decided to get ready for bed.</span></p>
