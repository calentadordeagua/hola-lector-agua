<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 2:<br/>The Sudden Beginning of<br/>Our Tumultuous Troubles</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">he timing couldn’t have been worse.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">While searching for a place to take a good selfie, I stumbled into something. Even a certain famous, diminutive detective would have held his breath when witnessing such a tense situation. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The whole thing had started about ten seconds ago. Someone made a trivial comment, which upset the other party. That led to vicious insults, which turned into a fistfight. No, a “fight” wasn’t the right way of putting it. The other three male students lay on the floor, writhing in pain. A red-haired boy stood over them, looking down in victory. It was an entirely one-sided ordeal.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">His right fist was covered in blood from the students he’d pummeled. This was the first brawl I’d ever witnessed. In elementary school I saw boys quarrel with each other in class, pulling clothes and pinching arms. This was different, though. I could feel the tension in the air.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Though I was terrified, I began capturing the scene with my camera. The shutter didn’t make a sound. After taking the pictures, I asked myself what I was doing. I&#160;couldn’t think clearly in my panicked state. I tried to quickly get away. However, my brain no longer seemed to function properly. My legs didn’t obey my command to move, like I was paralyzed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“He he, so. Do you really think that this is the end of it, Sudou?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Despite being barely able to move, one of the male students on the ground tried to taunt Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you wanna make me laugh? You’re in the sorriest state possible. You want to go for another round, huh? Next time I won’t hold back.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sudou-kun grabbed the beaten boy’s collar, and brought him closer. They were eye-to-eye now, only a few centimeters apart. Sudou looked as though he was going to kill and then devour his opponent, which was so overwhelming that the defeated boy looked away.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you scared? Did you really think you’d beat me if you had more people?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sudou-kun snorted, dropped the student, picked up his bag, and then turned and walked away as if the defeated three completely disinterested him. My heart rate skyrocketed. Well, that was natural. Sudou-kun was headed for my hiding spot. My potential escape routes from this building were limited.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had the idea to turn back down the staircase I’d used to come up here. However, I still couldn’t move, and my window of opportunity was closing. I’d heard that that when someone was involved in a crisis, his or her body would lock up, exactly like what was happening now.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What a waste of time. Tiring me out after practice. Give me a break,” Sudou-kun said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The distance between us was closing. He was only a few short meters away.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re the one who’s going to regret this later, Sudou.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The boy’s words stopped Sudou-kun in his tracks. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nothing’s more pathetic than a sore loser. No matter how many times you come at me, you won’t win.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He wasn’t bluffing.  He clearly had the confidence to back up what he said. After all, Sudou-kun had emerged victorious and unscathed from a three-on-one fight. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Tomorrow was the first of July, but given how much I was sweating you’d think summer was already here. I remained completely still in my hiding place. Sweat poured down the nape of my neck. I decided to leave calmly, quietly, and without panicking. I’d hate it if someone were to spot me and involve me in this mess. If that happened, it would cast a dark cloud over my otherwise peaceful school life.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I left the scene quickly and carefully.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is someone there?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sudou-kun, sensing my movement, looked to where I’d been mere moments before. However, I’d successfully escaped by a hair’s breadth. If I’d lagged a mere two seconds, he probably would’ve seen me.</span></p>

<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">2.1</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">M</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">ornings in Class D were always lively, because most of the students were far from studious. Today they were being even more raucous than usual. The reason was obvious. We were about to finally get points for the first time since we’d come to this school. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">My school, the “Tokyo Metropolitan Advanced Nurturing High School,” has adopted an unprecedented system known as the S-Point System. I’ll explain that in a bit.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I took out my school-provided cell phone, launched the pre-installed school app, and logged in using my student </span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">ID</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> and password. I then selected the option “Balance Inquiry” from the menu. From here, you could do many things. You could check your current personal balance, or you could see how many collective points the class had. There was also a function that enabled you to send points to another student.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There were two types of points listed. One of them was marked with a “cl” at the end, which was short for “class.” These were also referred to as “class points”—not points that an individual student possessed, but rather, points that the class had accumulated together. Class D had had zero class points since June. No points at all. The other points were marked with a “pr” at the end, which stood for “private.” These were our individual points.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">On the first day of every month, they multiplied the cl points, or class points, by 100, then deposited that amount into our private point accounts. We used these private points to purchase daily necessities, meals, even electrical appliances. At this school, points are currency. They are very important.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If you didn’t have any private points, you were forced to live day-to-day without spending money. You couldn’t use real currency anywhere on campus. Because Class D was sitting at zero points, we hadn’t received any private points for the month, and thus had to get by without cash.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When we first started here, we had 1000 class points.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If we had kept those points, we would have received 100,000-yen worth of points each month. Unfortunately, our class points fluctuated every day. Many things caused a reduction in points, like talking in class or getting a low score on a test. As a result, Class D had zero points when May came around. Things had continued in that vein until now, July 1.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In addition to determining our monthly allowance, the class points were used to measure our class’s merit. The classes were ordered by class points, in descending order from A through D. So in the event that Class D managed to get enough points to surpass Class C, our class would probably be promoted from D to C for the next month. Moreover, should we finally manage to get all the way up to Class A, then we’d have the chance to attend the college of our choice, or to get the job we wanted. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When I first heard about this system, I thought it’d be important to accumulate as many class points as possible. Private points would grant us only personal satisfaction. However, my perspective changed when I bought a point for the midterm test.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I’d been able to purchase a point for Sudou on that recent test. If I hadn’t, he’d have just barely failed. When I realized that the school would allow me to purchase exam points, I understood that our homeroom teacher, Chabashira-sensei, hadn’t been kidding when she told us, “At this school, you can buy anything with your points.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ergo, holding onto private points meant that it was possible to favorably change your situation. Upon further consideration, you could likely purchase more than just test points.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Good morning, everyone. You all seem more restless than usual today.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira-sensei strode into the classroom as the homeroom bell rang.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sae-chan-sensei! Do we have zero points again this month?! When I checked this morning, I didn’t see a single point deposited into my account!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, so that’s why you’re all so restless?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We worked ourselves half to death this past month! We passed the midterm, so why are we still at zero points?! No one’s been late or absent, and no one’s talked during class, either!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t jump to conclusions. Listen to what I have to say first. You’re correct, Ike. You have all worked harder than ever before. I recognize that. Naturally, the school understands full well how you all feel.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After being admonished by the teacher, Ike shut his mouth and sat back down.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well then. Without further ado, here are this month’s point totals.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She put a paper up on the board that listed the point values, starting with Class A at the top. Excluding Class D, all of the other classes had nearly 100 more points than last month. Class A now sat at 1004 points, slightly above where everyone had started when we were admitted. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This isn’t good. Could they have figured out a way to increase their point total?!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">My neighbor, Horikita Suzune, appeared solely focused on the other classes. However, Ike and most of the other Class D students didn’t care much about the other classes’ points. The important question for them was whether we had received more class points. That was it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Written next to Class D was our point total: 87 points.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? Wait, 87? Does that mean we actually went up? Yahoo!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ike excitedly jumped up and down the instant he saw our score.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s too early to celebrate. All the other classes saw a similar increase in their points. We didn’t close the distance at all. This might just be a reward first-year students receive for getting through the midterm. Every class seems to have gotten at least 100 points.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So that’s what happened. I thought it odd that we’d been awarded points so quickly.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita, who hoped to reach Class A, didn’t appear pleased with the result. She wasn’t smiling.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you disappointed because the gap between the classes has widened, Horikita?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, that’s not it. We managed to get something this time, after all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Get something? Get what?” asked Ike, now standing.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita, after attracting everyone’s attention, fell back into silence. It was as if she didn’t wish to provide an answer. The class leader, Hirata Yousuke, answered for her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I believe that Horikita-san is referring to the deductions we incurred throughout April and May. In other words, we didn’t see a reduction in points for talking in class or being late.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The sharp-witted Hirata hadn’t missed a beat. Splendid.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah, is that so? I suppose that even if we got 100 points, a lot of deductions would’ve brought us down to zero.” Ike, after this simple explanation, raised his arms in victory. “Wait. But then, why didn’t we get any points?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He lobbed his original question again at Chabashira-sensei. It was indeed strange that we hadn’t received 8700 private points in our accounts.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, this time there was a little trouble. The first-year students’ point distribution has been delayed. I’m sorry, but you’ll have to wait a bit longer,” she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? Seriously? If this is the school’s fault, then shouldn’t we get some kind of bonus as compensation?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The students grumbled in discontent. Once they found out that they would be getting their points, their attitudes had changed dramatically.  There was a significant difference between 87 points and no points.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t blame me. This was the school’s decision, there’s nothing I can do about it. Once the trouble has been resolved, you’ll receive your points. If there are still points left, that is.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There was deeper meaning behind Chabashira-sensei’s words.</span></p>

<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">2.2</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Though I doubted even Horikita could come up with a good enough plan to resolve this issue.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you think Horikita-san will help us?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t know. We’ll have to ask and find out. I don’t think she’ll just quietly stand by and watch as Class D collapses. Probably.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I lacked conviction. After all, this was Horikita we were talking about.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I know that you’re trying to dodge the question, but you’ll help, too. Won’t you, Ayanokouji-kun?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I thought I’d managed to steer the conversation in another direction, but Kushida quickly brought it back.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is it okay if I’m useless?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You won’t be useless. I’m sure you’ll be useful, somehow.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She didn’t clearly state </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">how</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> I’d be useful, though.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So what should we do? Sudou-kun said that it wouldn’t help, but I think it’d be good to talk with the three students he fought. To tell you the truth, I’m friends with Komiya-kun and the others. Therefore, it might be possible to persuade them. Hmm, it may be dangerous, though.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida couldn’t dismiss the idea of a conversation.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s risky. Aside from the question of who started the fight, those three reported it to the school. That means they have the upper hand. Also, I just don’t think it would work, since they started it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Getting them to admit they lied to the school wouldn’t be easy. If the school found out, Class C would face a severe penalty. They wouldn’t do something so foolish.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well then, I guess looking for the eyewitness is our best bet.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That would probably be just as difficult as persuading those three to tell the truth. Without any details to go on, finding the eyewitness would be next to impossible. Going around asking, “Did you see anything?” would be a waste of time and effort.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">No matter how much I thought about it, I couldn’t come up with any solutions.</span></p>
