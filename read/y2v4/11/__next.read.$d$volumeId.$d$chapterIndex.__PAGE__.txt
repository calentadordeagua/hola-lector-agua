<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 5:<br/>Under Siege: Kouenji VS The Free Groups</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">K</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">OUENJI’S RAPID ADVANCE</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> continued unabated even as the second half of the battle began. Until today, the tenth day of the exam, he had been continuously racking up enough points to stay neck and neck with Nagumo’s group. Just after five o’clock in the evening when the day’s exam tasks were done, Class 3-B’s Kiriyama finished a conversation on his walkie-talkie and quietly closed his eyes for a time. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He was somewhat surprised to see Kouenji’s name near the top of the rankings on the fourth day when that information was first disclosed. But at that point in time, neither Kiriyama nor Nagumo showed any signs of anything remotely resembling panic. That was because everyone thought that if a student was going it alone, they would reach their limit sooner or later.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Kiriyama, doesn’t it seem like Nagumo’s bein’ slow to respond? Being too reactive?” said Mikitani, a Class 3-B student. “He should have been miles in the lead by the time the second half of this battle started. But he delayed dealin’ with this so we’re ten days in and he still hasn’t been able to settle things. And now it’s looking like they’re evenly matched!” Mikitani showed Kiriyama his tablet as he spoke.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Displayed on the tablet was Nagumo’s group’s overall score of 236 points. Also shown was Kouenji’s score of 230 points. There was only a six-point difference between them. They were so close that simply getting to an area first and receiving the first place Early Bird Bonus would swap the two. Nagumo’s group had now become a large group by merging with another. They also greatly outnumbered other groups due to the fact that they held the One More card, allowing them to have seven members. They would be able to gain seven points for every Arrival Bonus if they managed to reach their designated areas on time.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">On the other hand, while Kouenji could only get a single point for the Arrival Bonus, he could obtain Early Bird Bonuses quite easily since he was working alone. He boasted the highest number of first place Early Bird Bonuses of any group.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“At the rate things are going, even if Nagumo does manage to pull ahead a bit and win this thing, you’ll end up in third place if you’re not careful,” said Mikitani. “And if you end up losing to a lone second-year, then </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">our</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> reputations will go straight down the drain too, since we supported you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kiriyama and his teammates currently had a cumulative total of 188 points. The gap between them and Kouenji was gradually beginning to widen. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Come to think of it, there was a rumor that went around a little bit after Kouenji first came to our school last year,” said Kiriyama. “He was getting all chummy with the second- and third-years at the time, asking about purchasing Private Points. What were your thoughts back then?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I thought he shouldn’t get all carried away just ’cause he’s rich,” said Mikitani.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Most students throughout the school thought he seemed like someone with a prominent level of academic and physical ability,” Kiriyama recalled. “But even though he didn’t stand out as a high achiever in terms of grades, he really does come from a wealthy family. Kouenji’s simply strange. That’s the impression most students had of him.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Mikitani simply nodded at that.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The biggest reason Kouenji hasn’t been recognized is that he hasn’t taken anything seriously,” Kiriyama went on. “He has a strong tendency to go against whatever students are supposed to be doing, and even on things like exams, he typically just gives up right at the very beginning.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was a fact that had spread not only amongst the second-years, but among the third-years as well. If Kouenji had been a more serious and sincere person, Nagumo would have recognized him as an enemy to watch out for much earlier. Kiriyama was sure that they would have seen Nagumo take notice of Kouenji and he would’ve taken steps to put him in his place.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I don’t know what happened, but Kouenji’s sure taking this exam seriously,” said Mikitani. “And ’cause of that, he’s the toughest enemy here on this island. His stamina is especially terrifying. He just never seems to get tired. I think he might even keep pushing ahead like he has been all the way until the very end.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kouenji was making the most of the advantages of being able to move independently, while also pushing on ahead with his inexhaustible energy. And even though they were third-year students, now that things had gotten this far, they had to think of something. If left unchecked, Kouenji would most definitely finish the exam in the top three. He could even end up completely eclipsing Nagumo, depending on how things played out. Losing to a junior would be a problem in and of itself, but, on top of that, losing to a student going it alone would bring shame for generations to come.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kouenji was an opponent that needed to be defeated, and that needed to happen as soon as possible. Of course, violent means were to be avoided as much as possible. If a third-year student were to ambush Kouenji and injure him in a fight, thus eliminating him from the exam, that would naturally be a problem. If the third-year students used violence to prevent Kouenji from getting into the top ranks, it was inevitable that the school would look into the matter. Therefore, Kouenji needed to be subdued as quietly as possible.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you decide on a plan of action, Kiriyama?” asked Mikitani.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. We’re going to be using the Free Groups, after all,” Kiriyama replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The “Free Groups” were groups of students hand-picked by Nagumo, five from each of the three classes below him, meaning 3-B, 3-C, and 3-D. There were fifteen groups in total with three students in each. They essentially functioned as Nagumo’s foot soldiers. Two of the people in each Free Group were meant to follow direct orders, while the third member was given the responsibility of hitting the designated areas so the group could avoid penalties. In other words, two students per group could move about freely.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I figured as much,” said Mikitani. “All right, so how many?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I have custody of six groups, and we’re going to mobilize them all,” Kiriyama said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Six? Are you serious? Kouenji is just one guy! And besides, even if Kouenji did have a bunch of people with him, sendin’ four groups after him, my group included, would be plenty. Just in case, the other two groups should—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The only threat here is Kouenji,” said Kiriyama, cutting Mikitani off. “We’ll have plenty of time to deal with anything or anyone else after we crush him. We’re able to view the leaderboards until the end of the twelfth day. We’re going to keep Kouenji thoroughly contained for two days, starting tomorrow. Kouenji’s working alone, so once he loses his momentum, he’ll never be able to get ahead again.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even if, hypothetically, Kouenji did merge with another group along the way, the end result would be the same.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Come to think of it,” said Mikitani, “didn’t Nagumo say there was another group he was interested in? If we send in all the available groups we’ve got after Kouenji, we’re not going to have enough manpower to handle the other one.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Mikitani hadn’t heard which group it was exactly, but if it was one in the top ten, then that meant it would likely be Ryuuen or Sakayanagi’s group from the second-years, or Utomiya’s group among the first-years.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s no need to worry about that anymore. Nagumo’s fears were unfounded,” said Kiriyama.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kiriyama knew which group Nagumo was wary of, of course, but that group had not appeared in the top ten even once over the past ten days. Even if that group began scoring points at a faster pace from now on, they couldn’t possibly finish on top.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nagumo made an error in judgment on that part,” Kiriyama added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…That sure is unusual, isn’t it? Nagumo misjudging something like that, I mean,” said Mikitani.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, it’s no wonder,” said Kiriyama. “It’s like there’s a ghost holding him by the shoulders, after all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The only person that Kiriyama could possibly be referring to was the only man Nagumo recognized as being worthy: Horikita Manabu. It was understandable why even Nagumo’s eyes had become clouded, even though he was someone who could typically look out over the battlefield in a comprehensive, holistic way.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay, so, we’ll have six groups handle Kouenji, and you’ll just keep collecting points like normal then, I’m guessing?” said Mikitani.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No,” replied Kiriyama. “I’ll take command to contain him.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You will? Wait, wouldn’t that be kinda inefficient? Let me do it,” said Mikitani.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kiriyama’s group currently sat in third place. If they went to try and stop Kouenji’s rapid progress, it would affect their score.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You want me to leave you in command?” asked Kiriyama.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This is a do-or-die moment. However, you want to win—I need to earn Nagumo’s acknowledgement. Let me handle this,” Mikitani insisted, though Kiriyama didn’t seem to be listening.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Unacceptable. If you use six Free Groups and fail, that’s going to be a tough pill for us to swallow,” said Kiriyama.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But you need to come in second place, don’t you?” argued Mikitani, anxious to get a meritorious deed under his belt. “Don’t waste your time on unnecessary things.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No one other than me or Nagumo can stop Kouenji. End of story,” snapped Kiriyama.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Mikitani furrowed his brow slightly and made a disgruntled face. However, Kiriyama wasn’t looking at him, so he didn’t notice it. A faction of six groups, led by Kiriyama, was frantically moving out in order to stop one single student. If Kiriyama were up against a normal opponent, that would be one thing, but Kouenji’s unfathomable abilities unsettled him. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The issue was where the first designated area for the eleventh day would be once it was announced at seven in the morning. Depending on which direction Kouenji was moving in, meaning east, west, south, or north, the encirclement around him would have to change. Therefore, the ideal scenario would be to fully set up the siege around Kouenji during the evening, between the time that he settled on a campsite and 7 a.m. the next morning, when he would not be moving around.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Fortunately, Kouenji’s current location in area B3 was relatively close to E3, where Kiriyama and his crew were located. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Students could only view the top-ten leaderboards until the end of the twelfth day. That meant it was only possible to check scores for two more days: tomorrow and the day after. They wanted to create at least a thirty-point gap between Nagumo and Kouenji by the end of that twelfth day.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How far are you plannin’ on marching today?” asked Mikitani right at the start of their long journey, throwing the question out there simply to stave off boredom.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“As far as possible. I understand that it’s risky to move at night, but I want to end up at a spot somewhere within one space of Kouenji’s location at the very least,” replied Kiriyama. “We have to catch up with him by seven in the morning.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Once Kouenji was on the move, it would become much more difficult to catch him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I think knocking him down a peg in two days is going to be a piece of cake, honestly,” said Mikitani. He turned back to look at the sixteen other third-year students. “We have seven groups, including yours, Kiriyama. And since your group has six, we have a total of eighteen people we&#160;can use.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t get careless. These woods are vast. There’s a good chance that he could slip away,” said Kiriyama.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Look, I get that he’s this crazy tough bastard of a&#160;second-year,” said Mikitani. “Doesn’t change the fact that he’s still a year below us.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Since neither Kiriyama nor Mikitani had actually seen Kouenji’s astounding physical abilities firsthand, it was difficult for them to make an accurate assessment. Even so, data about Kouenji’s physical abilities was pouring in from third-year students who competed with him in several different Tasks.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Use caution. Think of him as the greatest adversary you’ve ever faced,” said Kiriyama.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Greatest, huh,” replied Mikitani.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">There really was no way I could’ve let someone like Mikitani handle this after all,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> thought Kiriyama, in his heart of hearts. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If you were facing an enemy that had to be defeated no matter what, then you needed to be willing to go all the way, even if it meant going as far as choking the life out of them. If you were dealing with a situation like that without being serious, then you could find yourself being eaten alive instead.</span></p>

<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">5.1</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">T WAS JUST AFTER</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> six-thirty in the morning the next day, the eleventh day of the exam. Kiriyama’s group and the six Free Groups, including Mikitani, had successfully surrounded Kouenji.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What’s the situation?” asked Kiriyama.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Still no signs of movement in his tent, from the looks of things,” said Mikitani. “Guess he’s still sleepin’. It’d be a lot easier for us if he got sick and stayed in bed all day, though.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Mikitani then began addressing the members of the Free Groups.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“All right,” he said. “In that case, why don’t we surround his tent so we can block him before he comes out? If we make it impossible for him to pack up his stuff, Kouenji’s not gonna be able to move.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The Free Group members all agreed with this plan, thinking it would make things easy.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Keep in mind that, yes, if you get in his way while he’s trying to pack up his things, we can most certainly delay him in getting to his designated area. But what excuse are you going to come up with if a third party happens to see what you’re doing?” said Kiriyama. “Even if you really are planning to get in Kouenji’s way while he’s trying to clean up his campsite, you should avoid being so careless as to make what you’re doing obvious to anyone watching. You need to be discreet.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even if they were going to go ahead and violate the rules anyway, they still needed to eliminate as many risks as possible.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, why don’t we jus’ use a GPS search?” suggested Mikitani. “We got plenty of points to throw around.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Our tablets can’t track the locations of teaching staff. Don’t forget that the search function is not an infallible tool. We will spring our trap immediately when Kouenji starts putting his tent away, just as we originally planned. If you do happen to bump into any first- or second-year students, or any adults who are on their way to set up a Task, then immediately put a distance of at least two meters between yourselves and Kouenji,” cautioned Kiriyama sternly. He then told them not to get so close that they could touch one another.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As seven o’clock in the morning approached, the situation finally started to change.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Kouenji’s on the move,” said Mikitani.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kouenji got out of his tent and proceeded to break it down, humming a little tune to himself as he did. He moved swiftly and efficiently, acting as though he couldn’t even imagine that he was being watched at that moment. He finished getting ready to leave right on schedule, just before seven o’clock had officially struck. Then, tablet in hand, Kouenji stood ready to begin the exam for the day.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Let’s go,” said Kiriyama, deciding that this was the best time to make their approach. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He started to walk over to Kouenji, with Mikitani and the other Free Group members following at a short distance. Whether Kouenji was even aware of their presence as they quietly approached him or not, he didn’t stop fiddling with his tablet. In fact, he didn’t look up once. Even after being surrounded by a total of eighteen people, he continued on with his day like normal, as if he were totally blind to everything around him. Mikitani, having decided that Kouenji must have noticed them while simply pretending not to, tried to close in. But Kiriyama stopped him in his tracks with nothing more than a quick glare.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Could I have a minute of your time, Kouenji?” asked Kiriyama.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though his name had been called, Kouenji’s gaze remained directed at his tablet. He didn’t even bother to look up.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What business do you have with me?” he asked in return.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though that that was hardly the attitude that someone should have been taking with a senpai, Kiriyama simply continued the conversation without rebuke. He understood that the person known as Kouenji Rokusuke was someone who had no common sense whatsoever.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“None of us expected you to do so well in this special exam,” said Kiriyama. “If you’re so exceptionally talented, then why didn’t you take anything else seriously before?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is this something that we should be talking about right now?” replied Kouenji. “It’s going to be seven o’clock shortly. Shouldn’t you be hurrying along and getting ready to head to your designated area?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You have to understand what I’m getting at, Kouenji. You’ve scored too many points,” said Kiriyama.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kouenji spoke as though he was oblivious to the situation, but Kiriyama knew that couldn’t be true. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I want you to stay where you are for the rest of the day,” Kiriyama went on.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, does that mean…you’re telling me not to score any more points?” said Kouenji.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s right,” replied Kiriyama.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Naturally, there was absolutely no way that Kouenji would have simply nodded and agreed to such a request.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t know who you are,” the second-year said. “But, if you simply think this through for a minute, you’ll understand that what you’re asking is impossible. Even so, the fact that you have brought so many people with you here must mean…you’re prepared to try and get in my way if I don’t entertain your request. Isn’t that right?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even if you continue with this special exam, you will not get the first-place reward,” Kiriyama told him. “Nagumo, who is currently in first, has seven people in his group. My group, which is currently in third, has six people. You, in second place, are alone. I will admit that you have made excellent progress thus far, but during the second half of the exam, you’re going to start to tire out. And we can all expect to see a drop in the number of points you’re able to get.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, you don’t need to worry about me then, do you?” replied Kouenji.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This is just to make doubly sure. Besides, as a third-year student, it is unacceptable for us to compete with you, someone working alone, for the top spot. We won’t make things worse for you if you obey, of course. If you make an ally in Nagumo, the student council president, it will make your life at school much more stable.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There were two options made available to Kouenji here: either he could be kept in check by strong-arm tactics, or he could obey and get into Nagumo’s good graces. It was now exactly seven o’clock in the morning. The first designated area for the eleventh day had been announced and made available on the students’ tablets. After confirming that those were his choices, Kouenji slowly put his tablet away into his backpack. For a brief moment, Kiriyama and the others watched and waited to see if he would move or not.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m going to be hurrying on ahead now, so please excuse me,” said Kouenji, refusing Kiriyama’s request.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As soon as those words left his mouth, he instantly accelerated, dashing through a gap between several Free Group members.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“H-hey!!!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though Kouenji had been surrounded, there was still enough room between the third-years for a person to slip through—and he took advantage of those gaps. It&#160;would’ve been a lie to say that Kiriyama and the others hadn’t been caught at least somewhat off guard by that. They had underestimated the possibility that Kouenji would just ignore an order from a third-year student and make a break for it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“After him!” shouted Mikitani.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But by the time those words passed Mikitani’s lips, Kouenji had already disappeared into the woods.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t panic. Trying to keep pace with Kouenji is just going to be an exercise in pain,” warned Kiriyama.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This is no time to relax and take it easy!” shouted Mikitani. “He just got away!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“He may be able to get the Early Bird Bonus, but that’s all,” Kiriyama said. “If Kouenji chose to run, then that means he’s not going to be able to participate in any Tasks at his leisure. On the other hand, if he does have the audacity to sign up for a Task, we’ll catch up with him right then and there.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kiriyama understood that it was dangerous for them to just decide that the direction Kouenji ran in was the same as his designated area. It would be foolish to chase after him based solely on that. But he also understood that it was impossible for Kouenji to hide from a GPS search. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even so, perhaps because he was panicking, Mikitani started running after Kouenji, taking off at a full sprint.</span></p>

<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">5.2</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">W</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">ITH MIKITANI IN THE LEAD</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> Kiriyama and the Free Groups began tracking Kouenji.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What’s his location?” asked Kiriyama.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, get a load of this,” said Mikitani. “He hasn’t been moving at all for a while now. We searched three times and it’s been the same every time.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The fact that he hadn’t moved at all was entirely unnatural, considering that it wasn’t a break period at the moment. Kiriyama took a peek at his tablet to see if there was some reason for Kouenji’s inexplicable behavior.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It doesn’t look like there are any Tasks near him,” Kiriyama observed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Right. We’ll catch up with him in another 200 meters or so,” said Mikitani.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Let’s not let our guard down this time,” warned Kiriyama. “We’re going to catch him for sure. Got it?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You don’t need to tell me twice,” said Mikitani.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kouenji had pulled away from his pursuers earlier, but roughly six hours after they started chasing him, they met again, albeit in a rather unexpected way. The reason Kouenji wasn’t moving was because he was sleeping, even though it was the middle of the afternoon. The third-year students exchanged exasperated looks with each other. Mikitani approached Kouenji, acting as the representative of the group. While looking down at Kouenji’s face from above, he started speaking to him in a firm tone.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wake up, Kouenji. Seriously, taking a nap after running from us like that? You sure don’t have a care in the world. Or maybe you’ve been running as fast as you can for these past ten days, and you got so exhausted that you couldn’t help but want a little shut eye?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Yeah, he had to sleep, even if he didn’t want to,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> thought Mikitani. He couldn’t think of any other reason why Kouenji would be taking a nap in this situation, especially after his escape. Kouenji slowly opened his eyes and smiled at Mikitani.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, that’s only natural, no?” he said. “After all, I am a human being, just like the rest of you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, just be a good little boy and keep sleeping peacefully then,” said Mikitani. “Take the rest of the day off. I’m sure you must be exhausted after all you’ve done. Go on, take your senpai’s kind advice.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Take the day off? What a strange thing to say,” said Kouenji.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kouenji, not panicking at all over having been surrounded again, stood up. Mikitani had been looking down on him before, but he had to crane his neck now that Kouenji was standing up straight. The second-year student measured over 180 centimeters tall. Kouenji’s eyes seemed to be brimming with vitality; somehow, he appeared even larger than he did before.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Don’t ignore me,” said Mikitani, drawing closer to Kouenji aggressively, despite feeling intimidated by him. “If you just rest for a little while to get rid of your fatigue, then no one’s going to have any trouble.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There is no need for concern,” said Kouenji. “My physical strength has already been restored. I am now back to being in </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">perfect</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> shape. Do not talk to me as if I were some ordinary person.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">While what Kouenji said could have been interpreted as simple pretense, Kiriyama seemed willing to consider it as a possible truth and stepped in to address Kouenji himself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You certainly look well,” he said. “But like Mikitani said, you’ve been going all out over these last ten plus days. You’ve been going at this harder than anyone else. You may have repeatedly gotten the first place Early Bird Bonuses, but surely you’ve already reached your limit by&#160;now.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wouldn’t consider myself above the common masses if I had reached my limit,” said Kouenji.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Meaning that you haven’t?” asked Kiriyama.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He was becoming increasingly doubtful of Kouenji in their conversation, but Kouenji just continued on.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I am what you call a ‘super short sleeper,’ you see. As in, I’m someone who doesn’t need much sleep. I&#160;experience extremely short REM cycles,” said Kouenji, matter-of-factly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? Short REM cycles? What?” balked Mikitani, jumping on what Kouenji had just said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">For the first time that day, Kiriyama’s expression stiffened.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“A short sleeper… If that’s true, then we have quite a serious problem on our hands,” said Kiriyama.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do you mean, Kiriyama?” asked Mikitani.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“For the average person, somewhere around seven to eight hours of sleep a day is considered ideal,” Kiriyama explained. “That length of time is important for maintaining your health. You wouldn’t be able to say that you had gotten a good night’s sleep if you had any less than that. However, a short sleeper is someone whose constitution allows them to stay healthy while getting under six hours of sleep.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When people sleep, they alternate between states of REM and non-REM sleep cycles. REM sleep is the stage in which the brain is still active and awake, so to speak. Non-REM sleep, on the other hand, is a state in which the brain is asleep. Short sleepers spend less time in REM sleep, which allows their body and brain to be well rested even with only a short period of sleep. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I was thinking it was weird for him to just sleep out in the open like that. So that’s how it is, huh…” said Mikitani.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kouenji certainly had extraordinary physical abilities. But even so, after prolonged periods of intense travel and repeated tasks, fatigue should’ve started to set in more and more. By getting in a good rest right here, Kouenji was successfully able to keep a high level of physical readiness.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If what Kouenji said was true, that he was really a “super short sleeper,” that meant that not only did he have physical abilities far beyond the average person, but also that his resilience was similarly out of the ordinary. For the first time during this endeavor, Kiriyama felt a slight feeling of urgency arising within him. Everyone was feeling tired and fatigued, trying to find the right pace. Their legs screamed out for rest just from walking around. Their spirits were just about at the breaking point, wailing that they didn’t want to do this exam anymore. These were the commonly held feelings that most students had deep down, in their heart of hearts.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was precisely because Kiriyama had been working under that premise that he had assumed it would not be difficult to contain Kouenji. However, if that entire premise were to fall apart, then…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“By the way, do you still have business with me?” asked Kouenji.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Look, it doesn’t matter if you’ve got energy to spare or not. Just be a good kid and—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">An irritated Mikitani was just about to finish giving Kouenji that order when Kiriyama suddenly stepped in and interrupted him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, we don’t have any business with you. Don’t pay us any mind,” said Kiriyama.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kiriyama decided they should avoid direct demands as much as possible, and instead proceed in a calm, collected manner. Mikitani was frustrated, but ultimately complied with Kiriyama’s rather lenient approach.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Fu fu</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">. You say that, yet he still seems quite belligerent,” said Kouenji.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kouenji didn’t seem to pay any mind whatsoever to the “advice” or threats coming from the third-year students. While they were talking, the third designated area for the day had been announced. Kouenji looked at his tablet and immediately started walking in the direction of his next area.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“He’s not the sort of guy who’ll listen to advice, Kiriyama,” Mikitani huffed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You might be right about that,” said Kiriyama.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Besides, all that junk about being a super short sleeper or whatever? I’m sure that was all just a bluff.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, it was true that while many students were already experiencing a significant drop in efficiency, Kouenji had kept a good pace. His condition was almost completely unchanged from how it was at the start of the exam. It was obvious that he had been working out incessantly every day, and that he saw this special exam on an uninhabited island as nothing more than part of his normal training. At least that was how Kiriyama analyzed the situation, anyway. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kiriyama came to a decision on the matter. “We don’t have any other choice,” he announced. “We’re going to switch up our strategy. Suppress his ability to register for Tasks.” He gave everyone present orders to hunt down Kouenji.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, Mikitani must have felt frustrated with the order because his lips curled defiantly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m the one in command here. Do not break formation, Mikitani,” said Kiriyama, sternly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Tch…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The third-year students, though feeling bewildered at how Kouenji constantly did things in his own bizarre way, started to expand the scope of their coverage. The eighteen of them moved into a triangular formation, keeping Kouenji positioned at the center. In addition, Kiriyama kept in contact with his allies, communicating with them via walkie-talkie. Kouenji, meanwhile, continued walking along without a thought as to what would happen from here on out. He kept right on moving, never coming to a complete stop. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kiriyama had come up with a total of three plans. The first was to simply persuade Kouenji to give up on first place through persuasion. Of course, that method also involved having Kouenji surrounded by a number of people and applying pressure to him. The second was to stay on the move and surround Kouenji, keeping him in check. The third plan was to get ahead of any Tasks that Kouenji was going for. With a total of seven groups involved—meaning Kiriyama’s group plus the six Free Groups—if they blocked Kouenji’s way, it would inevitably make it that much more difficult for him to register for any Tasks. Furthermore, if everyone turned up and did whatever they could just for the sake of crushing the underclassman, then the chances of Kouenji winning those Tasks would be lowered, even if he managed to register successfully.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Still, Tasks had different conditions for participation, but certain criteria were fixed. To be precise, there were two distinct models of participation for Tasks. In one case, a certain number of total people could participate, each from their own respective groups. In the other, a whole group would take part collectively. In the latter case, since not all members of each Free Group were present, that meant they wouldn’t be able to fulfill the participation requirements. However, most of those Tasks still required two or more people from a single group to sign up. Therefore, since Kouenji was working alone, he could only sign up for Tasks in which a person was allowed to register alone. And even in those cases, the third-year students following him would be eligible to take part in those Tasks as well.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The third-year students managed to follow Kouenji around for a while without losing their cool, but little by little, they started to become impatient. Kouenji’s walking speed was so fast that an outsider observer might have mistaken it for racewalking. Just keeping up with him used a great deal of stamina. Walking at Kouenji’s pace was beginning to feel intensely tiring.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">They had to adjust to a completely unfamiliar walking speed, and the exhaustion was starting to overwhelm them. They felt like it would have been easier if they had just started running instead.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Kouenji! Quit tryin’ to look like such a tough guy!” shouted Mikitani. He was flustered, having concluded that Kouenji was just pushing ahead as a false show of bravado.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh dear, what a fuss you’re making. Well then, let’s pick up the pace a little bit, shall we?” said Kouenji as he started to walk again.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re not gettin’ away this time! Box him in!” shouted Mikitani.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The third-year students, who had been keeping themselves at a distance as they chased after him, started to surround the underclassman again. However, just before they could fully enclose him, Kouenji managed to slip through in an instant.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re kidding?!” wailed one of the third-year students, though his words were drowned out by the wind.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kouenji’s legs were practically a blur as he broke into a run. It was a brilliant dash, like he was running on a well-maintained track. Then, he proceeded to quickly weave through the trees at a speed that would make even a seasoned sprinter lose face. Out of the twelve students present that made up the so-called Free Groups, many were quite confident in their physical abilities. Even if you just looked at OAA, they all had rankings of B or better in physical ability. They were essentially soldiers, so to speak, that Nagumo and Kiriyama had assembled to monopolize Tasks.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“After him! Do </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">not</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> let him get away!” shouted Mikitani.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait, Mikitani! Don’t make a move on your own!” warned Kiriyama.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Shut up! Like hell I’m lettin’ him get away a second time!” roared Mikitani. “Catch him and hold him down by force!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Mikitani and several of the other students ignored the orders they were given and ran after Kouenji.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Unbelievable…” huffed Kiriyama.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He hesitated for a moment, wondering if he should have also given chase. But then Kiriyama calmly looked down at his tablet and re-thought his strategy. It was hard to imagine that Kouenji had just run off for no reason at all. He tried to reason whether Kouenji was headed toward a designated area or a Task. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There is one Task nearby, in E3, that Kouenji could sign up for,” he muttered, “and it looks like the reward for first place in that Task is eight points… However, it wouldn’t be surprising if he prioritized taking the first place Early Bird Bonus of ten points… Where is his designated area?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">D4 was the most likely candidate, but it could have been some other random area as well.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…He’s not the kind of opponent you can break down and analyze,” Kiriyama concluded.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The third-year had become acutely aware of the fact that he was dealing with someone for whom logic was not a valid option; simply put, Kouenji’s thoughts and actions were crazy.</span></p>

<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">5.3</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">N THE END</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> Kouenji went for the Task in E3. He arrived at his destination in the blink of an eye and successfully registered for the Task. A few minutes later, Mikitani and the rest who had been giving chase caught up with him. However, they were forced to wait until the Task was over; one other person had registered after Kouenji did and took the last spot so the Task was fully booked. The Task in question was an English test. Though there were participants representing all three grade levels, the level of the content was standardized.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In the end, a third-year student named Doumichi who was considered brilliant even amongst the third-year students took first place, but only by a narrow margin above Kouenji, who came in second. As a result, Kouenji was awarded four points. Since they were still under the watchful eyes of the teaching staff, Mikitani and the others decided to rush in only once Kouenji moved away from the Task site. Unfortunately for them, Kouenji dashed away before that could happen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Reacting too late to the situation, Mikitani and the others were forced to wait. They could only run after Kouenji, whose speed made it impossible for anyone to catch up with him. They didn’t manage to surround him again until just before three in the afternoon, when he arrived at his third designated area for the day. There, Mikitani and the others successfully managed to corner him for the third time. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hm. You lot are really giving it your all,” said Kouenji.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We can’t just sit around and let you do whatever you want!” shouted Mikitani.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">They tried to get ahead of Kouenji to whatever Tasks he was headed to on the eleventh day, but in the end, they hadn’t managed to stop him, not even once. It wasn’t an exaggeration to say that their pride as third-year students had been torn to shreds. Nagumo would be deeply disappointed when he learned about these results. The word “amicable” no longer had any meaning in this situation.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This is your last warning, Kouenji,” declared Kiriyama, giving the Free Groups the order to circle around him. “It’s just for tomorrow. Just one day. Just listen to what we’re ordering you to do and do nothing. That’s all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If they could hold him for one day, that alone would be enough to ensure that Nagumo would end up in first place. The important part was to prevent Kouenji from taking the top spot once more.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“H-hey, Nagumo said to keep him down for two days…!” said Mikitani. “Don’t you mean tomorrow and the day after?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We won’t be able to confirm which groups are in the top ten the day after tomorrow,” Kiriyama replied. “I don’t think any groups are going to be coming after us that strongly. We should focus on boosting our own scores rather than trying to keep someone else down.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was Kiriyama’s own decision that he had made after watching the situation with Kouenji unfold from up close. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It wouldn’t be a good idea for us to devote three whole days to Kouenji,” he added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, we should just leave the bare minimum number of guards with him so we can keep him tied down for the next two days!” shouted Mikitani.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you honestly think Kouenji will let that happen?” asked Kiriyama.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If they kept Kouenji down for just one day, he still had a good chance of being able to hang in there at either second or third in the rankings. However, if Kouenji had to forego getting points for two whole days, it was possible that he’d never make it to the winner’s circle at all.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There is no way he’d accept a situation where he would lose,” said Kiriyama.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, that depends on how we do things,” argued Mikitani. He had been feeling dissatisfied with Kiriyama all this time but had followed him up until now in spite of that. Now, however, he was staging a revolt.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…You think that you can do it?” Kiriyama asked him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, I can. And when I show you that I can pull it off, I’ll have myself a ticket into Class A.” With that, Mikitani stepped forward, pushing Kiriyama out of his way.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He then turned his attention back to Kouenji. “You heard what I said earlier,” Mikitani growled. “You’re going to sit tight tomorrow and the day after.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is that a request?” asked Kouenji.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, that’s an order.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I imagine that this is a question I won’t get an answer to, but what happens if I refuse?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Worst case, you’ll get expelled,” said Mikitani, as several of his allies moved in closer to Kouenji.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even without saying it outright, it was clear as day that they were willing to hold Kouenji down through violence. Despite being threatened, Kouenji’s fearless grin never faded. He simply waited to see what the third-year students would do.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Since you’re not giving me an answer, can I take it to mean you’re not gonna comply with my order?” demanded Mikitani.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t obey anyone,” said Kouenji.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, I’ve got no choice but to </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">make</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> you obey. That all right, Kiriyama?” said Mikitani, turning his way partway through.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“As long as Kouenji complies, I’ll leave it to your judgment,” said Kiriyama.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Mikitani laughed scornfully, acting confident and assured. But, at the same time the final designated area for the eleventh day of the exam was announced, Kouenji stood up. Upon seeing this, Mikitani hurriedly issued the order for the others to circle around Kouenji.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Didn’t I just tell you? You’re going to stay put,” he told the second-year.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Since they were so close that they could easily reach out and touch one another, the only way Kouenji could move forward would be to forcefully push the third-year students out of the way.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I can’t say that this is a beautiful situation for me to be in,” said Kouenji. “I do not have a preference for men.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What are you gonna do then, huh?” said Mikitani. “If&#160;you try and push us out of your way, we’ll take that as a declaration of war.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Fu fu</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">, yes, I suppose you might,” Kouenji chuckled, taking a step forward.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That one large step was enough for Kouenji to come into contact with Mikitani, who was standing right in front of him. However, he made no attempt to push Mikitani out of the way with his arm, not even as a pretense. He simply started walking forward normally, and they just so happened to bump into one another, shoulder to shoulder. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In short, it was an attempt on Kouenji’s part to push his way through by force without actually raising a hand against anyone. It could have been interpreted as a shove, but Mikitani was confident that he’d be able to stand his ground thanks to his physique. This was an opportunity for him to prove that being fast and being powerful were two different things.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Tch!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But Mikitani felt like a large rock had just slowly crashed into him, and before he knew it, he found himself forced to the side as Kouenji opened up a path for himself. Kouenji, on the other hand, moved like he hadn’t even hit an obstacle at all. He just quietly walked on ahead, as if nothing had been in his way.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, wait! You jerk!” shouted a flustered Mikitani, grabbing onto Kouenji’s shoulder.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But he was unable to stop Kouenji from moving with such superhuman strength. Mikitani told himself that if he simply allowed Kouenji to walk away right before his very eyes, then the situation would only repeat itself. He tried to put up resistance, but Kouenji’s legs kept moving. When Mikitani saw that Kiriyama was watching this happen, Mikitani clicked his tongue and turned to look at his allies.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He called one of them over and the two tried once more to stop Kouenji. Morooka joined in to try and stop the underclassman, but it looked like he was getting pushed away and he lost his balance. He fell over in an exaggerated fashion and made a show of being in pain. Once Mikitani saw this, he put himself directly in front of Kouenji and stopped him from walking any further.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ow! You might’ve broke my arm!” wailed Morooka, ranting and raving, making a spectacle like a soccer athlete trying to show how badly they were hurt.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Looks like you did something pretty terrible, Kouenji,” Mikitani said. “I think you really hurt Morooka.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It looks to me like he’s faking it,” said Kouenji.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No matter what you say, it doesn’t change the fact that you pushed Morooka out of your way,” said Mikitani.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">With their positions suddenly reversed, all of the third-year students were now trying to surround Kouenji to keep him from escaping completely. They had tossed the more conservative strategy that they had been sticking with completely out the window. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hm, I’m afraid this is a turn of events that even someone such as myself cannot overlook. What to do, what to do,” mused Kouenji.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Whoa there, Kouenji, you’ve got a look on your face like you want to punch all your senpais’ lights out. But you </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">do</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> understand that even if you did manage to beat us up somehow, you’d be in big trouble, right?” warned Mikitani. He felt sure that there was no way Kouenji would start throwing punches.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kouenji didn’t deny what Mikitani said. He just calmly went along with it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I do not intend to show any mercy to anyone who tries to stop my advance,” he said. “Much less anyone who&#160;dares to bare their fangs at me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So Kouenji was willing to resort to violence. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Mikitani’s expression stiffened for a moment. “And what do you think’s gonna happen if we report it to the school?” he demanded.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What’s going to happen?” repeated Kouenji. “Well, your names as third-year students would be sullied, branded as those who’d lower themselves to gang up on a student from a lower grade level. That’s all that would happen, no?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There was no need for Kouenji to bother confirming whether Mikitani’s and the other third-year students’ watches were working properly. After all, if they were broken, it would’ve been completely pointless to try and reach Tasks ahead of him in order to enter before he could sign up.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I think it’s about time I take my leave now, don’t you agree?” Kouenji said. “You hanging around me has been putting a damper on my chances of getting the next Early Bird Bonus.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">More than ten minutes had passed since the designated area had been announced. Kouenji knew that his rivals would already be headed there and that he ought to have been moving toward it too. It was certainly still possible for him to come back from this delay and get to the designated area first, but it was unclear how things would play out.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry… But we’re not lettin’ you go,” said Mikitani, with firm determination, telling Kouenji in no uncertain terms that he was willing to throw down.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You realize that I can’t keep being nice to you forever, you know,” replied Kouenji.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, what, you’re gonna bare your fangs at us, then?” asked Mikitani.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The third-year students had been utterly bewildered by the way Kouenji had been acting, but now they remembered what their role was here. Even though they understood the shamefulness of this scenario, with a bunch of them ganging up on a younger student, they also figured that if this was the only way that they’d survive this situation, they couldn’t afford to worry about appearances. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Normally, the person being cornered in a situation like this would have realized by now that there was no way out, but Kouenji was different. He was a man who wasn’t interested in anything but himself. He was thinking only of how to take this turn of events and transform it into something beautiful.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even on an uninhabited island, Kouenji had kept his blond hair impeccable; it was so beautifully lustrous that it put any girls’ locks to shame. He lightly stroked his disheveled bangs for a moment and smiled broadly. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Mikitani, momentarily awe-struck, backed away.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Time is money,” said Kouenji. He slowly reached out his arms in a gesture that signaled he was ready and willing to accept whatever the third-year students could throw at him. “Come at me quickly.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you sure about this, Mikitani?” asked one of the third-year students. “Are we </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">seriously</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> going to do this?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Yeah. If push comes to shove, we’ll just pin him down. Let’s do this!” shouted Mikitani.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As soon as those words left his mouth, three people rushed at Kouenji. One of the students approached him from behind to try and get him in a Nelson hold, while the other two rushed him from the front and the left, respectively. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">At first glance, it might have seemed like it would be difficult to deal with three people all at once. However, the third-years weren’t particularly experienced when it came to fighting, nor were they very well-coordinated. They had simply came at Kouenji at the same time. No one had been seriously trying to land a punch or anything. If anything, all three of them were expecting that one of the others would handle it. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kouenji avoided all of them with a brilliant side-step, forcing the shocked third-year students into a three-way head-on collision. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ow! Hey, watch where yer goin’!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re one to talk!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was far from beautiful teamwork. The third-year students bickered as if their fight was with each other.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Mikitani, the only one accustomed to fighting, shouted at his allies, “Hey, don’t forget about who we’re really after here! Kouenji’s the one we want!” Unfortunately, his allies were on the verge of self-destruction.</span></p>

<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">5.4</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">J</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">UST MOMENTS LATER</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> the entirely exhausted third-year students were all down on their knees around Kouenji, broken and out of breath. Kouenji had not thrown a single punch, and yet their spirits had been crushed as he rendered their attacks useless.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huff, huff… Damn it… Who, or what, the hell are you, anyway?” wheezed a terrified Mikitani, starting to back away. “You’re like a monster or something, seriously… Wouldn’t it have been a lot easier for you to just shake us off instead…?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kouenji noticed that a gap had been opened.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’d be a hassle having to deal with you following me around forever,” he replied simply. “It’s like having dead leaves blowing in my face all the time. Not a pleasant feeling.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hearing that, Kiriyama began to calmly analyze the scene, despite the tricky situation that they were in.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see,” he said. “I suppose it’s certainly true that Mikitani would have been prepared to chase after you, no matter how far you went. At least, he would have before. But, if he were to see for himself the overwhelming difference in ability between you and him, his spirit would most likely falter. You’re probably the only person who would come up with the idea to break your opponent’s spirit without fighting back </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">and</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> have the ability to follow through with such an extraordinary feat.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Though Kouenji had given up on the Early Bird Bonus for his designated area, he had completely shut down the third-years, rendering them unwilling to attack further. Thanks to Kouenji’s decision, Kiriyama and his followers found the rug pulled out from under them.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you okay, Mikitani?” asked Kiriyama.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Y-yeah,” groaned Mikitani. “I’m not hurt, but… Ugh.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Some students had fallen or been knocked to the ground as a result of their own actions, but they were basically unharmed. At most, they had only slight scrapes on their hands. In the face of such overwhelming power, Kouenji had managed to show them the difference between his abilities and theirs without taking a single swing at any of the upperclassmen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well then, I’ll be on my way now,” he announced. “I&#160;trust you don’t mind?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do whatever you want, Kouenji,” huffed Mikitani.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, please excuse me. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Adieu</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">!” said Kouenji, jovially.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There wasn’t anyone remaining who could stand in his way. With that, Kouenji simply left. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Afterward, Mikitani muttered to himself, heartbroken, “What the hell </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">is</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> he, anyway? Is he really a high schooler?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There are always people who’ll do something beyond your initial calculations. Just like Nagumo,” said Kiriyama.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In the end, is that really all we can do? Just crawl on the ground like this forever?!” Mikitani pounded his clenched fist against the ground, frustrated with his own inadequacy. “Ugh, that little freak! He made a fool of me! Damn it! Damn it!!!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Our fight is not over yet,” said Kiriyama, looking over in the direction in which Kouenji had left, though he was already out of sight by now.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kiriyama took his walkie-talkie in hand.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you plannin’ on reporting my failure to Nagumo?” asked Mikitani.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What would there be to gain by doing that?” Kiriyama replied. “I’ve already decided that I’m going to go for the&#160;win.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Y-yeah, guess you’re right.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t worry, Mikitani. I had already assumed from the very beginning that Kouenji was beyond the realm of the ordinary. But every opponent has their weak points. As the saying goes, ‘the greater embraces the lesser,’ after all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Mikitani nodded quietly, feeling somewhat grateful for such words. Kiriyama, on the other hand, wasn’t even the least bit shaken or upset about what had just transpired. On the contrary, he had expected it from the very start. His overarching strategy was to catch Kouenji by surprise once the underclassman was convinced that he had gotten rid of every obstacle standing in his way. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Though many people had gone after him to block his path, they hadn’t really done much harm to Kouenji at all. As a result, Kouenji would have gotten the distinct impression that the third-year students really weren’t that big a deal. And that was exactly what Kiriyama wanted.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-before: always;"/>
<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">5.5</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">T WAS JUST BEFORE</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> five o’clock in the afternoon on the eleventh day of the exam. I had arrived at my final designated area for the day, J10, just in time, and found myself getting momentarily distracted by the scenery. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Getting points and gathering supplies from Tasks was important, but what concerned me more was manipulating my score. It was surprisingly difficult to keep myself in eleventh place. I needed to make sure that I hit my designated areas in order to avoid being penalized, but at the same time, I needed to make sure that I kept my point total close to that of the group in tenth place. Yesterday, on the tenth day of the exam, the third designated area had been randomly assigned. The second area was in F4, but the third was in B9. I decided to give up on trying to get there almost immediately. Then, the fourth designated area for the day was in C9. I didn’t manage to make it to that one either, and that left me with two consecutive misses. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This morning, however, I managed to make it to my first designated area on time, which was in area C8. That meant I successfully avoided getting a penalty. However, I hadn’t been able to make it to the following area, which was another randomly assigned one in H9. I did make it to I9 when it was designated afterward, but I had a rough time doing so. It had been a very difficult day.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If you had to cover a long distance to reach a designated area, even just once, the rest of your travels would be affected by it. Once again, I became painfully aware of the main reason my overall score was not improving. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I reached area J10 after making my way through a path full of steep inclines and rocky terrain. When I got there, though, I could hear the voices of people talking with each other up ahead.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Thinking that I might know who they were, I decided to take a quick peek. The voices sounded as if they were coming from the west—the same direction as the ocean. When I reached them, I found a single group made up of three girls from Class 2-B: Isoyama Nagisa, Morofuji Rika, and Shiina Hiyori. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">And…then I saw there was actually another group of second-year students there too. I hadn’t seen them at all since the exam had started. The second group included Ishizaki Daiichi, Nishino Takeko, and Tsube Hitomi. I was sure that these groups must have started in different Tables originally. Did their designated areas overlap this time?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh? Why, if it isn’t Ayanokouji-kun,” said Hiyori.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The other five people were still in the middle of their conversation so they hadn’t realized I was there yet. Only Hiyori seemed to have noticed me, perhaps because she had sensed that someone was nearby. As soon as our eyes met, she waved at me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re looking like you’re in better shape than I thought you’d be,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s thanks to everyone’s hard work,” she replied. “We expanded our maximum group size to six people.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">From the sound of it, Ishizaki’s group must’ve merged with hers. To be completely honest, there seemed to be many students here lacking in ability, but I figured that Hiyori could make a strong contribution thanks to her smarts. She’d likely support her group from that angle. However, even if I was being generous, I couldn’t exactly say that she was endowed with strong physical abilities. But considering the overall makeup of the group, I supposed they were well-balanced in terms of functionality.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Had you planned on joining up with Ishizaki and the others from the beginning?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, that’s right,” said Hiyori. “There were several groups on the short list to merge and we were one of them.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She didn’t deny it at all, and instead openly admitted it. She then turned her gaze toward Ishizaki and the others. They were happily chatting away while watching the sun go down, as if the setting would help relieve their fatigue. Everyone in the group seemed to be getting along, because just about everyone in it had come from Class 2-B. Tsube, the only student from a different class, blended into the group quite well.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ayanokouji-kun, have you had any changes in your health?” asked Hiyori.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though she could see that there wasn’t anyone following me anymore, Hiyori didn’t seem especially concerned about that. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Not really, no,” I replied. “So far, so good, anyway.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I didn’t think that I needed to worry, but please be careful,” she said. “A single injury could result in elimination.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I know,” I assured her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hiyori beckoned me to come join her with a wave of her hand, so I decided to sit down beside her.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-before: always;"/>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN__And__Page_Break"><img alt="" height="100%" id="INTERIORIMAGES_4" src="/hola-lector-agua/images/books/y2v4/OEBPS/Images/INTERIORIMAGES_4.jpg" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style=" top: 0px; vertical-align: top; height: 100%;"/></p>
<div style="page-break-before: always;"/>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Three more days to go,” she remarked. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Seems that way, yeah,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t think that there was any particularly deep meaning behind her comment. We sat there and quietly stared out at the ocean, letting it lift our spirits. Generally, in a situation like this, when people met with a dear friend or someone that they were close with, they typically ask about how things were going. This was a battle for survival, so people would inevitably be concerned about their friends.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, Hiyori didn’t seem like she had any questions for me, like how many points I had or anything. I&#160;didn’t get the sense that it was because she wasn’t interested though. Rather, it was more like she believed there was no way I could get expelled.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, Ayanokouji!” shouted Ishizaki, beaming. He must have finally noticed me there because he was grinning at me now. He had an incredibly happy look on his face for some reason. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The rest of the group seemed to have noticed me there right after that, but they quickly grabbed Ishizaki by the shoulder when he tried to walk closer.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What gives?” huffed Ishizaki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t get in their way,” said Nishino.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh?” said Ishizaki. “It ain’t like Ayanokouji hates me or nothin’, right?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s not what we’re talking about…” said Nishino.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Now, now,” said Tsube. “That’s exactly one of Ishizaki-kun’s good points, don’t you think?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Uh, good points? I’d say it’s more that he simply doesn’t have a clue about social situations, but whatever,” said Nishino.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, that’s… I suppose I can’t really deny that,” said Tsube. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It seemed like Nishino and Tsube had opened up to one another quite a bit. This was probably what you’d expect to see in many groups after spending such a long time fighting together on this uninhabited island. If people cooperated to the best of their ability to avoid getting expelled, it’d be easier to overcome the trivial barriers that separated them.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But at the same time, there was something cruel about it too. Once this special exam was over, the class-based battles would resume, and a future where classes tried to take each other down awaited us. When that time came, there would probably be more than a few students who would not be able to make normal, rational decisions.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Since I was from Class D, I determined that I probably wouldn’t have much to talk to this group about, so I tried to get up and leave. But when I stood up to walk away, Ishizaki quickly scrambled toward me in a panic, grabbing me by the shoulder.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, sorry for keepin’ ya, dude!” he said. “But, hey, I’m feelin’ kinda outta my element here, bein’ the only guy. Why don’t you stick around for a bit, Ayanokouji?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Stick around…?” I repeated.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I mean, we don’t got any more exam stuff to do today. I’m sure you were plannin’ on campin’ around I9 anyway, right?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">J10, which had been my last designated area, wasn’t the most suitable place to set up camp. The ground there was rocky and there were intense winds. With that in mind, I had intended on staying somewhere around I10 while avoiding the coast, as Ishizaki had suggested. But,&#160;well…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s a marvelous idea,” said Hiyori, in complete agreement with Ishizaki. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She got up and approached me as she spoke. I was relatively close with Hiyori and Ishizaki, I supposed, so there wouldn’t be many problems in that regard. I had to wonder how the other girls felt about it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sure, that’s fine with me!” said Tsube. “Don’t you agree? I mean, Ayanokouji-kun seems like he wouldn’t hurt a fly.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah,” Nishino agreed easily.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Well, apparently, there wasn’t a single objection to the idea. I thought to myself that this group had such a comfortable, friendly vibe about them that it somehow made you forget that an intense special exam was underway. This was the kind of feeling you tended to see in Ichinose’s class. I supposed this meant that little by little, Ryuuen’s class was starting to undergo a gradual transformation. </span></p>
<div style="page-break-before: always;"/>
<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">5.6</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">“A</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">YANOKOUJI-SENPAI.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> Ayanokouji-senpai…!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Late into the night when I was fast asleep, I found myself woken up by the sound of someone calling my name. They were speaking in a hushed whisper, quiet enough so not to be heard by anyone else in the surrounding area. The voice seemed to be coming from right next to my tent. I checked the time on my wristwatch and saw that it was just after two-thirty in the morning. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s me. It’s Nanase.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I immediately shot up, fully awake, and peeked my head out of my tent. Out in the deep darkness, I could see a panicked Nanase, illuminated by the light of her tablet.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What are you doing at this hour?” I asked. “You’re not hurt, are you?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m perfectly fine,” she said. “I was in I9, just like you, senpai. To tell you the truth, I saw you from afar yesterday evening. I decided to avoid making direct contact then since I was working with Housen-kun at the time.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…And?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I have some urgent news that I wanted to tell you,” she said. “So, today… Well, no, I suppose it was technically yesterday, to be precise, since the date’s changed, but… Anyway, I overheard Housen-kun talking. The first-year students are going to launch a large-scale attack against you on the twelfth day of the exam, Ayanokouji-senpai.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“A large-scale attack?” I asked. “Did he say that he wanted you to be part of it too, Nanase?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, uh… No. Well, let’s see. Perhaps I should start from the beginning…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After calming down and catching her breath, Nanase began to explain it all to me. It was unclear at what time of what day this had happened, but apparently Housen had been asked to go to a meeting with Takahashi, Yagami, Tsubaki, and Utomiya, but he ignored them. However, a student who was likely a messenger of one of those four appeared on the ninth day of the exam with a walkie-talkie in hand, and once again requested for Housen’s cooperation with them on something.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The request was that he would help force me into being eliminated during the endgame stage of the uninhabited island exam. They also intended to hunt down and take out the other upperclassman students who were going it alone in this exam in a similar fashion. The specific details of the plan were to be communicated on the day it would occur, and apparently Housen still had the walkie-talkie on him, even now. From the sound of things, Nanase had been told that Housen had no intention whatsoever of cooperating and that he was only pretending to go along with it. He was planning on finding some way to take advantage of the situation.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So, they were going to come after me in the endgame of the exam… Taking steps in advance had been worth it. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“They made the right call, not conveying the exact details of the plan until the very last minute,” I remarked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If word had gotten out, such as what exactly they were planning to do and when, it would have made it that much easier for me to come up with countermeasures. The truth was that they hadn’t yet given any details to Housen because there was a possibility that he would betray them.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Who is going to give the orders?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I do not know,” Nanase replied. “But I do know that it was mostly Tsubaki-san that I heard speaking over the walkie-talkie.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“She seems like the kind of person who doesn’t come out in the open very much though,” I observed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I am of the same opinion,” Nanase agreed. “If I had to say, I got the impression that Class C mostly rallied around Utomiya-kun, if anyone. It’s just that, well, Utomiya-kun and Housen-kun don’t get along at all. Whenever they get into a discussion, they start fighting almost immediately. It’s possible they specifically chose Tsubaki-san to function as an intermediary in this case.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was possible. But it was also possible that someone like Yagami or Takahashi might be pulling the strings from behind the scenes.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, even though we only know the date that this is going to happen, I’m grateful. Anyway, even though it’s this late, it’s probably best that you don’t stay too long. If&#160;they find out that you gave me information, it could spell trouble later,” I warned her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">My safety aside, it was possible that this could disrupt Nanase’s life at school in the days to come. For better or for worse, she still had to spend her days in Class 1-D with Housen. I instructed her to leave before Housen realized that she was gone.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay,” she agreed. “I will be sure to contact you if there are any other major developments.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, no, that’s okay,” I told her. “I appreciate that, but what you’ve done for me so far during this exam is plenty. Even if you do happen to see the first-year students making a move, you don’t have to come tell me. There’s no need to help me so recklessly, against your better judgment.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ve gotten plenty of information from you, Nanase,” I insisted, cutting her off. “Now you should do what you need to be doing, as a member of Housen and Amasawa’s group.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If Nanase lost all her credibility with her classmates, I&#160;wouldn’t be able to get any information from her in the future. If that happened, her value to me would drop dramatically.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you say so, Ayanokouji-senpai, then…okay, I understand,” she replied, bowing deeply.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">With that, she left, running off into the darkness. When she had completely disappeared from view, I took out my tablet and thought things through a little. My drowsiness was completely gone, and I stared at the screen. I could assume that the information Nanase had overheard was the genuine truth. However, whether things would actually proceed as described was another matter.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The details of Class 1-D were still unknown to me, but Housen was the type of person who used a similar power as Ryuuen to subjugate his class. However, while they were alike in that respect, when dealing with obstacles, Housen tended to put himself at the center of his plans whenever he decided to make a move. In consideration of that tendency, he had kept Nanase by his side ever since he had started at our school.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Nanase certainly had incredible mental fortitude, far beyond that of ordinary first-year high-school students. There was no doubt that her respectable academic abilities and elevated physical prowess made her a valuable asset. But the degree of trust that Housen placed in Nanase was still completely unclear. If he didn’t trust her, then would he have let her hear about the first-years’ surprise attack?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I doubted that Housen would think that Nanase was possibly my ally all on his own. However, I wouldn’t be surprised if he felt like there was something off. If&#160;Amasawa got involved and let something slip, then it was possible that he knew something. In any case, the idea that the first-years were planning to attack me wasn’t surprising. I had assumed from the very beginning that they’d come after me during this uninhabited island exam since I already had a bounty on my head anyway. I&#160;appreciated that Nanase had come to report this to me, but nothing about what I was planning to do was going to change. </span></p>
<div style="page-break-before: always;"/>
<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">5.7</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;"> </span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">MANAGED TO GET</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> in a little bit of sleep after that. Then, when the clock struck six o’clock that morning, I&#160;used the GPS search. If today really was the day that the plan was being set in motion, then I should have been able to see some unusual movements, primarily among the first-year students, including Housen. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And it looks like…there’s nothing unusual about their positioning,” I muttered to myself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Housen’s group, who were also in my Table, was positioned nearby. Aside from that, though, the rest were at least three spaces away from me. No one seemed to be setting up any kind of attack at the moment. Since it was difficult to imagine that they would make a move out in the open and in front of other people, I supposed that as long as Ishizaki and the others were near, I should be safe.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hiyori, Ishizaki, and the rest of their group started to wake up and were getting ready for the twelfth day of the exam. Once everyone was prepared to move, we all started walking together.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ugh, this sucks, dude, havin’ to go uphill already first thing in the mornin’,” grumbled Ishizaki, who was apparently still a little sleepy and not fully awake yet.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Nishino shot him down. “Nothin’ we can do ’bout it. If we suddenly found ourselves in a designated area, we’d lose out on points.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">They probably had many back-and-forth exchanges just like that over these past ten plus days. The rest of the group focused on walking, ignoring Ishizaki and Nishino’s bickering.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ayanokouji-kun, haven’t you ever felt lonely being all alone in this test for so long?” asked Hiyori as she walked beside me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Not especially, no. If anything, I feel like it’s been pretty easy,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I…guess I think it would be a little lonely and scary, myself</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">” she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Scary, huh? I can’t really imagine you being scared of anything,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had the impression that she had really thick skin when it came to these kinds of topics, since she was always so nonchalant about things. I expected that even if she were to see some kind of psychic or spiritual phenomenon, she’d just clap her hands and say, “Wow, that’s amazing” or something.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m actually a pretty big scaredy-cat, you know, even now,” Hiyori admitted. “That’s why I think you’re amazing, Ayanokouji-kun. Honestly, I do.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I think what Horikita and Ibuki are doing is more amazing, though. Don’t you?” I said in return.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The longer that those two battled against solitude, the weaker their metal states would become. They’d start worrying about things that didn’t really matter. They would begin to see or sense things that weren’t really there, rattled by the sound of the wind or the shaking of a nearby tree. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, that’s true… A girl trying to make it on this uninhabited island all alone… That would be impossible for me,” said Hiyori.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When she imagined it, Hiyori had a somewhat fearful look on her face, just for a moment. I wondered if I was able to see this rare side of her just because of this uninhabited island. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Before I realized it, Ishizaki, who had been walking on ahead of us, turned back to look at Hiyori and me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Man, though, you two sure get along great, huh?” he said as he watched us.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, you really need to stop stickin’ your nose where it doesn’t belong,” hissed Nishino, grabbing him by the scruff of the neck.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ishizaki didn’t seem to pay it any mind though and went on, “You guys should jus’ start datin’ already! Then you can come on over to our class, ’kay? ’Kay?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You are jumpin’ WAY too far ahead!” shouted Nishino. She swung a closed fist down hard on Ishizaki’s head, giving him a good whack. Ishizaki clutched his head and let out a yelp.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ishizaki-kun sure is a funny one, isn’t he?” said Hiyori, giggling lightly, seemingly unbothered by his words.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yeah, it’d be a hassle if you were to honestly entertain every little thing that came out of his mouth. I decided to just casually ignore it, letting it float in one ear and out the other.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Damn, dude! That </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">hurt</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">!” said Ishizaki. “Dontcha think that’s what we need if we’re gonna bring Ayanokouji over and make ’im our ally?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, I don’t think so at all. Besides, </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">you’re</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> the one who seems so super enthusiastic about Ayanokouji-kun here,” replied Nishino.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I supposed that, from Nishino’s point of view, since she didn’t know the specific details about our situation, this must have all looked rather strange. I wouldn’t be surprised if she just saw this as Ishizaki trying way too hard to invite me into their class just because I got a perfect score on a test.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nah, man, it’s more like, well y’know, </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">that</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">? …It’s like we’re on the same wavelength. Yeah, that,” said Ishizaki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wavelength. Yeah, I can’t imagine there’s anyone on </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">your</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> wavelength,” Nishino said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Shot down by Nishino’s harsh criticism, Ishizaki apparently couldn’t take it anymore because he shot me a pleading glance, looking for me to help him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s not true,” Hiyori spoke up. “If you look at Ishizaki-kun this way, you, well, get…it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Everyone present cocked their heads to the side, puzzled.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“‘It’? What’s ‘it,’ exactly?” asked Nishino.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s just it,” said Hiyori. “I won’t answer anything more than that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…O-okay. Anyway, aren’t you glad? Gettin’ complimented by Shiina-san and all,” teased Nishino.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Y-yeah!” said Ishizaki. “I mean, I don’t really get what ‘it’ is s’posed to mean, but gettin’ complimented doesn’t feel too bad!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hiyori probably just hadn’t been able to think of anything specific. But there was no way that I could’ve said such a cruel thing aloud, so I just quietly let their conversation wash over me. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When the clock struck seven o’clock later that morning, our first designated areas for the day were announced. Mine was area H10. Hiyori and the others had a different designated area, J9, so it didn’t seem like we were going to be competing with one another. I wouldn’t have welcomed the idea of fighting people in my own grade level, so thank goodness for that. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Looks like this is where we split, Ayanokouji. See ya later,” said Ishizaki.</span></p>
