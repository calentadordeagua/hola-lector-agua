<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 4:<br/>Fighting Against Solitude</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;"> </span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">BRUSHED THE SPIDERWEBS</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> off my clothes, slowly took my backpack off, and set it down. The ninth day of the uninhabited island exam was as hot and humid a day as ever. Having safely arrived at my fourth designated area, I let out a deep sigh. I had reached my destination on time, just as I planned. The sweat beading on my forehead slowly trickled down to the bridge of my nose, and I wiped it off with my arm.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">My fourth designated area for the day, which was announced at three in the afternoon, had been quite the trek. It took me from area H9 all the way to D5. Making it there in time had been a rather arduous journey. One Task that I could have snatched up had appeared while I was on the move, but I decided to forego it to reduce my risk of getting hit with a penalty.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Despite it taking nearly two hours for me to get to where I needed to go, few other groups had come to this area, including students from other Tables. I successfully managed to arrive early enough to get the third place Early Bird Bonus. Though I was, by and large, not dissatisfied by the results, I wasn’t able to meet up with Sakayanagi because I wasn’t near the starting point. I’d&#160;used up lot of my energy to push on ahead already, and I didn’t want to overdo it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I passed by several groups of Class 2-A students and reached out to talk to them, but unfortunately, none of those groups had walkie-talkies. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Should I push my luck tomorrow morning? No… That’d be dicey.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> I set the matter of contacting Sakayanagi on hold for the time being and proceeded to summarize the day’s events.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, after adding up all of the points I got today, that brings me to a total of a 112… Huh,” I muttered to myself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kuronaga’s group had maintained their position at tenth place in the rankings, with a total of 123 points. I&#160;had moved up to thirteenth place—and there was now only an eleven-point difference between my score and Kuronaga’s group’s. Considering that it would soon be five o’clock, there was a good chance that the day would end with this gap as it was. My goal had been to get to eleventh place, but I supposed that an eleven-point difference in scores was more or less acceptable.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Although I had gotten here later than I planned due to the whole incident with Nanase and the severe weather, I had reached the perfect position: the spot&#160;I&#160;had been aiming for since the beginning. That’s right: I had been&#160;aiming for eleventh place since the start of the uninhabited island special exam. I was in thirteenth place today, a little below where I wanted, but the important thing wasn’t necessarily getting to exactly eleventh place. What was important to me was making sure I got to just barely below tenth place and that I stayed there.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I needed to work hard to accumulate points so that I ended up on the winning side. That was unavoidable. But whether I was working alone or whether I was in a large group that had seven people in it thanks to having the One More card, if I broke into the top ten, I’d stand out even if I didn’t want to. That was because the top ten groups were announced publicly. If I were to stand out, then my rivals would become wary of me, and the risk of sabotage in the near future would surely increase. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">To avoid those risks while still giving me a chance&#160;to shoot for the top ten later, the ideal ranking for me to&#160;reach was eleventh place. However, this strategy did have several drawbacks. Because of the nature of this plan, I needed to be extremely careful in managing my score. Any failure in that department could result in me popping into the top ten too soon, even if just for a moment. If that happened, then my strategy would fail. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">An even bigger drawback was that this plan was highly dependent on the group in tenth place. The closer that the tenth-place group got to the first-place group, the easier it would be for there to be a shakeup. But on the flipside, the more points they needed to catch up to first place, the more difficult it would be to pull off a surprise victory later. This was precisely why it was so important to have those groups at the top drag each other down for&#160;me. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Well, that hadn’t been happening as overtly as I’d hypothesized, and now, some groups had basically pulled far, far ahead of everyone else. I wouldn’t call it compensation or anything, but the second-years as a whole had been able to fight this battle from a relatively advantageous position. That is to say, we weren’t feeling much pressure pushing up against us from below or pressing down on us from above. Trying to sabotage another group more or less amounted to an act of self-sacrifice, which meant that it was difficult for anyone to actually pull some kind of stunt unless they had the luxury of extra points to spare.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">What concerned me most was whatever Nagumo was up to. I anticipated that he might have tried to do something to Kouenji since he was competing with him for first place, but from what I could tell by checking their movements via the GPS, there was no sign of Nagumo interfering with him in any way. I supposed that could have been because Nagumo was focusing his efforts more on building up his own group’s score rather than kicking opponents off the board, but…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, even if I don’t win, if Kouenji takes first and I get second, I wouldn’t really have anything to say to that,” I muttered. “That’d be fine.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If I maintained my place in the rankings, staying somewhere close to eleventh, then I wouldn’t stand out. And even if I did lose time due to sabotage by Amasawa and the first-year students, I wouldn’t drop down into the lower ranks either. What I needed to do was to keep hiding in plain sight among the higher ranks until the end of the twelfth day. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I took it easy for a bit, resting in the shade of a big tree and wiping off my sweat. Then I slung my backpack over my shoulders once again and headed toward the neighboring area, which was only a short distance away. I decided that I wanted to find a clearing that was a little further out of the way from the boundary line. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The sun was starting to set, and I needed to decide where to set up camp for the night. Just then, I noticed a lone single-person tent had appeared in my field of view. Someone else must have gotten here already. Since the tent’s entrance was shut while it was so hot outside, I wondered if the tent’s owner, whoever it was, was away at the moment. They could be conducting reconnaissance of the surrounding area, or perhaps they were using the bathroom.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s a nice spot,” I remarked out loud, to no one in particular.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There didn’t seem to be that many places in the nearby vicinity that were reasonably open and flat. For my part, anyway, it would be quite easy for me to set up camp in this area. However, now that I no longer had Nanase accompanying me, I was a single guy traveling alone. If the owner of this other tent happened to be a girl, then carelessly approaching them could lead to trouble.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">More importantly though, I had to wonder—what did it mean that there was a sole single-person tent here? Was this person acting separately from their group? Or had they been going it alone from the start? If it was the latter, then whoever this person was, it was almost certainly someone that I knew. Regardless of whether I ended up setting up camp here myself, I wanted to identify this other tent’s owner. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I decided to stand around for a while to see what was going on. If they were out walking around, they’d be back before the sun went down completely. And if I heard any noises coming from inside the tent, then I could just call out to them without delay. I knew that it would be more efficient for me to just try calling out to them right away, but… Well, you get the idea.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I proceeded to wait for about ten minutes or so. But even after waiting that long, I didn’t see or hear any sign that they were coming back. I wondered about the possibility that maybe they had gone to bed early, by some chance. Since there was no sign of any of their comrades coming this way to join up with them, I decided to steel myself and just say something.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is anybody there?” I called out from beside the tent.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I held my breath and quietly waited a few seconds to see if I could hear any kind of reaction, but I didn’t hear a single thing.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry for the bother, but I was thinking of setting up my tent near here. If that’s an inconvenience, let me know,” I added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After assuming that the other camper wasn’t around, at least for the time being, I lowered my backpack to the ground. Of course, I made sure to set up at an appropriate distance from the other tent. Although I was a little curious about who this other person was, I finished setting up camp in short order. I was once again impressed by just how much easier it was for me to set up a tent this year compared to when I was on the island last year.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I also rather liked single-person tents because I didn’t need to worry about anyone else. Well, I suppose thinking that way might have been the reason I didn’t really have many friends. A cheerful person, I figured, would find a tent that couldn’t accommodate that many people to be rather boring. I wondered if a day would come when I felt that same way.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…I can’t really imagine that happening,” I muttered to myself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was a future that was never going to come to be.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Just as I was getting ready to get changed and stuff, though, I heard someone call out to me from behind.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I thought I saw something strange. Turns out it’s just&#160;you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Apparently, the owner of the solitary tent nearby was none other than Ibuki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Was I being loud?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Not really.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She only said a few words, and she glared at me right after answering. I thought she was going to say something else, but then she quickly ducked back inside her&#160;tent. There was something about the way she looked that made me uncomfortable, so I decided to go over to her tent to check on her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, do you have a minute?” I called out to her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t get a response. However, I could hear a faint sound coming from within her tent. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I just wanted to ask you something,” I said, trying a different approach.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t get any response that time either. I thought that maybe she was just ignoring me, plain and simple, but it felt like she was up to something sneaky instead.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, I’m going to open your tent and come in,” I said. “Okay?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I waited about thirty seconds, just to be on the safe side, and then I proceeded to open the entrance to her tent. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…What?” she huffed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When I peeked inside, I saw Ibuki sitting down with something in her mouth.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re really— Wait, what are you eating?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Jerky,” she replied.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Jerky? That wasn’t in the uninhabited island manual they handed out to us.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That meant she must’ve gotten her hands on some fresh meat by purchasing it or some other means and prepared it herself. However, it would take a considerable amount of time and effort to make jerky all by yourself. What’s more was that Ibuki had issued a challenge to Horikita right at the start of the exam, and then had immediately set out for her designated area. It was obvious that if she had been walking around with fresh meat on her, it would’ve spoiled in less than a few hours during the summer season. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In that case, could I assume that all of Class 2-B knew how to make their own jerky? Maybe some groups had taken it upon themselves to make it for everyone.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That way, it could be done readily and at a considerably lower price. Dried foods were quite effective options for quick, portable meals. However, pre-prepared foods like jerky that could be stored for an extended period of time didn’t have a great cost-to-performance ratio, considering the high number of points needed to buy them outright. That was why even if you were preparing the same amount of food, it would be more cost-effective to make the jerky yourself from fresh beef since it was cheaper to produce in large quantities. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Although I hadn’t seen any of Ryuuen and Katsuragi’s food, I felt it was safe to assume that they were operating in much the same way, carrying around emergency rations, namely jerky. Even if they could only scrimp on just a few meals by doing so, that still meant they’d be able to pass up a hotly contested Task that offered food without worry.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What does it matter anyway? It’s none of your business,” snapped Ibuki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Although I had taken the liberty of imagining what could be going on with the jerky situation, it didn’t sound like I was going to be able to hear the truth from Ibuki. At any rate, even though Ibuki had been tackling this exam all on her own, her name hadn’t appeared in the bottom ten up until this point, at least as far as I knew. She must have been pushing ahead as hard as she could, collecting point after point. In her case, it was hopeless to try and get high scores in any Tasks that involved academic ability, so her main sources of points must have been Arrival Bonuses for reaching her designated areas and Early Bird Bonuses.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I supposed that with her skills, that narrowed down the Tasks she could take on to ones that primarily focused on physical ability. That meant, of course, that she would inevitably become more fatigued than the other students. And because of that, she probably sustained quite a lot of psychological suffering as well. It was plain for anyone to see that, and it wasn’t an exaggeration to say that she’d already pushed herself past her limits.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How many people have you talked to since the exam started?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh…?” she huffed. She must not have slept very much either, as she had dark circles under her eyes. “Horikita. I told her I wasn’t going to lose to her,” she said. “You heard it too, didn’t you?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In other words, you haven’t had an actual conversation with anyone since the exam started,” I concluded.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I figured that, at most, she had probably only opened her mouth to say yes or no to staff members at Tasks sites when she was trying to register. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You should probably talk to someone, even if it’s just a little,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t talk to enemies,” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Then you should talk to your classmates. If you wander around a bit, you might run into one of them,” I suggested.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t consider anyone in my class my friend.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So, she had been hiding within her shell, and that was how she was continuing to act, even today. I was impressed that she had lasted for nine days in this state, but there were still five days left in the exam. If she were to let it all out, even just for a second, she’d most likely immediately fall apart. Of course, if Ibuki got eliminated, then it was inevitable that she’d get expelled, since she was on her own.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The collective understanding that we all shared for this special exam was to avoid letting groups from our same grade level get expelled as much as possible. The best thing for her to do would be to spend an entire day resting. I&#160;didn’t count the seventh day as rest, even though it had been cancelled anyway. But if she could spend a full day peacefully doing nothing at all, then that alone would restore a great deal of her strength. It wouldn’t be impossible for Ibuki to survive the other remaining four days with the strength she had recovered from that day of rest.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But reality wasn’t that kind. It might sound simple on paper, but it was exceedingly difficult to actually spend an entire day just resting. Even if Ibuki forced herself to take a physical break, whether she could revitalize her spirits was another matter. While she was taking it easy, her rivals would be scoring more points. She’d be pressured by the threat of being overtaken and sinking down to the bottom of the rankings. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Under those circumstances, any ordinary person would find it impossible to spend time recuperating and clearing their mind. Besides, simply failing to reach your designated areas would lead to a loss in points too. If she racked up more penalties, then she would suffer even more in the days to come.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Get out,” said Ibuki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…I’m going.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though this was Ibuki I was talking to, she was still a girl. Peeking into the tent of someone of the opposite sex at a time like this when it was getting dark out was absolutely not the right thing for me to be doing. Even if Ryuuen had been here right now, I was skeptical that we could’ve fundamentally solved this issue anyway. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After leaving Ibuki’s tent, I got back to checking out my clothing situation which I had been in the middle of doing. The wind blew calmly outside now, so it seemed like I’d be able to stay at least somewhat cool tonight. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ibuki must have settled down more now that she had finished whatever she needed to do. She got out of her tent with a wobbling, dazed gait, but she walked straight ahead. She then stuffed her hands in her pockets and approached me. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How many points do you have now?” she asked. Just when I was thinking about how surprised I was that she had finally emerged from her tent, she came out and asked me something so audacious. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We’re enemies, you know,” I reminded her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you can’t tell me, huh,” she said in a low mutter. It sounded like she was implying I was stingy and tight-fisted.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There wasn’t a single person on this entire island who would benefit from knowing I was in thirteenth place.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yep, that’s how it is,” I answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay then, in that case, just tell me if your score is higher or lower than mine. Mine is—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I cut Ibuki off with a wave of my hand just as she was about to go ahead and divulge her own score to me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry, but I can’t answer that question in any way, shape, or form,” I said, interrupting her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even if I were only telling her that my score was above or below hers, I’d still be giving her a clue. That’d be true even if I lied about my score. It might have seemed like a safe bet if I told her that I was ranked lower than she was, but if she thought that I was struggling to get points, then she might come after me aggressively and try and force me into a corner. I had to avoid letting information carelessly slip and allowing it to take on a life of its own. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ibuki, hands still in her pockets, clicked her tongue at me. “Oh well… Fine. Whatever,” she snapped. “Dealing with you is a waste of time anyway.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There you have it. Besides, your real favorite is Horikita, isn’t it?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The second I uttered Horikita’s name, Ibuki’s attitude immediately changed. She had been looking worn-out, but she made the effort to yank her hands out of her pockets and glare at me with her middle finger raised. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you see her, tell her that I am never, ever going to lose to her.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s all well and good, but I’m not the one you should be giving the finger to, am I?” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re the same as her. Since you two are such good pals,” she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Well, no, we weren’t, actually. I wasn’t close with Horikita, but I guessed it was all the same to Ibuki. She must have only come out of her tent to ask me about my score because she soon turned to go back inside.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait a second,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ibuki stopped and turned back toward me, and I took a few steps closer to her. She was clearly wary of me. When I reached my hand out to her, she grew even more suspicious and moved out of the way. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? You wanna go?” she said defiantly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She must have determined that I was arbitrarily fixing for a fight with her. She balled a hand into a fist.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That is absolutely not my intention in the slightest,” I protested, quickly reaching my hand out toward her once again. I didn’t give her the opportunity to escape and grabbed onto her wrist.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The hell are you doing?!” she shouted.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Flustered, she lashed out with a kick, but I blocked it with my other hand. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you not going for the top ranks yourself?” she asked me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Unfortunately, I can’t really envision myself making it into the top ten,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. I thought for sure you’d get a score close to mine.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She seemed to be extremely interested in me. Well, to be precise, I didn’t think that I was the only one she was interested in though. Kiryuuin was, in her own way, looking at and analyzing the strategies being used by students throughout our entire school across every grade level.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This is probably when most groups will start seeing a drop in their efficiency,” she said. “Don’t give up and keep giving it your best.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kiryuuin was someone I hadn’t known at all until just recently, but I gathered that she was an exceptionally capable person. She was a third-year student with excellent intuition and perception, which you wouldn’t glean just from looking at OAA alone.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That being said… From what we can see on our tablets so far, how do you feel about the fact that no groups have been completely eliminated yet?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“All I can say is that in this situation, we can’t let our guards down. Not even for a moment,” I answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I stopped by the starting point yesterday and picked up a little information,” she said. “Apparently, some groups suffering from a lack of food and water are trying to avoid collapsing altogether by having individual members spread out in an effort to endure the situation.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s a wise decision on their part,” I remarked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">No matter how many points a group collected, if every member of the group was eliminated, they’d be disqualified and expelled immediately. In that case, it was much safer to send one or two people back to the starting area even if it meant a drop in efficiency. Water was readily available there, and you could more easily avoid getting sick if you could maintain proper hygiene.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sure that the groups in the bottom ten are wishing to themselves, ‘I don’t care what group it is, I just hope some other group gets eliminated,’” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“People who have no regard for appearances will use any means necessary,” said Kiryuuin. “Just be careful, okay?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Shouldn’t you be worrying about yourself in that regard, Kiryuuin-senpai? As a girl?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hmm. Well, I suppose that, yes, as a fair maiden, I should be aware of the dangers,” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Though I had asked her that only as a joke, she was surprisingly serious about it. “If it comes to that, then… I suppose I’ll just push through by force.” Kiryuuin then tightly clenched her hand into a fist.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Her answer was completely unlike what you’d expect from a “fair maiden.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I can’t tell how serious you’re being about this,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“Fu fu,”</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> she laughed. “Sorry for taking up so much of your time. After all, you and I both need every moment we can get. We can’t spare a single second of a single minute.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">With that, she gently waved goodbye and started to walk away. Judging from the direction she was headed in, I guessed she must have been aiming for a Task. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Before she was out of sight, though, she paused. “You’re not coming? There might still be room for you to register for the Task too, if you leave right now.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thanks, but I’ll pass,” I replied. “I don’t think that I can compete with you, Kiryuuin-senpai.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">At this point in time, it looked like there were only enough openings for up to two groups to register for this Task. In addition to the fact that there were three or more rival groups headed that way, Kiryuuin was </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">also</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> headed to that Task, so my chances of being able to register weren’t very high at all.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As I watched her go, even though she should have been in a hurry, she stopped again and looked back at me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hm, so that’s how it is… Well, I guess I’ll just go and see for myself in person,” she said, speaking as though she had deduced my strategy. Kiryuuin finally left and headed toward the Task.</span></p>

<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">4.3</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HE SUN HAD SET</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> on the tenth day of the exam, and it was now after nine o’clock at night. It was time for me to check the GPS information for the top and bottom ten groups. Suddenly, a bright light flickered from outside my tent.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is someone out and about at this hour…?” I muttered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was risky, but I supposed it was conceivable that someone could have been trying to reach their final designated area for the day if they hadn’t been able to until now. I couldn’t help but follow the light with my eyes as I sat inside my tent. It wasn’t as though the light was being shined in my direction though. Rather, whoever it was seemed to be sweeping the light to and fro as they walked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The movements of their flashlight seemed unstable, as if they were desperately searching for something. I got curious, so I decided to pop out of my tent. Actually, it looked as though they were desperately searching for </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">someone</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">. Was Amasawa looking for me because she wanted to get close and try something? No, I couldn’t imagine that she would have been using her flashlight so haphazardly if that was the case. After closing the distance between our positions by using GPS, she should have been able to take advantage of the darkness and gotten closer to me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yume-chaaaan!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I heard a faint voice, and it sounded like it was coming from the same direction as the the flashlight. I&#160;didn’t know who the owner of the voice was, but nicknames aside, I knew that there was only one person in our&#160;school named&#160;Yume. I was certain that they were calling for Kobashi Yume from Class 2-C. In that case, it was probably correct for me to assume that whoever was speaking was either someone from that class, or who had a connection to it. If&#160;I remembered correctly, there was a girl named Shiranami Chihiro in Kobashi’s same group. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">At any rate, it sounded like whoever was calling was about to burst into tears at any moment. I could have ignored her and let things be, but since this was a student from Class 2-C, she should be able to get in touch with Sakayanagi from Class 2-A since those classes were cooperating on some level. Our tablets had a flashlight function, so I took mine out and turned the flashlight&#160;on. It wasn’t as dependable a light source as a regular flashlight, but it was more than enough for someone to see in the dark. Shortly thereafter, the girl noticed me and pointed her flashlight in my direction.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yume-chan?!” the girl said, sounding panicked, her voice getting more audible as she drew closer.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After getting flashed by the dazzling beam from her flashlight, I could slowly start to see the flashlight’s owner come into view.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yume-chan!” she shouted once again.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, sorry, but I’m not Yume,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The person that had emerged from amidst the trees was none other than Shiranami, after all.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, um, Ayanokouji-kun… G-good evening,” she stammered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Although we were hardly close, she seemed somewhat relieved. Had her situation really been so upsetting that she was happy to see even me? </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s pretty dangerous to be out at night all alone,” I&#160;said. “Where are Kobashi and Takemoto?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, um, well… I actually don’t know where they are… I was walking along in a hurry, and I lost track of where I was going, and…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wasn’t going to ask her such an uncouth question like, “Why are you all alone in the woods in the middle of the night?”</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;"> </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This was an expansive forest; everywhere you looked, it was an ocean of trees. If you pressed on into the woods without being totally prepared, thinking to yourself, “Oh, they probably went this way,” you’d end up losing your sense of direction in the blink of an eye. As a result of her getting lost, Shiranami had probably wandered far, far from the rest of her group.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“About how long has it been since you lost track of them?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not sure… I think maybe fifteen…or, like, twenty minutes, maybe?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even if she had traveled in the opposite direction from her teammates, they shouldn’t have wandered so far apart that it was worth getting depressed over. But, at the very least, she had gotten far enough away from her teammates that they were no longer able to hear each other.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“At any rate, walking around aimlessly is just going to make this situation even worse,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“O-okay,” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I figured that for the time being, I’d take the lead and give her instructions to follow me while using my tablet to light the way ahead. It’d be a hassle if I ended up getting lost too, after all. Still, I couldn’t just leave my tent and stuff here while I went out to look for Shiranami’s group with her. I was sure that some students must have had similar troubles and got lost in the same way.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Some might have managed to find their way back through pure chance, but for others, it might have taken a long time. If students couldn’t find their way back,&#160;though… Well, making it through the woods in the middle of the night wasn’t exactly an easy thing to do. Even if they didn’t have any major physical problems in doing so, it was still difficult emotionally. Shortly after, we reached my campsite, and I talked to the anxious Shiranami there.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There are lots of bugs out,” I told her. “For the time being, you can stay in my tent.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh?!” Her shout sounded more afraid than surprised.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not going to go inside, so you can relax,” I added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had some problems trying to explain the situation to her, but in the end, I basically forced Shiranami into the tent as she was still unable to wrap her head around what was happening. After that, I closed the entrance behind her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I-I’m sorry… I was just resting, and…” she wailed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s all right,” I assured her. “More importantly, were Kobashi and Takemoto both looking healthy? Normal?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah,” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In that case, they must have been panicking as well since Shiranami hadn’t returned. I assumed that they must have been discussing whether to go out and search or to stay where they were.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Did your group make a plan for what to do if you got split up?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I thought I’d ask and see, but Shiranami simply shook her head.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s possible that Takemoto, the guy from your group, might go out to look for you by himself, Shiranami,” I&#160;said. “But if he did, there’d be a risk of someone else getting lost and being in distress. They’d be taking quite a big risk if both your teammates left their tents and stuff behind to go out looking for you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Additionally, packing up their tents and backpacks before setting out to search for Shiranami wouldn’t exactly be an effective option either, since Shiranami could very well return all by herself in that time and find herself all alone at a deserted campsite. For them to do things as safely as possible, it was preferable to not walk far enough away as to lose sight of their own tents, but to stay in the nearby vicinity and rely on using lights and loud shouts to find Shiranami, hoping that she would notice them.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But if Shiranami’s group hadn’t made any detailed plans ahead of time, and since one of the girls had gotten separated from the group, I wasn’t sure if her teammates would be able to keep level heads. It was entirely possible that they might panic and go out searching for her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do I do…?” wailed Shiranami.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She was talking to herself, not asking me for my opinion on anything. I supposed you could say that this was trivial, but on the other hand, you could consider what she was doing to be a major mistake. It was understandable why she was doing it, since she was overwhelmed with anxiety. The problem was the other two people in her group. Well, actually, it was possible that there were even more people.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you still just have the same small group of three people? Or have you increased your group to four or more people?” I asked her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s…” she started, but then trailed off.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So far, Shiranami had openly explained everything that had happened in detail, but now she suddenly stopped talking. Since she should’ve known the size of her own group well enough, she must’ve hesitated to tell me for a different reason. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Right now, Ichinose’s class was working together with Sakayanagi’s class in a cooperative partnership. I was sure that there were also friend groups that further transcended those boundaries, of course, but the majority of the groups were originally created via that arrangement. Obviously, to tell me the details about the inner workings of these groups would be considered an information leak. In that sense, it was appropriate on Shiranami’s part not to casually mention whether there had been any additions to her group since the start of the exam. There was value in that. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I understand, and you don’t have to tell me the exact details of your situation. But, for the time being, just hear me out,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">With that out of the way, I went on to elaborate.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If I were a member of your group, Shiranami, then I would first look at the situation at hand. I would determine that a girl was wandering alone in the dark forest, having lost her way back.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Shiranami nodded meekly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wouldn’t just leave things be then, of course. First, I&#160;would raise my voice and shout her name loudly, to see if she answered back. But, like I said before, if I didn’t get a response by doing that, then I’d have to try something else. Now, let’s assume for example that it was Kobashi who had gotten separated from the group. What would you and Takemoto do in that case?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I think that we would probably…go out looking for Yume-chan, together…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even if you ran the risk of running into trouble yourselves? You could end up getting hurt and eliminated from the exam,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“She’s my friend. I couldn’t just leave her,” said Shiranami, resolutely.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was definitely an answer I’d expect from someone from Ichinose’s class. Whether that was an advantage or disadvantage was a separate issue entirely. Takemoto, being from Class A, might initially try and stop Shiranami from going, but he would probably end up going along to help her. The best approach for me to take here would probably be to let her use my tent to wait things out while her teammates came to meet up with her. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Besides, I was sure that if the situation called for it, her teammates would use the GPS search and come looking for her. Still, with this thick cover of darkness, I didn’t know how well a search would help, even if you came into our vicinity once or twice.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you have points to spare? Would you worry about your spot in the rankings if you used the search two or three times?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I…I don’t know, actually,” she replied. “I don’t think it would be a particularly good idea for us to use it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">From the sound of it, her group certainly wasn’t maintaining an outstanding position in the rankings. Before the exam concluded, there would be no way of knowing if using the search would have any impact on their position, or if the points used to search spelled the difference between victory or defeat. As for Shiranami, I was sure that she’d feel guilty and upset at herself if her teammates used points to come searching for her in the same way.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In that case, I supposed the best course of action would be to just wait and see. But we couldn’t necessarily rule out the possibility that her teammates would be coming to look for her, or that they simply wouldn’t be able to find her. Besides, in this case, since I couldn’t use the tent myself, that meant I’d be spending the night outside. That would certainly disrupt my pace, which I had been able to keep at a steady rhythm up until now. If I was going to take action, then…I supposed now was the time to do it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Got any energy?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh?” she stammered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I mean, do you have enough energy left to walk?” I clarified.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Y-yes. I think I should be okay to do that…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I urged her to come out of the tent.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Let’s get moving now, so that you can join up with your group,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But…how?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This isn’t a problem that we’ll be able to solve by just stumbling around in the dark. We’re going to use this,” I explained, showing her the tablet in my hand. “If we use the GPS search, then we’ll know which direction they’re in, and we can find out approximately how far away they are.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Still, it wasn’t going to be easy for Shiranami to meet up with her teammates. Making our way through the woods under the cover of darkness like this was going to be an extremely arduous task. And for average students like Shiranami, it was going to be impossible without repeatedly using the GPS search.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why are you helping me…?” she asked meekly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why? Well, I guess that one reason for it is that this exam is a battle between grade levels, more or less,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But still, going so far as using your GPS search for me is…” she protested.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Using one or two points wasn’t that big of a burden, at least from my perspective. I could always collect more points again and catch up, as long as it wasn’t going to put me any higher than eleventh place. Since there was no point in talking about this any further, I figured I’d just try and say something that sounded reasonable.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I guess if I had to say why… It might be because it’s Ichinose’s class,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The instant I said those words, I turned to look back at her and saw Shiranami’s face stiffen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait, could you…?” she said quietly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Did I say something awkward? </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hm?” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait, Ayanokouji-kun, could you and Honami-chan…?” She trailed off again.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though Shiranami had gotten far enough to mention both my and Ichinose’s names, she suddenly stopped, closing her mouth. I was able to more or less understand what she was trying to say, though it took me a minute. I thought back to what Ichinose’s other classmates said to me the other day, when I ran into them.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s nothing,” I said, trying to answer her preemptively.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But Shiranami’s expression remained visibly stiffened with surprise. At any rate, for the time being, I put the subject on hold and went ahead with using the search. Kobashi and Takemoto’s GPS signals were displayed on top of one another, which meant that they were most definitely still together. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We walked on ahead in search of Shiranami’s group. For about ten minutes, we headed in the direction of their GPS signals.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Chihiro-chan!!!”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nah. I’m sure that you guys would have found each other eventually,” I replied. “I just hope I wasn’t overstepping or anything.”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I figured that I’d go ahead and broach a certain subject with Takemoto now, while I had the chance. “Hey, I wanted to ask you something. Do you have a walkie-talkie?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? A walkie-talkie? Yeah, I do, but…” he replied, but he didn’t finish his thought.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you don’t mind, I was hoping to see if I could talk to Sakayanagi for a minute,” I told him. “I wanted to ask her if a Class D student that I’ve been worried about has gotten back to the starting point.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh yeah, if that’s all, then I’d be happy to. Hold on just a minute,” he said.</span></p>

It was, essentially, a function that allowed you to talk only with specific people one-on-one without allowing your communication to be intercepted by others. The groups that had gotten walkie-talkies for this exam had probably prepared codes so they could prevent information leaks from happening.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Takemoto used the walkie-talkie to call Sakayanagi to see if she was available. Shortly after he reached out to her, he got a response, and then he handed me the walkie-talkie.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’d like to talk with Sakayanagi in private for a few minutes,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kobashi, Shiranami, and Takemoto all nodded happily, agreeing to my request. They graciously gave me some space. I still made sure that they could see the walkie-talkie, of course, to show them that I wasn’t trying to pull any cheap tricks. I talked with Sakayanagi for a while, and then I handed the device back to Takemoto.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s all then, Sakayanagi. Sorry for bothering you this late at night,” said Takemoto, saying a few words to her himself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">My communication with Sakayanagi had ended with that exchange between Takemoto and her, which served as proof that the conversation had been concluded without any problems.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You really helped me out,” I told Takemoto. “I was able to get the information I needed from Sakayanagi, thank goodness.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s great! Oh, also, Sakayanagi asked me to give this to you, Ayanokouji,” he replied, handing me a walkie-talkie.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay, thanks,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We really need to say thank you too. Isn’t that right?” said Kobashi, gesturing to Shiranami.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thank you so much for all of your help, Ayanokouji-kun,” said Shiranami.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The three of them thanked me once again, and it was decided that the four of us would spend the night here together.
