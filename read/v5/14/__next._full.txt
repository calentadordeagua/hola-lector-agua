<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 8:<br/>What You and I Lack</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HE BELL RANG</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> and the second half of the sports festival began. It was time for the recommended participants’ events. Only elite students from each class would take part in the remaining four competitions. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re participating in the scavenger hunt, right, Ayanokouji-kun?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’d rather not, if possible.” There was nothing I could do about it, though. I’d lost at rock-paper-scissors. Six people from each class were going to participate in the scavenger hunt. Due to the low number of participants, there was one group of four people running in the race. Thus, the number of points you could obtain was greater than what you could get during the individual competitions.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The problem is that Sudou-kun’s not here.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We’d decided Sudou would participate in all the recommended-participant events, but he was gone, so he’d simply be marked absent. Had we prepared a substitute competitor? Horikita also had yet to return to our camp. Hopefully, that meant that things were proceeding well.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If it’s all right with you, I’d like your opinion on something, Ayanokouji-kun. I would’ve asked Horikita-san as well, but she’s not here.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hirata, you don’t need my opinion to make a sound decision, right?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Right, Horikita had yet to return to our camp. I’d thought that in the worst-case scenario, she would have returned to the camp alone before the afternoon events started, but this was unexpected. Maybe things were going well after all.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I think we need a substitute. In the individual competitions, our class ranks right at the bottom. To win in overall points, we need to win the next events,” he replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, who do we choose as the substitute?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We need 100,000 points to put in a substitute. I’ll figure something out with the points. I think we’d do well substituting Ike-kun or Yamauchi-kun,” said Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s because, if they do happen to take first place, they’ll be able to put points toward their tests. Right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. We can use that to our advantage.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was a good plan for the scavenger hunt, in which luck had a significant part in the outcome. Ike and Yamauchi played a round of rock-paper-scissors. In the end, Ike won, and triumphantly joined the scavenger hunt team. “All right. I’ll do my best, for Sudou’s sake!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He seemed to have fighting spirit to spare, at least. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The referees explained the competition before it started. “Some items in the scavenger hunt are quite difficult to procure. To pick a new item, you can request a redraw, but there will be a thirty-second waiting period. You must make your redraw request to the referee when you draw your lot during the competition. The game ends when three players reach the goal. That’s all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After that explanation, we started getting ready for the second-round scavenger hunt, which I was participating in.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey.” Someone called to me. I didn’t even need to look to know that it was Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, that muscle-headed moron won’t be participating in the scavenger hunt, huh? I thought he’d take part for sure. Suzune’s not here, either. They couldn’t be doing each other backstage, right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No idea. That has nothing to do with me,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What a shitty answer.” Losing interest in me, Ryuuen left. It looked as though he would be running in the second race, too. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Pretty soon, the first race started. The other classes had put forward their more athletic students, so Ike was overtaken right at the start. But speed wasn’t all that counted in the scavenger hunt—the real deciding factor was which item you drew. Ike chose his lot and checked the contents.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The students in the lead searched this way and that way, leaving the field to find their items.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Whoooooooa!” Ike struck a victory pose and took off running back toward the starting point. “Ayanokouji! Lend me your left foot! Your left foot!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“My left foot?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Your shoe, dude! Your shoe! That’s my item!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ike showed me a slip of paper with the words </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“Your classmate’s left foot (shoe)” </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">written on it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If I give it to you, I can’t run anymore, though.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Geh?!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He’d run over to me since I was close by, but he couldn’t use a competitor’s shoe. Ike panicked and rushed toward the camp. However, it looked as though the other students were also having a hard time with the scavenger hunt, since no one was heading toward the goal. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Thanks to his luck during the draw, Ike—somehow—ultimately found his way into first place. “Not bad, not bad at all,” he said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Moments later, Class A came in second, followed by Class B, and then Class C in last place. Soon afterward came the signal for the start of the second race. I went to draw a lot, lagging slightly behind the other students.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I reached inside the box, touching several slips of paper in turn. Carefully, I took one out and undid the four folds it. “Now then, what will mine say?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“Ten friends.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re kidding, right?” I felt myself blacking out. Just one friend would have been bad enough, but ten? They had to be screwing with me, right? I couldn’t even think of ten people I was on speaking terms with.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Dude, why are you spacing out? Hurry up, Ayanokouji!” Ike sounded a little full of himself, still on a high from taking first place. But there was nothing I could do. Two of the only classmates I could count as friends, Horikita and Sudou, were absent. Since Ichinose and Kanzaki were currently my enemies, I couldn’t count them, either.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’d like to request a change.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The other students were already running off to find their items. In accordance with the rules, I waited thirty seconds, then redrew my lot.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“A person you love.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, no, no. No, no, no, no.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">What in the world was up with the slips I had drawn? They were screwing with me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ch-change, please.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I could sense Class D’s confusion, but there really was nothing I could do. What on earth would someone else have done in my position? If I showed that slip of paper to a member of the opposite sex, it would have been the same as confessing romantic feelings for them. Even if I lied and asked them to play along, it would have been incredibly embarrassing. So, before I could even start looking for my item, I had to take on a one-minute handicap.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“A table clock.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">My third draw finally produced something I could actually obtain, but I’d have to go inside the school to find a table clock. I checked the teachers’ tent, just in case, but came up empty-handed. While I did that, the other three contestants reached the goal.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This isn’t good.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Lady Luck had snubbed me, and I came in last. It wasn’t a question of holding back; I just couldn’t do a thing about it.</span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">8.1</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HE AFTERNOON COMPETITIONS</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> would be starting right about now. At least I’d found the red-haired student I was looking for sitting on a sofa in the dormitory lobby.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sudou-kun.” I called out gently, so as not to startle him. He turned to look at me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Horikita.” He appeared surprised. He probably simply hadn’t expected me to show up. “Why are you here? Don’t tell me you came to persuade me to go back?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do I look like the type to come all the way here to persuade you?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That… No, you don’t. So, did you come here just to scold me or somethin’?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t know about that. I must admit that I’m not sure what to say at all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh?” Sudou-kun tilted his head like he didn’t quite understand. I wondered why that was. I’d finally found Sudou-kun, and I felt as though I couldn’t really say anything. I tried to remember why I’d tried so hard to find him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you stay out of the competitions, Class D has no hope of winning.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Probably not. Guess we’re in a lot of trouble right now, huh?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. We’re at the very bottom of the rankings. To turn things around, we need to take first place in all the recommended-participant events. Even then, we won’t make the top spot.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Despite having excellent athletes like Sudou-kun, Class D as a whole was clearly inferior in this sports festival.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And after I carried everyone on my back. That Hirata,” Sudou-kun huffed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“He did nothing wrong by stopping you from going on a rampage. On the contrary, you should be grateful to him. If you’d hit Ryuuen-kun, you might have been disqualified,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I just couldn’t stand being on the receiving end of that. What Ryuuen did was foul play.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You may be a bit of a problem child, but you certainly gave it your all today,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sudou hadn’t acted like himself. That alone was a miracle. For his classmates’ sake, he’d been as good a leader as he knew how to be. He was hot-headed, as usual, but at the root of that was a desire to win. He’d performed excellently in the group events, and I needed to acknowledge his worth.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That said, there’s still a lot you need to work on,” I continued. “The fact that you’re here alone right now is proof.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The heck does that mean?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If people could truly rely on and trust you, you’d have a lot of classmates coming after you, not just me. People who wanted to convince you to come back, I mean.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sudou-kun kicked the table in irritation.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s the problem right there,” I continued. “You try to bully your way through Class D. It happened during the midterm exams, and during the dispute with Class C. Now you’ve snapped and flipped out. If you keep doing these kinds of things, no one will follow you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re seriously tryin’ to preach to me? Give me a break, Horikita. I’m really pissed,” Sudou grumbled. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Agitated by my lecture, Sudou-kun fidgeted, his legs shaking as he desperately tried to let out his frustration.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Look, I messed up, but I just can’t stop myself from doin’ it. Nothing to do in that case, right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I thought you said you were going to carry everyone in the class?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I never said that. The other guys were asking me to, remember?” he replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even so, you have a certain degree of responsibility.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“God. Shut up, already. I don’t care about that,” he huffed.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If you were just looking at the participation table, you could’ve done it whenever. In other words, even if we had done some scheming behind the scenes, you would still find out the order as long as you kept checking it. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida… No, Ryuuen definitely would have done that.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But if you hid the table until the last possible minute before submitting it, even if someone </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">did</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> happen to see it, they wouldn’t have had time to meddle. You still should’ve been able to prevent leaks.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s true enough, I suppose.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah, but doing something like that might end up throwing the rest of the class into chaos later. That wouldn’t be good, either.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida was right. In order to prevent spying, we would’ve had to exercise caution from the beginning. If we’d waited to submit the table just before the deadline, like she said, we’d have reduced the likelihood of traitors—but also severely confused our classmates. Likewise, the other students would’ve resented us if we secretly changed the roster without consulting them. The ideal countermeasure would have been to consider the possibility of a leak from the very beginning, come up with multiple versions of the participation table as a class, and submit one at random. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If we had done that, then no matter which one we submitted, we would’ve been able to compete. Additionally, it would be a countermeasure against leaks, and there wouldn’t have been any pushback from within the class.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I understand what you’re getting at, but I’m not the culprit, okay? I don’t want to suspect any of my classmates, either,” said Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, how about we check with Chabashira-sensei? I’m sure she can tell us whether any students specifically asked to see the participation table after we submitted it,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was especially true for Kushida. If Kushida had gone to ask about the list after she admitted that she had taken pictures with her phone, then she would have seemed even more suspicious.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…………”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida shut her mouth. For the first time that day, her smile disappeared. She’d just implicitly confirmed my suspicions. However, a thin smile crept back onto her lips right away.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hee hee. You really aren’t an ordinary person, are you, Ayanokouji-kun?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She laughed. This was a face I’d seen before. The face of the secret Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I suppose there’s nothing I can do, now that I’ve been found out. Yes. I leaked the participation table,” she told&#160;me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You admit it?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. If you asked Chabashira-sensei, you would’ve found me out anyway. It was just a matter of time. Besides, even if I tell you the truth, Ayanokouji-kun, I’m confident I won’t be exposed. You haven’t forgotten, have you? About my uniform, which you touched? If I revealed that to everyone, wouldn’t that be a big problem for you?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She was threatening me. If I were to tell anyone that she was the traitor, she would submit her uniform, which had my fingerprints on it, to the school officials. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No. I can’t expose you as the culprit. That’s certainly true. Incidentally, this reminds me—during the exam on the cruise ship, we ended up with the results we did because you had Ryuuen tell all the students that you were the VIP, right? Then you asked Ryuuen for a favor in exchange for that information.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And what would such a favor be? What could I want enough to betray the whole class?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’ve been acting so brashly throughout the festival that I would’ve seen through you even if I didn’t want to. It’s what you asked me about before, isn’t it?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah ha ha! Yes, that’s it. You really figured it out, Ayanokouji-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, why </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">would</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> you betray your class? I’d like to hear it from your own lips,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wanted to expel her. Expel Horikita Suzune. That’s what,” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I just don’t understand why you’re going after Horikita so relentlessly.” I’d hoped for the two of them to resolve their issues before the sports festival, but it hadn’t worked out that way.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry, but I’m going to get Horikita-san expelled, no matter what you say. I won’t change my mind,” said Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And you’re okay with sabotaging Class D to do that?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s right. I don’t care if I never make it to Class A, if it means Horikita-san gets expelled. Oh, but don’t misunderstand me—once she’s gone, I’ll unite everyone, and we’ll work toward reaching Class A together. I promise,” said Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Apparently, there was no changing her mind. She was entirely focused on her goal. If she had to, she’d probably rope in people like Katsuragi, Ichinose, or Sakayanagi to aid her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, but I reconsidered one thing. I added you to the list of people I want expelled, Ayanokouji-kun. After you and Horikita have been eliminated, </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">then</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> I’ll aim for Class A,” said Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She still wore that gorgeous smile. Her expression was almost dazzling.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Have you considered that Ryuuen might expose you?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not an idiot. I obviously wouldn’t do anything that left any evidence behind. Ryuuen-kun is a liar who entraps people without batting an eyelash. Well, I gambled on whether or not he would betray me,” said Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wanted to tell her that there were plenty of ways to deceive someone. It appeared as though Kushida was serious about wanting to crush Horikita. With the way this school was designed, if there was a traitor amidst your allies, then you would repeatedly end floundering in desperation. The order of our participation table, our strategy—all of that information had been leaked. Trying to win with that in mind would be unreasonable.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">A real mastermind would have found a way to make someone else the traitor in their stead. A pawn to help them achieve victory.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Horikita-san’s taken a real beating in this sports festival,” Kushida added. “It’s too bad you couldn’t save her, isn’t it?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wasn’t so sure about that. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">With that conversation done, we ran the three-legged race, silent hostility in the air between us.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">8.3</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">A</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">LMOST AN HOUR</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> had passed since Sudou-kun left. That meant the final competition would begin any minute now. I imagined that Hirata-kun and the others would put up a fight, but they couldn’t really expect much. We were all feeling Sudou-kun’s absence.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was helpless. I could do absolutely nothing except stand around in a pointless daze. I’d spent an hour right in front of the elevator.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even if I returned to camp and withdrew, I didn’t have the funds to get a substitute. Ryuuen-kun was getting all my points using blackmail. But that wasn’t why I couldn’t leave. If Sudou-kun returned to find me gone, he’d be heartbroken. Besides, despite the fact that Class D’s defeat was practically set in stone, I wanted to do whatever I could. I believed Sudou-kun would come back. That was all there was to it. And then…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Did…did you seriously wait around here?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You came back, Sudou-kun.” I kept my cool, but inside, I was glad—so much so that I’d called out to him as soon as I saw him board the elevator. I was happy they had installed cameras inside the elevator; I was able to take some time to calm down.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s already over, isn’t it? The sports festival, I mean.”</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><img alt="" height="100%" id="INTERIORIMAGES_15" src="/hola-lector-agua/images/books/v5/OEBPS/Images/INTERIORIMAGES_15.jpg" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style=" top: 0px; vertical-align: top; height: 100%;"/></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It might be. But if we go back right now, we may still make it in time for the last competition,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So? What’s the point? We’ve already lost,” said Sudou-kun.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re right that the outcome is bleak. I had to withdraw due to my injury, and Kouenji-kun was absent from the start. And you left partway through, Sudou-kun. Compared to the other classes, our classmates don’t stand a chance at winning.” The recommended-participant events I’d wanted to compete in would’ve ended disastrously, anyway. “Since you’ve come back, can I assume you want to return to the competition?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nope. I just came down here to see if you were still hanging around, is all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. Well, while I waited for the past hour, I’ve gone over some things. What kind of person I am, what kind of person </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">you</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> are… Things like that. I concluded that you and I really are similar after all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When I was alone and had calmed down, I felt like the answer had finally become clear to me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We ain’t got anythin’ in common. You and I are way too different.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No. We’re very similar. The more I think about it, the more I see that it’s true.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I spoke from the bottom of my heart.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Always alone. Always solitary. But you believed you could do it, and you tried,” I continued. “If there were a difference between us, it would be that one of us wants acknowledgement from a single person, and the other wants it from a large group. You already know a bit about the student council president, right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, that prim-and-proper guy?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“He’s my older brother.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh? So… Wait. You said something about fighting with him, right?” asked Sudou-kun. He fumbled a little with his words.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I started talking to him about my brother, like I was giving a monologue. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“My relationship with him is far from good. Because I lack talent, we aren’t close. My older brother is incredible, and dislikes being associated with someone incompetent like me. That’s why I’ve tried my absolute hardest to become an incredible person, too. I put everything I had into trying to achieve that goal, from academics to athletics. Even right now, I’m trying.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“W-wait a minute. Aren’t you already really smart and good at sports?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“From the average person’s perspective, I suppose. But compared to my brother, I’m nothing special. I just do what’s expected of me.” He had likely reached my current aptitude while he was in junior high, or perhaps even earlier. “That’s why I’ve been running straight ahead, chasing after my brother without caring what anyone else thinks of me. When I turned to look back, no one was following me; I’ve always been alone. I thought that was fine, though. I believed that, as long as I was an astounding student, my brother would care about me. I thought that, if I participated in a ton of competitions during the festival, he would stop and look at me. That was why I told you that I wanted the anchor position in the relay. I&#160;had this idea that, if I was the anchor, he might cheer for me. Worrying about the class or even my own feelings came second.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">By confronting Sudou-kun’s weakness, I’d also confronted my own.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“He really doesn’t acknowledge you? Even though you’re tryin’ so hard?” asked Sudou-kun.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“He doesn’t. But I finally realized something: I’m not incredible. Ryuuen-kun’s thoroughly beaten me just like he planned, and I haven’t gotten one single result I’m satisfied with, either. I want to make it to Class A to earn my brother’s respect. That hasn’t changed. But the methods I’ve used to achieve that goal have been wrong. I’m </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">not</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> alone. If I have allies, I’m closer to my goal.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re not givin’ up?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If there’s a difference between you and I, that might be it. I will never, ever give up. I’ll strive to become someone who isn’t a disgrace, someone worthy of my brother’s admiration,” I answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That sounds like a painful path to take.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I suppose. If you were the only person in the world, you’d be able to live peacefully. But we aren’t alone in this world. There are billions of people on this planet, and innumerable people in our lives. We can’t ignore them.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">A person couldn’t survive alone. They absolutely had to move forward with someone, together. The sports festival had certainly been an ordeal for our class, but at the same time, it had been revelatory. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I said that you’d turn to violence again, then coldly pushed you away. But that wasn’t the right answer. If you ever stray from your path in future, I’ll bring you back to it. So, until we graduate, please lend me your strength. I promise that I’ll lend you all mine, too.” I looked him right in the eye and didn’t avert my gaze. I wanted him to accept my determination.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Just a little while ago, it didn’t seem like that was the case at all. Why do your words seem so serious now?” he asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It might be because I’ve finally admitted the truth, which is that I’m a worthless person. I’ve avoided that fact.” I wouldn’t have said that to just anyone; he was just like me, so he was an exception. “I’ll ask you again, Sudou-kun. Lend me your strength.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Horikita…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sudou-kun balled his hands tightly into fists, then hit himself on the forehead.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah. What the hell am I feelin’ right now?! I don’t get it, but it’s like…I feel like I’ve finally had my eyes opened.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He stepped toward me. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll work with you, Horikita. I…I feel like someone’s finally noticed me outside basketball.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As he said that, I felt myself smile. I’d never experienced this before. I wondered what the intense throbbing in my chest was. I knew that it wasn’t friendship, or love. It was something else. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Embarrassing as it still felt to admit to myself, I now had an ally. That was different from Ayanokouji-kun, or my brother. It was something I’d lacked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I still had a ways to go, to be sure. But perhaps I had just taken my first small step forward.</span></p>
