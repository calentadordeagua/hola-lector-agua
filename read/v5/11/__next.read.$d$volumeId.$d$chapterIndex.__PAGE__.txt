<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 5:<br/>There’s a Reason for Their Relationship</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">W</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HILE EACH OF THE CLASSES</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> were spying, Class D was making some small moves of its own. What were people good at? Were they athletic? That kind of information was everywhere. Most people had already begun to realize this, but all the spying was kind of pointless. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Regardless of how much you discovered about others’ strengths or weaknesses, the key to victory lay in the composition of the group-event teams. If we managed to acquire our opponents’ participation tables, our odds of winning would improve dramatically. Of course, the other classes wouldn’t just share that information. It would’ve been a death sentence to do so, so everyone was protecting it as best they could.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There was one exception, and it was the bomb waiting to go off inside Class D. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Two weeks before the sports festival, I made my move immediately after school ended for the day. I&#160;called to Horikita, who was gathering her things next to me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hang out with me today.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And if I say I don’t want to?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You can say whatever you like, but then it won’t be my fault if Class D gets in trouble.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I cut straight to the heart of the matter, knowing it sounded like a threat. Horikita was momentarily at a loss for words.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay, I can’t possibly ignore that. Fine, then. What do you want?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’ll understand better if you come with me.” Saying that, I walked away from Horikita and called out to yet another target. “Kushida, do you have a minute?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida was happily chatting with some other girls from our class. “Hmm? What is it, Ayanokouji-kun?” she asked. Kushida glanced at Horikita, who seemed slightly uncomfortable.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you have any plans for tomorrow?” I asked. Saturday would be a day off for Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t really have any right now. I was just thinking of cleaning my room,” Kushida said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If it’s okay with you, would you mind giving me some time in the morning?” I cut right to the chase. If Kushida looked as though she disliked the idea, I wouldn’t press&#160;her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay,” said Kushida. She smiled, as if to dispel my anxieties. “You know, it’s really unusual for you to invite me out, Ayanokouji-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, I suppose it is. By the way, Horikita will be coming, too.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait a second,” Horikita started. I motioned to her to stay quiet.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay. I don’t mind, but…why in the morning?” asked Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I was just thinking that I wanted to spy on the enemy again, but with you along—you know, as someone who knows a lot about other classes. Horikita asked me to help her, but there are lots of things I don’t know.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was being mostly honest, though the part about Horikita was completely made up. I knew Kushida wouldn’t come unless I told her the truth about what I wanted. Also, I needed her to understand her role. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida nodded. “Yes, I suppose I might be the right person for the job. Okay. What time were you thinking? The earlier the better, right?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. I was thinking sometime like ten o’clock. Would that be okay?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Completely okay. We’ll meet in the dorm lobby tomorrow morning, then?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sure. Thanks,” I answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As she left, Kushida waved at some girls waiting in the hallway, and they walked together toward the dorms. She must have promised her friends she’d go back with them. As I started to head back myself, Horikita grabbed me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What are you planning? I knew nothing of this.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s because I never talked about it. Doing a little reconnaissance isn’t a bad thing, wouldn’t you agree?” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t understand the reason for inviting me. If all you’re doing is spying, then you and Kushida should be enough.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you really think that?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wouldn’t say something like that as a joke.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Apparently, I couldn’t afford to send Horikita back yet.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We stand out here. Let’s talk while we walk.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I took the lead, leaving Horikita behind, and eventually, she followed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You remember what happened to your team on the cruise ship?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course. Everyone discovered our VIP’s identity. A humiliating outcome,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s right. It shouldn’t have happened. There is most definitely a reason why it did,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, but I don’t know why. No matter how hard I try, I just can’t find the answer. I assume Ryuuen-kun was involved somehow,” mused Horikita. It sounded as though she’d come to a dead end. Doubts and incomplete theories, unending and irritating, were probably flooding her&#160;mind.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I don’t have proof, but I have a theory.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita looked at me as though she were genuinely shocked. “Are you saying you’ve figured out Ryuuen-kun’s strategy?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. To be more accurate, it’s not just Ryuuen. One other person was significantly involved in the outcome on the cruise ship,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We reached the main entrance and retrieved our shoes from the rack. Walking outside, we continued our conversation.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you think about it, there was no way the VIP’s identity could have been discovered. You and Hirata never told anyone else that Kushida was the VIP. Correct?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But what about Kushida herself? What if she intentionally exposed her identity?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita probably couldn’t understand what I was trying to say. Well, that was only natural; it wasn’t the kind of thing you’d think typically think about. There wasn’t an idiot out there who would have intentionally revealed themselves to be the VIP. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s impossible, right? She’d have nothing to gain from that at all,” answered Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You can’t say that for certain. For example, what about making a deal under the table, in which she tells another party that she’s the VIP in exchange for private points?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even if that’s possible, it would hurt Class D. And it would be very risky,” replied Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That risk depends on the timing, though. There are many ways to establish trust,” I answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you’re saying that Kushida would betray her allies to gain a few measly points?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Maybe, maybe not. Only Kushida knows.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was why I’d invited Kushida. To learn the truth.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you want Kushida and me to come with you to…uncover the truth?” Horikita finally seemed to suspect that Kushida might have turned traitor.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You and Kushida seem to have some kind of connection. A shared fate, maybe. It wouldn’t be strange for her to betray us over something worth more than private points, right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita awkwardly averted her eyes. “Kushida-san and I have no bond whatsoever,” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, can you say with 100 percent confidence that she wouldn’t betray the class and you?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you’re uncertain, you should confirm it. If we don’t make sure, it’ll all be over, won’t it? No matter what the exam, our class has no chance of winning if there’s a traitor in our midst,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">During the previous exam, the exam before that, and even in this sports festival, I understood just how easy it was for the entire class to collapse because of a single traitor. Before we knew it, we were at the dormitory. We entered the first-floor elevator and hit the buttons for our floors.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re free to choose whether or not you come tomorrow, but if you want to lead the class, think about it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I got off on the fourth floor, leaving Horikita with those parting words.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No thanks.” Why did he buy such unappetizing ramen?</span></p>

The disabled girl. Remember her?”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah.”</span></p>

Kushida was absolutely the best person for the job. </span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Good morning, Horikita-san.”</span></p>

Hirata was among those playing. Since the teams consisted of a mixture of students from first year through third year, I didn’t know everyone. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I feel kind of like a secret agent, spying on clubs to gain information about other classes. It’s so exciting, my heart is pounding!” said Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s not that big a deal. The information we can get here doesn’t amount to much,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But Horikita-san doesn’t think that. Right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Information is invaluable. We don’t know what might be the key to our victory,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s true. But it was really kind of you to do this for Horikita-san’s sake, Ayanokouji-kun,” said Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I didn’t exactly have a choice. She would’ve given me grief if I hadn’t,” I replied.</span></p>

Surely something big was coming.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As Kushida grinned, I felt uncomfortable about the three of us hanging out together like this. Surprisingly enough, the highly agreeable Kushida decided to drop the pretense first.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ayanokouji-kun, you were the one who decided to invite me out today, weren’t you?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why do you think that?”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“To be completely honest, it’s not that I don’t understand it. Rather, it’s like I half believe it, and half don’t.”</span></p>

Seeing that he couldn’t score a goal if he tried for a shot from right there, he passed the ball to a teammate, a Class B student we already knew.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, Shibata’s in the soccer club.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. Hirata-kun often says that Shibata-kun is better than he is. It seems like they’re close,” answered Kushida. As expected, she was especially well-informed. As the game started back up, Shibata got the ball and quickly wove through the opposing team.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“He’s fast.” He seemed about equal to Hirata—no, even faster. Hirata hadn’t been lying.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wow, they’re really going at it! They’re all fired up! This is the best!” A tall guy in a soccer uniform walked past the spectators, and by spectators, I mean us. I had assumed that he probably played some kind of sport, so this just confirmed it was soccer.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Good morning, Nagumo-senpai!” said Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Apparently, she knew him. Horikita, meanwhile, had a small, nearly imperceptible reaction. Nagumo was a candidate to become the next student council president, his abilities on par with her older brother’s.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh? Right, you’re Kikyou-chan. I see you’re on a date. Nice,” said Nagumo.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ha ha! No, it’s not like that. I was just curious, so I came here to watch,” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Great. Enjoy. We don’t really hold back here, so I&#160;think this is a good way to measure our players’ strengths,” said Nagumo.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He winked at Kushida and headed down to meet up with the others. Apparently, he’d guessed what we were doing. The soccer club seemed excited when Nagumo joined them.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is it okay for someone to be on the student council </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">and</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> in a club?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It looks like he’s not really in the club anymore,” Kushida replied. “But even though he quit, he’s still the best player. He shows up to practice from time to time, to help guide the rest of the team.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you all set, Nagumo?” asked one of the students.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, dude. I overslept, but after running some laps, I’m all warmed up,” Nagumo replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He swapped places with another student, and the game resumed. Both the ball and other players moved toward Nagumo immediately. He seemed like a reliable teammate and a dangerous opponent. He looked to be on the team opposing Hirata and Shibata. As the situation changed, Nagumo shined in play. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata challenged Nagumo and tried to steal the ball. His movements were as sharp as before, but Nagumo handled him with ease. Shibata charged at Nagumo, too, but Nagumo feinted several times before slipping past. I’d thought that both Hirata and Shibata were skilled, but Nagumo was in a different league.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After running past yet another person, Nagumo took a powerful shot from midfield. The ball flew in a terrifying curve out of the goalie’s reach, and Nagumo scored.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, the title of student council president isn’t just for show, huh?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“He’s just really athletic, that’s all.” Horikita did not intend to acknowledge Nagumo’s obvious skill. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As I spoke to Horikita, I snuck a glance at Kushida. She was all smiles; not even a hint of her dark side could be seen. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“When you stare at me like that, it’s embarrassing,” said Kushida. Her eyes met mine, and she laughed, as though she guessed what I was thinking.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If I promise not to ask more questions, would you please tell me one thing?” I asked. Despite Horikita’s presence, I decided to go for it. “Why don’t you and Horikita like each other?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Asking me to tell you something by saying you won’t ask anything else is unfair,” said Kushida. Maybe my request was psychological manipulation, but Kushida understood both my tactic and my question. “If I tell you, that’s it, right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. I promise.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s me,” she replied, still watching the game.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I hadn’t expected that. So, even though she was at fault for their bad relationship, she still hated Horikita? That was kind of a contradiction. When someone hated someone else, they’d normally claim the other person was wholly at fault.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was relatively good at observing people, but couldn’t quite read Kushida. I was starting to think that I understood Horikita a little less, too. She had known that Kushida hated her from the very beginning, but she never talked to me about it. However, based on Kushida’s answer, Horikita might know the cause of the girl’s hatred.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Of course, if I asked, Horikita probably wouldn’t tell me. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Why is that?</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> I wondered. Was there something that they each didn’t want the other to know?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I get the feeling that just thinking about this is a waste of time,” I huffed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. I suppose so. Our priority right now is to spy and gather information anyway, right?” asked Kushida. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I guess.” I shrugged.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The player handling the ball right now is Sonoda-kun from Class C. He’s quite fast, isn’t he?” mused Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">All the students in the soccer club were nimble. The only ones from our class who could probably hold their own were Sudou and Hirata, but even they’d be hard-pressed to keep up.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But Horikita-san is thinking about our class as a whole. That makes me happy,” said Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I aim to do whatever is necessary to reach Class C, so I’ve no other choice,” replied Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I have to work harder, so that I can contribute towards everyone else, too.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I couldn’t sense even the slightest bit of modesty from that. After a while, the soccer players took a break. Nagumo called to Hirata. Then, perhaps because he realized we’d been watching, Hirata approached us.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Good morning. It’s unusual to see you here,” he said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Shibata, who’d seen us all from a distance, came running over as well. That created a rather unusual little five-person group.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Good morning, Kikyou-chan. Oh, and…Ayanokouji and Horikita-chan, right? Ayanokouji, are you on a date with these two beautiful girls?” asked Shibata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, it’s not like that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Shibata and I were acquainted, but I hadn’t known that he remembered my name. That made me kind of happy, though I kept from grinning.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, what’s up? This is an odd gathering,” Hirata said. He didn’t seem to suspect anything.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I boldly decided to tell him the truth. “We’re spying. We’ve come to identify students from other classes whom we think we should watch out for.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah. So, that means you’ve noticed Shibata Man already, huh?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Shibata quickly stepped forward and showed off his footwork. He had a cheery demeanor and made no attempt to hide his athletic abilities. I wondered if that was because he was in Class B, under Ichinose’s leadership, or just because that was his personality.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Shibata-kun really is fast, just like the rumors say,” replied Kushida. “Ayanokouji-kun and I were shocked.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">At being complimented by a cute girl, Shibata bashfully rubbed his nose with his index finger.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We need to be especially wary of Shibata-kun. He’s the fastest in Class B. Personally, I’d rather not compete against him,” said Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t sell yourself short, Yousuke. I know you’re really fast, too. How about you, Ayanokouji?” Shibata asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m a member of the go-home-and-do-nothing club,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Shibata crossed his arms and laughed as if to say “Sure, okay.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We left soon afterward, on the pretext of checking out other club activities; that was just a cover. There was something else I wanted to know, and I had set the stage for it. How it would play out…well, that was up to the two girls.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You bore me, Kushida-san,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wow, that’s pretty harsh,” Kushida replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But I have to ask you something,” Horikita continued.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Between you and Ayanokouji-kun, I’m getting a lot of questions. Okay, what is it?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“During the cruise ship exam, did you tell Ryuuen-kun or Katsuragi-kun that you were the VIP?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I’d expected Horikita to ask in a straightforward way, but she really cut straight to the heart of the matter. Kushida looked shocked, but Horikita kept talking.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s fine if you don’t answer. It’s meaningless to dig up the past. That’s why there’s only one question I find important. Can I trust you to be our class’s ally from this point on?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course. I want to get to Class A, alongside everyone from Class D. That’s what I’ve said from the very beginning,” said Kushida. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida’s feelings hadn’t changed at all, then.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t know why you’d ask something like that, but I want you to trust me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Though Kushida smiled at Horikita, she looked very serious.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well then, I’m heading back. I’ll leave the rest of the reconnaissance to you two,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? What are you saying, Ayanokouji-kun?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Horikita came up with this strategy in the first place. If you have Kushida’s extensive network and connections at your disposal, you should be fine. Right?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">With that, I left.</span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">5.3</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">W</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">ITH EVERYONE</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> working hard, the days passed quickly. Finally, only one week was left until the sports festival. We needed to decide who would participate in each competition and submit our participation table before the day finished. While Hirata stood at the podium, Kushida faced the blackboard with chalk in hand to record the preparations.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Without further ado, let’s decide on the entries,” said Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">To choose the order for our winning strategy, Hirata consulted his notes, which contained aggregate results of our entire class’s daily records. We had written down the order, everyone’s roles, and the competitions they’d enter. Not a single student objected to Hirata’s results, which were based on hard data and clear testing of people’s abilities. Everything proceeded without a hitch.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay. For the final event, the 1200-meter relay, Sudou will be our anchor.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That seems fair.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was impressed by how Hirata managed to respect everybody’s individual wishes while also taking everyone’s abilities into account. In the relay, the final event, the star players would be the fastest students, like Horikita. No&#160;one could have come up with a more ideal lineup. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, Horikita—who sat next to me—continued to stare at the blackboard with an unconvinced expression. Immediately after the discussion ended, she stood up. While I wondered where this was going, she went right to Sudou. Curious, I tried to eavesdrop.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What’s up?” he asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I want to talk to you about something. Can you come with me?” Horikita replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“S-sure.” Sudou hurriedly stood straight up at Horikita’s command.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As she started to walk away, Horikita suddenly called out to Hirata as well. “Oh, Hirata-kun. Can I also have a moment of your time?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sudou, whose heart must have been pounding for a&#160;moment, immediately looked disappointed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I want to discuss the participation table we decided on earlier. I’d like you to give me the anchor position in the 1200-meter relay at the end of the festival,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sudou looked confused. “But, well…the anchor position is normally for the fastest person, right? Do ya feel uneasy having me as the anchor?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There is a fundamental difference in physical ability between boys and girls. Horikita was the fastest of the girls, but if she were pitted against the boys, then she couldn’t beat Hirata. It was only natural that Sudou, who was equal to or faster than Hirata, should be the anchor. He probably wouldn’t accept anything less.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, that’s not it. I know your abilities from seeing you practice,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay. So, I can handle it, right? I mean, if the anchor is the fifth runner, then—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I have my reasons. You’re good with the starting dash, right, Sudou-kun? I think that having you breeze past our opponents as the first runner is a viable strategy. Then you’ll be able to carry the advantage. If it were a solo competition, you could protect your lane with a starting handicap, but that doesn’t seem possible here. Starting with the second runner, we’ll allow everyone to take the lane they like on a first-come-first-served basis. In the event that we’re overtaken, the rules state that, from the second runner onward, we’re allowed to use the outer lanes to overtake other runners,” Horikita explained.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In other words, Horikita wanted to have Sudou as the first runner so he could get a lead on the competition.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But…” Sudou didn’t appear convinced. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was certainly true that, if Sudou did well at the starting dash, the rest of our relay team from the second runner onward would have an easier time. However, if we used up Sudou’s turn right at the start of the relay, our later runners would be under pressure if the competition closed the gap. On the other hand, Sudou could provide a burst of power in the relay’s final leg as the anchor. If&#160;there were targets for him to chase, he’d be all the more spurred on to win.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s just, y’know, the anchor position goes to the fastest team member.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This school is a meritocracy. Let’s not make choices based on assumptions or preconceived notions. The other classes will be coming up with various strategies as well,” Horikita reasoned.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I understood her logic, but I felt as though she was pushing a bit too hard. It didn’t make much difference who the anchor was; both she and Sudou were sure to perform well. Worst-case, a clumsy starting dash wouldn’t set us too far back. That meant there was another reason Horikita wanted to be the anchor. If it were Ike or Yamauchi, it would probably simply be that they would want to stand out, but I couldn’t imagine Horikita would be like that. That left only one alternative.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll definitely produce better results than I’ve gotten in practice,” Horikita asserted.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Her claim was based in a gut feeling—a “where there’s a will, there’s a way” mentality. There was nothing to back it up.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In fact, her proposal was such a mystery that Sudou immediately shot it down. “I ain’t convinced. This ain’t like you, Horikita,” said Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Excuse me. Is it okay for me to chime in?” Kushida hesitantly entered the conversation, probably out of curiosity. “Uh, I’m sorry. I just think maybe there’s some other reason Horikita-san wants to be anchor.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, Horikita, would you mind telling us why? If you have a request, Sudou and I will take that very seriously. But if we’re changing the order that everyone decided as a class, I need more information,” said Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I agree with Hirata. We need a reason,” said Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita wore a complicated expression. Perhaps she thought speaking the truth was the only way she could get the anchor position. “It’s because I think my brother…is an anchor,” she said quietly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Your brother? The student council president?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. That’s my brother.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Everyone knew the student council president, but not everyone had made the connection that he and Horikita were related. Sure, some people may have a vague idea that this was the case, but their family name was by no means uncommon. They also didn’t really look that much alike, and Horikita herself had never said anything about it. Everybody seemed surprised by Horikita’s statement.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you want to be an anchor with your brother? Is that it?” Kushida didn’t seem as though she fully understood. Apparently, Horikita wasn’t going to elaborate further, so I decided to cut in and give her a little help.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Some stuff happened. I guess Horikita and her brother aren’t really on speaking terms. She probably wants this opportunity to patch things up with him,” I offered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It wasn’t exactly the truth, but it wasn’t a lie, either. It was just a perfectly digestible tidbit. Horikita glared at me for an instant, since I had eavesdropped on her conversation, but immediately looked back to Sudou and the others.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I was wondering what the matter was. So, that’s it, huh? Well, to be honest, I do still want the anchor position. But if that’s the way you feel, then I don’t mind handin’ it over to you,” said Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I think it’s fine, too. If Sudou-kun is satisfied, then everyone else will be too, right?” Kushida added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Right. Sounds good. Well, I’ll submit our list after I swap Horikita-san and Sudou-kun’s places. Is that okay?” asked Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thank you,” said Horikita quietly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If not for an opportunity like this, Horikita probably wouldn’t be able to get closer to her older brother. Even if she didn’t have the courage to contact him herself, however, a competition like this would force them together. However, Horikita’s decision wouldn’t necessarily pan out well. I couldn’t imagine that anything would happen even if she were to get closer to her stubborn brother.</span></p>
