<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">A</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">T LAST</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> the day had come. The curtains were about to rise on the sports festival, and it looked to be a long day. The entire student body, all wearing their jerseys, marched together as one. Well, we called it a march, but most people just strolled along, taking the movement just seriously enough not to disrupt the order.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“All right, time to really show off for Kikyou-chan!” shouted Ike as he walked directly behind me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">How did he plan to show off when he was bad at sports? The guy was all bark and no bite.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Fujimaki, from the third-year Class A, gave a speech during the opening ceremony. Although there weren’t many, a number of spectators watched from the school grounds’ outskirts. They were probably adults who worked on campus. The school didn’t seem to have put any strict restrictions in place regarding who could attend the festival. Occasionally, one of the spectators smiled or waved.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Meanwhile, the teachers themselves watched us without the vaguest hint of smiles. Medical personnel could also be seen setting up some kind of cottage. </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> could fit around twenty people and was equipped with an air conditioner and water cooler. The school’s preparations were as meticulous as they had been on the deserted island. They provided tents for the Red Team and White Team; the tents faced each other on opposite sides of the track. The school didn’t want the teams mingling with each other, except during competitions.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Man, they sure are prepared. They even went so far as to install cameras to help them make the final decisions.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The first event of the festival was the 100-meter dash, and I saw that a camera had been installed and pointed toward the finish line.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That way, they’ll definitely avoid any incorrect judgments or vague results.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The line between victory and defeat could come down to a hair’s breadth in competitions like these. I supposed that was why they hadn’t included events like cheerleading, which were challenging to score.</span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">6.1</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">“W</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HAT GROUP</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> are you in for the 100-meter dash, again?” Horikita asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Seventh,” I answered, looking over at the program table, which had the participation order and schedule.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hopefully no one exceptionally good turns up. I’ll be cheering you on for the class’s sake.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll do my best not to come in last, then,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">With that unambitious goal, I headed to the track with the rest of the first-year boys. The festival ordered events such as the 100-meter dash by grade level, starting with the first-year boys and ending with the third-year girls. Then there’d be a break, after which the order would switch. We’d resume competitions in the opposite direction, starting with third-year girls and ending with first-year boys. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The competition was about to start. All the pairings had been decided according to the printouts each class had received in advance. Now we’d finally learn who would run from which class, and in what order. The first-year boys formed ten groups in total; mine was seventh. Each group contained eight people, two from each class. Sudou was in the first group.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">All of Class D watched, holding their breath. This festival’s outcome depended greatly upon Sudou. If we won this premiere event, we’d have Sudou run first for us so he could take the wind from our opponent’s sails. Everyone on our team could use that momentum to get hyped. If Sudou did poorly, it would probably put a dent in our spirits.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“From my point of view, nobody here’s really that big a deal. I see lots of fatties and twigs. Sudou’s got first place covered!” shouted Ike.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I couldn’t see any students of note from the other three classes in Sudou’s group. Like Ike had said, Sudou’s victory was probably assured. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Then again, depending on how you look at it, this could be a big loss for us.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ideally, if we were using an incredible athlete like Sudou, then it would have been better to have him compete against fast opponents. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, there’s nothing we can really do about that. It’s&#160;just luck.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There was definitely Something about seeing Sudou hunched and ready at the starting line inspired feelings of absolute confidence. Even if he did happen to take a spill in the middle of the race, he’d still be all right. He could do this.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As soon as the signal sounded, Sudou shot forward like a bullet. He quickly outran all the other boys, leaving them in the dust. He reached the goal with such an overwhelming lead that no one else came anywhere close. He&#160;won by a landslide.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">While everyone watched, Sudou took first place, as anticipated. Meanwhile, the Professor—the second Class D student in the first group—managed to come in last. We’d anticipated that, too.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The signal to start the next race came right away, without any time to bask in the afterglow. The signals came at about twenty-second intervals, which meant that it would take four minutes for all the first-year boys to finish running. This event would repeat for all the boys and girls in all three grade levels, which meant that the 100-meter dash should finish in about thirty minutes.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Just as we’d expect from Sudou-kun.” Hirata, my partner, seemed genuinely impressed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. Feels like the other classes were pretty dumbstruck, too.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He didn’t simply take first place; he’d also left a strong impact on everyone. Just like Sudou and the Professor, we in the seventh group also had our roles to play. Hirata, who belonged to the soccer club and was a fast runner, was sure to place high. I’d take a spot at least one place after him, so there was really nothing to be anxious about. It was just a question of how to stand out while remaining inconspicuous. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There were several students from other classes to be wary of. I wondered which groups had athletic students like Kanzaki and Shibata, and which boasted students with a strong presence like Ryuuen or Katsuragi. The third group hurried to the starting line.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, Baldy’s—I mean, Katsuragi’s—in the first lane,” said Ike, pointing at Katsuragi’s head.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sunlight shone on Katsuragi’s scalp, lending it a brilliant sheen. Next to him, I saw another guy I knew: Kanzaki from Class B. He looked pretty calm. So, Katsuragi and Kanzaki would be competing against each other, huh?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The formidable Kouenji was also supposed to be in the third group, but there was no sign of him in Lane&#160;5, which he’d been assigned. No one from the school bothered to search for him. They just marked it as an absence and immediately started the race.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I thought that the third group would be a close match, but Kanzaki was fastest. Katsuragi was by no means slow, just outclassed. The race ended without much fuss. Kanzaki took first place and Katsuragi third. As the races continued, Hirata noticed something.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ayanokouji-kun. There,” he said, gesturing at the temporary cottage that had been erected.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When I strained my eyes to look, I saw Kouenji inside, fussing with his hair. He certainly hadn’t just come back from a run. Still, it was too soon for him to just withdraw.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Apparently, he’s not participating.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kouenji had cooperated all the way up until the opening ceremonies, but in the end, it appeared that he planned to bow out of the competitions. Kouenji would probably make some kind of excuse to get out of it, saying his feet hurt or he wasn’t feeling well. If he wasn’t participating in any of the competitions at all, then that meant he wouldn’t be able to get any points, not even last place. His absence weighed heavily on both our class and the Red Team. Class A had Sakayanagi, who also wouldn’t participate in any events, but at least she had a valid reason.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If Classes B and C had no absentees, the Red Team would need to make up for their two missing members. That was quite the handicap. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The rest of the competition progressed smoothly, and soon it was time for the seventh group. I went and stood in Lane 4, with Hirata next to me in Lane 5. There was also Yahiko, from Class A, but the rest of the competitors were guys I wasn’t familiar with. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This was the first sports festival of my life. I started off with a medium dash, neither fast nor slow. Hirata slowly but surely passed me and joined the top runners. I saw four people’s backs in front of me, which meant that I was in fifth.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Because there wasn’t a huge difference in speed between us, we all kind of clustered together. As we continued to run, our order didn’t change. In the end, I finished in fifth place. Hirata narrowly took the top spot.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Whew. Good job,” he said, taking a deep breath.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry. I dragged us down,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s not true. Everyone was fast. It was a good race,” said Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Despite my disappointing results, he didn’t blame me at all. Instead, he gave me a smile. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We hurried off the track and headed over to the tent. The next group would be starting, and we were in the way. After the first-year boys finished running the 100-meter dash, they returned to their seats and focused on watching the girls with a predatory intensity. They cared about the race’s outcome, of course, but they also just wanted to watch the girls run.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Where’s Sudou?” I asked. He should have returned to his seat by now.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Who knows? Maybe the toilet? Dude, we’ve got more important things to look at. Check out those jiggling boobs, man, those </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">boobs</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">!” said the chipper Ike. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I immediately had a bad feeling about Sudou’s absence. He should have been cheering Horikita on, so his disappearance was strange.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No way.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I looked over toward the cottage to see that my intuition was correct. Sudou was approaching Kouenji.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This isn’t good. I need to stop them,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah,” replied Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Having both noticed the same thing, Hirata and I ran toward the cottage, where things were apparently already heating up. His hand balled tightly into a fist, Sudou confronted Kouenji. “Hey, you. What’s the big idea, sitting this out?! Don’t disregard the rest of us, jerk!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When we opened the door, we could already hear Sudou’s threatening voice. He seemed ready to throw a punch any second now, but Kouenji pretended not to notice. It was a bold move. He just kept admiring his own reflection in the window, which only made Sudou angrier.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Looks like you ain’t gonna understand unless I beat you up, Kouenji,” Sudou growled.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No. You can’t do that, Sudou-kun. If the teachers find out—” Hirata tried to stop them, but Sudou wasn’t the kind of guy to be deterred.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t care. This is a class problem, right? It don’t even matter if I beat him up, long as this punk doesn’t go crying to the teachers,” said Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re quite foul, aren’t you? I came here to enjoy some me time. Alone. As you can see, I’m feeling quite ill today. I decided to withdraw so that I wouldn’t be a burden,” said Kouenji.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t give me that bull crap! If this was just practice, that’d be one thing, but you’re skipping out on the actual event!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I understood why Sudou was shouting. Kouenji looked to be in perfect health. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No. Don’t, Sudou-kun!” Hirata panicked. But before he could intervene, Sudou threw a punch. He probably intended to knock sense into Kouenji, but Kouenji stopped the blow, catching the other boy’s fist in his palm. He was always doing the unexpected.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">A dull </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">slap</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> echoed through the cottage.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Stop. You cannot hope to beat me.” Kouenji didn’t even look at Sudou as he spoke.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sudou hadn’t held back with that blow. Now that the other boy had blocked it easily, he had to realize Kouenji’s real strength, but that only seemed to fire him up even more. “Bring it on. I’ll smash in your nose,” Sudou growled.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Goodness. Both you and that girl seem to consider me unreliable,” replied Kouenji.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“‘That girl?’ Who?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That cold girl who’s so inflamed your passion. She’s been telling me over and over that she wants me to seriously participate in the sports festival.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Horikita?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Apparently, Horikita had foreseen the possibility that Kouenji would be absent from the start. Considering the fact that he had retired right away back on the uninhabited island, it made sense. Even so, I didn’t know that Horikita had been working on Kouenji without my knowledge. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In any case, leave now. I’m not feeling well,” said Kouenji, waving us away.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You jerk!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">To prevent a second punch from flying, Hirata stepped between Sudou and Kouenji. “Let’s all just calm down a little. Kouenji-kun’s attitude is a problem, but if he says he’s not feeling well, then he has the right to rest. Besides, violence is bad, no matter the circumstances.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But he’s definitely lying! He said the same thing back on the island,” said Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That is a baseless accusation. My proud carriage serves to disguise how poor my health is,” said Kouenji.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you’re planning on skipping the rest of the competitions, too, huh?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If I recover, I will of course participate. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">If</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> I recover,” replied Kouenji.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sudou couldn’t contain his anger anymore, but we couldn’t wait around on Kouenji forever.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The next competition is about to begin, Sudou-kun. You’re our leader, so your absence impacts our morale,” said Hirata, switching tactics.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Fine. I’ll head on back,” groused Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thank you,” said Hirata. He escorted Sudou from the cottage, and I followed a little way behind. We made it to the Red Team tent, where Sudou sat back down, clearly still irritated. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Damn! Next time, I’m seriously going to punch that jerk,” he huffed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">His anger, rather than dissipating, only continued to swell. In keeping with the adage that “a wise man keeps away from danger,” people started giving him a wide berth. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ike, however, was too engrossed in the girls’ race to notice Sudou’s frustration. Before I knew it, the girls’ 100-meter dash was nearly over. The final group had just entered the track.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What are you doing, Ken? The girl you like is about to start, dude!” Ike cheerfully slapped Sudou on the back. Sudou grabbed his arm and forced Ike into a headlock.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Gah! Dude, the hell?!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Stress relief,” said Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ouch, ouch, ouch! I give, I give!” shouted Ike.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was a pitiful sight, but taking his anger out on Ike seemed to help Sudou. He began to calm down a little. Meanwhile, Horikita got onto the track as the race for first-year girls began. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I suppose that I feel better watching Suzune, at least…” said Sudou. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If that was all it took to heal him, then I’d let him heal. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As I watched Sudou, Sakura appeared next to me, completely out of breath.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ahh…ahh…! I-It hurts…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She must’ve used up all her energy when she ran. She was breathing very deeply, as though she was in a lot of&#160;pain. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“W-were you watching me, Ayanokouji-kun?” she asked.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She looked up at me, her eyes sparkling behind her glasses. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You did well,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">My response was brief, but filled with emotion. One fact that I could be absolutely certain of was that Sakura definitely gave everything she had.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Th-thank you! This is the first time I didn’t come in dead last.” She beamed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Whenever we’d practiced, Sakura was always the slowest out of everyone by far. It seemed that she’d finally beaten someone else—because of her own hard work, too, not an opponent’s mistake.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Just don’t push yourself too hard. You might hurt yourself,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“O-okay!” she replied with a smile.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakura stood beside me, still breathing heavily, and looked over at the next race. I focused on the other girls who would run against Horikita. In the third lane was Ibuki Mio, a Class C student. Horikita was in the same group as her most bitter rival. What a bizarre coincidence.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita didn’t even look at Ibuki, but Ibuki wore a furious expression, as though fire shot from her eyes. She was determined never to lose to Horikita, no matter what.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, I wonder if Ibuki-chan’s good at sports?” the other boys speculated.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hell if I know. All I know is Horikita’s gonna win, no doubt.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There was no way the other guys could have known, but Ibuki was highly athletic. Personally, I couldn’t say for certain who would win. </span></p>
