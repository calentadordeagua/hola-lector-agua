<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 2:<br/>The School Sports Festival Begins</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">“T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HE STUDENTS’ SURNAMES</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> were the key to figuring out the VIPs’ identities. They were assigned in the order of the animals of the zodiac.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We sat at a table all the way in the back of the crowded café, Pallet. Summer vacation had just ended, and I was seated with a bizarre crew that included Hirata, Karuizawa, and Horikita. We were trying to review the special exam that’d been held on the cruise ship during our vacation. We were checking our answers in the search for the VIPs, comparing notes on the test that divided us up into groups based on the twelve zodiac signs.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The rabbit is fourth in the zodiac. If you put the names of the students in the rabbit group in alphabetical order, it would be Ayanokouji-kun, Ichinose-san, Ibuki-san, and then Karuizawa-san,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. In that case, yeah, I’d be fourth. That’s why I was chosen as VIP,” said Karuizawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Karuizawa nodded, seemingly impressed. At first glance, you’d have thought that the two girls sitting with me were completely incompatible, but Hirata’s presence made the discomfort between them mysteriously disappear.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But isn’t that really, really simple? I mean, practically anyone could figure it out. Like, the fifth person in the dragon group, the one Horikita-san was in, that was Kushida-san. So, she was the VIP, right?” asked Karuizawa. She poked a straw into her milk carton and took a sip.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s right. It </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">was</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> that simple. However, figuring that out in the middle of an exam wasn’t so easy. With only three VIPs in your own class, you wouldn’t have had enough solid proof of any rules behind the selection,” explained Horikita. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If we’d known the names of the three VIPs in another class in addition to our own, perhaps we’d have been in a position to see the possibilities? However, even if we’d had a theory about the names corresponding with the order of the zodiac, there was no changing the fact that we could only answer once, and it was risky. If our answer was off, we could have taken a considerable amount of damage. On the other hand, if we’d bet on it and won, then we might’ve turned everything around in one fell swoop.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m worried about Class C, though. I think Ryuuen-kun figured it all out in the middle of the test,” said Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata was probably correct. If that wasn’t the case, then Ryuuen wouldn’t have been able to do much at all.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s weird, though. If that’s true, why did he mess up?” asked Karuizawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wondered about that, too. Even though there was a huge risk involved, if he understood the rules, he should have managed to discover all the VIPs in the end. In other words, he shouldn’t have made any mistakes,” said Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Depending on how you looked at the situation, Class C had given the wrong answer, yeah. Horikita offered a different perspective on the matter.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Though it appears Ryuuen-kun rules Class C alone, perhaps they’re not a monolith after all? There must be more than a handful of people who’re deeply dissatisfied with his dictatorship.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s definitely true. All students had the right to put forward an answer, so one of the students who didn’t cooperate with Ryuuen-kun’s plans, or whom Ryuuen-kun couldn’t control, must have been the one who put forward something incorrect. If they had given the correct answer, they would have stood to gain a lot of points, right?” Hirata said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita and Hirata were onto something, but we still couldn’t be sure. If there was a traitor, Ryuuen would’ve searched for them relentlessly. Even if that person had deleted the email and managed to make it through the situation, Ryuuen would probably have gone so far as to check people’s private points.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do you think, Ayanokouji-kun?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata and Karuizawa looked to me at the same time. I broke out coughing, discomfited by the force of their combined attention.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Dunno. I honestly don’t have a clue,” I lied. Horikita and Hirata looked away, losing interest all at once. Only Karuizawa still stared at me. I met her gaze, and she diverted her eyes.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In any case, our first priority is to cement this relationship. I’m glad we’re discussing this, Horikita-san and Ayanokouji-kun,” said Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita had never cooperated with Hirata before. However, her attitude had finally begun to change after the two special exams. Being driven into a corner made her start to realize the truth: that she couldn’t do this alone. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, we don’t really have a choice. The zodiac exam was a special case; it couldn’t be done alone. If we assume that future exams may have similar elements, though, then a certain degree of cooperation will be necessary,” replied Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That seemed to be the biggest factor in why Horikita had changed her mind. She was absolutely correct; there were limits to how much of a fight you could put up alone. These many tests were like a microcosm of what the real world would bring, and we certainly couldn’t take it on by ourselves.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even so, you evaded Ryuuen-kun’s clutches quite well.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The last part was aimed at Karuizawa, who had been my group’s VIP and managed to get through the exam without being discovered. Class D had been well rewarded for that victory.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I guess. I </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">do </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">have a surprisingly good poker face. Right, Yousuke-kun?” asked Karuizawa. She hugged Hirata’s arm and looked at him with upturned eyes. You’d never have thought that their relationship had been strained, though whether that was genuine or an act didn’t interest me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Someone else gave the wrong answer before Ryuuen could answer himself. It was all thanks to that,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Wait a minute—</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Yousuke</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">? When had Karuizawa suddenly started calling Hirata by his first name? I kind of wanted to call him by his first name, too, but it was impossible. Hirata and Karuizawa had forged a new relationship out of their complicated situation, which most likely made them closer friends. Hirata returned Karuizawa’s smile, then turned to Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I actually have a proposal. Want to hear it?” Hirata asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita gave no response—her way of telling him to speak.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“First, to bring our class together, I’d like to rope in Kushida-san as an ally. I think she’ll complement our group well and add qualities that the four of us lack. She’s one of the few who could potentially unite a lot of the boys, starting with Ike-kun and Yamauchi-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">True, Kushida was probably a well-qualified candidate to keep our classmates in check. However, I didn’t know whether Horikita would agree to that so easily. Ever since we enrolled in school, the relationship between the two of them had been pretty poor.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Unnecessary,” replied Horikita. “I won’t deny the fact that she is capable of control, but we can do anything she can on our own. That’s why I selected you and Karuizawa-san. With your combined talents, we can solve any problem that faces us…unless you plan to be uncooperative, like a certain someone.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She side-eyed me. How rude.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s certainly true that Ayanokouji might not follow our lead,” added Karuizawa. She and Horikita nodded, though Hirata did not.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Look, you’re mistaken if you think </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">I’m</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> the uncooperative one. I’m the guy most likely to quietly go along with a group. I’m exactly the sort of person you can control. I have no presence,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Those who claim that they have no presence usually cast long shadows,” replied Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay. So, are </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">you</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> someone with no presence?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Me? There’s no way </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">I </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">could have no presence. How dare you? Do you take me for a fool?” she demanded.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Y-yeah, okay.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The whole conversation was devolving into some half-baked comedy sketch, although Horikita didn’t appear to be joking around. You could never quite tell when she was playing, but I thought she was definitely serious.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">2.1</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">O</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">UR AFTERNOON CLASS</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> became a two-hour homeroom period. When Class D’s teacher, Chabashira-sensei, arrived, she began to instruct us in a detached, matter-of-fact manner. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Starting today, classes begin again. However, from September to the beginning of October, we will be holding more physical education classes to prepare for the school sports festival. We’ll distribute new weekly schedules, so please review them carefully. In addition to the new schedules, we’ll also distribute materials related to the festival. Students seated at the front, please pass handouts to those behind you, and so on.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The moment Chabashira-sensei uttered the words “sports festival,” the class erupted in an uproar. Some students were undoubtedly excited by the prospect, but just as many loathed the idea of anything focused on physical exertion.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The details on the printed handouts can be found on the school homepage as well. Please refer to them if necessary.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sensei, is this festival another of the special exams?” asked the class representative, Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We expected Chabashira-sensei to say “Naturally,” but her response was vague.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re free to interpret this however you wish. In any case, this event will massively impact each and every class.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The students who loathed exercise continued grumbling. At an ordinary school, you could find a way to sit out or skip a sports festival. If this festival affected the entire class’s fate, however, then even the least athletic of us had to participate.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">On the other hand, some students were really fired up about this. Particularly those who, like Sudou, were confident in their athletic abilities. This was probably the first exam that would let them contribute to the class meaningfully. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ayanokouji-kun, here!” While everyone else agonized over what was happening, Horikita—who’d been reading through the handout—pointed something out to me. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I flipped the page over and checked what Horikita was referring to. It was, rather unexpectedly, a description of the sports festival’s testing method. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">For an instant, I felt as though Chabashira-sensei was looking at me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Some people may have noticed this already, but for this year’s festival, we’re going to divide all the students across all grade levels into two groups and have them compete against each other. You in Class D are assigned to the Red Team. Class A is also assigned to the Red Team, so they will compete alongside you. That means they’ll be your allies this time around,” explained Chabashira-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Classes B and C were assigned to the White Team. It would be Red versus White.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Whoa! Seriously?! We’re really doing that?” shouted Ike. It didn’t come as a shock that he’d be surprised.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Whether it was a written exam or a special exam, the fundamental idea was that this was a competition between classes. He’d probably assumed that this exam would pit all four classes against one another, same as always.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">What would a team battle be like? This sports festival required a different strategy than we’d had during the special exam on the cruise ship. It demanded cooperation between students from different years.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita appeared calm on the outside, but I was sure she was panicking internally. Her older brother, Horikita Manabu, was a third-year student from Class A. Depending on the circumstances, we might have to work with him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“At least you have an opportunity to get in touch with him now, huh?” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t talk about that here,” Horikita muttered.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She might be able to argue Ike down, but other students probably felt the same way. However, it seemed her remark had lit a fire under Sudou.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I understood what Sudou probably wanted to say, and Horikita’s silence suggested she did too. Few would complain if Sudou, who was always at risk of getting a failing grade in the written exams, racked up a number of perks.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Still, not </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">everyone</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> would agree to it. The potential rewards of winning were very attractive to poorly performing students. Those who were constantly at risk of expulsion probably wanted this chance so badly they could taste it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sudou, I sympathize with your desire to participate in all the events,” Horikita said. “However, that doesn’t mean I can support throwing caution to the wind by entering you in every competition.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Whaddya mean?” Sudou balked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Stamina isn’t an unlimited resource. If you participate in one event after another, you will naturally become exhausted. It will be difficult to clinch consecutive wins.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But it’s better to leave it to me than put forward someone unathletic, right? Even if I get tired out, I can still move better than those guys,” Sudou huffed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He glanced over at the guys—me included—and gave a snort. Ike and Yamauchi appeared frustrated, but said nothing. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We’re not coming to any decisions right now. Let’s talk more in our next homeroom period,” said Horikita, quickly bringing our discussion to an end.</span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">2.2</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">A</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;"> </span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">CROWD OF OVER</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> four hundred people, including the instructors and the entire student body, gathered together in the gymnasium during our second homeroom period. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita scanned her surroundings restlessly. She was probably looking for her older brother, Horikita Manabu, who served as the student council president. However, with this many people around, she wouldn’t be able to spot him easily.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She was worried about causing trouble for her brother, but took care not to draw too much attention as she searched for him. If she loved her older brother that much, then it would have been better for her to take some initiative. But being vulnerable was harder than anything else for Horikita. Now that I thought about it, she’d never gone to meet with her brother before. He’d always been the one to initiate contact. </span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m Fujimaki, from the third-year Class A. It’s been decided that I will assume command of Red Team.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Apparently, Horikita’s older brother wasn’t going to take charge. I would’ve thought that he’d take the lead, being student council president. This just made me wonder when he would make a move. </span></p>

However, each and every one of them is a crucial battle in which you stake your survival at this school,” said Fujimaki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">His words were somewhat vague, yet still helpful.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You may not feel motivated right now, since you’re still feeling it out, but we’re going to try and win this thing. I want you to hold on to that feeling,” Fujimaki continued. “We all need to.”</span></p>

Aside from that, all the other events are divided up by grade level. So, please, feel free to gather and discuss your strategies, starting now.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In response to Fujimaki’s words, the first-year students from Class A, led by Katsuragi, began to assemble in droves. The first-year Class D students, on the other hand, appeared to flounder. They felt nervous in the company of such elites. In the first semester, Class A’s grades had been overwhelmingly better than Class D’s. None of us had even come close.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I feel the same way, Katsuragi-kun. I look forward to working with you,” replied Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The two of them had openly expressed their desire to cooperate. From Class A’s perspective, there was no benefit in teaming up with the lowest-ranked class of all. However, if our classes didn’t work together, we’d drag each other down. We weren’t agreeing to trust one another like siblings, but we were making a pact not to get in each other’s way.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, check out that girl,” whispered Ike, standing beside me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I understood why he was whispering. I felt the same way, and I figured Horikita did too. He was pointing at a Class A student, a girl whom I’d never seen before. She stuck out like a sore thumb, but no one said anything. It didn’t feel like we </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">could</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> say anything right now.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Every individual class has its own strategies, but—” Katsuragi just continued talking, blithely ignoring Class D’s concerned looks and furtive whispers.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you don’t intend to hold a discussion?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">A girl’s rising voice echoed through the gymnasium, interrupting Katsuragi. Everyone’s attention switched to what was happening. The speaker was a first-year Class B student named Ichinose Honami. In front of her, almost an entire class was in the process of leaving the gymnasium. Among those students, Ryuuen Kakeru, Class C’s leader, turned around with his hands in his pockets.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You understand that I’m leaving out of goodwill, right? Even if I offered to cooperate with you, I can’t imagine that you’d believe me. In the end, you’d just probe me for information to see whether I can be trusted, right? It’s a waste of time,” he said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. So, you’re just saving us the hassle, then,” replied Ichinose.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yep, that’s it. You should be grateful.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ryuuen sneered and continued walking, with all the Class C students trailing behind him. The spectacle merely confirmed Class C’s status as a dictatorship.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, Ryuuen-kun. You really think you can win without cooperating?” asked Ichinose.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She had fired off one last question during Ryuuen’s departure, as if she was intent on trying to cooperate with him all the way to the bitter end. But Ryuuen continued walking.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Heh. Hmm, I wonder.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He chuckled to himself and led his Class C entourage out of the gym.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As Class D watched, Karuizawa’s expression soured for just an instant. During the special exam on the cruise ship, she’d gotten in a scrap with Manabe and some other girls from Class C, which had led me to discover her history as a victim of bullying. She’d been keeping it hidden all this time. Yukimura was the only other one aware of her past, but not the extent. For just a moment, Manabe looked back at Karuizawa. Then she immediately averted her eyes, as if nothing had happened, and followed Ryuuen out.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Class B seems to have it really rough, getting paired with Class C and all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It wasn’t as though Class D would’ve had any way to take charge either, but compared to being with Class C, this was better for us. The spectacle we’d witnessed was a reminder that Ryuuen really did hold all the power in his class. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Katsuragi offered Horikita some advice. “Since we’ll be allies from here on out, I should warn you. Don’t underestimate Ryuuen. He’ll laugh while he attacks you in the same instant. Don’t let your guard down.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I appreciate the warning, but I wonder if you’re speaking from personal experience,” replied Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ve warned you.” Katsuragi left it at that and returned to the topic of the festival.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wonder if he’s going to make a move right away,” someone whispered, looking in the direction Class C had gone. It was the girl I’d been curious about a little earlier. She was petite, and sat quietly all by herself, her eyes downcast. She held a thin cane. It was clear that she had trouble walking.</span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><img alt="" height="100%" id="INTERIORIMAGES_2" src="/hola-lector-agua/images/books/v5/OEBPS/Images/INTERIORIMAGES_2.jpg" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style=" top: 0px; vertical-align: top; height: 100%;"/></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The one who offered us an explanation wasn’t Sakayanagi herself, but rather Katsuragi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s Sakayanagi Arisu. She’s disabled. Please be sensitive about that,” said Katsuragi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So that’s Sakayanagi…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This girl was Class A’s other rumored leader, who had supposedly split the students into two distinct factions between herself and Katsuragi. She was so thin that you could assume her condition was why she had been absent during the test on the uninhabited island. It seemed she had a chair specially prepared for her, most likely because moving around could be a struggle for her. Although everyone around her was staring, the girl paid no attention to them. She had short, silver hair, a particularly eye-catching feature. I wasn’t sure whether she dyed it. Her skin was pale, and her name—Arisu—reminded me of a certain mysterious girl who fell down a rabbit hole to Wonderland.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Dude, she’s super cute!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I supposed it was no surprise that the Class D boys would be smitten by Sakayanagi. She was as cute as Kushida and Sakura, and had a dreamlike quality that made people instinctively want to protect her. Yet none of the boys made a move to hit on her or crack jokes like they normally would have. Though she appeared weak, she emanated strong willpower. You could see it in her large eyes. The guys probably felt like something bad would happen if they got too close to her. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakayanagi smiled at us, aware of the stir she’d created. “Unfortunately, I won’t be very useful in this competition. I’m afraid I’ll be consistently absent,” she said. Apparently, she recognized her own weakness. “It seems as though I will be causing trouble for not only my own class, but for Class D as well, then. Please accept my humblest apologies.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You have nothing to apologize for. No one’s going to give you a hard time over that,” said Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">From Hirata to Sudou, no one was going to blame her for something outside her control.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The school sure is harsh. They should’ve come up with some accommodation for you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, that’s right. You don’t need to worry!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Your kindness overwhelms me,” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Contrary to what we’d expected, Sakayanagi seemed extremely polite, mature, and gentle. She gave no hint of aggression. Her presence wasn’t particularly strong at all, despite the things that set her apart. However, Katsuragi watched her quietly. Students like Ike and the rest, who didn’t know anything, probably thought Class A and Class D were sitting with their classmates as normal.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, I could see from the way the Class A students arranged themselves that there was a clear line between Katsuragi’s and Sakayanagi’s followers. The class definitely had two factions. I’d considered Katsuragi’s faction equal or even superior at first, but I wasn’t so sure anymore. There were some boys and girls with Katsuragi, including Yahiko, but nearly all the students sat with Sakayanagi. Perhaps she was deliberately demonstrating her own power.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakayanagi had not participated in the island or cruise ship tests. Though the school hadn’t made a statement about it, it was certainly possible that she’d incurred a penalty for not participating on the ship, yet she’d managed to gain this many allies. Rather than attracting others with her cute countenance, she had most likely earned her classmates’ trust by steadily racking up various other accomplishments. Meanwhile, Katsuragi’s own failures had probably affected his popularity.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wasn’t fully aware of the other classes’ circumstances, but Katsuragi was a firm and deliberate kind of guy. He wasn’t the type to slip up repeatedly. I wondered if the girl was somehow related to his failures. After apologizing for her shortcomings, Sakayanagi showed no sign of wanting to speak further. Instead, she quietly observed Katsuragi, Hirata, and the others. Maybe I was overthinking it. Maybe she stayed quiet because she knew she wouldn’t be of any use in the sports festival. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Katsuragi, ignoring Sakayanagi, continued to talk with Hirata. “About our alliance—I was thinking that it’d be best if we just keep out of each other’s way. You don’t mind, do you?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you won’t share any details about the competitions you’re participating in?” Hirata asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Right. Letting that information slip might lead to unnecessary conflict down the road. For example, if info were leaked to Class B or Class C, then we would begin to doubt Class D—which would inevitably put a wrench in our alliance. Constantly comparing ourselves to each other will only increase the divide between us,” said Katsuragi. “We will cooperate equally and fight side by side to the bitter end. It’s the ideal strategy.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, I suppose you’re right. It’s pretty hard to trust other people in this school, Katsuragi-kun. Also, even though we’re allies in a way, I suppose there’s no changing the fact that we’re still competing with one another,” replied Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He looked to the rest of our classmates for the final decision. No one voiced objections. Neither class could just suddenly start trusting the other and reveal all of their plans. Keeping each other at an appropriate distance would be the safest option. Even Horikita didn’t say anything, so she must’ve been convinced, too.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“At any rate, we’ll need to meet like this sometime soon to discuss the group competitions. Is that all right with you?” Hirata asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, that should be fine. I’ll consult with everyone&#160;else.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thanks. I’m counting on you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">They certainly didn’t waste time. It seemed as though everything was going smoothly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do you think the trick is here, Ayanokouji-kun? What are they playing at?” Horikita, on the other hand, seemed to have some ideas of her own.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s a sports festival. The school’s probably trying to find out which students are athletic and which aren’t.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re fundamentally correct, of course. This competition is going to determine our abilities. But what’s another factor, other than athleticism, that might influence the results?” asked Horikita. “Mere luck?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Luck, huh?” That suggestion was out of character for her, but she may have been right.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Unlike our other written and special tests, opponents on this test will be chosen at random. We don’t know who’ll take on whom. Luck is a huge factor.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">True, much would be decided by the matchups. In that regard, it was down to luck. Even Horikita, who would normally win against 80 percent of the people in the gymnasium, could lose if she happened to go up against someone from the remaining 20 percent. On the other hand, somebody very unathletic, who could only best a measly 10 percent of their opponents, might actually win if they were lucky enough to be matched up with someone even less athletic.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not talking about random chance, though,” Horikita said. “I’m talking about something definite. I&#160;mean, a method that doesn’t </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">just</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> rely on luck—one that still relies on athletic skill. On the island and cruise ship, there were secret clues and pathways we could have detected. This feels the same. This time, surely…” Maybe because of her shame over her mistakes on the island, Horikita was now even more obsessed with victory.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do you think makes this different from the previous special tests?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Different? I think it’s the same kind of special exam.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I won’t deny that they’re </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">similar</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">, but I doubt that the school considers them the same.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t understand. You think it’s different because we’re cooperating with Class A this time? But we had to work with students from other classes on the ship, too, and a battle broke out.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, that’s not it. The basic premise is different.” I&#160;could tell that Horikita was growing frustrated with my coyness, so I explained. “The school has never once called the festival a ‘special exam.’ Only the first-year students have been calling it that. The teachers, including Chabashira-sensei, all referred to it as a sports festival. That third-year student, Fujimaki, called it that, too. The words ‘special exam’ weren’t anywhere in the handout,” I&#160;told Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She didn’t seem to have realized that, but even if she had, she still wasn’t convinced. “Well, why does that even matter? The structure that dictates how the sports festival plays out, and the large potential increase or decrease in points, make it function pretty much exactly the same as a special exam.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s true, there’s no real difference in the content. But there </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">is</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> something different at its core. Putting aside the fact that you can buy and sell test points, the regular written exams are meant to test our abilities. It’s not about employing cheap tricks, or coming up with a strategy or tactics—no, I think that the class that genuinely challenges themselves in this festival will demonstrate their true worth,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Of course, that didn’t necessarily mean there’d be </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">no</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> cheap tricks. It would be much like how, even though you could do things before or after a written exam to help your results, you were rather limited during the exam itself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The crux of the sports festival is that we should properly prepare for the event. Then, once it starts, we need to get results. That’s all there is to it; simple is best,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I already said that I want us to prepare. I definitely want Class D to win,” Horikita retorted.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wrong. You’re not talking about preparation. You’re trying to find a crafty strategy and look for loopholes.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t understand the difference in our strategies,” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“When I say ‘preparation,’ I mean figuring out who’ll participate and in what order, knowing which students in other classes are athletic or unathletic, and so on. Finding out what order </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">they’ll</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> participate in. And also, making sure that they don’t discover those things about </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">us.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> On the other hand, ‘looking for loopholes’ means trying to force someone to bow out of a competition or withdraw. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I supposed that was a natural thing for Horikita to think, considering that she had always tried charging in head-on until this point and had kept losing. It was normal to want to do something to ensure that you wouldn’t be overtaken by your opponents in the sports festival. If it were a simple matter, though, then no one would be agonizing over it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you’re saying that we need to fight clean and fight hard to win?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had no intention of confirming or denying whichever answer Horikita chose. As for the reason why, well, that’s because the strategy to win didn’t just consist of one part, there was always another side to it. Whether it was the uninhabited island or the cruise ship, or even this sports festival. It was possible to win by “attacking head-on” or to win “via a loophole.” The point is, it’s important to choose a fighting style that suits the person.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Right now, she doesn’t really favor one side or the other, she isn’t either heads or tails. At this stage, she could become one or the other. If I had said that Katsuragi and Ichinose were heads, and Ryuuen and I were tails, which side of the coin would Horikita choose? I understood why she currently wanted to resort to “tails,” considering that the people who’d won thus far were sneaks.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, I was warning her precisely </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">because</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> going with “tails” in the sports festival would be extremely difficult. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What you do is entirely up to you. What advantage do you think Class D has right now, Horikita?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, a dispute between Classes B and C will probably help us.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I thought about ignoring it, but I had to address the real problem. Horikita Suzune lived her life in such solitude that she had tunnel vision. “You’re not thinking big enough,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you think we should dismiss the fact that Ryuuen-kun refused to cooperate with Class B? I think that’s a positive thing for us.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">really</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> think that?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, they might still reconcile and work together. It’s not as though Ichinose-san particularly </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">likes</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> Ryuuen-kun, but for the sake of victory, she might put aside her own feelings. Can’t we acknowledge that it’s a good thing for now, though?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s exactly what I mean by ‘not thinking big&#160;enough.’”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s rude. Fine, do enlighten me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do you know about Ryuuen? He’s always looking to win. No matter how he behaves, or how polite he is, he’s always coming up with strategies. So, why did he suddenly refuse to cooperate with Class B? Do you think he never considered it?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait. Are you saying that Classes B and C are working together behind the scenes?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She was on the right track, but I had to stress the main point here. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Our relationship with Class B is different than Ryuuen’s. There’s a good chance that he has a plan in place. If he didn’t, he’d have no reason to leave. Even if that was a bluff, on the surface, he would gain more by talking with Class B, right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t think that’s right. It seems unlikely,” replied Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, because earthquakes and fires are unlikely, there’s no need to have emergency measures in place, just in case? I don’t think you get how important it is to be prepared for catastrophe.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If nothing happened, then nothing would come of it. But in the event that something did happen, you’d be ill-prepared for assuming everything would be fine.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I think that Ryuuen has, at the very least, one or more tactics in mind.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But if that’s true, it’s crazy. We only just learned about the festival. To have a whole plan in place so soon…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s why we need to understand his kind of crazy. What would a frontal attack from Ryuuen look like? What loopholes could he find? Can </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">we </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">come up with something similar? Are there precautions we can take? We have to think like that to rise to Class A’s level,” I&#160;said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If we thought about what winning strategies that he had come up with at this point, then we would naturally narrow things down. Of course, that was something I’ve started to see after getting a clear read of Ryuuen’s strategies and thought processes ever since the fight that nearly got Sudou expelled. Could Horikita still not see that? </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I suppose it’s fine as long as we do what we can. Let’s craft a plan in case someone makes a mess and we have to clean it up,” said Horikita. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Can you </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">not</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> jump to the conclusion that someone’s going to make a mess?” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was looking forward to finding out just how far ahead she could think.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">2.3</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">A</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">FTER CLASS FINISHED</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> for the day, I remained in the classroom alone. Outside the window, I could hear students engaged in their club activities. With the sports festival drawing near, each and every one of them was in high spirits. No one slacked at all in their daily training regimen. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I plugged in my earphones and opened the file I’d received earlier on my phone. “I see…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I basically had a good understanding of the situation now. I’d thought that I might have to lay a few traps, but it seemed that wouldn’t be necessary. Pleased with this turn of events, I decided to return to my dorm. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As I passed through the main gate, I ran into Chabashira-sensei. She was spraying water around with a hose.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><img alt="" height="100%" id="INTERIORIMAGES_3" src="/hola-lector-agua/images/books/v5/OEBPS/Images/INTERIORIMAGES_3.jpg" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style=" top: 0px; vertical-align: top; height: 100%;"/></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You stayed behind longer than usual, Ayanokouji.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I suppose you’re right. Are you on duty today?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Something like that. You could say this is sort of my post,” she replied. She kept spraying water in a manner that suggested she was used to the task. “Working adults tend to multitask, unlike children. That’s especially true now that the sports festival is imminent. Anyway, what were you doing today? This is the first I’ve seen you roaming around after class alone.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t you think that’s an exaggeration?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you prepared for the sports festival?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I think the last homeroom explained everything, more or less. Didn’t it?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira-sensei should have heard about all of our strategies, including Hirata’s, Horikita’s, and Sudou’s. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In your case, I would have thought that you’d already have some eccentric idea or strategy in place,” she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, nothing like that,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nothing? But—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When her eyes met mine, she stopped talking. Discussing these things publicly wouldn’t help anyone.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I haven’t forgotten what you told me, sensei. However, I’m free to decide my own path.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, you’re certainly right. I shouldn’t interfere with you unnecessarily. All this ends the moment you try to kick back and take it easy, though. If you don’t show me some results soon, I’ll stop covering for you. It goes beyond the scope of an ordinary teacher’s duties, after all,” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t know what she expected from me. Feeling irritated about her invading my day-to-day, I decided to leave. I shouldn’t have gotten myself tangled in such an aggravating situation. No…really, this confrontation had just been a matter of time. It would come to a head sooner or later.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Excuse me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sure. Take care.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Nice of her to urge me to “take care” on my arduous return trip of a couple hundred meters.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I went back to the dorm.</span></p>
