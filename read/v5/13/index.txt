<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 7:<br/>For Whose Sake?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">I</span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 0.80em;"> </span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HEADED TOWARD</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> the nurse’s office alone, feeling battered and bruised after Ayanokouji-kun’s verbal beatdown. Since he usually followed his so-called policy of noninterference, I never would’ve imagined he would speak to me like that. I’d been so shocked, I couldn’t give him a satisfactory answer.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, that’s not it,” I said to myself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ayanokouji-kun had been absolutely right, and I’d had nothing to say in reply.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Tch.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">At any rate, if I would be chasing after Sudou-kun, I had to take care of my leg, which still wouldn’t move as well as I wanted. Emergency medical treatment was available on the field, with personnel monitoring the students, but I wanted to avoid standing out as much as possible. Instead, I headed to the nurse’s office at the school.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When I got to the infirmary, I saw that someone else was already there. Of the three available beds, one was curtained off. I couldn’t see who was in it. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How’s it look, sensei?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">During our brief break before lunch, I’d had an emergency first aid staff member tape my leg, but that only had a minimal effect.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After inspecting my leg, the nurse looked up. “I already said this, but participating in more competitions will be difficult.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The nurse had diagnosed me with a sprain, and it didn’t seem to be getting any better or worse. At this point, I could only barely run, but at least I </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">could</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> run. I&#160;just couldn’t summon up enough strength to win a race. I’d desperately fought to make it through the individual competitions, but the events for recommended participants would likely be even more difficult. If I took part, I’d never win. I couldn’t let that happen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you plan to take part in the recommended-participant events?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I was planning to, but I think I’ve changed my mind. If I tried to participate with my leg like this, I’d only drag the rest of my class down,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s a wise decision.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Fortunately, I’d obtained numerous points during previous exams. Even if I withdrew, I could compensate for my absence. If I prepared a substitute participant to take my place for all three competitions, the total cost would come out to 300,000 points. That was no small expense, but if it would raise our class’s chances of victory even a little, then I had to pay it. My dream of running alongside my brother would end, though.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Well, worrying about personal issues was meaningless. What mattered was who my substitute would be.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thank you very much.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Once I had received medical care, I gave my thanks and then left the infirmary. I headed toward the main entrance to make my way back to the field. In the window, I saw my limping reflection. Feeling pathetic, I bit my lip. I did suspect Kinoshita-san of tripping me after she’d called out my name, but I was to blame for falling and getting injured. There was no changing that. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I tried my hardest to appear calm and collected, so that others wouldn’t notice my pain. When I was just about to leave through the main entrance and go back outside, Kushida-san ran up to me in a panic. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m so glad I found you, Horikita-san! I need to talk to you about something,” she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What is it? I have some business I need to take care of, so please make it quick.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay. This isn’t a good place to talk, though. Sorry. Would you mind coming with me? Things are going to get difficult,” said Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Can’t you explain here? I’ll decide after hearing how difficult this is,” I answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After Kushida-san looked around, she whispered in my ear. “It sounds as though Kinoshita-san actually suffered a serious injury. It’s bad enough that she can’t even get up right now. So, that’s why…well, Kinoshita-san wants to talk to you, Horikita-san.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I couldn’t hide my surprise. It certainly did seem like she’d been injured, but to think that things had taken such a turn…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Where is she?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This way.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida led me back to the nurse’s office.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">7.1</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">W</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HEN I RETURNED</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> to the infirmary, Chabashira-sensei was there.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Good. I was just saying that you’d barely missed Horikita-san,” the nurse said to her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I had Kushida fetch you. Looks like she was quick about it,” said Chabashira-sensei. Kushida-san listened to the adults talk, appearing uneasy.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What in the world is going on?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Behind that closed-off curtain I’d noticed earlier, I could hear someone sobbing. Chabashira-sensei pulled the curtain open, giving me a glimpse of Kinoshita-san, who lay in the bed. Then she let the curtain fall back into place and gestured me into the hallway.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Kinoshita fell on the obstacle course this morning. Do you remember that?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course. She bumped into me,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That incident had thrown a wrench in my plans for the sports festival.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, Kinoshita says that you deliberately knocked her down, Horikita.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">For an instant, I couldn’t understand what Chabashira-sensei was saying.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s not true at all. It was completely by accident. If&#160;anything, it—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“‘If anything, it’ what?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I’d been about to say that it was part of Ryuuen-kun’s strategy, as Ayanokouji-kun stated. I believed that Ayanokouji-kun was completely right, but we had no proof.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Never mind. It was just a coincidence, that’s all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’d like to believe that, but the situation isn’t good. Kinoshita claims that, during the race, you repeatedly looked back at her. We checked the video footage, and you certainly did look back twice,” said Chabashira-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s because she called my name,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“She called your name? I see. Even if that’s true, though, she claims you kicked her. In fact, she’s been absent from all the subsequent competitions. We had a teacher inspect her injuries, which appear rather severe. They believe those were caused intentionally.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even if she’s badly injured, she’s still lying. I didn’t do anything,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I believe you’re innocent. However, Japan is a country that favors uplifting the weak. Our school is no different in that regard. As long as we have no proof one way or the other, we must consider the possibility that it was intentional. We’ll have to deliberate on the matter,” said Chabashira-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s idiotic.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s not all. The other teachers already know about this, of course. But, if this drags on, the student council will hear of it. Things will only get more difficult from there. You haven’t forgotten what happened to Sudou after his fight, have you?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If this continued, my brother would hear about the incident. He’d feel irritated and ashamed of his idiotic little sister. However, since I was innocent, I had no choice but to plead that innocence. Whether this was Ryuuen-kun’s strategy or just an unfortunate accident, I couldn’t lie.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you called me here to ask what happened, I told you the truth. I didn’t do anything. Now, I have some business to take care of, so if you’d excuse me?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I needed to find Sudou-kun as quickly as possible and bring him back. But, as I turned to leave, Chabashira-sensei spoke.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Under the circumstances, it would probably be easy for the school to believe that the incident was intentional rather than accidental. Kinoshita-san has been absent from festival events ever since the obstacle course. If we prove that you committed foul play, that will invalidate the scores you’ve acquired thus far, and you won’t take part in the recommended-participant events. Well, with your leg in that condition, I suppose participating would be impossible anyway, but… Kinoshita is an athletic student. In terms of speed, she’s as good or better than you. It’s hard to believe that her injuries are a mere coincidence.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If even she told me all that, I was innocent, so there was nothing I could do. I could easily continue my appeal, but I didn’t have time to deal with this any longer.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I was going to withdraw from the recommended-participant events anyway. Ever since the obstacle course, my rankings haven’t been terrible. I don’t mind being marked absent for those events, like Kinoshita-san. However, I didn’t intentionally cause her to fall and get injured.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wondered whether that would be enough, looking to the teacher for approval.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Kinoshita won’t accept that,” Chabashira-sensei replied. “She says she’ll report this incident to the school. Her testimony, and the footage, make this fairly damning. From her perspective, she’s suffered a massive loss. Class C is also in a tough spot due to Kinoshita’s absence, and it seems unlikely that they’d sabotage themselves like that on purpose. You understand what this means, right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s a case of the Devil’s Proof, isn’t it?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">A logical dilemma as old as time. To prove that aliens existed, all you had to do was capture one alien. But to conclusively prove that aliens </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">didn’t</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> exist, you’d have to search every inch of the entire planet and of outer space itself, which would frankly be an impossible task. That was the definition of the Devil’s Proof.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira-sensei was saying that, if it was impossible to prove my innocence, I’d need to prepare myself. Rather than confirm or deny, she crossed her arms in silence.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How did you hear about this, Chabashira-sensei? Who else knows?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Kushida consulted me on the matter. She said she didn’t want it to blow up, but didn’t know what else to&#160;do.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry, Horikita-san. Kinoshita-san asked me to talk to a teacher,” said Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I appreciate your concern. If a teacher from another class heard about Kinoshita-san’s claim, this might have escalated quickly. However, I have some questions. Why exactly did </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">you</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> hear about this from Kinoshita-san?” I pressed Kushida-san.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She anxiously looked toward the infirmary. “I’m good friends with Kinoshita-san. When I went to check on her during our break, she told me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was a believable reason, considering that Kushida-san had a vast social network. At any rate, the only ones who knew about the accusation right now were Kinoshita-san, Kushida-san, Chabashira-sensei, and me. I wanted to resolve this here and now.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“May I speak with Kinoshita-san?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not sure about that. She seems frightened, and might be emotionally unstable,” replied Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Please. I don’t want to aggravate things,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I bowed, and Kushida-san bowed her head in return.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Please allow me to do this, sensei,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay. I’ll let you try,” replied Chabashira-sensei.</span></p>

However, it would take more time than I had to scout a campus this large. When only ten minutes remained, I returned to the field. Maybe Sudou-kun had come back after all. He’d been trying his hardest to take first place out of the entire school. I hoped he still wanted&#160;that. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But it was no use. He wasn’t on the field.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, he didn’t come back.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When I thought about places I still hadn’t checked, my mind went to the Keyaki Mall and the dorms. He also could have been somewhere in the school building. It was proving to be very difficult; I couldn’t seem to find&#160;him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Then, he…Ayanokouji-kun…appeared right before me. I wondered if he had finished eating his lunch.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You sound like you’re out of breath,” he said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m looking for Sudou-kun. He hasn’t shown up, has&#160;he?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nope. Does this mean you want to talk to him?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. He’s a valuable asset. I accept it, even though I don’t like it,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And that means?” Ayanokouji-kun seemed to notice how poorly I was doing, but there was no use telling him about Ryuuen-kun. What could he have done, anyway? I wanted to keep that embarrassment between Kushida-san, Chabashira-sensei, and myself. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Our break was nearly over, but no one had seen any sign of Sudou-kun. Class D was in dire straits already, but in Sudou-kun’s absence, our defeat became a certainty. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you have any idea where Sudou might be?” Ayanokouji-kun asked me. “Time is almost up.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, I don’t. But there’re only so many places he can go. If he wants privacy, he probably returned to his dorm room,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is your leg all right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, but I can run. Are you coming?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll pass. I’d only end up getting in your way.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see.” It might be more convenient for me as well. I&#160;swallowed the pain and started walking.</span></p>
