<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 9:<br/>The Turning Point</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HE LAST EVENT</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> of the sports festival, the 1200-meter relay, was about to begin. Everyone—other than Class&#160;D—was amped.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The final event, huh? I guess we need to choose a substitute for this one, too.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh…! Puh…! Hey, sorry to keep you all waiting! What’s going on?” Sudou, completely out of breath, returned with Horikita lagging slightly behind him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sudou-kun, you came back!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, sorry. My bad. It took way longer to take a dump than I thought.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sudou looked as if he was in a bright, cheerful mood—all smiles. However, many students glared at him icily. He didn’t flinch from their stares.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry. I punched Hirata and crashed our morale because I lost my temper. It’s my fault that Class D is about to lose,” he continued before anyone could blame him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sudou bowed deeply. The Sudou of an hour ago couldn’t have done that, even if it were an act. Something must have happened. After a stunned moment, Hirata laughed happily. His cheek was slightly swollen, and looked painful, but he didn’t seem to care about that.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The heck, Ken? This isn’t like you,” said Ike.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I gotta admit, I did somethin’ wrong after doing somethin’ wrong. I want to apologize to you, too, Kanji,” Sudou replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s not like it’s your fault I lost or anything. I’m just no good at sports, really. Sorry I wasn’t useful,” said Ike.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">One apology gave way to more. The students who’d been glaring at Sudou really hadn’t been able to perform as well as he had.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you haven’t decided on a sub for the relay, please let me run,” said Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re the only person we’d want doing this, Sudou-kun. Right, everyone?” said Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Both guys and girls would run the final 1200-meter relay. Three guys and three girls from each class had to participate.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Can I ask for a substitute? I wouldn’t run very well with my leg like this,” said Horikita. She sounded apologetic.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you okay with that, Horikita?” Hirata asked. “You really wanted to be in this relay.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s nothing I can do about it. In the state I’m in right now, I’m not sure I could even win against Ike-kun. Sorry,” Horikita replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She bowed deeply, as Sudou had done earlier. I wondered if she’d ever been that honest before. Ryuuen had crushed Horikita’s body and spirit. She’d craved the anchor position, reached for it, because she envisioned herself running alongside her brother. Now—though her hands trembled in apparent frustration—she accepted the reality that, if she forced herself to compete, then Class D would lose the relay. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata nodded, and decided that Kushida would take Horikita’s place. Our list included Hirata, Miyake, Maezono, and Onodera, with Sudou at the top. With Kushida as Horikita’s replacement, that made six people. There really weren’t any other sprinters in Class D who stood a chance. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After confirming the team members, Hirata and I locked eyes. He opened his mouth to speak. “Um, I apologize for the suddenness of this, but…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, some other guy cut Hirata off. “Sorry, but…would you please let me withdraw, too?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Miyake was speaking. He looked as though he was dragging his right leg a little.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“To tell you the truth, I twisted my ankle before lunch during the 200-meter dash. I thought it would feel better after I rested, but it still hurts.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Apparently, we still had students who had sustained injuries.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, it looks like we’ll need a replacement from the boys as well.” Hirata looked around, but no volunteers came forward. Clearly, no one was confident enough in their speed. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I decided to speak up. “In that case, would it be okay if I ran? I’ll pay the points to go in as a substitute, of course.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You will, Ayanokouji?” Miyake asked. “Wait, though. Are you that fast?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">No one really thought that I was, of course.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I support him. I’ve been watching everyone, and I think Ayanokouji will do well,” said Hirata. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was all it took to shut down dissenting voices. As he was someone who had earned the trust of his peers day in and day out, Hirata’s words had weight.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“However, we can’t really say that Class D is putting forward its best runners,” Hirata said. “That’s why getting a head start on the competition should be our strategy. What do you think, Sudou-kun? If you get us off to a good start and overtake the other runners, I think we might be able to put some distance between us and the other classes. I’ll maintain that lead and make sure the next student keeps things going.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">At last, it was time for the final relay. Lanes couldn’t be prepared for each of the twelve competing students, so we had to start side-by-side. The rules stated that you could take an inner lane from an opponent after you overtook them. In other words, your initial position was the most important one. If you could get ahead of the others during the starting dash, you wouldn’t end up in a chaotic free-for-all.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, guess we don’t got much choice. There ain’t any other way for us to win,” said Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He would go first. Hirata, sufficiently speedy himself, was second. After that the three girls—Kushida included—would have their turns, and then I’d go last. Class D must have been counting on me more than I expected, to have made me the anchor. They must have wanted me to make up for the slower students before me; it would save us some time.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The chosen elites assembled in the middle of the field. Horikita’s older brother, and that second-year student Nagumo, were among them.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We’re leaving it all to you, Sudou-kun!” shouted Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida and the other runners also cheered. Sudou, looking motivated, got into his lane. The first-year students appeared to be in a slightly advantageous position, since Class D had the innermost lane. They’d arranged it so that the third-year Class A students were in the outermost lane. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There were three girls on the lanes for the third years, so one might imagine we would have an overwhelming starting advantage. The excitement was finally reaching its peak. Class D didn’t have any chance of winning the festival, but if we were victorious here, it might help us down the line. That was the hunch people had, at least. Supportive cheers came roaring from our camp.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Man, that was close. A couple seconds more, and I would have withdrawn,” said Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. Miyake’s injury was unexpected,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The plan from the start had been for me to participate in the final relay as Hirata’s substitute. Of course, only Hirata and I knew about that.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This is okay, right, Ayanokouji-kun?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. Sorry that you had to make all the arrangements.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s only natural for me. Besides, I’d hate it if Ryuuen-kun beat us again. Think he’ll be surprised that you’re running?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll do my best not to disappoint you. More importantly, though, let’s cheer Sudou on.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The instant the signal sounded, Sudou got off to a strong start. He didn’t look nervous whatsoever. His starting dash had the best time of any I’d seen thus far. He launched himself forward with such momentum that, after his first stride, he managed to pass eleven people. I&#160;could tell that he was moving at an incredible speed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Whoa!” the racers shouted. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Dude, he’s so fast!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even Shibata, who had been standing beside me, was so impressed that he had to praise Sudou. The second-year guys and girls were left to struggle desperately for a position, and got caught up in a chaotic free-for-all. Sudou, taking advantage of that opening, continued to leave the others in the dust. When he finished his leg of the relay, he’d secured an advantage of at least fifteen meters.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s up to you, Hirata!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Class D brimmed with excitement. Sudou passed the baton to the next runner, Hirata. As a man who excelled in both studies and sports, he was glorious. The racers chased Hirata, one after another, but they couldn’t reduce the gap we’d created. We maintained our lead as we transitioned to the third runner, Onodera. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This was where problems might start. For a girl, Onodera was fast, but the guys chasing her were starting to close in. A guy from the second-year Class A overtook her as she ran.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">New students ran one after another. One third-year Class A guy also passed Onodera, and other third- and second-year Class A students after that. By the time Onodera passed the baton to our fourth runner, Maezono, our lead was almost gone. Despite our hopes for first place, our upperclassmen were truly strong. That was the competition for you, huh?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, the race was full of surprises. A girl from the third-year Class A, who was her team’s fourth runner, took a spill and fell about fifty meters behind the runner she was supposed to pass the baton to. Panicking, the girl tried to get back in the race, but a Class A second year took advantage of that opening and created a significant gap. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">By the time Maezono passed the baton to Kushida, our fifth runner, Class A had overtaken Class D, and we’d fallen to seventh place. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So, the other classes surpassed our abilities, eh? I had set my sights on the winner’s podium, but this was really becoming an arduous battle.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The first-year students just couldn’t compete at the same level as the seniors. Only Class B’s first-year students had managed to get into third place, and their ace, Shibata, had apparently received the role of anchor. He was waiting his turn, just like me. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As the fourth runner from the third-year Class A tumbled, the situation between the guys in the anchor positions changed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Looks like victory is ours this time, President Horikita. I would’ve liked to run against you directly, if possible.” Nagumo laughed as the second-year Class A’s top runner approached him. There was a thirty-meter gap between him and the third-year Class A student in second place. If they were equal, there was no way for the second-place student to clinch it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Looks like we’re going to win in overall points, too. Guess this is the dawn of a new era, eh?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you seriously plan to change this school?” the student council president asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re too traditional. And even though you’re strict, you’re weak. Your rules are too generous, and they stop people from getting expelled. All I’m going to do is help make this school the ultimate meritocracy,” said Nagumo.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He walked forward as he spoke, getting in position to receive the baton as it came his way. Not too long after Nagumo nabbed it, Shibata also received his baton. “All right! Nice! Leave the rest to me!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Fire blazing in his eyes, Shibata chased down Nagumo. My eyes met Horikita’s brother’s for an instant. I could tell this man was fighting some internal struggle.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“To think that you’re the anchor,” he said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m just a substitute for someone who’s been injured. Originally, your sister planned to be in this position,” I replied.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The girl just told me to wait and walked off on her own. She headed around the corner and quietly whispered, “Can I go back now?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. Excellent work, Masumi-san. I’ll count on you again in future.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes.” Masumi quietly nodded and left. The voice’s owner slowly came into view. Carrying a cane in one hand, she looked at me with a cold smile. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">First-year Class A Sakayanagi. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You wanted to see me?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakayanagi didn’t answer. For a silent moment, she and I just stared at each other. The school building began to get dark.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You drew quite a lot of attention in that final relay, Ayanokouji Kiyotaka-kun.” That was all she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, sorry. Can I send one little message? I’ve got someone waiting,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Go ahead.” Sakayanagi smiled, not appearing displeased at all.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I sent off the message I’d prepared via my phone. “So, can I assume you’re the one who called me here?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">An immediate answer, huh?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, What do you want? I’d like to wrap this up quickly, if possible.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“After seeing you run, I remembered something. I called you here because I wanted to share the shock I felt with you. It’s almost like the buildup toward a romantic confession, don’t you think?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I really have no idea what you’re talking about.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Clack. Clack.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> Sakayanagi, still gripping her cane, moved right next to me. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s been quite a long time, Ayanokouji-kun. Eight years and 243 days, actually.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re joking, right? I don’t even know who you are.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Heh. No, I suppose not. It’s just me who knows you, after all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Clack.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Clack.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Her cane’s tapping gradually grew quieter. What in the world was this about? I decided I was done here, and turned to walk away from Sakayanagi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“White Room,” Sakayanagi said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I stopped dead in my tracks at those two simple words. They cracked my composure. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s unpleasant, isn’t it? When only your opponent knows your secrets.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This is a reunion. I just wanted to come give you a proper greeting.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Reunion? Though I kept my back to Sakayanagi, I turned my head to look at her. I had never seen her before. I didn’t remember this girl, and I wasn’t missing any memories of my past. I had met Sakayanagi for the first time at school. There was no arguing with that fact.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, it’s quite all right that you don’t know me. But I know you. We have a strange sort of bond, you could say. To reunite with you in a place like this… To be honest, I never thought I’d see you again. But now, all the mysteries have been cleared away. The island, the cruise ship, and Class D’s expulsion uproar. I couldn’t imagine that everything was on account of Horikita Suzune. So, </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">you </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">were the one pulling the strings.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What are you talking about? There are lots of smart people in my class,” I retorted. I needed to be calm. I&#160;needed to get through this without panicking. I’d have time to think later.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“When you say ‘smart people,’ are you referring to Horikita Suzune-san? Or perhaps Hirata Yousuke-kun? Either way, now that I know who you are, I suppose it doesn’t really matter who anyone else is,” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Apparently, she wasn’t lying. She really did know me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Please, relax,” she added. “I’ve no intention of telling anyone about you at present.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wouldn’t things be easier if you did?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t want anything to get in my way. I’m the perfect person to bury false genius.” </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Clack</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">. Her thin cane clattered against the floor. “I’ve found little pleasure in this boring school life.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Can I ask you something else?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Please, go ahead. I’m honored to have you ask me a question. If you’d like to know, I’m even happy to tell you how I know about you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, I’m not interested in that. There’s just one thing I want to know.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">My eyes met Sakayanagi’s.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Can you bury me?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hee hee!” Sakayanagi chuckled softly to herself, and smiled once more. “Sorry for laughing. I don’t intend to insult you. I know quite well how incredible you are. I’ve been looking forward to this. I’ll be able to realize my dearest wish by destroying the greatest masterpiece that your father ever made.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wanted that, too. My destruction—my defeat—would mean that the old man would lose. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wanted the sad contradiction that I carried within me to be destroyed. I wished for that from the bottom of my heart.</span></p>
