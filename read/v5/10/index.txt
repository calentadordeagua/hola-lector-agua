<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 4:<br/>Everyone’s Calculations</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">S</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">TARTING FROM OUR NEXT</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> homeroom period, we’d be practicing independently to prepare for the sports festival. During Class D’s free period, we changed into gym clothes and made our way to the athletic grounds.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Whoa, check it out.” Ike, wearing an unpleasant expression, stared at the main school building. Several students were watching us through the windows of their classroom.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, they’re from Class B, right? Guess they’re already spying,” mused Ike.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Scoping out the other classes to gauge their athletic abilities was totally understandable, whether we were enemies or allies.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Class A is checking us out, too.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If we wanted to train in such a conspicuous place, it was only natural that we’d be watched. Even if we held back, so the others wouldn’t see our true skills, that would only hamper our practice and hurt us in the end.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“They started right away.” Horikita had also noticed the curious stares. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">For my part, I was more worried about Class C. Not a single one of them was looking at us. It was like they were saying that Class D wasn’t any threat to them.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you worried about Ryuuen-kun?” Horikita asked&#160;me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, a little.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m surprised that he doesn’t seem to be carrying out some kind of reconnaissance. However, he </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">did </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">refuse to cooperate with Class B. He doesn’t appear interested in any sort of strategy.” Horikita shot me a look that seemed to say, </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">I understand, </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">before continuing. “Or so I would have thought if you hadn’t warned me. Surely the other students must think they’re safe, though.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She glanced at the students, who were throwing themselves into practice.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What you mentioned earlier, about Ryuuen-kun already having a strategy? I suppose this means that his plans are already in place. Reconnaissance isn’t necessary for him.” Horikita no longer appeared optimistic. On the contrary, she now looked clearly bewildered. “Anyone would want information on the other classes. They should want to know who is physically gifted, who’ll participate in which contests, and so on. But him…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That in itself proved that Ryuuen already had a secret strategy.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The important thing is not to be satisfied just with knowing that Ryuuen has a plan,” I explained.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do you mean?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Normally, when someone has a secret plan in place, they do whatever it takes to ensure that their enemies don’t figure it out. However, Ryuuen’s not even trying to hide the fact that he’s up to something. He’s making it obvious by not bothering to do any reconnaissance,” I&#160;said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s almost like he’s showing off then,” Horikita replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Thinking more deeply about the implications of Ryuuen’s behavior would reveal his thought process. I&#160;wondered how much of it Horikita could see at present.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You know, I can’t help but wonder where your powers of observation came from. But, since you’ve forbidden me from asking questions, I suppose I must be quiet.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">What a disagreeable and Horikita-esque way of putting it. Of course, no matter how much she poked and prodded, I wouldn’t budge.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Suzune. You have a minute?” asked Sudou, startling Horikita with his late arrival. Sudou interrupting her train of thought clearly irritated her. Something else seemed to be bothering her, too.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ve told you numerous times. Please do not call me by my first name.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Aw, what do you mean? Does it really bother you that much?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. I don’t want someone I’m not close to using my first name,” said Horikita. As always, she went right for the heart, sparing none of Sudou’s feelings. “If you continue to call me ‘Suzune’ after this warning, I’ll have to take more direct action to make you stop.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">A terrifying proposition, truly. If at all possible, I didn’t want to hear the details. He no doubt desperately wanted to call her by her first name, but he would really suffer if she came to hate him. Whatever his thoughts were, I wouldn’t find out, because Sudou changed the topic.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, if I get the best results out of anyone in Class D during the festival, will you let me call you by your first name?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Oh?</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> It was a rather modest request for Sudou, but I didn’t know whether Horikita would acknowledge it. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hard work is its own reward. Why should I agree to such an idiotic bet?” Horikita probably wasn’t aware of Sudou’s feelings for her. I wondered how he intended to answer.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, it’s just… Not too long ago, you saved me. That’s why I want to do things properly, to show you how I feel… No, I think I’d like to be friends with you, first. This is the first step,” said Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I can’t understand why you’d go out of your way to request something like that. But fine. If you produce the best results, I will allow you to use my first name. However, I won’t be satisfied if you’re just the best in our class. Show me that you can be the best out of everyone in our grade,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She’d given Sudou an incredibly high hurdle to clear. But Sudou showed no sign of balking. “All right! It’s a deal, then. If I place first in our grade level, I’ll call you by your first name,” he said happily.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“However, in the event that you don’t get first place, I’ll forever forbid you from saying my first name. Prepare yourself,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Y-yeah.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was a Herculean task, though not impossible. He nodded vigorously in response. Based on what I’d seen of the other classes, Sudou’s skills were top-notch. I&#160;doubted he’d have any trouble during the events. The only person who might rival him was Kouenji, but he was totally unmotivated and most likely wouldn’t be a problem. That meant it was just a matter of how many competitions Sudou could dominate.</span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">4.1</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">W</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">E STARTED TESTING</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> everyone’s aptitude in earnest. Although Hirata’s policy didn’t encourage forced participation, roughly 90 percent of the class took part in the various tests. Only a few, like Kouenji and the Professor, sat them out.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hah…ah…phew!” Sakura finished in last place, looking as though she was about to collapse, both hands on her knees.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Good work, Sakura. You really gave it your all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“A-Ayanokouji-kun. Ah…hah…” Sakura wheezed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakura had always been rather unathletic, so she wasn’t terribly proactive when it came to sports. But recently, she’d worked hard to contribute to the class. Unfortunately, her lack of physical endurance was still a problem.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, hey! Let’s go!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Meanwhile Sudou, usually the least serious class member, worked harder than I’d ever seen. There was a certain magnetism to him. He couldn’t afford to disappoint after talking such a big game, but he needn’t have worried. When he was in top form, Sudou was unbeatable. No student in our class could stand as his equal.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wow, just like we’d expect, Sudou-kun! You always end up taking first place in our class, no matter the event. That’s amazing!” said Kushida. She jumped up and down in excitement as Sudou finished running the 100-meter relay race. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Heh, guess so. Even so, I dunno what’ll happen if </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">he</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> runs.” Sudou glared at Kouenji, who showed no interest whatsoever.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You know, that reminds me. I’ve never seen Kouenji seriously run before.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Back when Kouenji had faced off against Sudou in swimming class, he’d beaten Sudou’s time. His skill was obvious, but he wasn’t going to budge unless he wanted to. He’d let us do whatever we pleased while he sat on the sidelines, not lifting a finger.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But, seriously, you’re amazing. Really, Sudou-kun, you’re definitely the sports festival leader,” said Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Leader? Me?” Sudou repeated the word, appearing to be a little taken aback.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes.” Hirata, who was recording everyone’s scores, apparently agreed with Kushida. “After all, the sports festival really is an athlete’s time to shine. You’re especially qualified, Sudou-kun. Would you mind leading our class for this?” Hirata himself was qualified, of course, but he had judged Sudou to be the far superior choice.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I ain’t really suited to be a leader or anythin’…” Sudou looked over at Horikita for her opinion. He seemed a little bewildered, as he only ever acted alone or within a&#160;small group.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re not the type of person to speak eloquently. As a communicator, Hirata-kun is certainly the superior choice,” she said. “However, based on your sprint earlier and your other athletic records, I can see Hirata-kun’s point. You shine when showered with attention. Besides, brute strength will be necessary to pull our class along. I&#160;wouldn’t object to you serving as leader in this.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She didn’t encourage him, but she didn’t reject him, either. She’d acknowledged him. After all, she wasn’t arbitrarily participating in practice; she was trying to get a firm grasp of everyone’s talents.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay! I’ll lead our class to victory,” said Sudou. Perhaps being so lovestruck was foolish, but he clearly wanted to live up to Horikita’s expectations.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t get overconfident and sloppy, because I’ll make you pay for that,” warned Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She walked away and returned to practicing. Sudou blushed, clenching his fists as he watched her go.</span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">4.2</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">S</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">UDOU WAS SERIOUS</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> about being a leader. The next day, he began coaching the other students, starting by teaching them how to win the tug-of-war. I watched from a slight distance away.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re just pointlessly strainin’ yourself. There’s no strength at all in your pull. At this rate, you wouldn’t win, even if you could,” said Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He grabbed the short length of rope tightly to give us a practical demonstration. Ike and Yamauchi faced off against him. The looks on their faces suggested that they expected to win, but when the match began, Sudou pulled with an overwhelming amount of strength. A disappointingly short time later, Ike and Yamauchi fell down and sat on the ground.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“See? You ain’t putting any power into it at all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t get it. Hey, Sudou, is there some kinda trick or something?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, power’s important, but also, don’t just use your arms. Use your hips, too,” said Sudou. His manner was rough, but he gave each and every student thorough guidance. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, Sudou-kun. Can you come look at this for us? We’re not doing well with our carriage for the cavalry battle.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sure. Wait a sec. I’ll be right there.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There were more than a few unathletic students, which meant that several people asked Sudou for help. I was honestly surprised that even girls asked him for his opinion.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, he seems to be taking this rather seriously.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s the first time that people have relied on him. Leadership might actually suit him, don’t you think?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In theory, it probably felt nice to be relied on. That was doubly true for students like Sudou, who lived their lives in solitude.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“As for me, though…well, I wouldn’t mind praising him, but—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Before she could finish the thought, we heard an angry voice.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Look, I’m tellin’ you that ain’t it!” Sudou kicked at the dirt, sending it flying toward Ike and Yamauchi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Gah! Puh…yuck! Dude, c’mon. Stop!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita sighed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sudou’s rashness was still a problem. A leader needed to understand the fact that others were fundamentally different from them. A leader also had to be patient, like Hirata, who always employed gentle teaching methods. He was currently checking some girls’ positions in the carriage formation for the cavalry battle to make sure that they were comfortable.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, I think this formation is great. But don’t you feel a little cramped?” Hirata asked them.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. My shoulders hurt a little, I guess.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Let’s change the positioning a bit. If you just move a few centimeters, it’ll feel different.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, wow, you’re right! That feels much more comfortable. Thank you, Hirata-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, can you help us out, too, Hirata?” another cavalry group asked, and he responded with a broad smile.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why don’t you help teach the girls, too?” I asked Horikita. She was one of our top athletes; she had a lot going for her as a teacher.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t want to teach them. Besides, I don’t think anyone wants me to teach them, either.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">With that bold statement, she started warming up by herself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m already trying my hardest to produce results for myself. How can you be so laid back? I suppose, if you’re sure you can win, that’s fine,” added Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nope, I’m not confident.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That sounds about right. You always get average scores. You’re neither fast nor slow. Your results don’t stand out at all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You know that?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I try hard to uncover my classmates’ true abilities.” She had observed me closely, even during physical education. “I’ll ask you this just once, but…are you holding back, like you did with your test scores?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Would I do something so pointless?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The odds of that are about fifty-fifty to me. So, do&#160;you?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sorry to disappoint, but what you see is what you&#160;get.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you’re neither good nor bad. That means I shouldn’t expect great results, hmm?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yep, that’s right.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Then you should practice more, starting now.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If I could improve in such a short time span, this wouldn’t be a problem. Unlike studying, trying to do this all at the last minute the night before is kind of pointless.” Physical ability only improved through repeated conditioning.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wouldn’t it be a good idea to focus on events where you </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">can</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> improve quickly, though? For example, just learning how to grip a rope or form a carriage will increase our chances of victory.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Maybe.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I’d tried to slack off and cut class, but Horikita had firmly boxed me in. Well, there wasn’t any getting around it. I needed to practice for the recommended-participant events I’d somehow gotten roped into.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey,” Horikita called out to me again as I walked away.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hmm?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Every class’s physical abilities will determine who wins and who loses. Right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This is a sports festival. Physical abilities are key.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. Still, that kind of thinking is limited. If I focus on my own performance, I’m confident that I can produce good results, but something’s been troubling me for a while. I might not be able to reach Class A just by improving my own skills,” said Horikita. It was an uncharacteristically timid remark coming from her. It seemed that she’d been deeply affected by her mistakes in the prior tests. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay. Let me ask you this. What should we do in the sports festival to produce results? To reach Class A?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In reply to my question, Horikita just gave me a confused look.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Could it be that you’ll win if you enjoy it? This is the long-awaited sports festival, after all. Forgetting that this is a test and having fun is an option,” I said, as if trying to change the topic.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You promised me that you’d cooperate, didn’t you? You said you’d help me reach Class A,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s what I’m doing, isn’t it?” I spread out my hands, showing that I’d nothing to hide. “I’ll participate in the festival. That’s cooperating.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you serious?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You said so yourself, didn’t you? Physical ability will determine victory or defeat.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But there are other aspects to the competition,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In other words, she meant something besides athletic ability that would influence the outcome.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay. So, on the day of the festival, should I give the Class B and C students stomachaches and make them bow out? If I do that, we’ll have a total victory. We’ll win by an overwhelming margin,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Stop joking.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s about the answer you expected from me, though, isn’t it? The sports festival is a challenge to be taken head-on. Clumsy little schemes will backfire. Each person should strengthen their abilities and strive to outdo the competition,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was what the school would be looking for.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you wanted me to expand on your way of thinking, it’s like you’re saying athleticism alone won’t be enough,” I added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you agree that something else is required?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’ll learn the answer to that soon enough,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Somebody was walking toward us. “Horikita-san, you’re up next for three-legged race practice.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Having been called for, Horikita left. Apparently, she was partnering with Onodera, a girl from the swimming club who was supposedly a great sprinter. Each person’s athletic abilities were important, sure, but so were their abilities to cooperate with their fellow classmates. As I watched them tie their legs together, I wondered how Horikita would handle it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The five pairs of girls lined up at the starting line, then launched into the race. When it came to their overall skill level, Horikita and Onodera were the best pair. However, the results had yet to be seen. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">They weren’t slow, but they weren’t fast, either. They came in third. The worst team was Sakura and Inogashira, a most unathletic pair. They were slow as molasses.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Dissatisfied, Horikita and Onodera decided to practice the three-legged race again. They felt the hopes of the entire class weighing on them. Their time was no better than the first, though.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Those two’re kinda slow, huh?” mused Sudou, unexpectedly. He was offering an outsider’s perspective while all eyes were on them.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Finishing their second run, the pair immediately untied the cord and faced each other. “Hey, Horikita-san, can’t you try to keep pace with me better?” Onodera sounded slightly annoyed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s certainly true that we aren’t moving in sync, but that’s not my fault. You’re too slow.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Shouldn’t you strive to match the faster partner? Deliberately slowing my pace to suit yours makes no sense,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It seemed that my worst fears were being realized. Trying to keep pace with the speedy Horikita was no easy&#160;feat.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay. How about we</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;"> </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">give it a try, Ayanokouji-kun?” asked Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Roger.” I didn’t have time to waste on Horikita, who was fighting with her partner. The three-legged race was a first for me, too.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“To begin with, let’s just focus on running. Then we can try and fix whatever goes wrong. Okay?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I nodded and tied our legs together per Hirata’s instructions. It was too tight for my liking, and made me feel shackled. Honestly, even if we were both guys, it was a little embarrassing to be that close to somebody. Particularly Hirata, the darling of all the Class D girls.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay then. Let’s take our first step,” said Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I nodded, and waited for Hirata to move his leg so I could match him. Keeping up with his rhythm, I stepped with my outer leg.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This is really uncomfortable.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It is, isn’t it? But, as you run, try matching your breathing to our movements. Okay? I’m going to start running.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata picked up his pace slightly, and I copied him. Really, my run was only at about power-walking speed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, that’s it. That’s it! You’ve got it!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Anyone could’ve kept the pace, but being complimented really did make things easier. As I got used to three-legged running, I realized that it was surprisingly simple. If both partners understood each other and maintained a similar pace, everything got smoother.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata and I ran a small lap, came back, and untied the cord. Loud, high-pitched cheers could be heard from the girls. “So fast! Just like we’d expect from you, Hirata-kun!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s really, really easy with Ayanokouji-kun as my partner. Let’s all practice and do our best during the festival, okay?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yeah, he really was supportive. He’d just finished his own practice, and now he was heading off to give other students advice again. Just another day in the life of Hirata, a truly superior man.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">4.3</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">T WAS MID-SEPTEMBER</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> and the sports festival was less than two weeks away. Sudou, who couldn’t study to save his life, remained steadfast and practiced tirelessly. Tempering his spirit day in and day out with basketball had made him tenacious. Some students among us were holding back and cutting corners, but Sudou always gave everything he could without flaunting.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This degree of effort was probably the bare minimum that the school was looking for in the sports festival. That was especially true for direct competitions where we’d be facing off against other opponents, like in the cavalry battle or the tug-of-war. The outcome could probably be greatly influenced just be our formation or strategy. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Of course, Hirata hadn’t forgotten about our relationship with Class A. He periodically held meetings with Katsuragi during which they discussed how best to compete. Class D, normally just an inch away from disaster, was doing almost too well. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Looking at the big picture, I saw two remaining issues at hand.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">First, Horikita Suzune. She could become an invaluable asset to the class, but she wasn’t quite there yet. No matter how many times Horikita changed partners, they always ended up fighting and dissolving the partnership. Eventually, Horikita decided to compete paired with the girl who best matched her speed, but even that had fallen apart. Now she just spent her time silently alone.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you have a minute?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What?” Perhaps because of stress, she seemed even pricklier than usual.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I think it’d do you some good to compromise a little more,” I said. I’d watched her practice, but had seen no sign of improvement. Horikita’s overly forceful nature was getting in the way.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Many people have told me that,” she said, rubbing her forehead in exasperation. “I won’t compromise because I’m trying to get the best time. Isn’t that a good thing? The three-legged race is different from a normal one; even someone who’s slow should be able to keep up.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you have no intention of yielding?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s right. I don’t intend to accommodate someone else’s slowness.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But that’s why no one wants to practice with you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When it came time to practice the three-legged race, Horikita was left outside of the circle our class had formed. If things continued like this, she’d have no hope of improving her time.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t understand. If I’m expected to yield, my partner must put in the effort first. I can’t work with someone who refuses to even try to improve,” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I understood what Horikita was trying to say. The girls who paired up with Horikita suggested dissolving their partnership as soon as they realized their timing didn’t match. There was a fundamental reason behind it, however.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Stick your foot out.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What are you getting at?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Partner with me for the three-legged race.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why should I?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s a mixed-gender three-legged race. Can’t we determine how compatible we are as partners?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you think you can keep pace with me? You’ll only drag me down.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“According to your theory, my speed isn’t the issue, only my effort.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Fine. I’ll tie us together.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita crouched and tied the cord around our legs, as if telling me not to touch her. Everyone nearby was focused on practicing, and no one paid attention to us. Even Sudou, who’d probably get angry if he saw this, was too busy with other people.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well then, let’s go!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">At the beginning, I mirrored Horikita. However, as we picked up speed, I started going at my own pace.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“H-hey!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Despite Horikita’s panic, I mercilessly went faster. She did everything she could to keep up with me, but her stamina and strength were far inferior that of a boy’s, she couldn’t match mine.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You said that keeping up with your partner isn’t difficult, right?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s… I know!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She was obstinate, and she refused to give up. I decided to shift gears. In the three-legged race, speed alone wasn’t enough; that much was clear now that I’d tried it myself. The important thing was to find a tempo that suited both partners, then find your best stride. If you tried to pick up the pace without doing this, you’d end up with a mismatched hobble.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Tch!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Eventually, Horikita had to admit defeat. I grabbed her by the shoulder as she stumbled, then came to a stop. She breathed raggedly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This wasn’t about fast or slow. Your practices went wrong because you didn’t look at your partner,” I told her. Without saying another word, I untied the cord around our legs. “The important thing is to work with your partner. How about letting them take the lead?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Think about it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita’s athleticism meant that she needed to discern her partner’s ability level and then work with it. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t know whether she would learn and mature. That was up to her. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida Kikyou was the second problem. She was something of a supporting character—working backstage, but never taking the spotlight. Although Hirata and Karuizawa often outshone Kushida, most of our classmates liked her, which gave her a level of influence even those other two didn’t have. In addition to Kushida’s exceptional communication skills, she was both academically and physically gifted, and had been blessed with an incredible figure. In a sense, her assignment to Class D in the first place was quite the mystery.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, I knew about the darkness within her. Not too long after starting school, I caught her angrily ranting to herself on a secluded rooftop. And, although I didn’t yet know why, it was a fact that Kushida hated Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But both Horikita and Kushida were vital to Class D’s improvement. Therefore, the only way to resolve the issue was to have them confront one another.</span></p>
