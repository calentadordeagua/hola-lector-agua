<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 5:<br/>The Turbulent Double Date</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">C</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HRISTMAS DAY HAD ARRIVED</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">. In the past, this day had meant little to me, but that wasn’t the case this year. For the first time in my life, I’d spend Christmas Day with a member of the opposite sex. I wondered whether Satou would enjoy hanging out with me. Much as I’d have liked her to, we still didn’t know each other all that&#160;well. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I’d never been on a real, honest-to-god, one-on-one date before. That should’ve been significant, but there was still so much I didn’t understand. “Just go with the flow, I guess.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I left my room and took the elevator to the lobby. If I remembered correctly, Satou and I were going to catch a movie. We’d agreed to meet at 11:30, but I decided to get there a little early.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-before: always;"></div>
<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">5.1</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">U</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">NFORTUNATELY, IT WAS</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> cloudy outside, and it promised to be cloudy all day. I got to our meeting spot ten minutes early, but when I looked up from checking the time, I saw Satou walking toward me. She seemed somewhat uneasy as she scanned her surroundings. Then our eyes met, and she grinned.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Good morning, Ayanokouji-kun!” she called, hurrying over. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When she came up to me, I couldn’t help noticing that she smelled nice. “You’re early,” I remarked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You too, Ayanokouji-kun. Did I keep you waiting, by any chance?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nah, I just got here.” A cliché line, but true. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Really?” Satou leaned forward, playfully eyeing me like a cat that spied a mouse.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I nodded, feeling slightly overwhelmed already. Well, we had a few minutes left until our date was officially supposed to start, but perhaps we should just begin. I was ready to get a move on, but for some reason, Satou looked around the area again. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Should we head out?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“O-oh, yes. S-sorry, just give me a minute.” She reached into her bag and rummaged through it, muttering to herself loudly enough for me to hear. “Did I forget it…?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You forgot something?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, sorry. I just wondered what happened to my phone.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Looking into Satou’s bag, I noticed a long, narrow box covered in wrapping paper. I strategically averted my gaze. “I can call your phone for you,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, thanks. You’re really kind, Ayanokouji-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Helping someone find their phone didn’t seem especially kind. Most people would’ve done the same.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Satou continued to speak, sounding a bit awkward. “If I remember right, this morning…” She paused. Then she shrieked, “Ahh! Found it!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Good news, then.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She held up her phone and put it in her pocket, laughing. “Sorry to keep you waiting! Shall we go?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But then…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Good morning, Ayanokouji-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Turning around, I saw Hirata Yousuke, looking as bright and cheerful as ever. I offered a wave in reply. Next to Hirata was his “girlfriend,” Karuizawa Kei. Were they on a Christmas date? I knew their relationship was fake, but they might’ve been doing this to keep up the act. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Very&#160;clever.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Good morning, Karuizawa-san,” said Satou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Morning,” Karuizawa replied, smiling. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This is a rare sight, you two together,” said Hirata. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you on a date, too?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. I didn’t really make any Christmas plans, just in case,” he told me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So, he’d left his schedule open for his fake girlfriend Karuizawa’s sake. Hirata always put others before himself. Though I admired him, I acknowledged that it wasn’t easy to pull off.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I thought one of your friends would’ve invited you out. No?” I asked Hirata. He was popular among our classmates, and even among the seniors in the soccer club. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, I think they probably wanted to give us alone time,” he replied, glancing at Karuizawa. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">From the outside, Hirata and Karuizawa embodied an ideal couple. No one wanted to get in a happy couple’s way at Christmas. However, as long this fake relationship continued, Hirata couldn’t get close to another girl. I felt kind of bad for him. Even if he developed feelings for someone, he’d never do anything to hurt or embarrass Karuizawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">No wonder she chose him as her protector.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Karuizawa-san’s always been popular with the girls in class, but I didn’t know she was this close with Satou-san,” whispered Hirata, looking at the girls warmly, as if they were his sisters or daughters.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I thought they hung out a lot, like, on their days off. Is&#160;that not the case?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I didn’t think so.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Really?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do you think’s going on?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I dunno.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In any case, I figured we shouldn’t hold up Hirata and Karuizawa any longer. I checked my phone. It was already 11:40, close to the movie’s start time. We should’ve made tracks for the theater, but Satou and Karuizawa were deep in conversation, apparently enjoying themselves. They were whispering, so I couldn’t pick out the words.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Feeling lost, I met Hirata’s eyes. He seemed to grasp the problem and interrupted Karuizawa. “Karuizawa-san, I think maybe it’s time to let them have their fun. Shall we?” he asked in his usual gentle tone.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, when did you guys start going out, anyway?” asked Karuizawa. It was a perfectly natural question.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? I-It’s not like we’re actually dating or anything, right?! R-Right, Ayanokouji-kun?” said Satou. She looked flustered. I shook my head in response.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, Karuizawa regarded us with obvious suspicion. “Eh? I mean, you guys are on a date on Christmas Day. That means you’re going out, right? Don’t you think so, Hirata-kun?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well…I suppose that’s what people would assume, yes.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s, well, um… I only invited Ayanokouji-kun to hang out,” said Satou, fidgeting bashfully. “I-Is this okay, A-Ayanokouji-kun? Spending Christmas with me?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If I didn’t want to, I would’ve declined your offer,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hee hee!” Satou lightly scratched her cheek.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hunh. So, does that mean you’re interested in Satou-san, Ayanokouji-kun?” asked Karuizawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“C-come on. Stop it, Karuizawa-san,” said Satou, blushing bright red. She fanned her face, as if attempting to cool it. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Karuizawa kept going. “So, why don’t you just start dating already? Christmas is a time for romance.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I really don’t think it’s our place to say that to them, Karuizawa-san.” Hirata politely tried to rein her in.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry, sorry. I’m being nosy, huh? Sorry, Satou-san.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, no, it’s okay. I don’t mind,” replied Satou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, don’t you think a double date sounds like fun?” asked Karuizawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“A double date?” I replied. Hirata and I glanced at each other.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, yeah! Me and Hirata-kun could go with you two. Doesn’t that sound like fun? I just thought it wouldn’t be a bad idea to hang out, you know?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If we’d arranged this beforehand, that’d be one thing. But Karuizawa proposing a spontaneous double date baffled me. The plans Satou and I had made would go up in smoke. Judging from Hirata’s expression, he felt the same. However, Satou didn’t show even the slightest hint of surprise.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sure that these two have other plans,” Hirata told Karuizawa, but his words had no effect. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She continued, undeterred. “Satou-san just told me that she thought it sounded fun. Right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, it does sound fun.” Apparently, they’d already discussed this.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Regardless, Hirata looked concerned. “How about next time? If we’re going on a double date, I think it’d be better to plan in advance.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I guess. But doesn’t the fact that it’s unplanned make it kinda fun?” asked Karuizawa. She sounded excited, as if her heart was set on the double date. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Unlike Hirata and I, who grew anxious without a plan in place, Karuizawa seemed to thrive on spontaneity. Maybe she was looking for a thrill precisely because her romance was fake? I wasn’t so sure. I knew her well enough to doubt this would really be that fun for her. Still, why else would she propose a double date?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Remember, it’s Christmas,” stated Hirata. He wore a troubled expression.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You don’t want to, Hirata-kun?” Karuizawa asked bluntly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m fine with it, personally, but isn’t this up to Satou-san and Ayanokouji-kun?” he asked. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Satou gave Karuizawa a look that seemed to say, “This isn’t too much bother, is it?” I wondered how she really felt about this double-date proposal. “It might be a little sudden, but I’d like to try it…I think,” she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Maybe Satou just couldn’t refuse a proposal from Karuizawa, the queen bee of Class D. That really didn’t seem to be the case here, though.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do you think, Ayanokouji-kun?” asked Satou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">First the baton had passed from Hirata to Karuizawa, then Karuizawa to Satou, and now from Satou to me. I&#160;couldn’t carelessly drop it. “Well…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hanging out with just one girl was stressful enough. But a double date? It was a daunting prospect for a dating newbie like me. Still, I didn’t want to be the sole dissenting voice. I supposed that, if Satou was willing, I&#160;shouldn’t object. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This still left some problems. Satou and I had planned to watch a movie, and I didn’t know whether that would even be possible anymore, since we might not be able to get seats next to one another. Or maybe ditching those plans was part of being spontaneous? </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This date wasn’t going the way I’d expected. Still, I couldn’t say a double date was </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">all</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> bad. There might’ve been awkward moments of silence if I were all alone with Satou, but Hirata and Karuizawa could keep the conversation going smoothly. Besides, even though Haruka said that she’d make sure Airi didn’t bump into us by chance, it might still happen. In that case, it’d probably look more natural if four of us were hanging out, rather than just Satou and me. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you three are okay with it, I don’t have any objections,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Karuizawa immediately took action. “All right. Where were you two planning on going now, anyway?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Satou looked just a little relieved. Maybe she’d been nervous, too? Maybe she was anxious about being alone with me. “Um, well, Ayanokouji-kun and I planned to see a movie,” she said. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The one that just opened today?” Karuizawa asked. “Oh, that’s lucky for us. We were also planning to see it. Whoa—it looks like we even picked the same screening! That’s amazing!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The two girls were incredibly excited about this coincidence. I couldn’t help noticing Satou’s stiff expression, though.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What a coincidence, huh, Ayanokouji-kun?” This serendipity seemed to surprise even Hirata. Since the movie had just come out, it </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">was</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> quite the stroke of luck. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do we do about the assigned seats, though? We can’t change them, can we?” I asked. It was time to see whether these coincidences just kept coming.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, we can’t. Let’s see.” Karuizawa fiddled with her phone.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How’s it look, Karuizawa-san?” asked Satou, peeking.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It looks like…we’re sitting in different places. Oh well.” Karuizawa showed Hirata their seats. We were in completely different spots. So, this was where the coincidences stopped.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay, I think it’s about time we get going, Ayanokouji-kun!” said Satou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When we first met up, she’d looked so meek and nervous. Running into Karuizawa and Hirata seemed to restore her usual vigor, and she stuck close to me as we walked. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Very </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">close. The four of us, now on a double date, made our way toward the movie theater. We walked through the mall lined up side by side: me, then Satou, then Karuizawa, and finally Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Whoa. The two of you look good together,” muttered Karuizawa, watching Satou and me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“R-really?” asked Satou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. I mean, you guys look like a couple that’s used to spending Christmas together. You know, you just give off those warm, fuzzy vibes,” said Karuizawa. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hee hee! She says that we look like a couple, Ayanokouji-kun. Isn’t that embarrassing?” asked Satou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Guess so.” Well, we </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">were</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> out on a Christmas date. The observation made sense.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But you two aren’t, like, officially going out?” Karuizawa asked. “Or </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">are</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> you, hmm?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“N-no, we’re not. Really. It’s not that kind of relationship!” sputtered Satou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Reeeally? If you’re hiding something, you better spill,” said Karuizawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Karuizawa was clearly teasing us, but Satou didn’t actually seem to mind. If anything, she seemed like she enjoyed it. It was difficult to wrap my head around at first, but the more I thought about it, the more it made sense. If people thought that I was dating one of the hottest girls in school, I’d be embarrassed when they teased me. But, at the same time, I’d feel pretty pleased with myself. That said, I doubted that Satou felt so strongly about me. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That reminds me, Satou-san. You don’t have a boyfriend yet, right?” asked Karuizawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Y-yeah, that’s right.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Karuizawa was relentless. I half-listened to the conversation, trying to decide how I could survive this double date with my dignity intact. Before I knew it, we were at the theater.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I guess we gotta part ways for a bit. Don’t mind us, you two,” said Karuizawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was it? She was just taking off? I could usually predict Karuizawa’s behavior, but so much was going on here that I didn’t understand. So, even though this was a double date, it’d just be Satou and me for a while? I didn’t get it, but I decided to go with it. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">More to the point, I didn’t know what to say to Satou. I knew almost nothing about her. I’d tried scrounging up information during these past couple days, but I’d gotten nowhere. I hadn’t been able to talk to her at all since before winter vacation. I was sure she was similarly out of her depth. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Granted, I had a few generic questions up my sleeve about things like favorite foods, hobbies, and so on. Now that the moment was here, however, those questions evaporated right out of my head. I didn’t want her to think something like, </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Whoa, this guy does whatever online self-help guides tell him to.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As I agonized over what to do, Karuizawa’s eyes met&#160;mine.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re being pretty quiet, aren’t you? Isn’t the quiet type a tough role to play on a date?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not playing a part. I genuinely have no idea what to do.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We communicated that with a mere exchange of glances. Or maybe I just imagined that was what Karuizawa was thinking. The seconds kept ticking, and no one said anything.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Satou-san, maybe Ayanokouji-kun doesn’t know what to talk about?” asked Karuizawa. Her question cut through our silence like an arrow.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Satou looked relieved. “Do you like idols, Ayanokouji-kun?” she asked. Maybe she’d wondered what to ask me,&#160;too. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She’d lobbed the conversation ball at me; a nice, gentle toss, and easy to catch. “Idols? To be honest, I’m not all that familiar with them. I don’t really like or dislike them, though. Do you like them, Satou?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, quite a bit. I like those ‘cool’ idols, but I guess girl idol groups are ‘in’ right now. Have you heard anything about those? There’re, like, </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">so</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> many of them.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. They’re on TV every day. You mean those groups that do their own original song-and-dance routines, right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, that’s what I’m talking about. I really love them. There’re so many good songs, too.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hunh.” I nodded at Satou’s relentless idol lecture.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ooh—I especially love this one group’s debut single! I’ll lend you the CD,” Satou added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thanks.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I’d made a conversational error. The discussion was going to fall apart if I only responded “hunh” and “thanks.” Satou would end up doing all the work. She’d tossed me the ball; now, I had to throw it back to her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What kinds of songs do you listen to?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This time, I’d toss the ball right. What kind of songs did I listen to? That was a surprisingly simple question, but the answer got stuck in my throat. If I opened up to Satou about my interests and hobbies, how would she respond? I mean, if I mentioned Beethoven or Mozart, that’d definitely be a swing and miss. On the other hand, saying that I liked “natural” sounds like rainfall or chirping birds would seem bizarre.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">I shouldn’t tell her about my tastes. She wants me to discuss current music, right?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There was a really popular movie this year, right?” I asked her. “An anime.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Uh, yeah. That romance movie, right? It was super moving, wasn’t it?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ve been listening to stuff by that one group…you know, the group that performed the theme song. That kinda stuff.” I didn’t remember the band’s name, but I </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">had</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> listened to that theme song countless times. I hoped that’d keep the conversation going.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah! I know that band! Yeah, I </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">so</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> love them, too!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I’d returned the ball, and Satou caught it. However, we couldn’t stay on this topic too much longer.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You really know a lot about them,” Satou added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You think? I just know basic things, really.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Man, girls were better at conversation than I’d anticipated. It might’ve had something to do with the different expectations that’d existed since the beginning of time for gender roles.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re not part of any clubs, are you? Weren’t you in track and field before?” asked Satou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This choice of topic was easy to understand; it was likely due to my participation in the relay during the sports festival. “No, I’ve never been in any clubs,” I told Satou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I mean, I’d been a member of the “go home right after school” club for as long as I could remember. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, my running skills had clearly impressed Satou. “Really?! Even though you’re super fast?! That’s incredible! I mean, you were even quicker than the student council president!” she exclaimed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Maybe because of Satou’s giddiness, Karuizawa cut in on our conversation. “Wasn’t the student council president just slow? Like, maybe it was a race between two slowpokes,” she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t think that’s true at all, Karuizawa-san,” replied Satou. “Both of them were really fast.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hmmm. I find that hard to believe. Ayanokouji looks like he’d be no good in a fight. Besides, he’s a really cold dude, you know. Like, if someone close to him came down with a bad flu, he wouldn’t even visit them,” said Karuizawa. Her voice positively dripped with sarcasm.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She’d brought up fighting for no reason at all. I understood the cause of her anger now, at least. Karuizawa resented me because she thought I didn’t care about her. After her horrifying ordeal with Ryuuen on the roof, she could’ve gotten very sick. Maybe she’d proposed the double date to interfere with my plans?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t see him that way, though. I think Ayanokouji-kun’s a kind person,” said Satou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? Really?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I think Ayanokouji-kun’s kind, too,” said Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, now I feel like you’re ganging up on me,” Karuizawa said. Even though she sounded annoyed, I got the feeling that she teased me precisely to make Satou rise to my defense. Almost as if she </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">wanted</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> me and Satou to be an official couple.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“U-um, well, it’s just…you know…well, um…” Satou stammered. She wasn’t smiling anymore. It seemed like she wanted to say something, but couldn’t find the words. “U-um, hey, is there anything you’d like to ask me, Ayanokouji-kun?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Well, the conversation had been pretty one-sided for a while. It was only fair that I take a turn.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You know how we can’t contact anyone on the outside while we’re here at school? Does that ever bother you at all?” I asked. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Satou pondered her answer. “Yeah. I’ve been worried about a lot of stuff.” She thought a moment longer, then continued. “Back in junior high, I had a cat. I think my mom’s taking care of it for me, but not being able to see it’s been really hard.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It made sense that being separated from family would be difficult for most. Not being able to see a beloved pet had to be tough, too—almost like a parent not seeing their child.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Not seeing your cat for three years certainly sounds hard.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Did you have any pets, Ayanokouji-kun?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Uh, no. I wanted a dog, but my parents didn’t allow it.” That was basically true.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. Speaking of dogs, I saw a little puppy on campus the other day,” said Satou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? Really?” asked Karuizawa. Apparently, she was done letting Satou and me talk. She seemed to have really listened to our conversation.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yep! It was someone’s pet doggy,” replied Satou. “It was so cute!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Since students can’t have pets, it was probably an employee’s or teacher’s,” said Hirata. True, a dog wouldn’t just wander around campus on its own.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’d be so nice having a pet,” said Karuizawa. “It’d be the best thing ever.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I agree,” Satou replied. “It’d be great to have a pet shop around here.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why aren’t we allowed pets, anyway?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I know! It’s totally not okay.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The two girls got all worked up while Hirata and I walked along in silence. Although pets did a lot for emotional health, I could see numerous reasons why the school wouldn’t want them in the dorms. Even one animal per person would mean potentially hundreds in the building. Besides, if everyone left their pet all alone half the day to attend class… </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The girls didn’t seem interested in logic right now, though. They were more preoccupied with the fact that dogs and cats were cute.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was being a stick-in-the-mud, and kind of unreasonable. Even I was aware of that much. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">What a stupid thought.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> Logic wasn’t the point here. If I cut in to remind the girls that they couldn’t have pets, I’d only end up souring the mood.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’d like to get a rabbit. They’re relatively easy to raise, and they’re docile,” said Hirata, joining the conversation with ease. Both girls smiled. Men who could easily talk about anything were always popular. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Before I knew it, the time came to switch topics. As I thought about what to say, my eyes met Satou’s. “H-hey, Ayanokouji-kun. Um…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Satou had been fine a minute ago, but now she stammered again. Her nerves always seemed to give way whenever she really wanted to ask something. Was she like this with everyone, or just the opposite sex? She seemed ready to speak, but then closed her mouth again. It was probably an extra-difficult question.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, what’s your type, Ayanokouji-kun? In girls, I mean?” Before Satou could get the words out, Karuizawa asked me that question.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I-I’m curious about that, too,” said Satou, sounding relieved. Maybe it was the very question she wanted to ask. In that case, perhaps this double date wasn’t such a coincidence after all. I’d had a vague suspicion of that from the beginning. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Either way, I needed to answer. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">The kind of girl I like, huh?</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> “That’s a tricky question,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Satou’s eyes sparkled, but Karuizawa glared at me. Hirata, meanwhile, looked like he was enjoying this. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Spirited, I guess?” I finally replied. The moment I said it out loud, I knew that wasn’t exactly the right word. Many—maybe even most—girls might consider themselves “spirited.” Satou and Karuizawa didn’t seem thrilled by my response, either.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hunh. I didn’t think you’d be into girls like that, Ayanokouji-kun,” remarked Karuizawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Were Satou and Karuizawa both the spirited type? They definitely weren’t as dour as Horikita. But Kushida and Ichinose were spirited, too. Right?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait, do you think there’re only two types of girls, Ayanokouji-kun? The spirited type, and the quiet, gentle type?” Karuizawa added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is that true?” Satou asked me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, it’s not. It’s just that I’m relatively quiet, so I thought it’d be nice to date a girl who was sort of the opposite. If I misspoke, I take it back,” I told Satou and Karuizawa. I had a feeling I might’ve insulted them.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, what’s going on between you and Horikita-san? What’s the deal there?” asked Karuizawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Still with the interrogation.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> I wanted to say it was none of Karuizawa’s business, but Satou’s expression had clearly changed. This might be another question she also wanted to know the answer to. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Very few people understood my relationship with Horikita, but I knew that Karuizawa did. Therefore, she’d asked for Satou’s sake. If Satou had any affection for me, she must’ve confided in Karuizawa about it, resulting in the double date. In other words, Satou must’ve asked Karuizawa to back her up, so Karuizawa was digging for information, helping where she could. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t know which of them initially came up with the double-date idea, but I assumed that Karuizawa concocted the plan’s finer points.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey.” Just as I thought, I’d jumped in the river and been spectacularly devoured.</span></p>

Now she was just trying to make me look like a jerk. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, you four. Got a minute?” As we were about to enter the movie theater, someone called out to us. We turned around. “You’re Ayanokouji, right?”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata stepped forward. He was probably the only person in our group who could face off against Nagumo. Still, he couldn’t just say “another time” to an upperclassman. I wondered how he’d settle this.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m joking. I mean, if someone like you joined us, it’d ruin the mood,” he replied with a scornful laugh. “So, you’re the student Horikita-senpai’s been so fixated on. I’m just following the rumors, is all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you talking about the relay race, Senpai?” asked Hirata, expertly jumping into the conversation.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. You watched that too, huh?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. I already knew that Ayanokouji-kun was really fast.” A lie, but Nagumo had no way of knowing that. “Besides the race, what’s Ayanokouji-kun done to make you notice him?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“On the outside, he’s an ordinary student, except for how fast he is. Hmm,” said Nagumo, wearing a stern look. He grabbed my arm.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Satou, Karuizawa, and Hirata looked shocked. It probably seemed like we were on the verge of a brawl. Even Hirata, who knew Nagumo, froze.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“President Nagumo, you’re totally nuts,” Karuizawa laughed, clearly trying to defuse the situation.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, did I scare you? Sorry. My bad,” replied Nagumo, looking at her amiably. He didn’t let go of my arm. “I do have a high opinion of Horikita-senpai’s instincts,” he told me. “If he sees something in you, you must be special.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You must really trust his opinion. The student council president’s, I mean,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Former </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">student council president. After he graduates, I have a whole year at this school. Won’t you play with me?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I knew things were fraught between the elder Horikita and Nagumo, but I hadn’t thought that they were intense enough for Nagumo to preemptively attack me. I’d taken him for the type who was happy as long as people around him were content. That didn’t seem to be the case anymore. He wanted to make a show of his strength as a threat.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“May I ask something?” I asked. Until now, I’d been completely passive. Nagumo smiled. “When you became student council president, you said that things would get interesting at this school. You said that talented students would rise to the top. What’re you planning?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I might as well ask at this point.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I imagine that you first years have had some shitty, boring tests so far. I’ve had it up to here with those banal games. What if we had a special test based on a popular online game? Don’t you think that sounds </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">really</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> interesting?” he asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“An…online game?” I blinked in surprise. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Nagumo chuckled. “Don’t be so serious.” He let go of my arm, still laughing, but his eyes remained dead. “Sorry for pushing in on your date. See ya around.” With that, Nagumo followed his friends toward the karaoke place. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We were all silent a moment.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Whew. Now, </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">that</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> sure was something, wasn’t it?” asked Hirata, relieved that nothing had happened. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Satou, who’d been utterly silent, suddenly bubbled over with excitement. “Th-that’s amazing, Ayanokouji-kun! W-wow, to think the student council president thinks so highly of you!” she exclaimed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s really not that big a deal,” I said, but Satou remained starry-eyed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I dunno. I mean, the only thing Ayanokouji-kun has going for him is that he’s, like, a fast runner,” said Karuizawa, giving Hirata a smile. “Yousuke-kun’s a hundred times more amazing. He’s super fast—like, the fastest. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">And</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> he’s really smart, too. I mean, if Nagumo had his eye on anyone, it’d be Yousuke-kun, right? Isn’t this weird?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I definitely think that Hirata-kun’s amazing, but…but…but I don’t think that he could beat Ayanokouji-kun!” Satou stammered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was nice that Satou believed in me, but she didn’t need to go that far. Besides, she’d make Karuizawa snappish if she kept saying stuff like that. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yousuke-kun couldn’t beat him? Uh, aren’t Ayanokouji-kun’s grades totally lame compared to Yousuke-kun’s, though?” asked Karuizawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“W-well, that’s… He’s still smarter than me!” said Satou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I mean, she wasn’t wrong—but I wished she wouldn’t say it so proudly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Isn’t that great, Ayanokouji-kun? Satou-san thinks so highly of you. Though it’s weird that you get all this attention just by running fast,” said Karuizawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I guess,” I said.</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;"> Ah yes, Karuizawa. My biggest admirer. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had a sinking feeling that she was going to be like this all day.</span></p>

<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">5.2</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HE MOVIE THEATER</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> was even fuller than it’d been the other day, which made sense, between them fixing the equipment malfunction and the release of some hot new titles. I didn’t see Ibuki. Maybe she wasn’t interested in an American animated film, or maybe she was just avoiding the crowds, planning to see it later.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Everyone got their tickets, and we went in. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, uh, that reminds me, Karuizawa-san. Would you please come with me to the bathroom?” asked Satou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sure. The movie’s gonna start soon, though, so let’s hurry.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Satou dragged Karuizawa to the bathroom, leaving me with Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How do I put this? Nicely done,” said Hirata. “Karuizawa-san’s the first classmate I tried to save, you see.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He was wasting his Christmas on a fake date with Karuizawa. Could it be that he genuinely had feelings for her? No. His neutral expression gave me the sense that that wasn’t the case. All I saw was Hirata Yousuke, someone who always put others ahead of himself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m really grateful for what you did for Karuizawa-san, Ayanokouji-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I didn’t do much.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m glad that you and Karuizawa-san were in the same group during the ship test. She can stand on her own two feet now, without me.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“She’s not quite there yet, though. Right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You mean, because I’m still pretending to be her boyfriend?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah.” Karuizawa had grown. She was stronger and more resilient, and Hirata sensed that. She wouldn’t truly be free until she left him, though.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s just a matter of time, I think,” Hirata said. “We barely talk anymore. Apart from her plans today, I don’t think she needs me now.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This might be blunt, but are you really okay with wasting your Christmas on this?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. I’m Karuizawa-san’s boyfriend, after all. I mean, I haven’t wanted to date any other girls. I probably won’t in the future, either.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Really?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You see, Ayanokouji-kun, if everyone else is happy, then I’m happy,” he said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you don’t need romance?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t. That’s how I feel right now, anyway.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata was blessed with such good looks, a great personality, and so much talent. What a waste.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What about you, Ayanokouji-kun? Do you want to go out with Satou-san?” he asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well…” </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">No, not really. But saying that would be like negating the date itself.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> “I don’t know. I can’t really say right now.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This might not be my place, since I just said that I don’t need romance…but I think it might be good for you to go out with someone, Ayanokouji-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What, are you gonna hit me with the ‘you’ve never had a girlfriend before, better get on that’ speech?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ha ha ha! No, no. I mean, it’s certainly true that you don’t seem to have had a relationship. But I don’t think that’s because you’re unpopular. Is it because you just haven’t found anyone you like romantically?” he asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Honestly, it’s both. I’ve never been popular, and I haven’t found anyone.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The White Room didn’t specifically forbid romantic relationships. There just wasn’t any way for romance to blossom. Playtime, holidays—they didn’t exist there. We were monitored constantly, except during toilet breaks and bath time. You couldn’t grow close to someone under those circumstances.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Isn’t it exhausting, though? Always putting yourself second? Sacrificing yourself for the class’s sake?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Exhausting? On the contrary, it’s a lot more exhausting to see the class in disarray. Honestly, my anxiety’s gone way down since I started at this school.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">True. Back on the island, when the class had nearly fallen apart, I saw Hirata come close to his breaking point. He’d clearly improved now that Class D was coming together. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata Yousuke was one of Class D’s real leaders. He was indispensable, but also worryingly fragile. The island test worked out fine in the end, but I couldn’t predict what’d happen to Hirata if the class broke down again…due to Kushida, for instance. Back in junior high, she’d caused her class’s total collapse. Even now, she was working against Horikita, implying that she’d destroy her own class if she deemed it necessary. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If that happened, Hirata might snap. If the class’s very heart stopped beating, who knew?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Since the two girls hadn’t returned yet, I switched topics. “How much do you know about Student Council President Nagumo, Hirata?” I asked. They’d been in the same club, after all. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Not much, really. Outside the club, I don’t really interact with him. And since he became student council president, we only really ever say hi to each other in the&#160;hall.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay then, what’s your impression of him?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I guess I thought that he was an interesting upperclassman. He introduced bold, exciting new ideas, even for soccer practice. They didn’t always work, but they were fascinating, even when they made practice especially tough.” Hirata chuckled. “Anyway, he always gets results. Apparently, he’s even led his team to victory in major tournaments.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, he’s the perfect upperclassman, huh?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I’m not sure I’d put it like that.” Hirata shook his head. “The road to glory is hard. Quite a few people have left the club.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I haven’t heard any rumors about that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Probably because those students aren’t here anymore. The second years who butted heads with Nagumo-senpai quit the club and dropped out of school.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Isn’t that a little extreme?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t know the details…like how much Nagumo-senpai was involved.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Maybe Nagumo wasn’t the sole reason the students had dropped out. Maybe they left for personal reasons. What bothered me was what Horikita’s brother had said earlier, about how Nagumo removed anyone who stood in his way.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Nagumo was the light, and those who opposed him were shadows. He wanted to thoroughly eradicate that darkness, but it wasn’t so simple. Where there was light, there were always shadows. No matter how many you eliminated, new ones arose. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you joining the student council, Ayanokouji-kun?” asked Hirata. A reasonable deduction, given our conversation thus far.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, I’m not.” I made sure to be very clear. Even if Horikita ultimately refused, I absolutely wasn’t going to join. That would be more than a small favor, and it would have a huge impact on my life. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">I’m sure I could install Karuizawa in my place as a puppet, if it came to that. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She wasn’t the best candidate for the job, though. I needed someone who would follow my orders without question, who was talented enough to join the student council on their own merits, and whose presence wouldn’t seem odd. Hardly anyone in our class cleared all three hurdles. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. I think you’d do quite well if you joined, Ayanokouji-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I should be the one saying that to you, Hirata. You’re student-council material.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not a good fit. Besides, I don’t want to quit my club.” He liked soccer that much, apparently. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If Hirata </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">did</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> join the student council, I’d have another card to play. I wasn’t going to press him any further, though. I was still happy in the outfield, catching whatever came my way.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, student-council issues aside, we’ll probably have a tough time starting next month, won’t we?” Hirata asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You mean, because the school will bump us up to Class C?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. A and B will be wary of us, and the new Class D will try to trip us up. If we make a wrong move, we could be Class D again as early as February.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was natural to be apprehensive. Class point totals rose and fell all the time. Even a trivial mistake could make Hirata’s fears come true.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I do think everyone wants to reach Class A, though,” Hirata said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you think they’ll still feel that way if it takes backbreaking work?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s exactly the problem. Aiming for the top requires a lot of the class.” Just as Hirata was about to say something else, the girls called us.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry to have kept you waiting, Ayanokouji-kun!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Karuizawa and Satou were back, halting my conversation with Hirata. Since the movie was about to begin, we headed into the auditorium together.</span></p>

<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">5.3</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;"> DIDN’T NORMALLY WATCH</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> animated films, but this movie exceeded my expectations. The animals were surprisingly expressive, and the story was moving, if simple. I left the theater with Satou, who tightly clutched the juice she’d bought.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That was really good!” she exclaimed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I couldn’t help but agree. I was just getting hungry, too—with perfect timing. Hirata and Karuizawa exited slightly after us, and we hurried to our lunch reservation. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">While we walked, Satou resumed our conversation. “Hey, um, Ayanokouji-kun. Do you mind if I ask you something that might sound kinda…insensitive?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Maybe because we’d both enjoyed the movie, Satou was walking closer to me than before. It wasn’t just physical closeness. Our emotional distance had closed by about a half step.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Fire away.” If I could answer, I would.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I have a question too!” Even though we were having separate conversations, Karuizawa butted in again. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata spoke up as well. “Why don’t we all take turns asking each other questions?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Hmm, not a bad idea.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> I could ask Hirata some things that’d been on my mind for a while.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sounds good! I’ll go first,” replied Karuizawa. She immediately looked at me. “Have you ever gone out with anyone before, Ayanokouji-kun?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata had asked me the same question. To be more precise, he hadn’t asked so much as discerned the answer. It was hard to believe this had been brought up twice in a single day. It wasn’t a comfortable topic, but Karuizawa and Satou both fixed their gazes on me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Right now, no.” I tried to keep it vague. It felt like Karuizawa was just messing with me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In other words, your age equals the number of years you haven’t had a girlfriend,” replied Karuizawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Well, that was brusque.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You know, Ayanokouji-kun, that’s just the kind of evasive answer an unpopular guy would give,” she added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Really? Even if I had a girlfriend in the past, I don’t have one right now,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you had one, then?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well…no.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“See, I told you!” Karuizawa exclaimed. She bounced up and down. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Satou seemed glad, too. “I don’t think that’s bad, though. Like, if you were obviously unpopular like Yamauchi-kun or Onizuka-kun, that’d be one thing. But it’s more like you’re just not in a rush. That’s all. Right, Ayanokouji-kun?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You understand Ayanokouji-kun pretty well, Satou-san.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I…wish I understood him, but I still don’t really know much about him at all. That’s why I’d like to ask him some questions. Hey, um, Ayanokouji-kun. Which do you like better? Girls with long hair, or girls with short hair?” Satou asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Fairly straightforward questions: the type of girl I liked, whether I had a girlfriend, and now, what kind of hairstyle I preferred. Together, these questions seemed designed to construct a specific girl’s image.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t really care. As long as it suits the girl, it doesn’t really matter,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s a boring answer,” Karuizawa shot back. She loved pointing out my failings.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I feel the same way. Whether it’s a guy or girl, if it suits them, it’s not an issue,” replied Hirata, swooping in with the assist.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was kind of fun hanging out in a group I didn’t normally spend time with—but exhausting at the same time. Doing this every day would’ve been extremely tiring.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“To be honest, if it were any other time, I would’ve refused.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I’m sure the mystery caller found this baffling. “There’s a secluded spot next to the school building,” they said. “Meet me there in ten minutes.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry, but I’ve got a little business to take care of. Is it okay if we meet in twenty?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Fine.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The call ended. It wouldn’t take longer than five minutes to reach the meeting spot, but I’d given myself a little time buffer. That meant I should conclude my business in the next fifteen minutes. Someone was waiting for me, and it was cold out here.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you stay there, you’ll freeze to death,” I said to the person in the bushes. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">No one answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I have to go. Is it okay if I leave you here?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Finally, a voice answered hesitantly, “When did you notice me?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Right at the start. You heard Satou confess her feelings, right, Karuizawa?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“N-not really. I only heard a little.” Karuizawa stood up from her hiding place. Since she’d hidden in the bushes, snow had fallen on her shoulders. “Brr…it’s cold.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What happened to Hirata?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Dunno. He probably went home.” She stepped onto the path, brushing the dirt and snow off herself. She’d waited in the cold so long that her nose was red.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s freezing, huh?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I guess.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Still acting tough, then. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, something was bothering Karuizawa more than the frigid night. “Anyway, why did you turn Satou-san down?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do you mean? You said it yourself, didn’t you? It’s despicable to go out with someone you don’t actually have feelings for.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, sure, but…don’t they say something like ‘if you don’t eat, you don’t get a toothpick’?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Uh, I think the expression you’re looking for is ‘not eating the meal set before him is a man’s shame.’ Like, it’s shameful to spurn a woman’s advances,” I replied. “Still, Satou’s a normal girl. She wants a normal romance. Do you really think I can give her that?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s…well, it’s kinda hard to picture.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Karuizawa understood me better than most people. She knew how much I’d actually have liked a normal life. Still, I couldn’t give Satou what she wanted. Even if I&#160;made myself date her, I’d waste her time. These few years at school were a precious resource.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Look, it might not be my place to say, but don’t you think you’re being a little too humble?” asked Karuizawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Humble?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I mean, yeah, you’re not like normal guys, Kiyotaka. On top of that, you pretend to be someone you’re not, right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I think it’s more that I choose not to reveal everything.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, yeah, some girls would probably be disappointed by who you really are. But when someone’s really in love, that kind of stuff doesn’t matter, y’know? I mean, I think Satou-san would’ve accepted you as you are,” said Karuizawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is that what you meant by ‘humble’?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. Well, since you already turned her down, I suppose you can’t take it back. Even though I, like, went through all the trouble of shooting love’s arrow at you. I&#160;didn’t even miss; it just bounced right off you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Love’s arrow?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Forget it. It doesn’t matter.” She gave a devilish chuckle. “Girls get over stuff fast. Satou-san will probably fall for another guy soon, won’t she?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nothing I can do about that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Whoa. I think I hear some regret in your voice.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Knock it off. This was my decision.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Karuizawa seemed unconvinced. “Couldn’t you have tried going out with her to see what it’s like? I’m sure you were aware of her feelings for you. Inviting someone out on Christmas isn’t what someone who’s ‘just your friend’ does. When you accepted the invitation, didn’t you know what she wanted?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Isn’t it possible that going on the date allowed me to determine that I’m not compatible with Satou?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s… Well, sure. But, from what I saw, things looked like they were going well. You seemed to be having a lot of fun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“To be perfectly honest, I’m not entirely opposed to the idea of dating Satou.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“S-see? I knew it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I probably could’ve had some good times with her,” I&#160;said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Karuizawa looked angry now. “What do you mean, ‘good times’?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I mean, exploring things. All the way. You know.” I tried to convey the idea as mildly as I could. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Karuizawa understood what I was getting at. “Huh?! Y-you mean, you would’ve gone out with her for such a gross reason?!” she shouted.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Haven’t you ever wanted to do it?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I-I don’t know! I don’t know anything about that kind of stuff! It’s a whole different world to me!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And you’ve never thought about exploring that unknown world?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s…that’s, well… Doesn’t it matter who your partner is?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I guess you wouldn’t just want to do it with anyone.” Ideally, you’d want your partner to be someone you genuinely cared for.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Obviously!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But that’s what I’m saying—I’d be okay with that person being Satou.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“W-well, why’d you reject her, then?! You could’ve experienced this whole ‘unknown world’!” Karuizawa barked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, don’t get mad.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not mad!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She most definitely was. I had a pretty good idea of why, too.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If I chose to date Satou, would you still be standing here with me?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s why I didn’t say yes to her.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If I’d started going out with Satou, school would’ve been better than ever. I’d have had a partner to share my joys and sorrows with, and our relationship would’ve deepened with time. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But I knew that dating Satou would affect Karuizawa. Choosing Satou would’ve meant that it’d be harder to work with Karuizawa. Karuizawa would’ve become warier of me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The rooftop incident had been a turning point for Karuizawa. Her trust in me grew exponentially, and I knew now that she’d never betray me. If Ryuuen, or Sakayanagi, or even someone like Nagumo approached her, she wouldn’t crumble. The only thing that’d break her would be my dating another girl.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She’d be scared that I didn’t need her anymore. She’d panic. She’d become useless to me, and I didn’t want that to happen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Now, if Satou was a good replacement for Karuizawa, that’d be an entirely different story. But after today, I knew that Satou couldn’t fill Karuizawa’s shoes. She wasn’t as charismatic as Karuizawa, or as good at thinking on her feet. The double date made that abundantly clear. Karuizawa had adeptly played it off as a coincidence, while Satou struggled to keep up the lie, looking visibly upset at times.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The confrontation with Nagumo had clinched it. Karuizawa took action to defuse the situation, while Satou just stood by. That boldness was important. I could ignore the student-council issue, but I couldn’t ignore Sakayanagi or my father. If either of those two really went to war, it’d jeopardize my life here. Until I eliminated those threats, I needed Karuizawa. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">On top of that, there was Chabashira-sensei and Chairman Sakayanagi. I didn’t sense trouble at the moment, but they were still potential targets. As such, Karuizawa Kei was indispensable. The chairman had power over us students, but I might be able to take him down if I used Karuizawa as a honeypot. She’d probably balk at sexual stuff, but she was still highly versatile.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Maybe I’m just crazy, but I feel like you only view people as tools, don’t you, Kiyotaka?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I’d used Karuizawa as a tool too many times to deny it. “I don’t view people like that because I choose to.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, um, this might sound naive, but…have you ever actually fallen in love with anyone?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Right now, no.” I’d have liked to fall in love with somebody. But the opportunity hadn’t presented itself. Maybe I was incapable of love. Though I understood the biological differences between men and women, everything else was alien to me. My time in the White Room had seen to that. “In the end…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Uh, nothing.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I’d left the White Room physically, but some part of me would always be stuck there, where we lived in a state of constant self-defense. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">You shouldn’t need your guard up at all times in normal, everyday life.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> Going out with Satou would’ve let me experience normal joys, a normal relationship…but I couldn’t visualize what “normal” might look like for me yet. I’d worked to protect myself against any opponent who might arise; I didn’t know how to stop. No one else mattered, so long as I won.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Maybe I’d be this way until the day I died. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As I walked, Karuizawa followed. She didn’t walk at my side, but stayed close enough that we could converse. This way, no one would know we were together if they passed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ugh. I put in all that effort for Satou-san’s sake, and it ended up being a waste of time,” she complained. Her tone was so playfully bratty that you’d never have thought she suffered a traumatic experience just a few days before.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re doing pretty well, considering everything that happened to you,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s been years since I was bullied like that,” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You said it started in elementary school, right?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Uh, yeah. That’s right. Sorry, Kiyotaka. I lied a little bit about my past.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You lied?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I told Yousuke-kun that I was bullied for nine years. That was a lie. I thought that saying I’d been bullied since elementary would make him want to save me more than if it’d just been since junior high. I mean, he wouldn’t want me to be the victim of even more constant bullying,” she explained. She chuckled and stuck out her tongue playfully. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ah, so she lied to manipulate Hirata. That just proved her resourcefulness and determination.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If anyone could stop that incredible student council president, it’s you.”</span></p>
