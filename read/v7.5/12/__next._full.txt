<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 6:<br/>Where the Arrow Landed</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">C</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HRISTMAS DAY</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">. Students were on their way back to the dorms from club activities. It was unlikely that anyone would see me, even a teacher. There weren’t enough lights on in the building to attract attention.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s cold. They’re not here?” asked Karuizawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“They’re late,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was twenty minutes past the agreed-upon time. No one was nearby.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“They’re this late after calling </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">you</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> out here? Talk about rude,” said Karuizawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Maybe they’re nearby and scoping us out,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What? That’s unfair, isn’t it? They confirm your identity, then head back?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sure they’d like to, but they probably can’t afford to do that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was almost certain that this person would contact me. I’d brought Karuizawa to add some camouflage. I’d look odd if I showed up alone in an isolated spot, but today was Christmas. Karuizawa and I could pass as a couple. Even if the mystery person tried calling me again via a blocked number, my phone was turned off. The only way for them to be certain of my identity was by talking to me directly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As Karuizawa and I waited patiently, a lone student approached us. I’d seen him before. The moment our eyes met, I understood. He was definitely not who I expected.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He didn’t say my name. “Sorry to keep you waiting.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We just got here, Vice President Kiriyama.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">His eyes widened in surprise. “It seems you’ve gathered some information on the student council already. Your name’s…Ayanokouji. Right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It wasn’t strange that Vice President Kiriyama remembered my name. He’d stood nearby when I spoke with Nagumo earlier today.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I never imagined that the vice president, of all people, would want to take on President Nagumo,” I remarked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Before we get into that, let me ask you something.” He looked at Karuizawa. “Who’s this? I don’t know her.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“She’s trustworthy. She’s my partner,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Karuizawa looked momentarily excited before she quickly regained her composure.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Trustworthy, huh? Well, why not? This situation’s already hopeless if I have to turn to a first year for help,” Kiriyama said. He’d accepted Karuizawa’s presence very easily. Either he was that dissatisfied with Nagumo’s regime, or he trusted Horikita’s brother that much. “Can we cut to the chase? I don’t want to draw this out.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Same. I’ll catch cold if I stay here much longer.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ve never agreed with Nagumo once. I joined the student council because I admired Horikita-senpai. He was my senpai from Class A. Well, he used to be, I suppose.” Kiriyama’s defeat at Nagumo’s hands had knocked him down to Class B. Frankly, I was surprised Nagumo had kept him on as vice president. “I wanted to stop Nagumo from becoming student council president, but it was impossible. It’s beyond the scope of my abilities.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The story about President Nagumo bringing all the second-year students under his control… How much of that is true?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Almost all of it. There are those dissatisfied with the situation, but not enough to vote Nagumo out. They’ve resigned themselves to his rule,” Kiriyama replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, Kiyotaka. I understand uniting your own class, but how could you possibly make </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">other</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> classes your allies? Isn’t everyone competing to reach Class A?” asked Karuizawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sure Vice President Kiriyama will explain.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nagumo promised reform,” Kiriyama told her. “He said capable students would be promoted to Class A, no matter where they’d started. There are a lot of dissatisfied people at this school who feel they were placed in the wrong class.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Karuizawa appeared slightly confused. I clarified. “He means people like Horikita and Yukimura.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. But that wouldn’t be enough, right?” Karuizawa asked. “I mean, most of the students from the lower classes aren’t that talented.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nagumo says that every student will be given a chance,” explained Kiriyama. “I don’t have any more concrete details yet.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Isn’t that kinda suspicious?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, but those are his terms. Anyone in Class B or below already feels the pressure mounting. The gap in class points between Class A and everyone else is widening.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Shouldn’t you be seizing the opportunity to return to Class A, Vice President Kiriyama?” I asked. “I mean, you oppose the president and lose, that’ll never happen, right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If I believed there was really a chance Nagumo’s idea could work, sure. But I don’t think he’ll actually give everyone a fair chance at climbing the ranks. There’s just no way. He can’t guarantee that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Didn’t you think about resigning from the student council when Nagumo became president? I mean, no one wants to work under somebody they oppose, right?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If I left, what then? Nagumo would be even more free to wreak havoc. I thought it best to infiltrate his inner circle and collect information…try to find an opening I could exploit. If I hand that information to Horikita-senpai, I’m sure he can do something with it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though Kiriyama spoke in a detached, matter-of-fact way, frustration seeped into his words. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you understand how hard this is for me? Having to stand by and grit my teeth, knowing that if I can’t stop this, the school’s done for?” he asked. Unfortunately for him, I didn’t. “Well, I suppose there’s no way you would. There are no first years like Nagumo. But that doesn’t mean you’re safe, either. Nagumo’s watching Horikita-senpai and the third years for now, but once they graduate…the first years will be his next target.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ugh. He sounds like the worst,” said Karuizawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So she said…but I could see benefits to following Nagumo. If he could make former rivals fall so readily in line, he had to be both competent and persuasive.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, forget about fighting back or whatever. Wouldn’t it weird for us to poke our noses in student-council affairs?” asked Karuizawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Until now, yes,” replied Kiriyama. “But you’ll be seeing a lot more of the senior students from this point on. Once the third semester begins, the school holds a special exam that brings all three grade levels together. I went through the same thing last year. First, and second, and occasionally even third years battle it out.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In other words, our class would be rubbing shoulders with upperclassmen this January. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s also when Nagumo will start compiling his list of persons of interest among the first years,” added Kiriyama. Students that could potentially threaten Nagumo’s reign. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I would’ve preferred to go unnoticed. Unfortunately, I had a hunch that my wish wouldn’t be granted. “What happened in last year’s exam?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Probably nothing resembling this year’s exam. Most special exams are designed to be completely different from year to year.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even so, information about last year’s exam might prove advantageous.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Maybe. But I can’t give it to you. Even if Horikita-senpai endorsed you, I can’t break school rules. If anyone found out, I’d be expelled.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Made sense. If Kiriyama was part of Horikita’s faction, that meant he held school rules in high regard.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Our options for fighting back against Nagumo are limited. Expulsion’s the surefire route, but hard to achieve lucky. The next-best option would be revealing to everyone that he’s unfit to serve as student council president. If Nagumo is removed from office, many second-year students will cut ties with him. That’d mean no harm will come to you first years, or to the incoming freshmen starting next year,” explained Kiriyama.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The problem here was that I didn’t know the </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">true</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> Nagumo Miyabi. Even Karuizawa couldn’t get me that information; we just had so little interaction with the seniors. Because Nagumo had accrued so much influence, was incredibly wary of others, and had Hirata’s respect and envy, I could only conclude that he was no average student.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I would’ve preferred Kiriyama to find second-year students who shared his opinion to help him take Nagumo down. Sadly, he didn’t have that option, which meant he had to hassle first years.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait. Getting Nagumo expelled, or pulled out of office—that’s pretty serious, isn’t it?” asked Karuizawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You wouldn’t resort to such measures in the face of a mortal enemy?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ve never even thought of doing something like that.” Karuizawa looked suspicious, but I ignored her. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t know how much I could trust Kiriyama. From what I saw, there was no doubt that he hated Nagumo. But I also saw that he was being very careful with his words, maybe to manipulate me. At present, I lacked enough information to reach a definite conclusion.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I’d only shown him one card I held: Karuizawa. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Tell us what you want,” I said. “We’ll decide how to go about it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you can’t trust me, is that it?” Kiriyama asked. “Maybe I sound like I’m going too far. I don’t need to be responsible for stopping Nagumo. But I can’t bear seeing my juniors go through the same hell I did. That’s the truth.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So, he was just doing this for his juniors, huh? I found that selflessness hard to believe. Honestly, if Kiriyama said that he hoped to get back to Class A by eliminating Nagumo, I’d have trusted him more. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Guess it’s just human nature to play the saint, huh?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Think whatever you want,” Kiriyama added, “but just remember this. Every student who got on Nagumo’s bad side has been expelled. Across the board.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If that’s the case, maybe I shouldn’t get on his bad side, either.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you won’t cooperate?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I will. I have reasons I can’t back down either.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“All right. You’re on Nagumo’s radar now, anyway. Sorry, but you’re about to find out just what kind of person he is. I’ll keep you informed on his movements from here on. As long as that’s within the scope of the school rules, of course. Beyond that, you can do as you please.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So, he was putting his fate in my hands? </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kiriyama seemed to sense that I wasn’t as enthusiastic as he might have hoped. “To be perfectly honest, you’re kind of unreadable to me. If it weren’t for that relay race with Horikita-senpai, I probably wouldn’t ask for your help at all. In fact, the relay was what made Nagumo notice you,” he said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Had I known about Nagumo in advance, I wouldn’t have drawn attention to myself during the relay. Unfortunately, what was done was done.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If I think you’re incapable of seeing this through, I’ll stop contacting you,” Kiriyama added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wouldn’t that put you in more danger, Kiriyama-senpai?” asked Karuizawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He silently nodded. “I won’t make direct contact with you after this. I’ll create a random e-mail account and get in touch through that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Good.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> Communicating via burner e-mail accounts was the safest option. </span></p>
