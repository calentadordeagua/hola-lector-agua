<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 3:<br/>Ibuki Mio’s Disastrous Day</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">T WAS THE MORNING </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">of December twenty-third, two days before my Christmas date with Satou, when I went alone to Keyaki Mall with a specific purpose. I walked briskly into a store and looked around for something I needed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ve never used this stuff before,” I muttered. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After doing some online research, reading reviews, and asking a salesperson for their thoughts, I chose two of the item in question. The clerk put them in a small paper bag, and I paid. Astonished at how expensive they were, I left the store with the bag in hand and headed back toward the dormitory. All I needed to do now was pick up a few things at the convenience store, and then my plans would be in place. Afterward, I’d go to Keyaki Mall and watch a movie nearing the end of its theatrical run. That was my plan for the&#160;day.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, my plan began to fall apart when I ran into a certain someone.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Before I reached the mall exit, a girl approached me slowly. She walked with a cane. “Good day to you, Ayanokouji-kun.” It was First Year Class A student Sakayanagi Arisu, the school chairman’s daughter, who knew my history with the White Room.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The school grounds appeared vast, but the spaces students could actually access were relatively compressed. You could bump into just about anyone when you were&#160;out.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re out and about early today, huh? Alone,” I said. Sakayanagi usually had an entourage hanging around, but they were absent.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I came to spend time with Masumi-san, but she’s not here yet,” Sakayanagi answered. She noticed the bag I carried. “Are you not feeling well?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, no, I’m completely fine. Healthy as a horse.” I&#160;spread my arms wide, showing her that I was okay, then put the small paper bag in my pocket.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m glad to hear that. If you aren’t busy, would you like to hang out for a bit?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t even try to pretend I wanted to. “I’ll pass, thanks. You’re conspicuous, and I don’t like drawing attention,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Heh. That’s a shame.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">At any rate, Sakayanagi almost certainly didn’t enjoy my company. She just wanted a chance to taunt me. If she intended for people to know about my time in the White Room, she would’ve acted already. But she’d told no one, not even Ryuuen. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakayanagi probably planned to deal with me on her&#160;own.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Then I assume standing here and talking won’t be a problem?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You want to talk here? Any particular reason?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“He gets ever so angry when I call him this, but Dragon Boy was after you, wasn’t he? ‘The puppet master who controls Class D’ was how he described you, actually. Whatever happened there?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Only the people specifically involved should’ve known what happened on the rooftop, and how the matter resolved. Still, I wouldn’t have been surprised if she’d managed to get her hands on some information. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It seems that there’s been a falling out between the Class C students. Things have gotten quite bad. Were you aware of that?” Sakayanagi added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was. The story was that Class C had suffered an “internal dispute.” I was sure Sakayanagi had heard about that.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t really know the details,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Supposedly, Dragon Boy quarreled with his underlings. But, somehow, that story doesn’t ring true. I was certain that </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">you</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> were involved, Ayanokouji-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why would I get mixed up in that? You’re assuming I’m this ‘puppet master,’ right? I just think the whole thing’s surprising. I mean, I thought that Class C was pretty organized.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Class C’s ‘pretty organized,’ hmm?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, they’re an efficient unit, even if it’s because they suffer under a dictator,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. Well then, it seems you had nothing to do with the incident, Ayanokouji-kun. From what I can see, you don’t appear injured at all.” Sakayanagi watched my expressions and gestures closely, but she’d get nothing. “The story about Class C’s infighting may be true. However, Ryuuen seems no closer to moving in on Class D,” she added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Probably because there’re quite a few talented candidates for puppet master in Class D. Kouenji, in particular, is very capable,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. Well, Kouenji certainly seems like a suitable opponent for Dragon Boy. I suppose we’ll understand the truth of the matter once the third semester begins,” Sakayanagi said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Can we change the topic?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, of course.” Sakayanagi didn’t comment on the bluntness of my request.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“When I saw you a few days ago, I thought it was strange that you and Ichinose were getting along so well. I didn’t think you’d be that friendly with someone from another class,” I said. I remembered Sakayanagi and Ichinose walking around together; they’d appeared close. You didn’t spend a precious day off with someone if you didn’t get along.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Heh,” Sakayanagi chuckled. “Please, enough with the jokes. She and I aren’t friends, you know.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Meaning?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Actually, she thinks that you and </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> are close, Ayanokouji-kun.” Sakayanagi paused. “Since Class C seems so obsessed with Class D, I’ve gotten a little bit jealous. I like messing around with Class B to stave off boredom.” Toying with her opponents entertained her, apparently. “Once the third semester begins, will you play with me?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry, but no. Play with Horikita and the others if you want,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“She isn’t a suitable opponent for me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Then pick a fight with Ryuuen or the senior students. I’d rather you just ignore me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m afraid that’s impossible. I’d like to fight you as soon as I can, Ayanokouji-kun.” Sakayanagi wasn’t backing down. My humble act probably wouldn’t work on her. As long as she knew about the White Room, she wouldn’t give up.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If I just ignore you, what then?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you sure you can afford to do that? If you won’t be my opponent, Ayanokouji-kun, I’ll just have to find another. I’m afraid you can’t hold me responsible if Class B, with which you’ve had such a collaborative relationship, collapses.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you already selected your pawns, huh?” This conversation was starting to get entertaining.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Until you’re ready to play, Ayanokouji-kun, I’ll amuse myself with Class B. Who knows? If that whole class falls apart, maybe you and the rest of Class D can rise one&#160;rank.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wasn’t ready to assume that Sakayanagi actually meant any of this. This might’ve been a provocation, or just her way of having fun. But if she turned her sights from me to Ichinose, she might leave me in peace.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Can you really win against Ichinose and her class?” I&#160;asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Whatever do you mean?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Class B has worked together since school started, while the Class A students have dragged each other down. Even if you claim you’re more capable, your credibility’s questionable.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. You think that, because this is all hypothetical, you can go ahead and say whatever nasty things you like,” Sakayanagi said. Her calm exterior was crumbling a bit.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I added a little more fuel to the fire. “I’ve also discovered your true identity. You’re the school chairman’s daughter.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. How did you learn this information, I wonder?” Sakayanagi took the bait. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That doesn’t matter. What’s clear is that your father’s influence played some part in your assignment to Class A. I can’t say for certain that you were selected for that class based on merit alone. You can boast about how you’ll beat Ichinose, but I have trouble believing you.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was betting Sakayanagi didn’t like having people question her abilities. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Then how do you explain the fact that many people within my class support me?” she countered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Support from your class doesn’t necessarily say anything about your abilities. Even Ryuuen and Ichinose, whom you deem beneath you, have their respective classmates’ support. If we’re talking about Class D, then Hirata does, too. He’s the best example, even. The ability to bring people together doesn’t always indicate skill in other areas,” I reasoned. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakayanagi threw her cane to the ground. It clattered on the floor. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It seems I can’t use simple tricks against an opponent such as you,” she said. “Transparent ploys won’t work. My apologies for such rudeness. However, Ayanokouji-kun, do you think you might be getting a little arrogant? I think you’re intoxicated with your own success—the first success to come out of the White Room. Don’t you think?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I hadn’t thought about it that way before. If I had to describe myself as either a success or a failure, I’d undeniably be a success. If I weren’t, then that man…my father…wouldn’t be so obsessed with me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You seem mistaken about one thing, Ayanokouji-kun. You think the White Room made you remarkable, don’t you? Certainly, the amount of knowledge you’ve acquired since childhood is extraordinary. And, while you’ve attempted to conceal your abilities here at this school, I have no doubts regarding the extent of your academic and athletic prowess. However, this institution is where the ‘have-nots’ receive the means to become geniuses, if at all possible. One might say that natural-born geniuses have no need for such a place,” Sakayanagi said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was certainly what my father believed—that genetics were irrelevant, and greatness was a product of the education a person received from the moment of their birth. Controlling every aspect of how someone was raised, from their sleep schedule to what they ate, was how you created a superior individual. My father thought that this was the only way to produce the manpower that could take Japan into the future.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why’re you so hostile toward me?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Beating you, Ayanokouji-kun, will prove that no ordinary person can defeat a natural-born genius. No matter how much effort you expend, we’re different breeds. That’s my hypothesis,” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So, she didn’t doubt the fact of her genius, huh? </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">At that point, Kamuro appeared behind us. She must’ve been searching for Sakayanagi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, this is where you’ve been? You shouldn’t just have left the spot we agreed to meet at. You’re the one with bad legs, after all,” Kamuro told Sakayanagi. She had to have noticed me, but she didn’t make eye contact.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I apologize. I arrived early, so I felt like taking a little walk,” said Sakayanagi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, at least let me know ahead of time.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Now that Kamuro was here, our conversation was pretty much over. Sakayanagi had absolutely no interest in making my abilities common knowledge. She’d hate it if others started gunning for me and snatched her prey from her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This may come off as rather sudden, Masumi-san,” Sakayanagi said, “but what do you think of Ichinose Honami-san?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re right. That really </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">is</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> sudden.” Kamuro seemed puzzled. My presence likely made the question even more awkward.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You see, I was just discussing how to conquer Ichinose-san with this young man here,” said Sakayanagi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Conquer, huh? Well, if you ask me, Ichinose’s a model student. She really cares for others, and she’s good-natured. Is that what you mean?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. Her being a model student is clear, isn’t it? She always scores in the top percentile on tests, and she leads her class well. What do you think, Ayanokouji-kun?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I agree,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you think that defeating a model student like Ichinose-san would be simple, Masumi-san?” Sakayanagi asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’d be hard, wouldn’t it? Class B’s pretty united. They won’t crumble under outside pressure. Bribery won’t work against Ichinose. You could launch a frontal attack, but even if our own class cooperated, it’s doubtful we’d&#160;win.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Indeed. At first glance, it appears that conquering Ichinose-san would prove a difficult task.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“At first glance? You mean, that’s not the case?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No. Everyone has a weakness, even Ichinose-san. An Achilles heel,” Sakayanagi laughed. “The fact that she’s a model student, which you both acknowledge, is undeniable. However, can you say with certainty that her caring, good-natured temperament reflects her true self? Don’t you think there’s another side to her? That she looks down upon others, deep in her heart?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I dunno. I think most people are like that, at least a little. They may say nice things, but we really have no idea what’s going on in their heads. That’s not necessarily a bad thing, though. It’s perfectly reasonable for someone to act in their own self-interest. But I do think that Ichinose might be genuinely good-natured,” Kamuro reasoned. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She was right; we all had a secret self. Usually, that hidden personality wasn’t as extreme as Kushida’s, but all humans had a dark side. However, Ichinose Honami seemed to have no secret mean streak. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You don’t think she looks down upon others?” Sakayanagi continued.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No. She’s extremely kind—nice without rubbing it in your face.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you’re saying that she’s stupidly good-natured?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. Exactly.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakayanagi smiled. “In that case, you and Ichinose-san are very similar—aren’t you, Masumi-san?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? Whaddya mean? We’re totally different. Are you being sarcastic or something?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Not at all. You might be surprised by this assessment, but you and Ichinose-san are remarkably alike.” Kamuro seemed exasperated, but Sakayanagi continued. “I mean that Ichinose has the same problem you do, Masumi-san.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The same problem? What?” asked Kamuro.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakayanagi looked at me, seemingly to check whether I’d caught on. Having no idea what she was talking about, I shook my head.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t you understand? Your secret, the one in my keeping, is the same as hers. Well, the problems are the same, but they played out to different ends,” Sakayanagi said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kamuro looked as if she understood. “So, you’re saying that Ichinose did the same thing I did?” She sounded shocked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Surely such an occurrence isn’t that uncommon,” said Sakayanagi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Did Ichinose tell you that? Do you know for sure?” asked Kamuro. She was normally calm and composed, but this clearly shook her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Naturally. She told me about it in detail, in fact. She opened her secret heart to me. I merely performed a cold reading,” said Sakayanagi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Cold reading” was a conversational technique by which someone extracted information through careful observation and leading questions. Perhaps Sakayanagi had gathered data beforehand and gone in prepared. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Human beings lie to make themselves look better. You and Ichinose-san are just the tip of the iceberg. People really are interesting creatures. No matter how exceptional they are, they slip up so easily.” Sakayanagi gazed pointedly at me. “I’m afraid those are all the hints I can give you right now regarding Ichinose-san. I intend to thoroughly crush her. Hopefully, you can figure out why.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakayanagi seemed to want me to compete with her, but sadly, I wasn’t interested. She could rampage as much as she pleased. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well then, shall we be going, Masumi-san?” They started walking away. I left as well, passing them. As I did, Sakayanagi spoke once more. “You didn’t say anything, Masumi-san.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? About what?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You saw Ayanokouji-kun and I speaking together. We were discussing strategies. Even so, you asked no questions. Normally, you’d barrage me with queries, but…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wh-what are you talking about? I’m not interested, that’s all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is that so? You tend to blather on about anything you notice. Yet this time, you’re incurious. I wonder why that is?” Kamuro didn’t answer. Sakayanagi continued. “Could it be that you already possess information about Ayanokouji-kun? If so, I wonder where you obtained it. Is it possible you two had a meeting of which I’m unaware?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Like a bloodhound following a scent, Sakayanagi looked sharply at me. I neither spoke nor made eye contact. If she had a problem, let it be with Kamuro. “Heh,” she laughed. “Well, since I’m in quite a good mood today, I won’t press it further. Have a wonderful day, Ayanokouji-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">With that, Sakayanagi left with Kamuro in tow. Kamuro sure had it tough, being used like a pawn by Sakayanagi even during winter vacation. Whatever Sakayanagi had on her was probably bad. Maybe it </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">was</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> for the best that we’d had this conversation. The part about Ichinose and Kamuro having the same problem, in particular, interested me. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakayanagi would get nothing out of lying to me, but I similarly gained nothing by taking what she said at face value. If the truth got out, and Ichinose did fall from grace, well, that’d be fine.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Should I at least tell Horikita about this now? Hmm.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita was currently allying herself with Ichinose, and might back the girl. Personally, I thought it’d be better to just leave things alone. But the class leader got to call the shots, and that was currently Horikita. Maybe I’d contact her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Later. This wasn’t urgent. With Sakayanagi gone, I strolled to the dorm. I was back on track to fulfill my original goal—delivering the items I’d purchased. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, my objective was once again derailed. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As I approached Keyaki Mall’s exit, I passed a rather energetic-looking girl. She didn’t notice me, since she seemed to be in a hurry. She was almost running. She went into a store to join up with her friend, then disappeared. After watching her, I decided to forego heading back to the dormitory.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Guess I’ll go see a movie,” I said.</span></p>

<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">3.1</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;"> WENT TO THE MOVIES</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> fairly often on my days off. Some people might’ve considered spending points on a film to be a waste of money, but I thought it was important to have a variety of interests, and films were becoming something of a hobby of late. In addition to being a way to relax, they let me acquire new knowledge, exposing me to different ideas, cultures, and points of&#160;view. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That said, the movie I saw today wasn’t award-winning cinema. Nor was it the type of saccharine love story couples flocked to over the holidays. I was watching an action story with gunfights, focusing on a conflict between small-town gangsters.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Some days, I just wanted to turn my brain off and be entertained. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Since the film was about to leave theaters after a short run, it was by no means a masterpiece. It was a pathetic B&#160;movie at best. Thus, I’d been able to book a ticket online and get a good seat for any time I wanted. Still, I kept hesitating over whether I was actually going to go watch it. In the end, I decided to go anyway, since I was already out for another reason.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I went to the counter, where I chose which movie I was going to see and the showtime I wanted. I received a laminated sheet with a seating chart on it. I realized then that something was wrong. The seats toward the back of the auditorium, the ones I always chose when I went to the movies, were already full. In fact, there didn’t seem to be many free seats left.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Apparently, a popular film scheduled at the same time had been postponed, so a lot of people had decided to see this gangster movie. With Christmas approaching, most of the seats had been reserved in pairs. People probably thought, </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Well, better to watch something, rather than nothing.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> Or something like that.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I told the employee that the exact center of the front row would be good. Fortunately, a few seats were still available. Why were the seats at the ends of rows so popular? Did it have anything to do with couples? I didn’t really know the specific psychology of movie theaters.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Since I had about twenty minutes until the movie started, I killed time by hanging around a rack of pamphlets. With ten minutes to go, I entered the auditorium alone. Couples sparsely filled the seats behind me. In the center of the front row, I waited for the movie to begin. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The seats filled up relatively early. I kept looking at the screen. I quite enjoyed watching the previews, seeing what movies were coming soon to theaters. That was why I always made sure to sit down before the previews started. Watching them on the big screen excited me in a way the previews didn’t at home, in front of the TV.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The auditorium lights were still on as a commercial advertising a convenience store played onscreen. I watched scenes of someone stirring soft, plump rice with a spoon, then lightly toasting </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">nori </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">on a net, until finally, children ate the finished </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">onigiri.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As it got close to showtime, and the seats gradually filled up, I looked around. My row was mostly full now. There were couples to the right and left of me, leaving one seat open. Both couples were taking advantage of the theater’s dim lighting to hold hands. I guess couples went to see action movies too. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Since the seat directly to my left was still open, it’d probably remain empty. I mean, who else would do something like seeing a movie all alone on Christmas Eve? I put my phone on Do Not Disturb mode, then, to be extra cautious, turned it off.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As I did, the lights dimmed, and the previews played. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">This is where the fun begins.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> Just then, a shadow appeared on my left. A lone student sat down in the vacant seat. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">So, another odd person.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> Someone who’d see a movie all alone on Christmas Eve. Honestly, I admired their taste in movies. I turned to look at my fellow cinephile.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“………”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">My mouth fell open. The lone wolf was none other than Class C’s Ibuki Mio. The incident on the rooftop popped into my head, and I felt awkward. Fortunately, the auditorium was dark; Ibuki didn’t notice me. Her eyes focused only on the screen. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was a firm believer in staying all the way to the end of the credits, but if I lingered until then, the lights would come back up. That settled it. Today, I’d make my escape as soon as the credits rolled. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, I’d miscalculated. It was the problem that came up time and time again in movie theaters: the armrest.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If I were sitting at the end of the row, I definitely could’ve used both the left and right armrests, but other seats were a constant battle. As far as movie theater etiquette went, there was no official rule, but the early bird got the worm, so to speak. Since the couple to my right had already made use of the armrest, I’d thought the one to my left would be open. However, Ibuki casually placed her elbow on it. There was enough space on the armrest for two people, but then our arms would touch.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Maybe Ibuki thought about the same thing, because she looked to see who was sitting next to her. Naturally, our eyes met.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Geh!” She gave a disgusted grunt. The previews were silent at just that moment, so I heard it quite clearly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I thought that saying nothing would be weird, so responded with something simple. “What a coincidence, huh?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ibuki just looked away. She intended to ignore me, apparently. Well, that helped make things easier. I concentrated on the screen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When the movie started, however, I felt Ibuki gaze over at me on occasion. Maybe she was just curious about my presence, but she didn’t seem focused on the movie. I wanted to turn to her and say something like, “How about you watch the screen instead of me?” I&#160;couldn’t speak loudly in the auditorium, though. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Should I try whispering it into her ear? No, she might snap at me. I&#160;should just watch the movie, pretend not to notice, and put up with her.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> Fortunately, I’d been used to people monitoring me since childhood.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wouldn’t let myself look agitated. I’d just watch the movie. </span></p>
<div style="page-break-before: always;"></div>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span><img alt="" height="100%" id="ClassroomElite-Vol7-5_INTERIORIMAGES_2" src="/hola-lector-agua/images/books/v7.5/OEBPS/Images/ClassroomElite-Vol7-5_INTERIORIMAGES_2.jpg" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style=" top: 0px; vertical-align: top; height: 100%;"/></span></p>
<div style="page-break-before: always;"></div>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, the film itself wasn’t particularly good—truly a B movie. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Seriously, the plot’s so repetitive and dull. Shouldn’t it try something different?</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> Well, at least we were nearing the climax, with the protagonist about to ride into enemy territory and save the day. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Just before that thrilling edge-of-your-seat conclusion, though, the screen suddenly went black. At first, I thought it was an artistic decision on the filmmaker’s part. We all remained silent and kept watching. However, after ten or twenty seconds, neither the picture nor the sound had come back. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Just as I started to wonder what was up, an announcement came over the auditorium speakers.</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;"> “We sincerely apologize for the delay. The screening has been temporarily halted due to an equipment malfunction. We understand that this may be an inconvenience. Please bear with us until the problem has been addressed.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The students grumbled and complained at first, then settled down to chat quietly and wait.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Jeez. I really have no luck at all.” Ibuki sighed, seemingly directing her comment at me. Did she mean to imply that I’d caused the malfunction?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m pretty shocked, too. I mean, I never imagined you’d come see a movie today,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m free to decide when I see a movie, aren’t I?” she retorted. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Guess she didn’t like what I suggested. </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, the same goes for me,” I said. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You—” Ibuki opened her mouth to speak, but stammered. She glared before speaking again. “All this time, you’ve made fun of me. I can’t forgive you for that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I understood why Ibuki might be angry. Consoling her, or saying that that wasn’t true, wouldn’t work. I adopted the strategy that I thought was best. “That’s power, Ibuki.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh?” A disquieting air hung around us. She gave me a sharp glare full of irritation and bloodthirst. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I continued speaking. “If you have enough power to overcome your opponent, you win. Whether your opponent hid the true extent of their abilities doesn’t change that. If you could’ve stopped me, Ryuuen would’ve won. Or, at the very least, we could’ve ended in a draw.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I would’ve looked extremely lame if I’d been beaten up on that rooftop after taunting Ibuki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s…” Ibuki definitely couldn’t argue. One should rely on one’s own strength. Whether an opponent hid their abilities should’ve been a non-issue. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Besides, unlike Ryuuen and Sakayanagi, I’m not aiming for the upper classes,” I continued. “Naturally, since I don’t want to stand out, I don’t make it a point to show off what I can do. I only fought Ryuuen after weighing many other options. There was no getting around it. It’s never been my intention to mock or condescend to my opponents.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I really don’t like that.” No matter how logical my argument, it was difficult for Ibuki to accept. “You say you don’t want to stand out, but that’s bullshit. If you hadn’t done what you did back on the island and provoked Ryuuen, things wouldn’t’ve turned out this way. Even before that, if you let the whole thing with Sudou slide, that would’ve been it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You may be right.” If I’d let Sudou get expelled, Ibuki defeat Class D on the island, and Ryuuen game the cruise ship’s test, then Ryuuen wouldn’t have had it out for Class D the way he did. He probably would’ve turned his sights on Class B long ago.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You say one thing, then do another,” Ibuki accused. “You used your abilities even as you stayed hidden.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She turned back to the blank screen, probably figuring that any further conversation would be a waste of time. I&#160;decided to let things go. With any luck, the movie would resume soon, and this encounter would come to an end.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-before: always;"></div>
<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">3.2</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">H</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">OWEVER, MY PLAN</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> to leave as soon as the credits rolled went up in smoke. I waited and waited, but the movie didn’t start. Either the equipment malfunction was just that severe, or the workers were slow. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ibuki sighed repeatedly. Well, it was perfectly reasonable to sigh in this situation. I’d already started to lose interest in the movie.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, what do you think’s going to happen?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I couldn’t take the silence anymore. Since Ibuki still hadn’t left, she was probably curious about the end of the movie. Or maybe, since no one else was moving, she&#160;couldn’t get to the exit without crawling over everyone.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ibuki rested her cheek on her fist, her arm placed firmly on the armrest. She made no attempt to look in my direction. Her body language screamed, “You’re annoying, so don’t talk to me!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Maybe I should quit poking the beehive.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> When in the world was the movie going to start? </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">A few students here and there grew tired of waiting and left. I thought Ibuki would follow, but she showed no sign of getting up. Maybe she simply wanted to see the rest of the movie after all.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">For my part, I wanted to see how it ended. If I didn’t stick around, then coming here would’ve been meaningless. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Time to display my tenacity.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I turned my phone on and checked the time. About twenty minutes had passed since the movie stopped. The equipment malfunction was going to impact not just this screening, but the next one as well. Looking around, I saw that the number of people remaining had dwindled. Only a few audience members stayed, including Ibuki and me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">People who came alone to the movie might stick it out, but couples probably didn’t want to waste their time in here. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Guess they left before the amorous mood died.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You aren’t heading back?” asked Ibuki as I stared at my phone. Her face was turned away, and I couldn’t see her expression. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ve already watched 80 percent of the movie. I’m honestly curious about how it ends. Since we’ve been waiting twenty minutes now, it should be back up anytime.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you’re that curious, you could just look it up online,” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t really feel like reading someone else’s opinion,” I answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I used reviews as a guide to determine whether I wanted to watch a movie, not as a means of evaluating it. Besides, if reading one or two lines explaining the ending was enough to satisfy you, why would you go to a movie in the first place?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t care about this movie anymore. I just don’t want to leave before you do,” she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re very blunt.” Ibuki wasn’t going to win this game of chicken. I had no intention of getting up until the movie ended. That was an advantage a guy who had no Christmas Eve plans possessed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">A sad announcement definitively ended the battle. The faulty equipment couldn’t be fixed, so they’d decided to cancel the screening. The staff explained how refunds would be processed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I really have no luck,” Ibuki sighed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If I wanted to know what happened, I’d have to either wait until the movie was available to rent, or read spoilers online. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though the movie was canceled, Ibuki showed no sign of moving. I decided to just get up and leave. My business here was done.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-before: always;"></div>
<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">3.3</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">M</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">AYBE BECAUSE OF THE TENSION</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> I’d endured, my shoulders felt stiff. After unsettling interactions with Sakayanagi and Ibuki, I didn’t feel like taking any detours on my way back. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As I left the theater, a voice called out to me from behind. “Hey. Do you really think you can just go on hiding your identity like this?” It was Ibuki. She’d chased after me to ask that? </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Did you not listen to a word I said? Just keep what happened to yourself,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This isn’t a joke. All this time, you’ve been laughing at me,” she replied. She didn’t need to repeat that she couldn’t forgive me. It was written on her face. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, what’re you going to do about it? Spread the word about me?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No. After all, I wouldn’t be the only one in trouble if I did. Right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s right. Depending how things played out, it wouldn’t just affect the people who were on the rooftop. Manabe and her friends might get caught in the crossfire.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If school officials followed the series of events all the way back, they might trace things to me. However, I could come up with as many excuses as I needed to give them the runaround. The most they could possibly do was suspend me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Besides, this school’s built on student conflict. You’re barking up the wrong tree by blaming me,” I told Ibuki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I know that. It’s just, well…I can’t stand you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I contemplated Ibuki Mio. She’d probably studied martial arts since childhood. There was hardly any difference in strength between male and female bodies before adolescence, so with her skill, it would’ve been easy to overpower the opposite sex. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">With time and puberty, though, things got more complicated. You could categorize Ibuki as fairly strong for an average high-school student. There was no way a man without martial arts training could contend with her. But against a man with the same talent, one who’d trained at the same level or higher, she couldn’t win. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ibuki wasn’t ready to acknowledge that. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You went quiet. What’re you thinking about?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I was contemplating how I can resolve this matter amicably.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Unfortunately, I couldn’t come up with anything. No matter what I said, nothing would convince you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">For the first time today, the corners of Ibuki’s mouth twitched into a small smile. “You’re right. I won’t back down.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As I’d suspected, I might need to employ an all-out attack. “So, do you like movies?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh?” I understood why she looked stunned by the non sequitur. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, I daringly continued a normal conversation. “I mean, you’d go see a movie alone. And an obscure one at that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So? That’s my goal.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">What an odd response.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> “Goal?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“To see every movie they screen at this school. It’s not a big deal.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was surprising. Everyone at this school had goals—to make friends, to go out on days off, to graduate without being marked late or absent even once, to always place first on tests. Ibuki’s “see every movie” goal might’ve seemed simple by comparison, but it was actually difficult to achieve. Going to a movie you wanted to see was easy, but sitting through something that didn’t interest you was tiresome. Most people probably would’ve considered such a goal frivolous. However, establishing a goal and then seeing it through was hugely impressive.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you making fun of me?” Ibuki glared, interpreting my silence as a bad thing.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hmm. I wonder.” I could’ve complimented her, but I was afraid to. It’d probably be best if we parted ways before people noticed us “hanging out.” “So, what’re you up to now? Want to grab some tea?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t be a jerk. I’m leaving.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wasn’t surprised by the refusal. “Well, if you’re headed right, I’ll turn left. Have a nice day.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Trust me, I don’t want to spend a second more with you, either.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Truly, we were a match made in heaven. Ibuki immediately turned right. I went left. However, she was suddenly beside me, yanking my arm.</span></p>
