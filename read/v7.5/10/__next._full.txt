<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 4:<br/>How We Spend Our Time</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">T WAS THE TWENTY-FOURTH</span><span style=" font-size: 0.75em; vertical-align: super;"> </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">of December. Christmas Eve. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Today and tomorrow, even busy couples would go out of their way to spend time with one another. To most students, these were still ordinary days, but I was curious about how people would spend them.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I left my room before seven o’clock in the morning. I had separate engagements with two different people—one whom I’d asked to meet, and one who’d invited me out. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Bizarre.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When I left the dormitory, everything around me had turned completely white. It seemed like a true winter morning.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Snow sure piles up, huh?” I muttered. Nature was certainly incredible. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The snow fell heavily, but according to the forecast, it was supposed to stop around seven. Even though the temperature was almost the same as it was yesterday, the sight of such heavy snow made me feel colder. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">I should probably start wearing gloves and a scarf now. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Since it was before seven in the morning, most students were still asleep; the campus was empty as I approached a bench near Keyaki Mall. Clearing off some snow, I sat. Soon, a man appeared. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s rude to ask somebody to come out so early in the mornin’,” he spat. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ryuuen Kakeru, Class C’s leader—no, its former leader—glared sharply at me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I needed to meet you when I was sure no one else would be around.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s your problem. It’s got nothin’ to do with me.” True. I had more to lose being seen with Ryuuen than he did with me. “So, what do you need?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I thought we could make small talk.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hah. That’s a real funny joke for a real shitty morning.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Come to think of it, I saw you yesterday,” I said. “Plus Ishizaki and some other guys.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What, are you happy you stopped me from dropping out?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m impressed. Even though you’re all alone, you’re not hiding in your room and sulking.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I do whatever I want, wherever I want. Does it scare you to see me out? After all, you don’t know when I’m gonna take revenge.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And after you take revenge, I’ll regret that I didn’t get you expelled?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ryuuen kicked snow off the bench, then sat beside me.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-before: always;"></div>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span><img alt="" height="100%" id="ClassroomElite-Vol7-5_INTERIORIMAGES_3" src="/hola-lector-agua/images/books/v7.5/OEBPS/Images/ClassroomElite-Vol7-5_INTERIORIMAGES_3.jpg" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style=" top: 0px; vertical-align: top; height: 100%;"/></span></p>
<div style="page-break-before: always;"></div>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’d like you to hold off on that, if possible. I’d rather live peacefully. Fighting you again would just be a pain,” I told him. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Then don’t ask me to meet you! Don’t draw my attention back to you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I decided to dispense with the small talk and cut straight to the heart of the matter. If I dragged this out too long, Ryuuen would just get up and leave. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Regarding what happened on the roof the other day… I wanted to add something to that story,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Add something?” Ryuuen sounded wary. Analyzing his defeat wouldn’t be fun for him. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Still, I had to show him the truth he couldn’t face. “If you’d been alone up there, you could’ve stuck it out. You would’ve been able to keep fighting.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, Ibuki, Ishizaki, and Albert had been there. Their presence, and the knowledge that they’d share the blame for whatever happened, might’ve hastened Ryuuen’s decision. He surrendered after seeing that possibility down the road, not just because of what happened in the moment. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was a wise move. Of course, I’d manipulated him into it. But in terms of living up to my expectations, Ryuuen had a great deal of potential after all.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You really are a piece of work, huh?” Ryuuen snapped. “I’m amazed at how far you’ll go just to screw with people. I thought that was </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">my</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> specialty, but you put me outta business.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m only speaking the truth.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m guessing that this benefits you in some way, and that’s why you used Ishizaki and the others to stop me from dropping out?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Hmm.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> I’d hoped that Ryuuen might catch on by now, but it seemed that wasn’t happening.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you really think you can still make me your puppet?” he demanded.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Puppet? What do you mean?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t play dumb. I’m talking about using me to take down the other classes. Why else would you keep me at this school?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re a man who enjoys battle, aren’t you?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even if, for argument’s sake, I crushed Classes A and B—with you around, it’d be meaningless.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Quite a definitive statement. “What? Did I break you that badly after just one defeat?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Anger sparked in Ryuuen’s eyes. “You wanna go? Right here and now?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I said too much. Please forgive me.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If the whole incident on the roof hadn’t happened, Ryuuen probably would’ve punched me out. This guy didn’t know fear.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But he’d learned it since then.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even so, Ryuuen would likely have fought me right here and now if I pushed him enough. Still, he had to avoid dropping out or being expelled if he was ever going to mature.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We’ve already settled the score between us,” I said. “I&#160;won’t bring up the rooftop anymore after this. I promise this is the last time. So, let’s talk.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Of course, Ryuuen didn’t believe a word I said. “Talk is pointless. I can’t see what I get outta this. I’m leaving.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He stood up, looking angry.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You might get something useful,” I replied, stopping him in his tracks. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ryuuen sat back down without looking at me. He’d probably risen to draw something out of me. He had no intention of going home empty-handed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Interpret this however you like, but don’t you think these simple battles are getting boring?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ryuuen looked annoyed. “Simple battles, huh?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Class D beats C, then B, and finally A. Then, Horikita and the rest of us become Class A ourselves,” I said. “It sounds like the formula for a big-budget movie. But we needn’t adhere to such a formulaic structure, right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Real life didn’t conform to a narrative. We were free to strike Class A before going for Class B, or even to ally with our enemies, Class C.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Interestingly enough, it seems that Class A will attack Class B once the third semester begins. We could take Class A out with a single strike while they’re focused on that,” I told Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He suddenly looked interested. “How credible is this information?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’d say about fifty-fifty.” I had to consider whether Sakayanagi was bluffing. If I read her correctly, though, there was a ninety percent chance she’d told me the truth. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If your information’s solid, this’d be a good chance. But I thought you Class D folks had a whole non-aggression thing going on with Class B? Striking at Class A is all well and good, but they’d crush Class B in the meantime. Ichinose can’t beat Sakayanagi,” said Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t care who wins or loses. I don’t plan on getting involved.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you’re just gonna watch her burn, huh?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If Sakayanagi crushes Ichinose, that saves me the effort. It could clear Class D’s path to Class A. Besides, this’s Sakayanagi we’re talking about. It might be time I looked up what kind of infractions the school punishes with expulsion.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s a lot I don’t like about this. You have no ambition to rise. Don’t you want to stay invisible?” Ryuuen asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I do, but if people around me take their own actions…well, I’m fine with that. I’m not opposed to the idea of us rising to Class A easily,” I told him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you’re just gonna sit back and observe?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I need a problem taken care of. There’s someone rather troublesome in my class.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Kikyou, huh?” Ryuuen didn’t even need to think about it. “Yeah, she’s certainly giving you a lot of trouble. The way this school works, having an enemy on the inside’s real bad.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida was targeting Horikita at the moment, and causing enough problems in the process that she—rather than moving up to Class A—was my primary concern. Since I’d been a bit reckless during the incident on the rooftop, I couldn’t afford to make an enemy of Horikita Manabu, the former student council president. If Horikita Suzune, his sister, was expelled while he was still at this school, he’d show me no mercy. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wanted to avoid anything that endangered my school life’s tranquility.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Kikyou contacted me the other day, actually. She was askin’ when I’d attack. Unfortunately, I was engrossed in hunting you down, so I didn’t respond. Seems she hasn’t given up on getting Suzune expelled. She’s looking for the opportunity to pounce. Heh. She’s quite a girl,” said Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you’d made good use of Kushida, you could’ve dealt a heavy blow to my class.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“To Suzune or your class, sure. But Kikyou’s way too weak to take down someone like you. You don’t care about anything.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">True.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, what’re you planning?” Ryuuen demanded. “You can slow a cancer’s growth, but it’s not going away till it’s excised, y’know? That shit can spread.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">And then we’d all die. “I know that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh? Then let’s hear your plan, Ayanokouji. How’re you gonna end Kikyou?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do I need to answer that?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Whether I help you might depend on your reply.” A faint smile appeared on Ryuuen’s lips, but maybe his mouth was still tender where I’d hit him, because the smile immediately faded. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It’d gotten colder. Staying outside for too long right now wasn’t wise.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In the third semester, Class D will be promoted to Class C,” I said. “However, we’ll probably fall back down to D when I get Kushida Kikyou expelled.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Heh heh. Ha ha ha ha!” Ryuuen howled with laughter despite his pain. “You really are terrifying. You’ll sink your class if it means taking down your enemies, huh? I&#160;knew you had it in you, Ayanokouji.”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you think that I’m lying, why don’t you confirm it yourself?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re being really stubborn.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Stubborn or not, just contact him.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Um…do you know his phone number?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No. Don’t </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">you</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> know it? As his sister?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t know it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, how about we try contacting Tachibana-senpai?” asked Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Tachibana? My brother’s secretary?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. I’ve talked to Tachibana-senpai a lot. I know her contact information.” Just as I’d figured, Kushida was making friends in the most unexpected places.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you really okay with me calling him, Ayanokouji-kun? If it turns out you’re lying, the consequences will be severe.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do as you wish.” Horikita’s brother would recognize this as part of my strategy and cover for me; he’d say I was telling the truth.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thank you, senpai. Yes, I understand. Goodbye,” said Kushida, who’d just called Tachibana. She hung up and fiddled with her phone. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita’s phone buzzed. Kushida had forwarded the contact information. “Thank you, Kushida-san.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, you’re welcome.” Putting on a friendly face with Horikita had to be quite the struggle for Kushida. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita stared at her phone’s screen. You might’ve thought that she’d call immediately, but her fingers didn’t move. She clutched the phone with both hands.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Whew.” Horikita let out a deep breath. Being this nervous about calling a family member definitely wasn’t normal. “If this all turns out to be a lie, prepare yourself.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You don’t need to remind me,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">My clear confidence unsettled her. I could tell she suspected I might be telling the truth. Horikita mustered all her courage and pressed the call button. She held the phone to her ear. The person on the other end of the line must’ve picked up, because her face grew even more nervous.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Excuse me. I-It’s Horikita Suzune.” Horikita spoke formally, as if talking with a stranger. “I asked Tachibana-senpai for your contact information… Um, well, so I could call you, oniisan.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Wearing a confused, flustered look that seemed out of place on her face, Horikita asked her brother the all-important question about the student council. Though I couldn’t hear, he seemed to confirm my version of events.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. Th-thank you very much. Goodbye.” Horikita ended the call and gave me an intense glare.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I told you the truth, right? Why the angry face?” I&#160;asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why’re you the intermediary? That’s what baffles me.” Gosh, she was paranoid. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you joining the student council, Horikita-san?” asked Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, I’m not.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait a minute. Your brother just told you to join, didn’t he?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“He told me that joining would be good for me…but I can’t imagine that’s true.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even if I pushed Horikita, no good would come of it. At this point, I just wanted to stop giving Kushida information. “I understand. Well, I hope to talk to you later.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wouldn’t further conversation be a waste of time?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Probably.” I stood.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“See you later, Ayanokouji-kun.” When Kushida spoke so gently to me, I sensed that something was amiss.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-before: always;"></div>
<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">4.3</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">T WAS NOW TEN O’CLOCK </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">at night. Christmas Eve slipped away with every tick of the clock. Rather than go out and party with my guy friends, I stayed in and watched TV alone. A live broadcast showed people in Tokyo celebrating, all chock-full of Christmas spirit. I&#160;flipped through the channels, but every single program was Christmas-related. I did find a show ranking presents to give girls—though Christmas Eve was a little late to think about such things—and also a ranking of presents for children, but none of it looked particularly interesting.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I shut off the TV and turned on my computer, craving something non-Christmas-related. I scrolled through the news at random, noting accidents and incidents, articles on foreign athletes, and other miscellaneous stuff. Apart from it being almost Christmas, it was a day like any other day. Nothing ever really changed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">My doorbell rang. Not the lobby intercom—my actual doorbell.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Coming.” I made my way to the entrance. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“G-g-good evening, K-Kiyotaka-kun!” stammered a familiar voice as I unlocked the door.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What’s up, Airi? It’s pretty late.” It was already past ten, yet judging from her appearance, she’d just gotten back to the dorm. “Were you out? I thought the gathering wasn’t until tomorrow.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, that’s right. I was doing something different. I’ve been hanging out with Haruka-chan since two in the afternoon.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see.” That was a long time to hang out. “Did you have fun?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m a little tired, but yes.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m glad to hear that.” I probably didn’t need to worry about Airi. At the very least, she’d be safe with a member of our group.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I heard from Haruka-chan that you have something going on tomorrow, Kiyotaka-kun. That means you won’t be able to come with us.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Ah, right.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> I had indeed talked to Haruka about that. Hanging out with Airi was probably her way of “handling” things.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, I do have plans. Sorry I can’t join you,” I told Airi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, that’s perfectly okay. To tell you the truth, I planned to give you something tomorrow, but…” Airi handed me a package tied in a cute red ribbon. “Um, well…I hope…that’ll you like it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">A Christmas gift.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-before: always;"></div>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span><img alt="" height="100%" id="ClassroomElite-Vol7-5_INTERIORIMAGES_6" src="/hola-lector-agua/images/books/v7.5/OEBPS/Images/ClassroomElite-Vol7-5_INTERIORIMAGES_6.jpg" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style=" top: 0px; vertical-align: top; height: 100%;"/></span></p>
<div style="page-break-before: always;"></div>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“For me?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah! I-I, um, got a gift for everyone else too.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In that case, it was easy to accept. I took the present, wondering what was usually done in these situations. Should I open it now, or after Airi left?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As I agonized, Airi spoke. “I-I don’t mind if you open it now.” Well, that answered that question. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Inside the package, I found warm-looking gloves. “I’ve thought for a little while that you could use gloves, Kiyotaka-kun. You don’t have a pair, do you?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I was thinking of buying some. Thank you, Airi.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hee hee hee! You’re welcome,” she answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The plain-blue gloves were simple in design, much more to my taste than the patterned ones I saw other students wear. I put them on right away—the first time I’d ever worn gloves, though I didn’t say that, of course. After putting on the left glove, then the right, I flexed my hands over and over to get used to them. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Airi happily watched me. “H-how do they feel?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“They’re the perfect size, and warm.” If I’d bought gloves for myself, I would’ve picked these.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m glad,” said Airi. “Well, uh, sorry for dropping by so late. Good night, Kiyotaka-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Airi turned to leave. I wouldn’t have minded getting her a cup of tea or something, but it was pretty late. On top of that, having a girl in my room on Christmas Eve might’ve gotten me in trouble. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As Airi headed for the elevator, she glanced back once, perhaps because she felt me looking at her. She gave a tiny wave, then hopped on the elevator. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After seeing her off, I went back inside. “When would be a good time to show my gratitude?” I muttered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If you received a present on Valentine’s Day, you gave one back on White Day. I knew that much. What about Christmas gifts, though? I’d have to look that up later.</span></p>
