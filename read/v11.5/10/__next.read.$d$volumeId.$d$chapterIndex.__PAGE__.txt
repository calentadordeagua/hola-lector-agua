<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 4:<br/>Lost Lamb</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">S</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">PRING BREAK WAS HERE</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> and April was just about to begin. Today was 30th March. I hadn’t really done much of anything the past few days, other than spend most of that time in my room and just enjoy my time off. I thought I might be able to just take it easy and welcome the start of the new school year like this, but…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When I woke that morning, just before eight o’clock, I saw that I had a message. The sender was a student from first-year Class B. Ichinose Honami. The message asked if we could meet up somewhere during spring break. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It seemed the remainder of my vacation wasn’t going to be passing by so quietly after all. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It sounded like Ichinose was fine with any day and time, but she wanted Horikita to come along too, if at all possible. From that part of the message, I guessed that Horikita was probably the main person she wanted to see, and I was nothing more than a bonus. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I could make a rough guess at what the conversation would be about—our final exam of our first year: the Event Selection Exam. Ichinose was bound to have gathered some information about what happened with our class, but I was betting she wanted to know more about our three wins and four losses against Class A. In addition to that, I was guessing she wanted to talk about becoming second-year students. More specifically about the alliance relationship that my class had with her. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Would that relationship continue? Or would it be dissolved? I’m sure Ichinose wanted to sort that out. Either option seemed equally likely at the moment, but the latter, in particular, was something best discussed thoroughly during our spring break.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Has Ichinose recovered? Or not?” I asked myself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I thought about the girl who I hadn’t seen even once since spring break had started. I was guessing the results of the year-end exam probably continued to weigh on Ichinose’s mind. Two wins and five losses. It was a crushing defeat for Class B. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though my own class had been demoted back down to D, the gap in points between classes was certainly shrinking. It now seemed quite likely that a single special exam would change the ranks of the classes. It wasn’t an exaggeration to say that Class D and C were now almost neck and neck with Class B. Given the situation, a discussion of what to do from here on out was necessary, sooner or later. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The alliance we’d entered into in the early stages of our first year was by no means a bad thing. If we were to keep this vaguely cooperative relationship into next year, it would lessen the mental burden on us. But we could also see that, in the near future, it was possible that this relationship could come to hinder both classes. And when the time came that the relationship grew strained, we’d probably have to dissolve it by force. And something like that would generally be called “dishonest.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">At any rate, to clarify what was happening, both the lower and upper classes would need to come up with policies to direct what they were going to do in the future.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If Horikita knew about Ichinose’s approach, then she was probably thinking the same thing I was. This wasn’t going to be a simple conversation. It would likely be a major point of divergence for both of our classes, which would predict where we went in the future. Even if Ichinose wasn’t in a state where she could think that far ahead, there was a good chance that Horikita would bring it up herself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">What I </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">could</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> say right now was that turning down this discussion was not an option. Which left the issue of timing. Today wasn’t a problem for me, but I had to wonder about Horikita. According to what her brother had said, he’d be leaving school on the thirty-first. I was sure that, deep down, Horikita wanted to talk to him in the little time that remained. It wouldn’t be surprising if they wanted family time, with no one else around, just for today. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As for whether Horikita’s brother would allow that, though—and whether Horikita could actually go through with meeting him—that was a different matter. For the time being, I figured I’d send Horikita a text.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">While I was at it, I added a note, asking if she’d gotten a chance to actually sit down and talk with her brother. Other than that, I simply told her that Ichinose wanted to meet up. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">A few seconds later, I saw that the message had been read. The reply came shortly after.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m fine with whenever.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was her response. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Well, okay, no, ‘whenever’ is </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">not</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;"> fine.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> As I commented mentally on the text she’d just sent me, I wondered what kind of response I’d get from her if I set the date for our meeting to be tomorrow, the thirty-first. But I supposed she wouldn’t be pleased to have me deliberately bring up something I knew bothered her. That was clear from the fact that she had completely ignored the subject of her brother in her reply.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, how about April 2?”</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I took the situation with her brother into consideration, deciding to leave today and tomorrow out of the equation. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m free today.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Her response back to me was immediate. And it had this intense feeling behind it, like she was telling me to mind my own business. Even if coming right out and saying she honestly wanted to spend time with her brother would’ve been difficult for her, she could’ve simply said she already had plans. Also, even if I told her that I had plans for today, it would probably be hard to convince her that was true.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay. I’d definitely want to get troublesome stuff out of the way quickly.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It would be tiring to push back against what Horikita wanted right now, so I figured I’d go along with it. Even if it was afternoon by the time our discussion ended, that would still leave plenty of time for her to meet up with her brother.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Yeah, that’s probably impossible,” I said to myself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It seemed unlikely those two were going to meet privately, except for when they planned to say their goodbyes tomorrow. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I sent my reply to Horikita, deciding to commit to meeting with Ichinose today. Afterward, in talking with Ichinose, we settled on a meeting time of ten o’clock, at the café on the second floor of the Keyaki Mall.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">T MUST HAVE BEEN</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> because April was just around the corner, but the temperature was gradually getting warmer. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There was still some time before we were to meet. I casually set out for Keyaki Mall and hit the button for the elevator, passing all kinds of students outside on vacation on the way. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But more and more of the graduates were leaving by the day, and now hardly any of them seemed to be around anymore. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…You again…” she huffed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The girl, who addressed me in such a displeased tone and then put some distance between us, was a student of first-year Class D. Ibuki Mio. For some reason, I found myself imagining what Ibuki had been doing during the long break. I was sure she was wondering the same of me. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Anyway, since we were in an elevator together, you could say that we were in a private space. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We’re on break. It’s not that strange for us to bump into each other now and again, is it?” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, no, but… I just don’t want to have anything to do with you anymore.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I know.”.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It seemed like she hadn’t been too pleased when she came to my room last time, either. If Ishizaki hadn’t forced her to accompany him, she probably wouldn’t have come. But even though Ibuki hated me, she’d still come along and helped for Ryuuen’s sake. That alone was proof that she felt that Ryuuen was an essential part of her class. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t really have the choice of not getting on the elevator, so I got on with Ibuki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s not going to break down again, is it…?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Now that you mention it, that did happen before, didn’t it?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It had happened during summer vacation, if I recall. Ibuki and I had gotten stuck in an elevator together. Although we were both wary about it happening again, that was obviously too much of a coincidence. The elevator reached the first-floor lobby and Ibuki immediately got off. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Apparently, she was also heading toward Keyaki Mall.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You don’t mind? Keeping pace with me, I mean.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She could have simply broken into a run if she wanted to get away from me right away.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why should I need to do anything different? Why don’t </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">you</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> just hurry up and start going faster instead?” she snapped. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Although she hated being around me, she apparently couldn’t stand the idea of backing down herself. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That being said, it would be weird for me to run just to get away from her. It would just be a waste of energy. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In the end, neither one of us backed down. We continued walking along, keeping a similar place. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">My destination was only about five minutes from the dormitory. We would be parting ways soon.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m glad that Ryuuen came back.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ugh, shut up. Just shut up. Don’t talk to me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wasn’t even allowed to make small talk. I supposed that I would hold back from saying anything unnecessary. We continued to walk alongside each other, me feeling like I was treading on eggshells the whole time.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yo, Ibuki! Wait up!” shouted Ishizaki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ugh, </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">shut up</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">! Don’t shout so loud when you’re so close,” snapped Ibuki.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When Ibuki heard him say those words, she immediately kicked the back of his knee.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ow! Hey, what the hell?!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You know full well why you got kicked. Besides, it’s awkward being around you. Get lost,” said Ibuki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What? Come on, it’s fine, ain’t it? I mean, we planned to meet up later anyway,” said Ishizaki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Apparently, they’d made plans to meet up at the Keyaki Mall.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m assuming Ryuuen is going to be there too?” I&#160;asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, he is—I mean, no… Uh…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After I had casually asked Ishizaki if Ryuuen would be there, he had inadvertently let the answer slip.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Moron.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It seemed these two had planned to meet up at Keyaki Mall separately, for whatever reason. It wasn’t hard to deduce as much, considering how strongly Ishizaki reacted when he heard Ryuuen’s name. They must have been planning to meet in secret.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“W-well, whatever, it doesn’t matter, does it? There ain’t no point trying to hide it from Ayanokouji.” Ishizaki got defensive, but Ibuki’s expression remained stern. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">does</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> matter. Because in the end, if we don’t defeat him, then we’ll never get to the top,” she Ibuki.</span></p>

If anything, I’d imagine he would resent me for it.</span></p>

If I did take this offer and join Ryuuen’s class, it was possible Albert might try to fight me again. Ishizaki had clearly come up with this idea all alone, and he was the only one who seemed to be getting increasingly excited about it. While I was grateful for his appreciation, I decided to give him a serious answer.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s not going to happen. What would you do about the twenty million points necessary for the class transfer? That’s the basic requirement.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though they had beaten Class B in the year-end exam, there was no way they had saved up that many points.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, y’know. Ryuuen-san would come up with something,” said Ishizaki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, there’s no way he’d do anything,” said Ibuki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You think? I think Ryuuen-san would lend a hand if Ayanokouji decided to join us.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I can’t imagine he’d lend anything.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I agreed with Ibuki on that point. He wasn’t the kind of guy who would entertain such good-natured ideas. Honestly, I didn’t </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">want</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> him to be the kind of guy who’d allow something like that, either.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She didn’t even turn to look at me once.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When I told her that, Ibuki responded with a show of disgust, acting like she was about to vomit. He needed to show me that he could win against Horikita, Ichinose, and Sakayanagi.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">J</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">UST AFTER ELEVEN-THIRTY</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> that same morning, two guys stood talking in the men’s restroom on the second floor of Keyaki Mall. And how would Horikita, facing off against those other three, factor into the mix?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Things would be quite different from how they were in our first year. And we’d be able to see those changes quite soon.</span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">4.6</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">J</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">UST AFTER NOON</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> it started pouring rain like someone was upturning buckets overhead. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There were lots of conveniences around campus. So even in sudden rainfall, it was never all that difficult to get back to the dormitories. Provisional umbrellas were made available for students who didn’t have any, and since they were free as long as you returned them on time, more than a few students took advantage of the offer. Some of the students who’d gone out to have fun this morning hadn’t brought umbrellas with them, to reduce the amount of stuff they had to carry around. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That being said, today was a bit of an exception. When it was raining this hard, you’d be completely soaked even if you had an umbrella.</span></p>

Right now, it seemed like they were talking about how the rain was really coming down.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What should I do, I wonder?” I muttered to myself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t feel like participating in the chat, so I decided to just leave the messages on read for the time being. I&#160;stared blankly at the screen, looking through the messages in the chat group. Then, I would look outside the window at the rain, as if I suddenly remembered something. I repeated that process several times. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">An unproductive waste of time. It was nice to spend time like this every once in a while, though. After listening to the sounds of the rain for about twenty or thirty minutes, I decided to leave.</span></p>

Maybe she had a lot going through her mind after she parted ways with Horikita and me.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As you’d expect, there wasn’t any sign of anyone else around, in the midst of this heavy rain. And then, she looked up at the sky. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">What was she feeling right now? What was she thinking about? It wasn’t difficult for me to read.
