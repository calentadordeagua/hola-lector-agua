<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 2:<br/>Graduation Ceremony</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">M</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">ARCH 24.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> The Graduation Ceremony. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Today was a big event. It marked the end of the third-year students’ studies, and was also the day they could finally bid this school farewell and enter the next phase of their lives. To the other students, this was just another ceremony they had to attend. But I, personally, was interested in watching it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The first thing I was interested to see was the result of the showdown between Horikita and Nagumo. I still had no idea how their battle—which they had fought, ceaselessly, to the very last moment—would end. Would Horikita’s brother be able to graduate from Class A? Or would he be defeated by Nagumo’s meddling?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I supposed I could have found out the outcome yesterday in my free time, but I’d stayed in my room because I had things to do. At any rate, all would be revealed today. Besides, I was simply interested in what the graduation ceremony would be like. It was only natural to be excited about things that you were experiencing for the very first time, whether that was a graduation ceremony or a closing one. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Since class time was approaching, I locked my door and headed toward the school building.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Good morning,” said Keisei, greeting me as I got into the elevator with him. I responded in kind. Since there were several students from the other classes with us, we didn’t really engage in any chit-chat. We just kept quiet, and once the elevator reached the lobby, we left the dorms together.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We finally managed to work our way up to Class C, only to get knocked back down by the end of the year. Still, I suppose we didn’t take as much damage as I thought we would,” muttered Keisei, his words trailing off like they were being sucked up into the clear, cloudless sky.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Class C being defeated in the final special exam at the end of our first year meant that we would be demoted, once again putting us in Class D. It was a considerable shock to the students in our class. Fortunately, our opponent having been Class A softened the blow a bit, as did the fact that I, as the one with a Protection Point, had helped mitigate some of the tension by playing the role of commander. I suppose you might even say that we’d simply put up a good fight, and that was admirable in and of itself. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though we’d been demoted to Class D again, the actual change in Class Points wasn’t too bad. The tentative Class Points as of late March were:</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakayanagi’s Class A: 1131 Points</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ichinose’s Class B: 550 Points</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita’s Class C: 347 Points</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ryuuen’s Class D: 508 Points</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">These figures would only take effect next month, though. Class Points were determined on the first day of every month, and it was at that point that the classes would change. So right now, we were still in Class C, not Class D. Also, Ryuuen and his followers hadn’t just re-emerged as Class C, but were now neck-and-neck with Class B in terms of Class Points. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If the point totals stayed as they were until the first of April rolled around next month, it would shake the classes up in a big way. But we had to remember there were a lot of variables at this school that could cause Class Points to fluctuate each month. Ichinose’s class had lots of diligent students, but you couldn’t exactly say the same of Ryuuen’s classmates, even if you were trying to be flattering.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was also likely that the way students went about their daily lives would make a difference in Class Points. I was sure the students of Class B were scared to death right now, but even so, the fact that Ichinose had been able to defend their position to the bitter end all through this year must be a relief. That being said, the difference between her class and Ryuuen’s was a mere forty-two points at present. There was a significant possibility that Ryuuen could seize Class B’s position, such as through the next special exam. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If you looked at just these facts alone, it might seem like we were the only ones losing ground, since we were back to being Class D. But what we needed to remember was how the Class Points were as of April and May last year. Last April, all four classes had started on an even playing field, with one thousand points each. At that point, Class A’s superiority and Class D’s inferiority didn’t exist. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In hindsight, that had probably been our best chance to really establish our position…but, well, we Class D students had lost all our Class Points in less than a month. As a result, the Class Points as of May 1 last year were:</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakayanagi’s Class A: 940 Points</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ichinose’s Class B: 650 Points</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ryuuen’s Class C: 490 Points</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita’s Class D: 0 Points</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">All classes had lost points between May of last year and now. In fact, you could say last May was the month the competition really began. But keeping that in mind, our class had gone on to gain three hundred and forty-seven Class Points in a year. We might lose some more due to factors like attitude, tardiness, absences, etc., but when all was said and done, we would still most likely have gained between three hundred and thirty and three hundred forty points. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Examining that information led to one conclusion. Which was that our class had seen the greatest increase in Class Points throughout the year. Our gains were far greater than Class A, who had the second highest increase, with one-hundred-and-ninety-one points gained. Considering the fact that we had plummeted all the way to rock-bottom early last spring and been immediately reduced to zero points, you could say we’d done quite&#160;&#160;well. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">And now that we were entering our second year, my classmates were expected to do even better. Taking into account factors like the growth of leadership skills in people such as Horikita and Hirata, and the overall improvement in our class’s abilities, it was well within the realm of possibility for us to compete with the classes far above us. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Once we were alone, Keisei opened his mouth, as if he wanted to tell me something… </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s okay. Most of our other classmates didn’t really blame you for anything,” he said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He probably thought I was troubled by our defeat, since I was the commander. I didn’t care at all, of course, but I did pick up on his particular wording. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Most?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was sure he’d intended to say something comforting, but instead, had said something conspicuous. That basically meant that there were some students, albeit a few, who were unhappy with me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, it’s…I suppose it’s not a perfect situation by any means. It’s just that…while it’s not like people are saying </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">you’re</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> at fault, Kiyotaka, I’ve been hearing some people say there should have been someone more capable in the role of commander,” said Keisei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Which was, in a sense, synonymous with blaming me. People could be irrational. It wasn’t surprising if some were voicing objections after the fact, even if they’d agreed to something before. Nor was it surprising if some of them felt dissatisfied, thinking the reason we’d lost to Class A was the difference in our commanders.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, even if they run their mouths at you, you’ve gotta stay confident, right? I mean, it wasn’t like anyone else </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">could</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> have been the commander, since no one else had a Protection Point,” he added, after considering that there might be students who’d come to me with their complaints.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I’m sure most people think so. But then there’s the example of Ryuuen, too.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When I said that, a wry smile appeared on Keisei’s face. He gently shook his head from side to side.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That guy is an exception. I think that his recklessness is all just part of his act. In fact, it was Ryuuen—the one person who didn’t have a Protection Point—coming out to compete that caught Class B entirely by surprise and led them to suffer a crushing defeat.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But that wasn’t true. It was part of a calculated strategy concocted by Ryuuen to achieve victory. His defenseless act and surprise entrance were nothing more than components of his strategy. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Hey, Kiyotaka, there’s something I want to ask you.” Keisei spoke up again once we’d exhausted that topic and reached a spot in the conversation where we could transition to another. “When I talked about going ahead and trying to win over Katsuragi on our own without consulting anyone else, why didn’t you report me to Horikita?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In order to win against Class A in the final exam of the year, Keisei had proposed an idea to Horikita. His strategy was to bring Katsuragi, who had confronted and been defeated by Sakayanagi, over to our side as an ally. But Horikita didn’t approve of his idea, and so Keisei went on to try to win over Katsuragi entirely on his own. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He’d failed, though. Well, in truth, his failure hadn’t had a significant impact, either. Although Katsuragi didn’t agree to cooperate with us, any harm we suffered as a result was entirely negligible. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, no harm, no foul, right?” I told him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That wasn’t really the important part to Keisei, of course. Even knowing that, I deliberately offered him words of comfort.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s just because Katsuragi isn’t the kind to use dirty tricks. If we’d been dealing with someone like Sakayanagi or Ryuuen, we’d have suffered a much more devastating blow,” he replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Keisei felt an intense sense of responsibility because he had forcefully tried to win Katsuragi over to our side. And he was worried about a future that had never come to be. Judging from the way he spoke, he’d told Horikita about his attempt to try to win over Katsuragi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Oh, yeah, that reminds me. I told Horikita about it. I figured that I should take responsibility,” he added. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He’d openly admitted what he had done, prepared for any reprimand that came.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Kiyotaka, were you certain Katsuragi would never betray Class A?” he asked, laying his concerns directly before me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, not really certain. To be honest, there was a definite possibility that Katsuragi could have changed sides. Don’t you think so?” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well… I suppose, but…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I put aside, for now, the question of whether there was a fifty percent or one percent chance of that happening. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I didn’t report it to Horikita because it slipped my mind is all. I was anxious about whether I could fulfill the duties that came with being commander, and my mind was occupied with those thoughts. In that sense, I had a big responsibility. If things had worked out and you did succeed in winning Katsuragi over to our side, I&#160;probably wouldn’t have been able to do my own duties well. We’re both to blame,” I told him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Now that we’d both come out and said our piece, that put an end to the whole Katsuragi discussion.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We’re both to blame, huh?” Keisei said. “Even so, I’m painfully aware of my lack of foresight now. Considering the risks, I shouldn’t even have considered trying to win Katsuragi over in the first place.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">While we couldn’t undo what had already happened, we could reflect on it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If we’re talking about a lack of foresight, then I’m equally guilty. I was there too, and I didn’t say anything,” I told him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hearing you say that makes me feel a little better.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">While many students remained passive during the exam, Keisei had been desperately trying anything he could to help our class win.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Besides, I think you figured it out now, right? You understand that you can’t easily succeed with a strategy like that,” I told him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">You could learn a lot from your mistakes. Whether or not you made the most of those mistakes depended on you, though.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Yeah, you’re right. I wanted to win so badly that I couldn’t see what was right in front of me. Man, it sounds pathetic now that I’ve calmed down enough to think about it,” he muttered quietly, as if reflecting on what happened. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Although his idea to try to win over Katsuragi had certainly been naïve, the fact that he’d taken on the challenge was praiseworthy. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, what did Horikita say to you?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“She didn’t blame me, even though our entire class would probably have suffered if I’d handled things poorly. On the contrary, she actually said that she wanted me to let her know the next time I thought of an idea. Of course, she did warn me not to be overeager, too,” said Keisei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It sounded as though Horikita had come to the same conclusion about Keisei as I had. People grew through repeated failures. You can’t be a leader if you care only about results, and smack people down for it…though people who did nothing but fail endlessly had to be eventually cut loose, of course.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“To be honest, I never really got behind the idea of Horikita leading us. Sure, her mind is sharp and she’s athletic. But there’s something about the way she acts—the way she condescends to people—that makes her hard to accept.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I couldn’t deny that point. At least, not at this current point in time. Horikita wasn’t the kind of person who led by virtue, like Hirata or Ichinose. While she’d been able to make a certain number of allies, she was inevitably going to make enemies, too. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But…I’m a lot like that, too. I thought that sports were entirely unnecessary, and I looked down on everyone who wasn’t smart. Horikita and I are basically birds of a feather,” said Keisei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When Keisei had first started school here, he tended to openly scorn students who weren’t academically inclined. He’d thought being a student came solely down to how well you did in your studies.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But you’re completely different from how you were a year ago, Keisei. You’ve changed a lot,” I told him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I suppose so. Honestly, even I find it kind of strange that I feel this way now. I mean, studying is the most important thing, of course. But athletics, communication skills, and even friendship are all important, too. I understand that I need all of those things now, and I suppose it’s the same for Horikita. She is changing, little by little. She’s becoming more dependable and trustworthy than before.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Keisei didn’t really trust anyone outside of the Ayanokouji Group. The fact that he praised Horikita so openly despite that, complimenting her on the things that she deserved recognition for, made me believe he was being honest about his feelings.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You might be right,” I agreed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It had taken a year, and direct contact with her, but the student known as Horikita was finally beginning to be recognized. Horikita’s classmates had gradually begun to accept her ever since the in-class voting exam, but the main reason for this was something other than the pointedness of her strategies and the excellence of her leadership. It was because the wall Horikita put up around her heart was slowly coming down. When that wall was up, she assumed every other student was nothing more than a liability to her, even concluding it inevitable that the weak would be disposed of. It was the same tendency Keisei used to have.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course, I don’t think doing everything Horikita says is the correct answer. If I think she’s made a poor decision, I will not hesitate to call her out on it. Am I wrong in thinking that?” Keisei asked, having gathered his thoughts. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He was saying he would trust what should be trusted, and doubt what he needed to doubt.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, I think you’re right. That’s how Class A should be, under normal circumstances.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">No matter how dependable she’d become, Horikita was still a high school student. She might make mistakes—serious ones, even. The more students who were willing to point out such mistakes when the time came, the better. We could stand shoulder to shoulder and talk things out, working to find a solution together. That wasn’t a possibility for the classes led by Sakayanagi and Ryuuen, which were run like dictatorships. If anything, our class might be becoming more akin to Ichinose’s now. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Besides, it wasn’t just important for us to close the gap. We had to do it in a way that worked for our class.</span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">2.1</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HE ENTIRE STUDENT BODY</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> was gathered in the gymnasium. So were all the teachers, as well as some adults that we normally didn’t see around. Everyone present, regardless of status, was fondly watching the graduation ceremony unfold. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This was the moment when the third-year students took their next big step toward a new chapter of their lives. Some would go on to higher education, some would get jobs, and some would remain at a standstill, unable to decide where to go next. They would no longer be categorized as children, but would be venturing out to become independent members of society. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">How will I look standing up there myself, two years from now? </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wondered. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">And what will I be thinking about?</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wanted to believe you could still envision a great many possibilities if you’d already decided what path to take. I wanted to believe that what I’d learned here would help me in life, like a source of mental nourishment.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Now then, we’d like to ask the representative of the class that fought valiantly for three whole years to graduate from Class A to come give a formal address,” the adult hosting the event said into the microphone.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The gymnasium grew even quieter than before.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Class A’s representative—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If the student whose name was called wasn’t Horikita Manabu or one of his classmates, then… Well, that would mean there had been a shift in the class rankings as a result of the final exam. Many of the current students were probably paying very close attention to this moment. That was because graduating from Class A was the greatest goal—the </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">only</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> goal, really—for every student at this school. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Horikita Manabu, if you please.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was sure Horikita Suzune was deeply relieved to hear that name. It was unclear just how much Nagumo had hindered him, but apparently, Horikita’s brother had managed to safely graduate from Class A. He proudly walked up to the stage and then looked out at all the currently enrolled students and other people attending the event.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Greetings to you all. We’re gathered here on this fine day, when the fresh spring air carries with it the scent of plum blossoms, to witness the graduation ceremony—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita Manabu began his speech. It sounded like he was expressing gratitude for this magnificent graduation ceremony. Then, he went on to talk about when he first started at this school three years ago.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I vividly remember beginning my academic career here at the Advanced Nurturing High School and feeling an atmosphere that was altogether different from other schools. I also remember vowing to make my three years here rewarding, while at the same time shouldering a great responsibility for the future.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There was a feeling of peace in the air as he spoke slowly and calmly. There was something different about the person who now stood in the same place he’d stood at the start of the year, addressing the entrance ceremony of the student council president. As Horikita continued solemnly giving his speech, I sensed a change. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">And it wasn’t just in Horikita Manabu. I felt the currently enrolled students had also grown significantly over these past few months.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Now, this is just a personal note, but as a representative of the student council, I had a few words for freshmen students at the beginning of the year.” Somehow, Horikita Manabu seemed to be in tune with my thoughts. “Compared to when I last saw you, standing up here at the beginning of the year, you have clearly grown.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">At the beginning of the year, Horikita had managed to get the restless first-years in line with his silence. That was something many students didn’t see at the time. Now, there wasn’t a single student speaking out of turn, having their own private conversation. And Horikita Manabu, who was now venturing out to establish himself as an individual in society, was looking warmly at the students who remained.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Also, I sincerely hope that the second-year students, who will go on to become third-years and be in a position to lead the other students, will demonstrate their abilities to the fullest extent while observing the rules and regulations at this institution.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">A few minutes later, he finally started to bring his speech to a close. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I promise you that what you are learning at this school will be more valuable and more useful than anything else in your lives ahead,” he added, looking out over the gathered students once more. “When the time comes for someone to stand up here and give a speech next year, and another someone the year after that, I am sure they will understand as well.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The person who’d give a speech next year… In other words, the leader of the graduating Class A. I supposed that Nagumo, who had just read the farewell address earlier, was probably the most likely candidate among the second-years. The first-years were still in the midst of a chaotic free-for-all, though. Would it be Horikita? Ichinose? Ryuuen? Sakayanagi?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Or would it be someone else entirely, replacing one of those students as the leader of their class? A third of our lives here at this school had passed by in a flash, but that </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">was</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> still only a third. The classes would continue&#160;to change, and the number of students would continue to&#160;decrease. Even so, only the leader of the winning class would be permitted to take the stage as a representative of that class.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita continued to read off the remainder of his speech, speaking slowly yet eloquently.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thank you very much for these past three years.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Soon, Horikita’s time here would be done. Having addressed the students, he next addressed the teachers, and then the school in general. As his brilliant speech came to a close, the graduation ceremony moved onto the next stage. </span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">2.2</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">W</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HEN THE GRADUATION CEREMONY</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> had ended, the current students were the first to leave the gymnasium, returning to our classrooms. The graduates, all the teachers, and the graduates’ parents or legal guardians, meanwhile, would be off to attend a party. Apparently, it was some kind of get-together meant for the graduating students and their guardians to show the teachers their appreciation.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The remaining students were permitted to return to their dorms. However, it seemed that those who were close to third-years, whether because they were friends or because they’d been in clubs together, planned to wait for the graduates to emerge after the party. Maybe they planned to give a third-year flowers, or confess a crush to them, or tell them something entirely different. Some students were excited, while others were quiet and nervous. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, even though we can talk about this during the closing ceremony tomorrow, let’s briefly recap this past semester,” said Chabashira shortly after everyone was seated, turning to look at us. “First of all, I must give credit where it’s due, and say that you fought admirably against Class A during your final exam of the semester. The teachers were surprised at how much you’ve matured.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though we’d lost, the normally sharp-tongued Chabashira was offering us genuine praise. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’ve changed significantly since last year, when you first started attending this school; I hardly recognize you now. You’ve really grown so much.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hold up, sensei. We’re back to being Class D again though, right? Isn’t that, y’know, super uncool?” said Ike, his voice filled with frustration.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, it certainly seems like we’ve been sent back to square one. But you’ve definitely all grown this past year. More than simply closing the gap in class points, you could very well say that you’ve gotten closer to the other classes in terms of ability,” said Chabashira.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay, the fact that you’re complimentin’ us this much actually kinda freaks me out. There’s gotta be somethin’ to this, right, </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">sensei</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">?” said Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was completely understandable why he’d react that way to our instructor praising us. Honestly, we wouldn’t be surprised if she then went on to say we had another exam or something. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, there isn’t. This is simply what I think, is all. I’ve been a teacher for four years now, and I’ve been in charge of two classes. Compared to the previous Class Ds I’ve taught, you really are a cut above. However, the same could be said of the other classes. Whether or not you can climb the ranks will depend on your ability to keep putting in a certain level of effort from here, without getting sloppy.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Tak!</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> Chabashira lightly knocked against the blackboard.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Tomorrow is the closing ceremony. Even though there are no classes tomorrow, please don’t forget that it is still a school day.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Having said her piece, she dismissed us. I had no idea how many students would be heading out to wait for the third-years, but I </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">was</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> wondering what my desk neighbor was going to do—the little sister of the guy who’d served as student council president and delivered a speech as Class A’s representative, earlier. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita stared intently at the blackboard, looking frozen in place. I was sure there were a lot of thoughts running through her head. Even though bothering her right now felt like kicking the hornet’s nest, I figured I’d try her anyway.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You heading out?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“To do what?” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Come on, you know what I’m talking about.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you asking if I’m going to go see my brother? If so, then no, I don’t plan on it,” said Horikita, averting her eyes from me as she spoke.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So, she wasn’t planning to go… Hm.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait, were you able to talk to him earlier or something?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s not like this is really any of your business, is it? We all have our own problems to deal with,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">I have a feeling you’re the only one with a problem to deal with right now, though.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you let this chance pass you by, it’ll be a while before you get another.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, that’s…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though the ice around her heart was starting to melt, she was still apprehensive and noncommittal when it really counted. I supposed that just proved how strained their relationship had been these past few years.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m gonna go see him.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? You’re planning on meeting my brother?” It was precisely because I normally didn’t get too involved with other people that Horikita looked so shocked. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We’re not that close, but today might be my last chance to see him,” I told her. It wasn’t a bad idea to at least say hello.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see…” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is there some kind of problem?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Not really, no. You’re free to go meet with him.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“Why </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">you,</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;"> of all people?” </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">was clearly written on her face, but I couldn’t exactly tell her. I stood from my desk. The teachers were all rushing to the thank-you party right now, which meant Tsukishiro, as the acting director, was sure to be there too. There was no way he wouldn’t attend.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Where are you going?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m going to kill some time, since I have nothing to do while the thank-you party is going on. If you decide you want to see your brother too, let me know and we can meet up later?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…I’ll think about it. I wonder how long the thank-you party is going to be,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She’d said before that she had no plans to see him, but it sounded like she was withdrawing that statement.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No idea. Maybe an hour or two, I guess,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Actually, the thank-you party was scheduled to go for ninety minutes, so we had quite a bit of time before it ended. I figured I’d take care of some business that needed attending to during that time.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">2.3</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">A</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">ND NOW</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> we’re going back in time a little bit. To yesterday, the twenty-third. On the night that the Event Selection Exam ended, I called a certain someone.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hello. This is Sakayanagi,” a calm, mature voice answered. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The person I called wasn’t Sakayanagi Arisu, a fellow student in my grade level, but her father, Director Sakayanagi. The man who had been forced into house arrest due to a trap laid by Tsukishiro. I supposed it wasn’t like he’d have my phone number memorized.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I apologize for bothering you so late at night, as well as for neglecting to call. This is Ayanokouji,” I replied, identifying myself so that he would first understand who was calling.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? Ayanokouji…? Ayanokouji-kun?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I could tell Director Sakayanagi was surprised to hear the surname Ayanokouji, and my voice. I had to give him that information right away, though, to prove this wasn’t a pointless prank call. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sorry to call you out of the blue like this,” I responded.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, no, no, it’s just a surprise, is all. How do you know my phone number?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I got it from your daughter. She said this is the number I should use when I want to contact a school official.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">While Sakayanagi and I were walking back to the dorms after the final exam of the school year, I’d asked her about it, and she immediately gave it to me. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I suppose you only gave your phone number to your daughter,” I told him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It probably wasn’t favoritism, but just that he doted on his beloved daughter. Or so I’d thought, anyway. Director Sakayanagi’s reaction was rather unexpected.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Arisu did…? But, well… I didn’t give my daughter my phone number, though,” he denied, sounding surprised. He gave a wry, strained chuckle. “How and when in the world did she find it?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t get the impression he was lying.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is the director’s phone number supposed to be kept secret, normally?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, all the teachers know it, of course, and I think it’s included in the materials that we hand out to related staff…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If so, it wouldn’t be that difficult to get his number. It wouldn’t surprise me if Sakayanagi had just seen it somewhere and committed it to memory. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But there was something that worried me. Director Sakayanagi was the sort of man who valued fairness, even when it came to his precious daughter. I couldn’t imagine him helping her if she came crying to him for something. Why, then, would she bother purposefully memorizing his number? I assumed it wasn’t so she could report to him on recent happenings or to have a chat. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I remembered how happily Sakayanagi had answered when I asked her for the number. Maybe she’d anticipated that I might be in trouble someday, and that I’d ask her for the director’s phone number?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, then… How should I respond to you calling me like this?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Apparently, the director considered that question more important than how I’d obtained his number. Well, I was certain he didn’t exactly welcome things like direct communication from a student with open arms.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So if he neglected his duties as director, the school would judge him harshly for it, hm?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. Most certainly.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In order to be able to do as he pleased, Tsukishiro had to play the part of a man who could do more than Director Sakayanagi. His watchful eyes would inevitably be a lot less watchful during that event.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Will the first-year homeroom teachers be in attendance, too?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ostensibly, the thank-you party is an hour long, but it typically extends a bit beyond that. It tends to last about ninety minutes. It shouldn’t cause too many problems if two teachers were to disappear for twenty to thirty minutes. It’s normal for teachers to be up and out of their seats, and essentially, the only teachers that are required to be there are the third-year homeroom instructors.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This meant that the most appropriate time to hold a secret meeting would be after the graduation ceremony and during the thank-you party. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“As for the place… I think the reception room should be fine. There are no surveillance cameras in that room. It might be best to take advantage of that,” said Sakayanagi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That meant there would be no clear record of our meeting. Besides, it wasn’t like I could have teachers come to the student dormitories. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I have no objections to this proposal.” I agreed with his ideas for setting up the forum for our discussion.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay, then here’s the first step,” Director Sakayanagi said. “I’ll go ahead and briefly touch base with Chabashira-sensei ahead of time. But it’ll be up to you to decide how much you wish to divulge from that point on. If you cannot persuade them, you may have no choice but to give up on this plan.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That is more than adequate.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If word originally came from Director Sakayanagi, then Chabashira—and by extension Mashima-sensei, who we would arrange to speak with later—couldn’t ignore this matter. I could say without a doubt that the phone call had yielded the greatest amount of support it possibly could have. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Once again, I’m terribly sorry for suddenly calling you so late at night,” I reiterated.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s all right… Oh, one last thing. I hope you don’t mind if I ask you something a little superfluous.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Superfluous?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I am honestly quite happy that you came to this school with dreams of a normal life. But have you thought about what comes after graduation? What do you want to do and where do you want to go, for instance?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t know how much you already know, but my fate has been decided,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…That would mean…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That reaction alone was enough.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“After graduation, I will return to the White Room and act as a mentor there. That’s the only reason that man has raised me all these years.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Once I left this school, the protective barrier around me would be gone. He could easily attack me in the night and haul me back to the White Room if I were living in a cheap apartment or something. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you’ve accepted your fate… And now you’re here, after having done so.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That is precisely why I intend to protect these three years to the bitter end,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Put simply, it was a rebellious phase. I was rejecting my father’s orders and doing what </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">I </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">wanted to do.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I hope this school will become a good memory for you. One that you will never forget for as long as you live.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thank you very much. That’s what I’m hoping for, too.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After I ended the call with Director Sakayanagi, I let out a sigh of relief. Although there was a part of me that wondered how much I could really trust him, at the very least, I knew for certain that he wasn’t on Tsukishiro’s side. The fact that his daughter was a student and in my grade would be an advantage, as well.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">2.4</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">S</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">UCH WAS THE DISCUSSION</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> I had yesterday with Director Sakayanagi. And now, I was headed to the reception room, which had been designated for the meeting ahead. I made no plans to meet anyone else beforehand, but headed straight there. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Was someone else already here? Or was I the first to arrive?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Please excuse me,” I said aloud. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After knocking on the door, I stepped into the reception room, and was greeted by Chabashira. She shifted her gaze toward me, still standing by the window. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re early, Ayanokouji. You still have ten minutes until the appointed time,” she told me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I didn’t want to get here at the very last minute. Besides, you seem to have arrived early yourself,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira looked at me with prying eyes, like she was trying to figure something out, but also seemed to be lost in thought and choosing her next words carefully. I could more or less guess what had gone through her mind when Director Sakayanagi spoke to her. Funnily enough, the sofa was vacant, but neither of us sat down.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And Mashima-sensei?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ve been talking to him. But he wasn’t able to slip away at the same time as me. Still, I have to say, you’ve made quite a bold move, Ayanokouji. I thought you wanted to live a quiet, peaceful life here at this school?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I supposed I could play along with Chabashira’s word games for a little bit. Just until Mashima-sensei showed&#160;up.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s funny that you say that, since you were the one who originally disrupted my peaceful life here,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I really don’t think that’s any way to act toward a teacher, no matter the circumstances. You have no intention of changing your attitude?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s convenient, considering how unbecoming your behavior is for a teacher,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She’d gone so far as to threaten me, a simple, unassuming student, to try to boost us out of being Class D. I felt a strong sense of distrust…no, a strong feeling of disgust for her because of that. Chabashira awkwardly averted her eyes from me, looking ashamed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I certainly cannot deny that,” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I supposed it just proved how strong her desire to reach Class A was. She couldn’t openly use me, since Director Sakayanagi trusted her and asked her to watch over me. But she really should have handled things better. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Well, actually…no matter what methods she employed, the result would have been the same. Even if she’d tried to persuade me, I wouldn’t have softened my stance. That being said, my circumstances had changed quite a bit over the year since I had first started school here. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You hate me. So why call on me, Ayanokouji?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It seemed like she couldn’t help but wonder why she’d been asked here. Even though she was just a way for me to recruit Mashima-sensei, I could certainly have excluded her from this meeting. It wasn’t surprising that she wanted to know why I hadn’t done that.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, it’s certainly true that at the very least, I don’t exactly like you,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It certainly seems that way.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">No matter my personal feelings, I needed to seize any advantage I could. My likes and dislikes were entirely different from my potential gains and losses. I had determined that having Chabashira here would help persuade Mashima-sensei to come over to my side, even if it only got him to budge one millimeter closer.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How much have you heard?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“He asked me to call Mashima-sensei</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;"> </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">and to arrange this meeting here. He also said that you had something very important to talk about and that he wanted me to help, but…” she replied, trailing off.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Had she not heard anything about Tsukishiro yet? It seemed like Director Sakayanagi wanted to give me complete control of this situation.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And? What business do you have with us?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll wait until Mashima-sensei comes. Saying it twice would be a hassle.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t know what this is all about, but if you’re going to come to me asking for help, don’t you think you should fix your attitude?” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Perhaps because she’d been on the defensive with me all this time, Chabashira seemed very resistant.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“As an instructor, I will essentially follow Director Sakayanagi’s instructions. However, that’s not an ironclad rule. Do you understand what I mean by that?” she asked me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Does my attitude rub you the wrong way that badly?” I asked in return.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, it does. You act so superior, but you’re still just a first-year high school student, right? And although the year-end final exam was a showdown between classes, you still fell behind Sakayanagi and were defeated. That means you don’t have the skills necessary to surpass my expectations, after all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So she was, quite selfishly, disappointed that I wasn’t as good as she’d expected me to be?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you have the skills, then I’m willing to overlook some of what you say and do. But if what you showed earlier is all you’re capable of, then that’s a different story,” she added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If I couldn’t beat Class A’s Sakayanagi, I wouldn’t be able to achieve Chabashira’s goals. Apparently, she intended to keep asserting her dominance over me, and she didn’t intend to be quiet about it. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira was a teacher, but her actions certainly deviated from her normal duties as an instructor. Depending on what I said to her, she might refuse to help, and perhaps even go over to Tsukishiro’s side. I could continue trying to assert that I was no longer completely under her control, but that would just be counterproductive. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I let out a sigh, relieved to see that she had at least some wisdom. “I understand. I will change up my attitude, Chabashira-sensei.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What?” she replied, shocked by how quickly I had given in to her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I supposed she didn’t think the level of resistance she was putting up would break me. Although I was only doing this for the sake of the conversation to come, I did want to leave her with the possibility that I could be tamed. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Well, no—that possibility alone wouldn’t be enough for Chabashira to trust me completely. She probably thought I was mocking her, laughing at her deep down. So instead, I hammered home the idea that I was a positive influence for Class D.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ve changed my mind. Starting in April, I’m seriously planning to shoot for Class A,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What kind of joke is this? What in the world are you even thinking, setting up a meeting here?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m telling the truth. By the end of our second year, I plan for our class to be out of D and C. There’s too big a gap in our class points to guarantee that we’ll be able to move up to Class A, but…I </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">am </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">planning to overtake Class B.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">What Chabashira desired most of all was for Class D to rise up to A. That was something no class at this school had ever managed to pull off before.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, this is an eye-opener… But it’s easy enough to make all the verbal promises you want.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s certainly true. But you do want to hold onto your ticket to Class A, don’t you?” I asked her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It didn’t matter whether that ticket was real or fake. It was far better than having nothing at all.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I told you before. You lost to Class A in the year-end final exam. Although you fought admirably, with three wins and four losses, a loss is still a loss. And though a great deal of luck was involved in that exam, I still won’t hear any excuses,” she answered, once again stressing the fact that my abilities had apparently been exaggerated. “I&#160;thought that you could win, no matter the opponent, no matter the test. But those expectations were excessive.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In truth, it was merely that she had this selfish fantasy about me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’ll see the truth for yourself today after this meeting,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll see the truth…?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Listen to the whole story. All the way to the end. If you still can’t believe in my abilities after that, then you can do whatever you want.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do you—” she began. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There was a forceful knock at the reception room door, cutting her off. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Please come in,” said Chabashira in response. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Mashima-sensei entered the room. “It seems like you’re both here already,” he said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">And then…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Good day to you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The Class A student, Sakayanagi Arisu, had entered with Mashima-sensei. Quite an unexpected guest. I didn’t remember inviting her, and I had a hard time imagining Mashima-sensei doing so.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m from Class A. So even if someone did see me with Mashima-sensei, it wouldn’t raise suspicions,” said Sakayanagi. It went without saying that she must have tagged along with him. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Chabashira-sensei reached out to me. But she said that she had some relation to this matter, so I brought her with me, but…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Director Sakayanagi had probably told his daughter that he had gotten a call from me, just to confirm I </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">had </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">actually had gotten his phone number through her—and if not, that I had betrayed his trust. But did that have anything to do with why Sakayanagi was here now? Had she been charged with playing some kind of role in this? Or was she simply here out of curiosity? If I were a betting man, I’d say it was the latter.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s no problem. This is within the realm of what I expected,” I answered, accepting this visitor as someone who should be welcomed with open arms. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakayanagi responded to me with a soft chuckle and a gentle bow. Then, without even sparing a single glance in Chabashira’s direction, she closed the reception room door. Chabashira seemed like she was struggling to keep up with the situation, let alone grasp why Sakayanagi was here. Mashima-sensei was probably having the same problem. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Regardless, all of the necessary people were in place. I&#160;needed to use this limited amount of time carefully.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I hear you have something to say to us, Ayanokouji. Purposefully going through Director Sakayanagi to orchestrate this gathering, and holding a secret meeting right in the middle of the thank-you party… This is quite a lot to take in. What in the world are you playing at?” asked Mashima-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll tell you right now,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">First, I urged the two teachers to have a seat. However, Mashima-sensei instructed Sakayanagi to sit first.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I will kindly accept your gracious offer,” she said humbly, taking her seat.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Mashima-sensei allowed the physically disabled Sakayanagi to take a spot, opting to remain standing himself, now with his arms crossed. It seemed whether or not he would decide to sit down depended on what I was about to say. Chabashira seemed to be taking his same approach. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The three other people in the room all gazed at me. They could only be away from the thank-you party for twenty to thirty minutes at most. Time was extremely limited. I had planned to get straight to the point, but I wasn’t sure how long it would take to make them really understand. This was an extraordinary situation. Too extraordinary to be easily communicated by a brief explanation. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Seeing as how there was no time to spare, I decided to start by talking about Acting Director Tsukishiro.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I asked you to meet with me during such a busy time because I wished to discuss something important. It’s about Acting Director Tsukishiro.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Something important about Acting Director Tsukishiro?” said Mashima-sensei. “What in the world are you talking about?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He looked increasingly perplexed by my throwing out such an unexpected statement right off the bat. I&#160;supposed it was only natural for him to react this way to a student saying something this absurd. Chabashira seemed to be having trouble keeping up as well, but she just briefly glanced over at Sakayanagi, the only unexpected person here. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakayanagi met her gaze directly, with a bold grin. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">I&#160;know much more about this situation than you two do, </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">her face seemed to be saying. As I looked at her, all I could get from her expression was genuine joy. It felt very typical of Sakayanagi, to be honest.</span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><img alt="" height="100%" id="INTERIORIMAGES_" src="/hola-lector-agua/images/books/v11.5/OEBPS/Images/INTERIORIMAGES_.jpg" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style=" top: 0px; vertical-align: top; height: 100%;"/></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The situation has become something that cannot be overlooked. I sincerely hope that the two of you can help me bring the situation under control, while being committed to total secrecy,” I told them.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I heard that this was something important, but… Are you playing some kind of prank on me, Chabashira-sensei?” asked Mashima-sensei, seeking an explanation from her. Apparently, he thought there was no way what I said could be true.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I assure you, this is no joke. Do you think I’m the sort who plays meaningless games, like Hoshinomiya-sensei?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“All right, fair enough, but I can’t understand what’s going on here at all. We’re right in the middle of a thank-you party that we should be attending.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This was supposed to be a valuable opportunity to mingle with the graduates for the last time. Mashima-sensei’s disinterest was clear. He had no time to listen to a child’s delusional fantasy.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What is Ayanokouji doing?” he asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t know,” Chabashira said. “It’s impossible for me to explain this to you. As I told you yesterday, all I did was prepare the meeting location on Director Sakayanagi’s orders. Nothing more. Like you, I’m still trying to understand what’s going on here.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Both teachers cast gazes full of suspicion at me. I tried to keep the discussion going.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What would you think, Mashima-sensei, if I told you that Director Sakayanagi is currently under house arrest due to allegations of fraud? And that Acting Director Tsukishiro came to this school because of me?” I asked.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It would be nearly impossible for students to stop him on their own. The only question that remained was whether Mashima-sensei was the kind of person who could turn a blind eye to something like that. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you trying to test me, Ayanokouji? …Very well. We will do our utmost to keep Director Tsukishiro from illegitimately involving himself in any further special exams or written exams.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Mashima-sensei had finally come out and said that he was going to be on my side.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Mashima-sensei, you do understand it’s not going to be that simple, don’t you?” cautioned Chabashira, after hearing his declaration. “Even if it is true that he’s doing something fraudulent, we could get fired if we slip-up.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I could understand why she’d say that. Rebelling against Tsukishiro would essentially mean putting their teaching career in jeopardy. He wasn’t the kind of opponent you could take on out of a half-hearted sense of justice.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Although I still don’t fully believe everything being said, if what Ayanokouji and Sakayanagi are saying is the truth, then this is a grave matter. A school official should never manipulate an exam or change test results in a fraudulent manner. If we are to do something, we must be thorough,” he argued. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But Mashima-sensei, wouldn’t it be better for us not to fixate on such troublesome issues right now? You just received a pay cut this morning for violating the rules yourself during the Event Selection Exam,” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakayanagi must have thought that what Chabashira said sounded interesting, because she immediately jumped on that statement. “A pay cut for violating the rules? Whatever did you do?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That is not something I plan to tell you two about,” snapped Mashima-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is it because of the conflict between Class D and Class&#160;B during the exam? We’ll be hearing about it sooner or later. Besides, if this incident is related to Acting Director Tsukishiro’s fraudulent activities, then we really must discuss matters that could be cause for concern at this stage. It could become a problem later, wouldn’t you say?” argued Sakayanagi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What happened is entirely unrelated to this current discussion,” said Chabashira, speaking up in place of Mashima-sensei. “But I’ll describe it to you. During the Event Selection Exam for Class B and Class D, the final event to be selected was judo, an event that Class D had submitted. And the student they had picked to participate was Yamada Albert. Class B’s Ichinose had completely lost her will to fight at that point, leaving her unable to choose a student to compete against him.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, if their opponent was Yamada-kun, that’s entirely understandable. I don’t imagine any first-year student could beat him in judo,” said Sakayanagi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course, Ichinose </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">had </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">originally had a student in mind for the judo event. But what do you think would have happened if her decision paralysis continued long enough to cause a student from her class to be assigned at random? Anyone could tell there might be unforeseen consequences,” said Chabashira.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If Ichinose had run out of time to select a student to compete, a student who hadn’t yet participated in any event would have been selected at random. And that student could just as easily have been a girl as a boy.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If the student simply lost the match at once, that would have been fine. But consider how tightly-knit Class B is. It was possible that the student who got selected would have tried everything in their power to win for Ichinose-san’s sake,” explained Chabashira.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">And it was entirely plausible that Albert would have used all his strength to knock down his opponent so hard that they couldn’t get back up, no matter who that opponent was. If that happened, it could’ve been a serious incident.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Which was precisely why Mashima-sensei, acting on his own discretion, determined that the fight was unwinnable. I suppose that’s what Acting Director Tsukishiro didn’t like,” she reasoned.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So that was why he got a pay cut, huh? I supposed what he’d done</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;"> was</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> actually a violation of the rules. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That incident and this situation are one and the same. If there’s something that we deem to be dangerous for the students, we put a stop to it. If there’s injustice, you correct it. We teach this to our students. What would we be if we didn’t adhere to it ourselves?” he answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">And for that reason, he would do this without any regret, even if it meant putting his own career at risk.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It seems I can’t stop you,” said Chabashira.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“As a teacher, I’ve always prepared myself for situations like this,” he replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was easy enough to say something like that and not actually </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">mean</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> it. But Mashima-sensei seemed to be an upstanding man. He was true to his word. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You… Well, all right. If Mashima-sensei is that resolute in his decision, then there’s nothing more to say,” said Chabashira.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, I’d say that we’ve reached an agreement for the time being, hm?” said Sakayanagi, turning to look at me as she spoke.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I nodded to her in response. Perhaps Chabashira had decided that trying to persuade Mashima-sensei otherwise would be pointless, because she backed down.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, if Mashima-sensei is agreeing to put his neck out for you, then I will help, too. I trust you’re okay with that, Ayanokouji?” she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The more people I can get on my side, the better. I&#160;welcome it,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Then let’s put this discussion on hold for the time being. And no one speaks a word of this to anyone outside this room. That’s not a problem, I trust?” she answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course not, no,” I answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was understandable, since it wasn’t like Mashima-sensei nor Chabashira had actually seen Tsukishiro’s wrongdoing with their own eyes. Also, the more teachers that they brought into the fold, the greater the likelihood that information could get out. Tsukishiro would naturally become more cautious if he realized people were working to expose his fraudulence.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I also intend to ally myself with Ayanokouji-kun for the time being,” said Sakayanagi. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sakayanagi. Just because you know about Ayanokouji’s situation doesn’t mean that you should give him special treatment. That would be a problem,” said Mashima-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Whatever are you talking about? It’s only natural that I give him special treatment. No, it’s my right,” Sakayanagi rebutted directly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Your right?” he asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s right, it is. Even though this school is built on the individual classes competing with one another, there are naturally a variety of circumstances that still come into play. Some students betray their classes because of a friend or lover they have in another class. Others may cooperate with students from other classes for financial gain, or be coerced into helping another class. A single shared emotion can cross the boundaries between classes, resulting in the formation of a cooperative relationship. Hasn’t that always been the case at this school? In fact—hasn’t that always been the case for human society, as a whole? Am I wrong?” argued Sakayanagi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She was asserting that everyone had someone they afforded special treatment. And no one could deny that.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even if I were to abandon all of my classmates in Class A and leave them to die, figuratively speaking, choosing to save only Ayanokouji-kun instead, the instructors would have no ground to criticize me. The only people who could resent me for it would be the students who’d been sacrificed,” she added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was sure Mashima-sensei</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;"> </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">was none too pleased about what Sakayanagi just said, but he didn’t argue.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That being said… I suppose he may not necessarily welcome special treatment. But the question of whether or not he would is a separate matter,” said Sakayanagi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do you mean?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It means I will be watching carefully until the acting director is gone. After that point, things will be different. And if Class D does become an obstacle for Class A, I&#160;will crush them, without mercy, whenever and wherever.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. Very well, then.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Mashima-sensei had understood and accepted Sakayanagi’s statement, acknowledging the force of her will.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Just to confirm once more, there is no evidence of Acting Director Tsukishiro’s wrongdoing anywhere?” he asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sure it’s already been erased. Even if we tried to investigate now, it would be pointless,” I replied. There was no way he’d be stupid enough to deliberately leave any evidence behind.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Then it seems like all we can do now is wait for his next move,” said Mashima-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The teachers knew much more than we did about the exams that awaited once we entered our second year. I&#160;figured I’d let Mashima-sensei and Chabashira figure out how and when Tsukishiro would be making his move at that time.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s been over thirty minutes now. We can’t stay away from the thank-you party forever. First, you students should leave. Then we’ll each head out separately.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We understand,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakayanagi and I left the reception room at the same time and went into the hall, walking side by side.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It was a drastic decision, but being able to bring Mashima-sensei over to our side as an ally is a big plus. As the person in charge of the first-year grade level as a whole, he can get closer to Acting Director Tsukishiro than anyone else,” said Sakayanagi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. Even if he can’t stop Tsukishiro completely, he’ll be an effective deterrent,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I suppose I am a little concerned that his sense of righteousness might be a bit too strong. That is one of his flaws,” said Sakayanagi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, I suppose you’re right. He is reliable, but it’s also possible that he might become a liability.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And if Mashima-sensei gets in too deep, he’ll likely get fired, which might be a mercy. Well, if he’s the kind of person who’d do this sort of thing anyway, I suppose that’s bound to happen to him sooner or later,” reasoned Sakayanagi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When I looked at Sakayanagi’s side profile as she spoke, she seemed quite happy.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You look like you’re enjoying this,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I am having quite a lot of fun. Aren’t you, Ayanokouji-kun?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I dunno about that. From my point of view, this is all just a big headache. And you’re here because—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, because it looked like so much fun. Was I a bother?” she asked, immediately admitting the reason why she came.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No. You coming here helped in persuading Mashima-sensei. I’m grateful.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m glad.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakayanagi turned toward me and smiled.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Besides, we absolutely must not let the school interfere in our battles again and again through such fraudulent means,” she said, expressing her intense indignance at Tsukishiro’s injustice.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Our goal was to completely and totally take down Tsukishiro. The battle ahead of us would be intense.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Our enemy is unprepared. We ought to settle things as soon as possible,” she added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">From Tsukishiro’s point of view, we were just mere high school students. He was underestimating what we could do. That was the weakness we could exploit.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ayanokouji-kun. For the present, please make every effort to eliminate Acting Director Tsukishiro.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, please feel free to let me cut loose,” I&#160;replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Whether or not she could be trusted wasn’t something that I needed to worry about right now. Based on our interactions thus far, I thought I understood Sakayanagi’s personality quite well.</span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">2.5</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">A</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">FTER THE TWO STUDENTS</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> left the room, Mashima let Chabashira know what his honest thoughts were.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There are still some things I can’t quite get a grasp on,” he admitted.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I feel the same way, Mashima-sensei. But the fact is, what Ayanokouji said was true.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Interfering in the way that this school operates just for a single student?” he lamented, finding he still struggled to make sense of this, no matter how much the people around him urged him to believe that it was real. “You’ve had your eye on Ayanokouji for this past year. What kind of person do you think he is?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s a difficult question, actually.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Not wanting to linger too long, the two teachers left the reception room about a minute after Ayanokouji and Sakayanagi had departed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“At first glance, he seems apathetic and indifferent. He’s a normal student, the kind who doesn’t stand out. The kind you might find anywhere.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The homeroom teachers leading the other classes probably had a similar impression of him. In fact, they probably didn’t have much of an impression of him at all. They’d barely be able to recall his name or his face.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But I can’t believe those eyes are that of a child. Those eyes remain unfazed by anyone, even adults, and they can see through anything and everything.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m still skeptical, though.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s certainly fair. I mean, for a first-year high-school student to be described in that way—it makes sense that you’d have doubts.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ve been a teacher for several years now, and I’ve seen all kinds of students at this school. In the past few years, I had gotten the impression that Horikita Manabu and Nagumo Miyabi are particularly outstanding students,” said Mashima.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I certainly can’t argue that,” replied Chabashira.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Both students had excellent academic and physical ability. They were the best in their respective grade levels, and each possessed unparalleled charisma, to boot.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I was under the impression this year’s crop of freshmen can’t compare to those two. There are some students who come close to matching them in certain areas, of course, but not in </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">every</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> area. What do you think is the extent of Ayanokouji’s abilities, overall?” asked Mashima.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Will what I tell you impact what you’ll do from here on out?” asked Chabashira.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, it won’t. Regardless of what kind of student Ayanokouji is, I have no intention of letting Acting Director Tsukishiro do as he pleases. I’m simply curious is all.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Curious… It’s not often that I hear you say something like that, Mashima-sensei. At any rate, I’m still trying to figure it out myself.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira was also one of the people who couldn’t help wanting to know more about Ayanokouji. The truth of the matter was that even if she wanted to give Mashima an answer, she couldn’t. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sounds like we’ve really gotten ourselves into a quagmire here,” said an exasperated Mashima, arms crossed. “Teachers are supposed to maintain an appropriate distance from their students and remain in a position to have jurisdiction over them. It is not wise to establish such a strange relationship.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And in order to do that, we need to get rid of Acting Director Tsukishiro as soon as possible,” answered Chabashira.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Will eliminating him really be enough to put an end to all of this?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do you mean?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s no guarantee another assassin won’t be sent in after we expose whatever injustice is going on here. And if that happens, this will grow from a personal problem for Ayanokouji to something that affects his entire grade… In fact, depending on how things go, the entire student body could be affected,” said Mashima, sounding uneasy.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That being said, he would never abandon a student in need. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m afraid this situation is going to become ever more dire,” he added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I agree.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If the situation did escalate, parts of the student body would be denied their chance at a fair assessment. That was something the teachers had to prevent at all costs.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I hope my prediction doesn’t come true.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The two teachers, imagining how the situation might unfold, hoped that their concerns were unfounded.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">2.6</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;"> </span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">KILLED SOME TIME</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> after my discussion with the teachers and Sakayanagi ended, then headed over to the gymnasium. Before long, the thank-you party would be over, and the third-year students would come pouring out. All I needed to do was wait. The first- and second-year students gathered here seemed to be getting more and more nervous as the time drew near.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Some of the third-year students would be leaving school today, immediately after the graduation ceremony ended. Some of the students here might have something that they wanted to say that they hadn’t been able to share before today. How many people were here in total, I wondered? As far as I could tell, there were nearly a hundred. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This included a familiar figure some distance away from the rest of the group.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you’ve come after all, huh,” I said, greeting Horikita as she stood amongst the other students waiting in the area.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She responded with a glare.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…What? Is it bad that I’m here?” she snapped.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, it’s not bad. In fact, I’m honestly a little impressed.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Impressed? I don’t understand what you’re trying to say.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I was just thinking that if you were still the person you used to be, you probably wouldn’t have come,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita seemed somewhat put off by my compliment. “Is that so? I’m just me. Nothing about me has changed.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She denied that she had grown. Or rather, she rejected self-reflection. Well, I supposed it wasn’t so much that she rejected it as it was that she couldn’t admit to it in front of other people. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The thank-you party in the gym must have ended, because the doors finally opened. It seemed the graduation ceremony was now officially over. This moment in time was officially the last chance for graduates and the current students to interact with one another. After being dismissed, the third-year students came pouring out of the gym. Many of them were aglow, but some of them weren’t smiling, perhaps because of the sadness of leaving school, or because they hadn’t been able to graduate from Class A. </span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you really okay with that? It’s possible that he could be leaving tomorrow.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s… look, I already know that without you having to tell me, it’s just…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita, faced with an insurmountable dilemma, seemed to be retreating, already deciding to make her way back to the dorm. I couldn’t exactly force her to stay put, so I decided to just watch her go.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re not heading back?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nah. I’m going to hang here for a little while,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see… All right then.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She seemed a bit curious about what I was going to do, but ended up turning around and heading back to the dorm anyway. As for me, I decided to see how Horikita Manabu and the other third-year students were doing, though there wasn’t anything particularly interesting about their present activities. If anything, it was just that I wanted to capture the scene and burn it into my memory. I tried to imagine myself there in two years’ time, but couldn’t really picture it yet.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The excitement lasted for a little while, but then I saw one person leave, followed by another person. Eventually, the crowd dispersed, and everyone started to go their separate ways. Horikita’s brother must have finished saying his goodbyes. He spotted me and then approached.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re still here?” he asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was sure he understood as well as I did that I kind of stood out here.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Were you waiting for me?” he added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Something like that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even seen from a distance, it was obvious I wasn’t talking with any of the other third-year students.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I thought this might be the last chance I have to talk with you. When are you leaving the school?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hasty as it was, I decided to come right out and ask him the all-too-important question. If he was going to be leaving right away, I needed to tell his sister about it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The afternoon of the thirty-first. I’m planning to ride the twelve-thirty bus,” he replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Meaning he’d be leaving a week from now, huh? It wasn’t this very day, it was still soon. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Looks like Suzune left,” he added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“She just burned the image of your three years at this school into her memory for now, then headed back.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We both briefly glanced in the direction of the dormitory building. Naturally, Horikita’s sister was no longer in sight.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is that so?” he asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I couldn’t really read any emotion in the look on his face: not joy, anger, or grief. At any rate, if things continued this way and they didn’t set something up, the two of them wouldn’t meet again before Horikita Manabu left. At least, that was what I feared…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If it’s all right with you, I’d like you to pass on a message to Suzune. Tell her that I’ll be waiting by the main entrance at noon on the thirty-first,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wouldn’t it be better if you told her that yourself? If you go after her now, you’ll still reach her in time.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If he was willing to meet with her right now, this talk could happen fast. Horikita Suzune would probably come running right away.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s possible she won’t be too receptive to that. I want you to be the one to tell her.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That might backfire. If I tell her, it’s possible that she won’t come.” She did have a stubborn side, after all.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If that happens, it just means that Suzune has made her choice,” he answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you really okay with that?” I asked to make sure, but he responded without any hesitation.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. I’m leaving this in your hands.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If I wasn’t going to be held responsible for her saying no, I had no reason to refuse his request. Besides, I was sure his sister would come running to see him when I relayed the message. The thawing of her heart had already begun.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wanted to talk with you a little more, but I have plans,” he added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It sounded like he’d gotten invitations from several of his kouhai. I supposed that for today at least, he wanted to forget about his sibling and just be his own person, as a student here.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And I suppose you probably don’t want a long, pointless conversation either.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, you’re right about that,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">No matter how much his popularity had declined, I supposed that the former student council president still stood out quite a bit.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you don’t mind, I’d like you to come see me off on the thirty-first, too,” he answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not really good at saying goodbye in front of a big crowd of people.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No need to worry. I don’t plan to invite anyone other than you and Suzune to see me off that day,” he replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In that case, I figured it would be okay. I gave him a gentle nod, agreeing to his request.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry for the bother,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">And with that, he left. He was the only person out of the graduates that I had wanted to talk to, so since he was no longer here, my business was finished. I figured I’d head on back too, then.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, Ayanokouji-kun, if you don’t mind, how about we head back together?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Just then, Hirata called out to me. Even though he was some distance away, I could tell he’d just finished talking to a large number of third-year students.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You all set?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. Even though today’s the graduation ceremony, most of the graduates will be staying here for a few more days. And it sounds like some of the people I’m pretty close with are having their own individual farewell parties anyway,” said Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Considering who Hirata was, I was sure he had invitations for several of those parties. I supposed some graduates would be staying here at this school as long as they could, which would be April 5. That wasn’t too far off. It was safe to say most of the students were trying to take care of what business they had left before then.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Since I had no reason to turn Hirata down, I decided to head back to the dormitory with him.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">2.7</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">W</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HEN WE WALKED</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> past the convenience store, Hirata turned to look at me. Then he turned back and faced forward again, as if nothing had happened. He’d done this repeatedly in the past few minutes, over and over again, like he was trying to find the right moment to strike up a conversation with me…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Eventually, Hirata opened his mouth to speak, as if he had finally made up his mind on the matter.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“To tell you the truth… there’s something I kind of want to talk to you about, Ayanokouji-kun,” he said ambiguously.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">For a moment, I’d thought he was going to bring up the year-end exam. But it didn’t seem like that was what this was about.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Something on your mind?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well… Yeah. I think this is something I want to discuss with you,” replied Hirata, after giving it a little thought.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not too sure if I’ll be able to help, but you can tell me anything,” I told him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It didn’t feel bad at all to have Hirata depend on me. But I couldn’t predict what he was going to ask me about at all. I knew he was still depressed about Yamauchi being kicked out of school, but that matter had already been resolved. He might still have some lingering feelings smoldering deep down inside of him, but they weren’t worth discussing. He should have dealt with those issues, at least to the point where he could handle the rest of it well enough on his own.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, this might come as a surprise to you, but…” said Hirata, prefacing his story with that statement. “It’s well, I, well, I don’t really know if… well, if I’m ready for a romantic relationship right now.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This certainly was unexpected. I never imagined a day would come when Hirata would come to me to talk about romantic relationships.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You don’t know?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">For now, I figured I’d listen to the whole story. I urged him to keep going.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I think it might be because I’ve never really liked a girl that way before, but…” said Hirata, sounding somewhat embarrassed by it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re saying you’ve never gone out with a girl before?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Putting aside the deal that I had with Karuizawa-san, yes, that’s right.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It might not be really all that surprising…but it was at least a </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">little</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> surprising. I had thought Hirata, who treated people equally regardless of gender, had to have had at least some romantic experience. Though I supposed his relationship with Kei didn’t count, after all. He’d only pretended to be her boyfriend in order to stop her from getting bullied. Still, saying that he’d never liked a girl that way before…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you saying that there’s no one who’s caught your eye, even now?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, that’s right…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The fact that he could see every girl in an equal light was something of an advantage, but at the same time, it was rather odd. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, what about Mii-chan, then?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Mii-chan was very eager to start a relationship with Hirata, and clearly had romantic feelings for him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I can’t really see myself being more than friends with her. But I can’t really come out and say that, either,” he replied.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><img alt="" height="100%" id="INTERIORIMAGES_3" src="/hola-lector-agua/images/books/v11.5/OEBPS/Images/INTERIORIMAGES_3.jpg" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style=" top: 0px; vertical-align: top; height: 100%;"/></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Mii-chan had said she wanted to start out by being friends. Naturally, she wanted things to develop from there until they became lovers. But if Hirata wasn’t willing to go that far, there was nothing that could be done. And if he avoided making that clear to her for no reason, but let this situation drag on instead, that wouldn’t be good for Mii-chan, either.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So that’s what this was about, huh? This was what he wanted to talk about. What was worrying him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I know I should come out and clearly tell her how I feel. But it’s hard,” he added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was difficult to make her understand without hurting her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sure that I’m…definitely not making any sense,” said Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I understand,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was precisely because Hirata was a kind-hearted person that he always found himself faced with such distressing dilemmas. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But this is just how you feel right now, right? You don’t know what it’s going to be like in the future, right?” I asked him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Romantic feelings weren’t something that you could control. They could come at any moment, completely unexpectedly, like a switch being flicked on.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">…Probably.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, sure, I suppose. If we’re talking about the possibility of something happening in the future, I don’t know that it couldn’t. But…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I supposed this meant Hirata just couldn’t see his relationship with Mii-chan going any further, huh? It didn’t seem like there was anything particularly wrong with her, in terms of looks or personality or anything. Of course, there were many things about love that couldn’t be measured by those aspects alone.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But I think I can say…with close to absolute certainty that I won’t have those feelings for her.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Though Hirata didn’t know exactly what the future held, he still seemed to have strong feelings about the matter, in his own way. In that case, there was only one thing I could say to him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You should spell it out for Mii-chan loud and clear, because she’d been hoping that for a while now that you would become more than friends,” I told Hirata, looking him in the eyes as I did.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If Hirata kept how he felt to himself, that would mean he’d be making Mii-chan</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;"> </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">wait, too. Given that, it was best to come out and tell her as soon as possible. If Mii-chan continued to have feelings for Hirata after that point, well, she was free to have them. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But Hirata momentarily averted his eyes from me. “…Even if it hurts her?” he asked. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It would hurt her more if you put this off, when you already have your answer now. Right?” I told him, once again looking him in the eyes.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata made eye contact with me again, but then quickly averted his gaze once more, looking off in another direction.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Y-Yeah. You’re right. Of course…” he replied, nodding several times, like he was trying to admonish himself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Then, he seemed to arrive at a conclusion.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m glad that I talked with you about this, Ayanokouji-kun. This gave me courage. I suppose if you’re not prepared to face the other person being hurt in a situation like this, it means you’re just running away,” said Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It seemed he’d succeeded in finding his answer, once again.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, are you able to tell her now?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t know if it’s the right way or not, but I know which option would hurt her more,” said Hirata, sounding like he had weighed his options.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He fell silent, but I could tell what he was thinking. When Hirata had realized that the latter option would be better for Mii-chan’s sake, his hesitation vanished. In the past, he would probably have continued to agonize over it, and it would have taken him a long time to come up with an answer. His thoughts and his emotions would have been lost in a labyrinth, as he continued to search and search for an option that would allow him to settle things without hurting the other person. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">A little while after his problem had been resolved, Hirata looked like he still wanted to say something else.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What’s up?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, um, well… it’s just, I was wondering… Is it okay for me to call you Kiyotaka-kun from now on?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was wondering what he was about to say, but that was completely out of left field.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And, um, if you like, you can call me by my first name too…” he added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Was it safe for me to say that this meant our friendship had taken a step forward? I suppose this was just like how my relationship with Keisei, Akito, Haruka, and Airi had deepened in the past, too.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you’re okay with it, then sure, of course,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When I said that, a huge smile appeared on his face, one so overflowing with happiness that he looked genuinely delighted.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Really? You mean it?” he asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I mean, we’re just talking about using first names, right? I’m sure it’s not out of the ordinary for you to be on a first-name basis with people, right Hirata? Oh, I mean, Yousuke.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Although I had the impression that Hirata usually referred to people by their last name, regardless of their gender, he was probably on a first-name basis with some people. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I suppose it wasn’t that unusual until that whole incident,” he replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He was referring to what happened when he was in junior high. When his best friend had been bulled and then had attempted suicide. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ever since then…I was afraid of getting close to people. Instead of trying to form real relationships and find someone really special to me, I just treated everyone equally,” said Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It had been about two years since then. Apparently, he’d only ever referred to people by last name during that time. Come to think of it, he really did treat every student exactly the same, no matter who they were. Even Yamauchi, who had been unanimously kicked out of our class. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It looked like Hirata was breaking out of his shell. And this time, he was doing it on his own. His growth was pretty significant, even compared to how much many of the other students had grown over this past year.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, I’m especially grateful to you…Kiyotaka-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He directed his gaze back toward me, after having averted his eyes for so long. From the look in his eyes, he was trying to tell me something.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That makes me feel kind of embarrassed,” I said. “You being so grateful to me and all.”</span></p>
