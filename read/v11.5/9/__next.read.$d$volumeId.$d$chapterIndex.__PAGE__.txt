<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 3:<br/>Hiyori Date</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HE GRADUATION CEREMONY</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> and the closing ceremony had both ended without incident. Spring break was finally here, and the students forgot all about competing with each other and welcomed the short break. Although current students obviously weren’t allowed to leave the campus, they didn’t feel particularly inconvenienced by that, largely due to the presence of Keyaki Mall.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The mall was an essential amenity not just to students, but to everyone affiliated with the school. I’m sure I don’t need to explain this in detail at this point, but it had everything you needed, from cafes to electronics stores, karaoke, and so on. If there was something else you really wanted, you could get it via mail order after putting in a request and getting permission. You were free to live life according to your own whims, as long as it was within what your stock of Private Points would allow.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Fortunately, no one in this year’s crop of freshmen would be going hungry due to lack of points. Even the lowest-ranked class, Class D, would be receiving an allowance of tens of thousands of points on April&#160;1</span><span style=" font-size: 0.75em; vertical-align: super;">st</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">. When you considered the average allowance of high-schoolers throughout the country, it was obvious that the amount we received was almost too much.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, more than a few of those students had troublesome situations on their hands. I could count myself as one of them. As part of a contract that I had with my classmate Kushida, I promised to provide her with half of my income. Although that contract had served my own purposes at first, things had begun to change. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">What should I do about the contract I had with Kushida—or rather, what should I do about my relationship with her in general? I’d have to decide that during spring break. Should I proceed as originally planned? Or&#160;should I choose a different option? It was no longer up to me to make that choice, though. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">At any rate, spring break had only just begun. There was no need to panic. I slipped into my casual clothes and got ready to head out. I had planned on spending most of my spring break just relaxing lazily in my room, but I had a brief appointment with a certain someone today. I’d thought it would’ve been a little later before I heard from them, but they contacted me surprisingly early. Once they did, I got in touch with someone else.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“One last check, I guess.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was the first day of spring break, so I’d need to make some adjustments. But that wasn’t a problem. My meeting today was of significant importance. Significant importance not for today, but rather, for the end of spring vacation.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HE DAYS BEGAN</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> getting warmer and sunnier in late March. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hello, Ayanokouji-kun.” Hiyori, looking quite striking in her casual wear, waited for me in front of Keyaki Mall.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re early.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I was the one who called you, so it wasn’t like I could keep you waiting,” she replied, flashing a small smile. “Sorry for inviting you out today on such short notice.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I didn’t really have any plans for spring break, anyway. Don’t worry about it. Besides—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“They finally stocked some new books at the library just yesterday,” said Hiyori, showing me the bag she was carrying. Her smile got even bigger than before. Shiina Hiyori, from first-year Class C, was a girl who loved reading more than anything else. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I thought I’d share this information with you as soon as possible, Ayanokouji-kun,” she added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The books by an author whose works Hiyori and I especially loved were hard to find at convenience stores and mall bookstores. They weren’t even available as ebooks, so the library was our only way of getting them. I&#160;supposed we could always put in special orders for them, but the library helped books reach a wider audience. I&#160;cherished being able to discuss a book with someone that way.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There are more people here than I thought there’d be.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Students filled the seats at tables all around the café. It was spring vacation, I supposed. Depending on the time of day, the café could get really crowded. Fortunately, there seemed to be some seats available at the counter, so we headed over there.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We’ve never really had the chance to meet up like this on a holiday. It’s kind of refreshing, isn’t it?” said Hiyori.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But then, her hand suddenly stopped. She looked up, as if just remembering something.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Before she could continue, a loud voice came booming from behind us.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Aw damn. This place is </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">packed</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">, dude. Aren’t there any free tables?”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In the midst of the café’s relaxed atmosphere, the two new arrivals sat down in the seats that had just opened up. They seemed to be in the middle of a conversation, so they didn’t notice us. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The two of them had seemed like they were getting closer, just a little while ago. Apparently, that trend was continuing.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She wasn’t leaning in close enough to be whispering into my ear or anything, but she was conscious enough about the volume of her voice that Ike and Shinohara wouldn’t overhear.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">For some reason, the two of us remained silent, leaning in slightly to listen to Ike and Shinohara’s conversation instead.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It looks like our monthly income has gone back down to being under thirty thousand again,” huffed Ike.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s just how the cookie crumbles. I mean, we were up against Class A. It’s not like we stood a chance of winning,” said Shinohara.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, you might be right ’bout that. And we’re gonna be back to bein’ Class D from next month, huh? Ugh, lame.” Ike scratched his head. He must have been thinking back to when we lost during the year-end exam.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But, well… Do you know why we lost?” asked Shinohara.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What? You mean whose fault it is?” said Ike.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">For a moment, I thought he was going to say my name, since I was the commander. But…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It was my fault. Mine,” said Ike.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Shinohara’s eyes went wide after she heard Ike make that surprising remark.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, technically, I guess I’d say it feels like I’m </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">one</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> of the reasons why we lost. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“W-Well, I suppose you’re right, yeah. But still, it’s a huge surprise for you to come out and say that, Ike,” said Shinohara.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, hold on now, I think you’re forgetting somethin’, Shinohara. You called me by just my first name. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, look who’s talking. You just called me Shinohara. So, I guess that makes us even,” she snapped back.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">They continued to reflect on what had happened at the end of the year, occasionally inserting some pointless tangents. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“When we start our second year here, I think I’m gonna try even harder. In both classes and sports,” said Ike.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh, really? I can’t imagine you’re going to keep that promise.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, I’m not gonna be perfect or anything anytime soon. But I was just thinking I’d take things seriously,” said Ike. From the way he said that, it sounded like it was more than simply a passing thought.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Just out of curiosity, why though?” asked Shinohara.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Ken and Haruki.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Not too long ago, those three had been good friends, referred to as the Idiot Trio by other people in our class. I remembered having gotten fairly close to them when I first started here, but we eventually drifted apart. More accurately, I suppose, I should say that I was ejected from the group.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I mean, Ken’s totally not the type for it, but he’s been studyin’ like all the time lately, right? <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It does seem like his grades have improved,” added Shinohara.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, for sure. His grades are really getting better, little by little, and he’s already super good at sports. It’s like, I get the feeling that there’s not a single thing that I can beat him in.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But you’ve gotten better at your studies, haven’t you, Ike?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If we compared Ike and Sudou as they stood now, Sudou had a better chance of coming out on top in both academics and sports.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“He’s…probably gonna reach new heights next year,” said Ike.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It seemed like he was happy about a close friend growing, on the one hand. On the other, he was terrified about being left behind. And the greater reason for this fear being instilled in him was…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If things stay like they are, I’m probably the next one to get expelled,” he added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ike…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The lower a student’s position within the class, the more likely it was that they would face expulsion. That fact was inescapable. Yamauchi had exhibited a lot of problematic behavior, and been sacrificed for it. Ike was beginning to feel like he’d be next.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t laugh. Don’t tell me that it’s not like me to say this kinda stuff,” said Ike.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, it is true that it isn’t like you to talk like this, but… You know, I’m pretty much the same as you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Shinohara didn’t exactly have good grades, nor was she someone with any significant talents. Despite their gender differences, they were both in a similar position. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Besides, I couldn’t laugh at someone who wants to work hard,” she added, with a determined nod. “I’m going to work even harder next year, too. There’s no way I’m going to lose to you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hell, ain’t no way I’m gonna lose to you!” replied Ike.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Their relationship seemed to be progressing well. In the future, there would be other students who were inspired to work harder themselves after seeing these two. If someone forged on ahead, others would follow. Mutually-beneficial relationships of that kind were extremely important.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, hey, Shinohara.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hm?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ike, who sat next to me, started speaking in a serious tone. But serious in a different way than before. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, it’s… There’s somethin’ I want to talk to ya about. Is that okay?” </span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, you know, it’s just like, it feels like we’re the kind of friends who fight sometimes and all, but… I mean…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hiyori and I looked into each other’s eyes. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah!”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What am I doing…? What, is there a problem with me being at the café?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“N-no, there’s not, but like, dude, you should’ve at least spoken up! I didn’t have any idea you were here! You totally snuck in out of nowhere!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I’d thought trying to talk to him in this kind of situation would be out of the question, though. Also, he said I snuck in out of nowhere, but I was here first.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Dude. Don’t tell me you heard what we were talking about. Did you?” he asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">were</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> you talking about?” I asked in return.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When I turned the question around on him, he averted his eyes, flustered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“N-nothing. I mean, does it even matter?” he replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Shinohara, who had been listening to Ike and I going back and forth, commented on something else, though.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Wait, Ayanokouji-kun, are you and Shiina-san dating?” asked Shinohara, who seemed to be able to tell we hadn’t come alone.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Of course, since she’d seen the two of us sitting here at the café together, I supposed that it wasn’t surprising for her to ask that.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, it’s not like that. What about you?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, no way, no. Ike and I aren’t really like that,” she replied, flatly rejecting the idea that they had </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">that</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> kind of relationship. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ike quickly jumped in, perhaps because he didn’t really like what he just heard. “Y-yeah, Ayanokouji. I&#160;mean, don’t get the wrong idea, okay? Like anyone would wanna be with this uggo!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh?! Who are you calling ‘uggo,’ uggo?!” snapped Shinohara.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You!” he shot back.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Wait, no, hold on, why are you two fighting now?</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">They both stood up and glared at one another, completely destroying the good vibes that had existed just moments before.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ugh, this sucks!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You took the words right out of my mouth. And here I went out of my way to make time for you during spring break and all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? Wait, what? Huh? I had no other choice </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">except</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> you! I was out of options!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What the hell?! You’re such a scumbag!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I thought they were going to sit back down, but for some reason, they walked off somewhere else, still squabbling as they went. We’d been on the verge of seeing a new couple be born—but alas, things took a sudden turn.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are they…going to be all right?” muttered Hiyori, also taken aback by the sudden change in the situation.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Anyway, though, you were about to say something before, weren’t you?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, well, yes, I was. Oddly enough, it’s actually something strikingly similar to what those two were just talking about,” replied Hiyori.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Strikingly similar? I unintentionally jumped a bit when I heard her say that. She couldn’t be referring to how Ike had been about to tell Shinohara how he felt, right? Something romantic?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Those thoughts went through my mind for a moment before I immediately rejected the idea.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s something I’ve wanted to ask you, Ayanokouji-kun, about the year-end exam,” she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Oh, well. I supposed Ike and Shinohara </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">had </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">been talking about the year-end exam, too.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What did you want to ask me?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sorry if my reasoning is off base here. I’ll just come right out and ask. Were you the one who changed Ryuuen-kun?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hiyori’s gaze was filled with curiosity, and not a hint of ill intent. Come to think of it, I had noticed the very first time we met that she possessed a keen insight.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Normally, my response to something like that would be, ‘What do you mean?’”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Playing dumb and pretending I had nothing to do with it would have been the best course of action for me to take. The reason I didn’t was because there was certainty in Hiyori’s eyes. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, I suppose so. But I figured you’d understand without needing me to explain, since you’re, well, you, Ayanokouji-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Changed Ryuuen. Most people would probably shake their heads to hear such a thing. The people who wouldn’t either had some degree of understanding of the situation, or were the person who had changed him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why do you think that?” Rather than trying to mislead her, I decided to come right out and ask Hiyori her reasoning. I was hoping that she could tell me why she was so sure.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It was just a matter of slowly putting all the pieces of the puzzle together. Ryuuen-kun was obsessed with you and your class, Ayanokouji-kun. But after a certain point in time, he just completely dropped off the stage. Ostensibly, it was because of a revolt led by Ishizaki-kun, but that just seemed like smoke and mirrors to me. I&#160;became convinced of this when I brought Ishizaki-kun and Ibuki-san, who were formerly close associates of Ryuuen-kun, back in contact with him.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It seemed Hiyori had executed several strategies which I’d been completely in the dark about. And she had her suspicions about Ryuuen bowing out so quietly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If this upsets you, I deeply apologize for that. I was really worried about whether or not to talk to you about this today. I thought that I might make you angry by bringing the matter up, Ayanokouji-kun. No matter what the truth may be, I knew from looking at you that you didn’t want to talk about this,” said Hiyori.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So you brought up the topic after preparing yourself for the potential consequences.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This was on a completely different level from everyday chit-chat. This was a decision that she’d made after careful consideration.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If we can’t be friends anymore because of this, then… I will definitely regret it. If I’m not able to be next to you anymore because of this, Ayanokouji-kun, I will absolutely regret bringing it up,” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In that case, it would have been better for her to have kept this to herself. But even so, Hiyori had decided to bring up the topic today.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I just thought that if I didn’t bring this up, we wouldn’t make any further progress,” said Hiyori.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Any further progress?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When I asked her that question, Hiyori’s mouth hung agape, like she was shocked. She seemed surprised by what she had just said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, well, um… I suppose I don’t really understand what I’m even saying, myself,” she replied, with a somewhat puzzled look on her face. “Um… Have you heard about the battle between our class and Class B?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Only the results.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t know the particulars. Changing the topic, Hiyori began explaining how they had won.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. Normally, what you did would be considered problematic,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s certainly true that Ryuuen-kun’s way of doing things has no shortage of problematic areas. But I also think there are some necessary evils we must commit for our class to move up to the higher levels. Do you think what we did was unfair?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I can’t deny that part, at the very least.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even if it wasn’t a praiseworthy method of doing battle—even if it meant people would talk about them behind their backs—it was a way for them to secure victory for their class. Society needed people like that. To fight a lonely battle that would earn you no praise, an indomitable force of will was essential. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s just, well, there can be no doubt we’ve crossed into extremely dangerous territory. I’m sure some students from Class B are starting to have their doubts, but I don’t think they’ll find any concrete evidence. We avoided all the security cameras that are installed everywhere throughout the school,” said Hiyori.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There were many security cameras installed through the campus. They were in the school buildings, of course, but places like Keyaki Mall and its surroundings were also under their watchful eye. However, it wasn’t like they were everywhere. There were no cameras in the bathrooms or private spaces like the karaoke rooms. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If Ichinose and the other students in Class B spoke up and said that there was something strange about what happened, there would probably be an investigation. But the investigation would probably stall out, unable to find anything conclusive. It was unlikely that there would be any developments from it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You got five pretty brilliant wins. You could say the plan went perfectly, couldn’t you?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Brilliant? I don’t think so. If anything, I would say that our methods were extremely flawed.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How so? That it was possible to get six or more wins?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Five wins is great. But, well, if anything, I think we got greedy. Ryuuen-kun adopted an extremely dangerous strategy in order to get those wins.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hiyori, reflecting on what had happened, analyzed what had gone down during the last exam. Then, she told me how they had won.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“While I think putting continued pressure on Class B students was fine, attacking their physical health was clearly a bad move. Even if it’s a move we resorted to because there are so many good, virtuous people in Class B, it’s not an acceptable one.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I felt exactly the same way Hiyori did. I knew the girl in front of me had led an entirely different life from me. There was no way we could be alike, and yet, I was certain there were some similarities in our thought processes and ideas. Which was precisely why some doubts started to creep into my mind when I heard her speak.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You knew of this before Ryuuen had employed his strategy. And yet you didn’t stop him?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you think that he’s the kind of person who would listen to my advice?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I supposed that unlike Ishizaki and Ibuki, Ryuuen might at least have lent an ear to Hiyori. But he probably wouldn’t have actually taken her advice. He’d never accept someone else’s suggestions—all he would do was sneer at them.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’ve got a point there. In that case, how do you think Ryuuen could be stopped?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wanted to see how far she had thought ahead and how much she had acted. That was the answer I wanted to get out of her. Perhaps Hiyori intuitively understood that, too. Understood the reasons that brought her here today.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“By someone equal… No, rather, by someone </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">more</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> capable than him. More than anything else, he would only respond to reprimand from someone who’d caught his interest,” said Hiyori.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ryuuen wouldn’t listen to anyone’s advice—unless it came from someone Ryuuen acknowledged. Which was precisely why Hiyori was telling </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">me</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> about all this.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hiyori. Would you mind passing along a message for me?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I deliberately chose not to use words that might confirm anything she had asked me about. What I’d already said would be enough. If I were talking to someone else, it might be different, but Hiyori probably wouldn’t use her current position to make trouble for me. She understood the significance of the fact that Ryuuen, who had recognized me as the leader of my class, didn’t make my existence public knowledge. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What is it?” she asked in return. Her face didn’t change, but she looked at me kindly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Tell Ryuuen that if it were me, I could’ve easily and safely taken the win in five or more events. Tell him that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Okay, I understand. I’ve committed the message to memory. I’ll make sure to pass it along.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hiyori smiled, eyes squinting happily, and lightly clasped her hands together as if in gratitude. Ryuuen had himself a good ally, in addition to Ishizaki and Ibuki. If Hiyori could control the tendency the three of them had to run amok, they’d become even more formidable. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">And so, we finished our discussion of the year-end exam.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, then…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Normally, I would’ve parted ways after getting to this point, but the most important part of our meeting was still to come.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you find one that catches your eye, please take it home with you and read it,” said Hiyori, once again opening her bag and taking out some books. It was these books that we’d originally planned to meet here today to talk about.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is that all right? These books were checked out under your name though, weren’t they?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ve already gotten permission from the librarian. Although it’s really not encouraged, the librarian said everything would be all right as long as the books are returned on time.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hiyori was probably something of a VIP in the library. It wasn’t surprising she was getting special treatment. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We engaged in an animated discussion about books for a while, then finished our drinks and parted ways.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It seems I need to change my assessment of her a little,” I muttered to myself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Up until now, I’d only thought of Hiyori as another student in my grade. Or more specifically, as a friend with whom I shared a common interest.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Shortly after parting ways with Hiyori, I met up with Kei, who had come to Keyaki Mall.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…What do you want?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The first words that came out of her mouth were spoken in a harsh tone. She didn’t seem to be in a good mood.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How about you take a seat first?” I asked in return.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I encouraged her to take the seat that had just been vacated by Hiyori leaving, but she refused, only briefly glancing at the spot. Her face suggested it disgusted her. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Weird rumors would start going around if people saw us sitting here together,” she replied, staring far into the opposite direction.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If a third-party were observing us right now, even from a good distance, it probably wouldn’t look like Kei and I were talking to one another.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Would it be a problem if talk like that got around?” I&#160;asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, it’d be a big problem. You understand that if you carelessly interact with someone of the opposite sex, word gets around like right away, right? It’s like you don’t get that at all,” said Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It seemed that was exactly what I was doing right now: carelessly interacting with a member of the opposite sex.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So? What did you want?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry. I completely forgot. When I remember, I’ll contact you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had already taken care of what I needed to do regarding Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What? Ugh, this is ridiculous… I’m out of here,” she huffed with an exasperated sigh, turning her back to me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t try to stop her, but just watched her go. That just worsened her mood, which I supposed was completely understandable. She was in a bad mood because I deliberately made her feel that way.</span></p>
