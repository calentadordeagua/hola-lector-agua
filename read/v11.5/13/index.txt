<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 7:<br/>Adolescence About to Begin</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">Q</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">UITE A NUMBER OF THINGS</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> had happened around me just during spring break. There was the incident with Matsushita, and before that, the ones with Horikita and Ichinose. There was the establishment of a cooperative relationship between Director Sakayanagi, Chabashira, and Mashima-sensei. And finally, there was my bargain with Tsukishiro.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I should probably be most vigilant of all about Tsukishiro. Unlike the other issues at hand, the problem he presented would just worsen if I ignored him. The next thing I knew, I might be getting told I was being kicked out of school. That was exactly why I needed to coordinate with the teachers to try to deal with this situation. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As for what Tsukishiro had said about sending in a student from the White Room, it wasn’t necessarily definite, but it was plausible. There was no way Tsukishiro could come and go in and out of the classrooms and the hallways all hours of the day. He could only attack me through indirect means, like the exams. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But if I was dealing with a student, that was a different story altogether. A student could come and go as they pleased in the classrooms and hallways. They could even come up and make contact with me at any time, affording them more chances to get me expelled. It would be a solid move in terms of gathering intelligence, too. If all of that did become a reality, it would be the biggest change that had taken place around me yet. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">And then there were Horikita and Matsushita. Problems internal to the class, so to speak, each having their suspicions about my true abilities and wanting to know my full potential. I’d promised Horikita I’d have this little competition with her, but there was probably nothing else I needed to do for the time being. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As Ichinose, that could wait a while. After seeing what battles lay ahead, all that remained was to casually, indifferently, do what should be done. However, that only applied to the things that were happening around me. As for my </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">internal</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> changes…well, those were still very subtle. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">At least…that was true until today.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There were only two days left until spring break ended. Just Tuesday and Wednesday. On these days, when students enjoyed their final rest before the new battles to come, I was determined to make a big change and take some action. If I was going to get things moving, this was the time.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was now past six, the time of day when the sun started to set and night began to fall. There was a question I’d like to ask a great number of people, if possible. Let’s say, hypothetically, that there was someone of the opposite sex whom you liked. How would you push forward and open a path to the point where you would tell them you had romantic feelings for them?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I supposed that if you were an exceptionally beautiful man or woman, you could immediately confess your feelings to someone without needing to go about it in a roundabout manner or draw things out. If you said you liked them, and they said they liked you too, it would be happily ever after for all involved. But most people weren’t so blessed. People were insecure about their faces, their personalities, their bodies. Complications like love-triangles might form obstacles on the path to telling someone you love them. Ultimately, it was clear that confessing your feelings to someone—the gateway to romantic love—was certainly no easy task. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Which was precisely why I was mulling it over so seriously in my head, letting my thoughts carry me. I&#160;thought&#160;hard, racking my brain to calculate the certainty of success of such an endeavor. Of telling someone how you felt. Ten percent? Twenty percent? Or was there a success rate of fifty percent? I supposed that you might have an eighty percent, ninety percent, or even close to one-hundred percent rate of success, depending on the circumstances.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But even so, there would be uneasiness. People would be afraid that if their attempt failed, they’d get shut down when they told the other person how they felt. That the relationship they had with the other person would change dramatically. There might be a few positive-minded people out there who could shrug off such things, but to high schoolers, school was our world, and that world was everything. The possibility that the relationships you’d built within that world might fall apart was terrifying.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">And so, people took such things very seriously. They’d think about what they could do in order to raise their chances of success, even if only by one percent, and start working toward that end. They might change their hairstyle to their crushes’ liking, as much as they could, or dress fashionably. They might study hard and work out, or try to approach their crush’s heart through their stomach by cooking them meals or going out to eat. They might even give them gifts. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">They would use every trick in the book to try to improve their chances. Sometimes, this might make said chances jump from one percent all the way up to ninety-nine percent. But a single failure could also make your chances plummet fall from ninety-nine percent down to one percent. People would try desperately to read the other person to sense what they were feeling. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Such was the process of telling someone that you had a romantic interest in them. And I…experienced that process in the same way. I thought and I worried, just like any other man or woman. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But these feelings weren’t limited to romantic love. Speaking more broadly, there were unseen probabilities in everything, and they could fluctuate depending on what happened from day to day, just like how studying could increase your chances of being accepted into high school and college. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Additionally, simply being aware of all this could greatly change your understanding of the situation. It didn’t stop at school entrance exams or romance, either. Even if you succeeded at those things, it wasn’t like that was the end. If anything, it was the start of many </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">new</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> things. If you failed in your attempts to seek higher education, it might lead you to leaving school or getting expelled. Also, your love life could fall apart through cheating or violence. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I envisioned what the future would look like. A&#160;month from now. Six months from now. A year from now. Sometimes plans do change, but I didn’t exactly like acting in the spur-of-the-moment. That was especially true when it came to self-initiated actions. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Now then, let’s go back a bit. Everything I’d done up until today had been to affect a certain probability. In other words—to increase the probability of success, of course. Whether I’d be met with success or failure would be revealed today. If I was reading things correctly, I should be hearing from someone right about now. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The phone I was already clutching in my hand rang. There was a nondescript 11-digit phone number shown on my phone’s display. The number wasn’t registered in my phone, but it belonged to Karuizawa Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s me. Sorry for bugging you earlier,” I said, answering the phone after it rang a few times.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had called Kei about thirty minutes ago, but she didn’t answer the phone then. She was just calling me back now.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s fine. What’s up?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You sound like you’re irritated.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Not really. I guess, rather than saying I’m irritated, it’s more like I just want to make sure of something with you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“About the time when I asked you to come out before, and didn’t call back later?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">On the day that I met up with Hiyori, I had asked Kei to come out. But in the end, I didn’t tell her anything we’d had talked about. All I’d said to her was that I could call her later if I remembered. And I didn’t even try to get in touch with her again until close to the very end of spring break.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It seems like you already know, then. What, are you </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">trying</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> to annoy me?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How about we talk about this in person?” I asked, cutting in. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“About what I said. That I’d tell you if I remembered. I&#160;remembered. Can you come now?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“For the love of… You do things based on your own time and convenience way too much, you know? But fine. But you know, if someone does happen to see me going to your room at this time of day, you sure you’re not gonna care what happens?” said Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There would be many students coming and going in and out of the dorms at this time of day. The chances that Kei could be seen visiting my room were high. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t worry about that,” I replied, encouraging her to come.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay. Oh, I do have plans at seven though, so I can’t take too long.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll make it quick. Probably about ten or twenty minutes.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, it’s no problem. See you soon,” said Kei, before hanging up.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Now then… Let’s get started, shall we? All preparations had been made. I looked around my room, which was even tidier than usual inside. I briefly glanced over at the mirror, gazing at my reflection with a serious look on my face. Then I quickly averted my eyes.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">7.1</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">K</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">EI SAT IN MY ROOM</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> looking quite displeased and sullen. Judging from the fact that she was dressed nicely, it did certainly look like she had plans to go out later.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So? What is it?” said Kei, giving me a sour look when I didn’t start by saying anything. I supposed it wasn’t like I could call her here and then just stay mum.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do you mean, what?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">you</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> mean, what? You called me here because you said you wanted to talk, didn’t you?” said Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, yeah, I did say that, come to think of it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“………”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“………”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The displeasure in Kei’s eyes got even more pronounced as I continued to act evasive.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Come on, what is it already?” she huffed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Come on now, no need to be in such a rush.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I told you this before, but I have plans. I’m having dinner with my friends at seven at Keyaki Mall. Understand?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s still plenty of time. Don’t worry.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay, this is kind of giving me some bad vibes now. It feels like you’re talking complete nonsense.” Kei was starting to get suspicious, since I was behaving differently than usual. Since I wasn’t saying anything, she elected to complain instead. “…That reminds me. I need to tell you that I’m </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">not</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> happy with you.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is there something you want to tell me?” To be honest, I really didn’t know what Kei wanted to say, so I just asked her that straight out.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Satou-san has some suspicions. About our relationship,” said Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Satou. A classmate whom I hadn’t interacted with much lately, but who’d taken a liking to me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I thought she’d hate me after I turned her down. What kind of suspicions?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“She thinks the reason I broke up with Hirata-kun was so I could start going out with you. She tried to confirm her hunch in a sort of roundabout way,” said Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Meaning that Satou had avoided directly saying anything about her suspicions, but said something ambiguous that could be interpreted that way instead.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I denied it, of course. But I have my doubts about how much she actually believed me,” said Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. I actually have a similar story.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? What do you mean, a similar story?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Matsushita. She has some suspicions about our relationship. Like if we’re dating.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When I told Kei about the conversation I had with Matsushita the other day, her face went as white as a sheet.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? HUH? You’re kidding, right? Or wait, are you serious? You’re not joking?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I nodded to show that it was certainly no joke. I&#160;continued speaking, explaining what happened.
