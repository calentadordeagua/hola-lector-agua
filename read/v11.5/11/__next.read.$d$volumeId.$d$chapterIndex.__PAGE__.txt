<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 5:<br/>From Older Brother to Younger Sister</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HE NEXT DAY</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> March 31, was quite a special day for me as well. Yes—it was the day Horikita Manabu left. Our agreed meeting time was exactly noon. As usual, I did things early and arrived at the front gate. He must not have told his other kouhai about him leaving today, because there wasn’t no else around except for me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">From time to time, while I waited for the others to arrive, I looked at the students headed toward Keyaki Mall in the distance. Come to think of it, I had come to this school through this very gate a year ago, hadn’t I? The location was one I often came close to, but never really walked right up to. There were times when we might head through the gate on a bus for things like special activities and exams, but other than that, the only way you’d walk through this gate would be if you graduated or got expelled. And since there was no system in place for students to repeat a year, we would inevitably see those things happen within our three years here.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I feel like I’ve been thinking about that all the time lately,” I muttered to myself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Now that we were about to enter our second year, I’d been reflecting back on my current state of mind more and more. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Just then, close to twenty minutes before we were scheduled to meet, Horikita Manabu arrived. After confirming that it was me, he briefly glanced around at his surroundings. I didn’t even need to bother asking what he was looking for.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Unfortunately, your sister still isn’t here yet,” I told him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Looks that way,” he replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Right now, it was around 11:40 a.m. She certainly wasn’t too late, not by any stretch. However, considering how little time there was left, it would have been better if she had arrived earlier. I thought back to when Horikita and I met with Ichinose the other day. The fact that she had gotten to the meeting place then with plenty of time to spare was still fresh in my mind. Was it possible that she had gotten into some kind of accident?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I think I should try giving her a quick call,” I suggested.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I figured that if I were to make the call, then Horikita’s brother would agree to the proposal quite easily. That’s what I had thought, anyway, but…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, there’s no need.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He refused my offer, gently stopping me with his hand.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If she fell or something, she would’ve let us know ahead of time,” he reasoned.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s possible she might just be sleeping in.” It was highly unlikely, but I thought I’d mention the possibility.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If that is the case, then there’s no need to wake her up,” he answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He was saying that if she were to oversleep on such an important day, that meant she wasn’t worth his time anymore, huh? Even though this was the last chance they had to meet, his attitude was the same as ever.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, there’s nothing to worry about. There’s still plenty of time until we’re supposed to meet.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I could also imagine pretty easily that she was sitting around nervously in her room until the last possible minute, since she was meeting with her older brother.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Suzune aside, though, I didn’t expect you to have gotten here so soon,” said Manabu.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Somehow, I had a feeling that you’d be getting here early, too,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We had agreed to meet at noon. Of course, we still had plenty of time until the bus departed. But this would be a final goodbye. I’m sure that both Horikita siblings must have anticipated that there would be a lot to talk about. And sure enough, Manabu had arrived twenty minutes early. The only thing that both Manabu and I didn’t expect was Horikita’s absence, as she was supposed to be the central person in this gathering.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Anyway, since she wasn’t here, that meant that we could only talk about things with just the two of us. It would be a complete waste to just spend our time in silence. After thinking for a little while, I opened my mouth to speak, telling him about something that had been on my mind lately.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sorry. I probably should have done a little more for you regarding the student council stuff,” I told him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita had come to me to talk, in order to find a way to stop Nagumo Miyabi from running amok. However, at the time, I couldn’t eagerly accept his request, because at the time, I had been wishing for a more peaceful life, much more strongly than I do now. So, in order to establish a connection, he had gotten me acquainted with Vice President Kiriyama, but that’s about as far as things got. In the end, I had gotten all the way here, to today, without enacting plans involving Kiriyama.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Everything has been my responsibility. It was wrong for me to try to impose that on you. Don’t worry about it,” said Manabu.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">For him, this school was a thing of the past. He was in a good position now, where he didn’t even need to worry about what was going to happen here in the future.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But even so, let me just give you one final warning. I basically see the policies of this school as fundamentally positive and lean toward agreeing with them. Even though the school is fundamentally based on the idea of a meritocracy, it leaves enough room for the lower-ranked classes to be able to win. Though it’s by no means an easy battle,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I can’t think that argument is all that convincing, considering you were in Class A for three years running,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But you could say that’s also because a lot of people haven’t realized the true nature of things. It is certainly true that there are many areas in which the school can improve. However, in retrospect, you should be able to understand something. Whether it’s the uninhabited island exam or the year-end final exam, there are always chances provided to the lower-ranked classes to beat the upper-ranked classes,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It wasn’t just with written exams. Other factors needed to be taken into consideration quite strongly for the special exams. For example, on the uninhabited island exam, it wouldn’t be that difficult to beat Class A or Class B by simply coming together and showing solidarity. It was the same kind of thing for the year-exams, too. Although it was an exam where luck certainly played a huge role, it was also proof that there was the possibility for the lower-ranked classes to win.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Luck plays a big role in determining victory or defeat. It’s a necessary consideration, so that the still inexperienced, immature first-year students can have a chance at winning against the higher-level classes. However… that is also something that is difficult for the upper-level classes to accept. It’s an element that they’d probably revile,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The school’s considerations for the students at the bottom would certainly draw complaints from students at the top. Putting aside the ability for students to move between classes by accumulating twenty million private points as a special case, the system this school operated in such a way by treating each class as a fundamental unit, and also, the system would not abandon those students who were less capable. In every class, there are superior students who excel, and there are those who struggle even at the lower levels.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I’m guessing that Nagumo had experienced a year going through examinations just like we did, and then had come up with an idea. He wanted to create a system that was even more meritocratic, which allowed individuals to rise up and win on their own power. A system where the people at the top would keep rising and the people at the bottom would keep falling.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What Nagumo’s trying to do might not necessarily be wrong, though,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">While I’m sure some students in our grade level could be dissatisfied with what he was doing, at the same time, there were many students who agreed with it. And in the case of the second-year students, you could say that the majority of those students would agree with Nagumo. Of course, that probably wasn’t simply because they just happened to be agreeable people. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I’m sure that more than a few students had no choice to agree to it, after being swept up by the state of things around them. If everyone was to excel, then every class would have to be fiercely competitive.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is there a pretty significant gap between second-year classes? In terms of their Class Points, I mean,” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. Class A, which Nagumo is in, has one thousand four hundred and ninety-one points as of March. Class&#160;B has eight hundred and eighty-nine points. Class C has two hundred and eighty points. And Class D has seventy-six points,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Considering the fact that they only had one year left, that meant that Class A was already in position to win. Despite that, though, Nagumo dared to propose something that could save the lower-level classes. It was certainly true that with only seventy-six points, it was&#160;nearly impossible for Class D to turn things around.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There are a lot of people who agree with him. If they have no way to win as Class A, then their only way to get to Class A is to cling to a system where individuals can rise to the top,” I reasoned.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You might be right. But Nagumo’s ways of doing things would make a great number of people unhappy,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If the system becomes too meritocratic and individualistic, then suspicion and distrust would grow, even among classmates. It was possible that everyone around you could become your enemy. Horikita’s brother, no, Horikita Manabu still insistently believes that the cooperation of the group, of the classes, is absolute. Not only that, but also, that it was an organization designed with the future in mind. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Won’t it be the same with how the system works now, though? All three classes except for Class A will be unhappy,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I could only imagine just what Nagumo’s ideal system would look like, but if a system that allowed individuals to rise up were to be established, it might add a little something extra, some added relief, for classes that had forty students or fewer.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, for example—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Just as I was about to say anything more though, Horikita cut me off.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Consolidate the Private Points of all the students in Class B and below all together and use them to compete in a bid to see who gets to move up to Class A. Or something like that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He thought of the exact same thing that I did. I&#160;nodded in response. Not taking expelled students into account, the total number of students between Class B through Class D was one hundred and twenty people. If you put together all of their Private Points, that would most likely exceed twenty million by quite a bit. It was possible that you might even get forty or sixty million.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Of course, not everyone would likely take part in that gamble. Although I don’t know how the system might have changed, not too long ago, there was a system in place where students could convert their Private Points into cash upon graduation. I’m sure there were some students who probably wouldn’t mind graduating from Class D as long as they were able to get that cash.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, students who could meet the conditions to participate </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">and</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> secure the number of Private Points needed really ought to take that gamble. Even if they can’t win with the rest of the class anyway, it’s not a bad idea to try to make one last gamble. Besides, that way, there should be a few more students who can get up to Class A. The larger the gap between Class A and the lower levels in terms of Class Points in your grade level, the easier it would be for this last chance to become a reality.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Have students not talked about that in your grade level?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’d be lying if I said that people haven’t talked about it. But no one actually did it. Because Class A and Class B were in such close competition, Class C and Class D didn’t have enough points to be able to make it happen,” replied Horikita. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I recalled a student from third-year Class D that I had gotten in contact with almost a year ago now, who seemed to be in desperate need of points. If you keep losing, it would become more difficult to get Class points. If you find yourself falling into a situation where you have to keep spending month after month with only zero points, you’ll be trapped in a downward spiral.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If that’s all there is to it, then there won’t be any effect yet. However, Nagumo is planning on stirring things up into a frenzy, one which will drag even his own class into it, Class A. In other words, he’s going to put even his own allies at risk as well.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That meant that the students in Class A whose abilities were meager faced the possibility of falling by the wayside. That was probably likely. If only Nagumo’s own class, Class A, was safely in the clear, then there was no way that everyone else would approve his appeal to create a meritocracy. Every class, whether it was A or D, would be placed on a level playing field.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t know how far he’s planning on taking things, but even still, it’s a decision that you need courage to make,” I reasoned.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“He just feels bored, he’s bored with his current situation, that his victory is already assured. That’s probably the reason for all of this. The reason why he originally joined the student council in the first place was largely just because he wanted to kill some time,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If he had the ability and also, the endorsement of others, then no one really had the right to complain, though.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The class is a whole, where everyone is in the same boat together. Everyone has a shared destiny. I don’t think we should move past that framework,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Which is why you can’t agree with Nagumo’s way of doing things,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Although he didn’t nod in response to what I said, Horikita’s brother seemed to accept what I said. I understood what he was trying to say, but you couldn’t say for sure which one of them was right. And besides…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m planning on seeing what Nagumo is trying to do, for the time being. If he’s going to change our grade level, no, rather, our entire school into a more meritocratic environment, then I can’t deny him without experiencing that environment,” I told him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I decided that I would simply state what I was going to do, with no lies. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. You’re going to soar to even greater heights than I did,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You think way too highly of me,” I told him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Furthermore, I just didn’t have the inclination nor the means to stop Nagumo right now. In that case, it wasn’t a bad idea for me to take a look at the world that Nagumo was trying to create. I was able to firmly etch this past year into my mind, this year that Horikita’s brother had been protecting.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m really not as great a person as you think I am,” I&#160;added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, sorry to say, but I don’t think that’s true,” he replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita firmly rejected my modesty.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In any case, it seems like your assessment of me in your mind isn’t getting any worse,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If there was anything that would cause it to get worse, it would have,” he replied. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Come to think of it, he hadn’t really changed his opinion of me at all for almost a year now. No matter what he knows or doesn’t know, that standard didn’t change.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I just can’t wrap my head around it, though. What exactly is it about me that you find so praiseworthy?” I&#160;asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The only bit of information that he had about me, information that was different from what other students had about me, came from that little fight we had, when I was trying to stop him from going too far and hurting his sister. In addition to that, he had knowledge of my test scores from when I had enrolled here. Test scores that frankly were a ridiculous joke. Other than that, what else he knew was general information: he knew that I was a fast runner, as evidenced when I ran against him in that relay. He didn’t actually know how capable I was in academics or in sports.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I can sense someone’s talents through my own senses and intuition, to a certain extent,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So, rather than anything concrete, it was an abstract idea, huh. For him to be able to evaluate me so thoroughly with that kind of method was impressive. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, what do I look like to you, exactly, with your senses or whatever you call it? I’d like to know, as a parting gift,” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was curious, so I thought I’d try asking him. In fact, I was thinking I’d compare his evaluation of me and see if it was the same as the one I had made of myself, in my own mind. I’m sure that Horikita’s brother would be able to give me an answer, without any kind of unnecessary filter.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“All right. From what I see, you’re…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita Manabu paused for a moment after saying that, thinking back on the past year that he had spent watching me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Based on everything I’ve experienced in my life thus far, you seem to be someone who deviates greatly from what I can predict. No matter what angle I try to come at you from, I can’t find any flaws with you. I don’t think anything would work on you. I don’t think I’d be able to deal with you in terms of brute force, such as in physical strength. Not to mention strategy and resourcefulness, either. Out of everyone I’ve ever met, you are the last person I’d ever want to fight,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That’s an extremely generous assessment. Especially considering that he’s saying all that simply by what he can sense.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, does that mean you’re raising the white flag? You’ve completely surrendered to me?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s a different matter. Even against a completely perfect opinion, there’s always a chance to win,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I felt slightly relieved after hearing his response to my question.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“At this school, it’s especially true that everyone competes as Class A, as a unit. No matter how outstanding the individual, there is a limit,” he added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s true. That’s exactly why I think it’s interesting,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ayanokouji. What kind of environment were you raised in? I’m certain that it’s not like all of your abilities happened to be instilled in you by accident, naturally. And they’re not the kind of abilities you could easily pick up because your family found you a thorough tutor,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You didn’t grow up in a normal family either, did you?” I asked in return.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If you were an elite student who even as student council president, you probably knew how to rise to the top.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s not as though I’ve stood at the top of everything since the beginning or anything. There were times when I struggled, when I only made little progress. But I have worked tirelessly in light of that fact. From early childhood and even to now, and beyond,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita’s brother was saying that he had gotten to where he was thanks to the accumulation of all the effort he’s made. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I suppose if I were to put my answer into that sort of logical pattern, then maybe I’ve put in even more effort than you have,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…I suppose so,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">To beat those who put in effort, you put in even more effort. That wasn’t the answer to everyone, but it was certainly one answer. Horikita Manabu then took out his phone. He showed me the screen, which displayed a phone number on it. Then, he changed what was displayed on the screen and showed me a different number.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Remember these two sets of phone numbers. The first is mine. The other is Tachibana’s. If you have any trouble after graduation, feel free to contact us, any time. If you can’t memorize the numbers right now, you can jot them down in your notes. But make sure you delete those notes later,” he told me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Contact with people outside of the school was forbidden, even by phone or other means. If I carelessly jotted down the numbers and made a record, that would only be a disadvantage for me. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“An apology…?” asked Horikita, her face still buried in her brother’s chest. She didn’t know what he was talking about.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…That was really cruel, oniisan.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I have no excuse.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Maybe her brother purposefully didn’t come clean about the lie because he was trying to detect some kind of change in his sister, whom he believed </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">would</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> change some day.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">E</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">VEN THOUGH</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> Horikita’s brother could no longer be seen through the gate, she and I still continued looking in that direction for a while.
