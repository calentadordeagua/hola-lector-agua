<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">T WAS NOW</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> just after eight thirty in the evening on Sunday. The day Nanase had chosen was finally here, and it was highly likely that the discussion we would have tonight would determine whether we could work with Class 1-D. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Well, no. We needed to make </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">sure</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> we could work together. Most students outside both our classes had already found partners. If we couldn’t come to an agreement, we might be forced into making some rather major compromises to avoid being hit with penalties. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was decided that me and Horikita would handle this discussion, with Sudou accompanying Horikita after he strongly requested to join her. I was sure he did just want to be around Horikita, but also that a big part of his reason for doing so was that he was wary of Housen. Depending on how things played out, Housen might very well raise his hand against a woman without batting an eye. So Sudou was there to be Horikita’s bodyguard, to protect her. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita protested of course, saying she didn’t need him there, but Sudou insisted. This time around, though, Horikita didn’t actually give Sudou permission herself, no matter how many times he asked. These negotiations were expected to be just that serious, and she had decided Sudou’s presence would be a hindrance.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, I made her reverse her decision. The reason I gave was that Sudou could act on my behalf, in the unlikely event something unforeseen occurred and things got dicey. Sudou’s abilities should be more than enough to keep the situation in check. In the end, Horikita gave Sudou permission to accompany her, on the condition that he absolutely not lose his temper during the discussion and not threaten anyone.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When I went down to the dormitory lobby to meet up with him, Sudou was already there, sitting down on the sofa, waiting. He smiled cheerfully and happily at me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yo!” he shouted.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Actually, I should make one small correction to what I said before. It seemed like he didn’t just want to be with Horikita. He </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">really </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">wanted to be with her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are your studies going pretty well? For the test?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Course, man. Still won’t be great, but I’m thinkin’ I&#160;should at least get two-hundred-and-fifty points this time ‘round,” said Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If Sudou, who currently had an academic ability ranking of E, got two hundred and fifty points or more on this text, that would be a fantastic achievement. I was sure his rating in the OAA app would probably jump up to somewhere around C starting next month. Sudou wasn’t just all talk, either. He was putting in enough effort that his claims were backed by confidence. He was arriving at class late far, far less frequently, and demonstrating a good attitude </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">in</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> class, taking his studies seriously.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Seems like you’ve changed a lot… It’s like you enjoy studying now or something,” I told him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, it ain’t really like I </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">like</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> it or anythin’. But it is pretty fun solving problems. On top of that, when Suzune compliments me, I feel like I get so amped up that I could just study and study all the time.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The prickly, snappy attitude he had when he first started school here seemed to have calmed down. It did seem like his tendency to quickly lose his temper wasn’t going to be so easy to fix, but if Horikita’s presence was enough to keep him grounded, that was good enough. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sudou stood up and looked at the screen showing footage from the camera inside the elevator, as if he couldn’t contain his excitement. Then he sat back down on the sofa, fiddling with his phone and with his hair. Shortly afterward, he stood back up again. He kind of looked like a young man who was about to go out on his first date.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, Ayanokouji,” muttered Sudou, still looking at the camera footage. Maybe he had realized that I had been looking at him. “If I were to tell Suzune how I feel about her today, do you think she’d be, well, okay with me?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The expression on his face was visible, even as I looked at his side profile. He looked serious. Seeing Sudou look that way, there was no way I couldn’t give him an honest answer.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Probably not,” I told him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">While that might have left him feeling dejected, those were my honest thoughts, looking at it as a third party. I had thought for sure he wouldn’t have been satisfied hearing that answer from me, but…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, you’re right.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sudou agreed with me without batting an eye, almost like he already knew the answer himself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I know that Suzune ain’t the type of person to talk ’bout stuff like love and romance and all that. But it’s not just that, either… I mean, there’s no way she’d be attracted to me right now. How much trouble has my cockiness made for her? How many times? No, not just her—how many times have I made trouble for everyone in our class?” said Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He seemed to be saying that there was no way Horikita would date him after taking all that into account. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And yeah, I’m really tryin’ my best now and all, sure. But I don’t think that cancels out all the burdens I put on our class or anythin’, of course. So over the next two years, I’m gonna work on improvin’ my strengths, and also on fixin’ my weaknesses, little by little. If I do all that, then I’m sure that by the time we graduate, I’ll have been useful to our class,” said Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. Yeah, you might be right about that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sudou could definitely become a valuable asset to our class, thanks to his exceptional physical abilities. I was sure he could come to be an essential part of our team, like Yousuke and Kushida. He’d also grown to be able to look at himself calmly and objectively, which was precisely why a question came to mind that I wanted to ask him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, so what if you put in a lot of effort and become the most admirable, exemplary person in our class…but even then, Horikita still doesn’t look your way. What would you do then? Would you start to hate stuff like studying?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There was the possibility someone could fall apart when they learned all their efforts had been for naught. That was particularly possible in Sudou’s case, since he was putting in all this effort </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">for</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, yeah, I’m sure I’d wanna just stop everything, y’know? I mean, I’d probably even feel like I wanna die. Maybe I’d even want to go out and punch somebody. But if I actually went out and did any of that stuff, I’m sure Suzune would be real disappointed in me. I mean, givin’ up on studying, going on a rampage? That’d be super lame. I definitely don’t wanna do any of that, no thanks,” said Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">An excellent response. I was sure he really believed it, too. Of course, the real truth of his words would be tested when that situation became a reality. No matter how much you claimed you were resolved to handle being faced with a negative outcome, chances were good you’d feel differently once the pain actually hit. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But that being said, if he was able to say all that right now, at this point in time, then there probably wasn’t anything for me to worry about for the time being.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, looks like she’s here!” said Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The camera showed Horikita getting on the elevator. Sudou must have been feeling restless or something, because he walked away, turned his back to the elevator door, and started taking deep breaths while stretching his arms out in an effort to calm himself down, like he was doing warm-up calisthenics or something.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Shortly afterward, the elevator arrived at the first floor. Sudou was still taking deep breaths.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry to have kept you waiting. What is Sudou-kun doing?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Looks like he’s taking deep breaths,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita had a puzzled look on her face for a moment, but she quickly went back to her usual stiff expression. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We were going to meet up in one of the karaoke rooms in the Keyaki Mall today. On both regular weekdays and on weekends, these were popular places for people to hang out late into the night, since we were allowed to use those rooms until 10 p.m. Karaoke was, of course, one of the recreational facilities we had on campus. It was often used for things like stress relief or to chat with friends. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But at this school, it had another major purpose. The rooms were private, which made them terrific locations to have detailed discussions, without running the risk of being seen or heard by others. They were the easiest places to have secret meetings on campus without being noticed. Of course, in terms of confidentiality alone, nothing beat the privacy of one’s personal room in the dormitory. But you were inevitably limited to only being able to meet certain people that way.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">With the exam coming up next week, there didn’t seem to be that many people around at this time of day. So you could say this was the best time to meet with Housen in secret and have a discussion.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, are you really sure we can get that cocky new kid to help us out?” asked Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wouldn’t have spent so much time on this if I didn’t think we could make a cooperative relationship work,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Exactly. It was precisely because we had determined it was possible that we were here today.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“At this current point in time, many of the gifted first-year students have been taken by Sakayanagi-san and Ryuuen-kun. And Ichinose-san has offered herself up, providing salvation for the weak. The only weapons in our arsenal are points or trust,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Guess you’re right, yeah… We ain’t gonna beat Sakayanagi or Ryuuen in points, and we ain’t beatin’ Ichinose when it comes to trust,” said Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Precisely. Which is exactly why Housen-kun presents both an opportunity and a problem for us.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Housen wouldn’t be swayed by the appeal of Class A’s reputation, nor by some paltry sum of Private Points. And he wouldn’t so much as even notice Ichinose’s offer of salvation. Which was why we, Class D, had a chance. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, the point is seein’ how much we can push them into makin’ a deal with us, without havin’ to make too many compromises on our end,” said Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Right. The more time that passes, the more it’ll be us second-years who start to panic. With so many students having already found partners, we’ll be at an unavoidable disadvantage.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If we declined the terms Housen presented us with, he’d show no mercy. He’d simply change plans and let us end up getting paired with people at random. He didn’t care whatsoever about his own classmates being penalized. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was interested to see how Horikita intended to challenge him.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sudou didn’t quite understand Horikita’s reasoning on that, but he kept quiet, and followed along. While Sudou seemed to think of our opponent as just a new kid, Horikita showed no signs of dropping her guard. It might seem like she was being cautious to the point of excess but considering that this was Housen we were dealing with, I supposed you couldn’t criticize her for being careful. </span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Got it,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I sent Nanase a message, basically telling her that we had already arrived. As expected, she replied saying they would arrive at the time we had planned on.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Let’s go ahead and order our own drinks, then,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Shouldn’t we wait for ’em?” said Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s fine.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After each deciding what we wanted to drink, we looked at the food menu next.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You can go ahead and order, if you’d like anything. What do you want?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, fries, I guess. Is that okay?” said Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sure.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita, using the landline phone installed in the room, ordered the food and drinks that we had wanted. After we placed our orders, Sudou seemed to be a little less tense. He picked up the microphone.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, um, well, I think we got some time until we’re supposed to meet. How about a song or two?” he said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not going to sing,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait, really, you’re not?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We had arrived at the karaoke room early, before the meeting, and had ordered food and drinks. I supposed Sudou had assumed the next step would be to start singing, since that was typically how things went at a place like this. He looked disappointed now, probably because he’d wanted to hear Horikita’s singing voice.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sudou-kun. I’m warning you once again. Do not say anything unnecessary,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I-I got it, yeah. But hey, shouldn’t you tell that to Ayanokouji, too?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“He doesn’t speak more than necessary. Actually, if anything, he’s the sort of person who doesn’t speak even when he should,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Well, that was far from a compliment. She was just airing her grievances about me. Sudou pouted, seemingly not too happy with the answer Horikita had given him. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When it was time for our meeting, the first to arrive was Nanase.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry to have kept you waiting,” said Nanase.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Outta the way, Nanase,” huffed Housen Kazuomi from behind her, forcing her to walk further inside.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, you’re right on time. And here I was sure you’d be really late,” said Horikita, suggesting she wouldn’t have been surprised if he’d arrived late on purpose, just to irritate her. Rather like how Miyamoto Musashi had arrived late to his duel on Ganryu-jima.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, I’m always on time when I decide I’m gonna go somewhere. And I don’t like folks who give me a hard time just because I’m a little late. Anyway, looks like you got here pretty damn early… What, did you really hate the thought of waitin’ for me that much? Come on, don’t be so nervous,” teased Housen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Can you please not let your imagination get the better of you? Don’t misunderstand. We were simply enjoying ourselves, since we came all the way to karaoke. Understand?” said Horikita, telling Housen that he needed to broaden his mind.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Upon the table were some partially consumed drinks, and half-eaten food. She had staged the scene to look as though we’d been having fun at karaoke right up until the last minute.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Looks that way,” said Housen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Which meant that the negotiating had already begun.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, whatever. I’ll find out whether you’re bluffin’ soon enough, when we get to talkin’,” said Housen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He plopped down hard on the couch, as if he were some kind of big shot, so much so that it was difficult to&#160;imagine he was a new student. He spread his legs wide, taking up enough space for three people all by himself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay, so? Nanase explained everythin’ to me. She said you wanted my class to help you out,” said Housen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">My</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> class, he said. From the sound of it, Class D was already completely under Housen’s control, then. It had only been a couple of weeks since school had started, but I sensed no weakness in his statement.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Not exactly. What I’m saying is that our two classes will work together. No one will be above or below the other. We’ll be on equal footing,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That so? So, you’re not bringin’ up the fact that you’re one grade level above us then, huh. Gotta say, not patronizin’ us, that’s a wise choice.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Nanase quietly observed what Housen was saying and doing. She didn’t speak up or refute any point in particular. Considering she’d been playing the role of mediator, and the fact that she was the sole person he’d brought here with him, we could probably be certain Nanase was someone Housen valued. I wasn’t sure if that was because he admired how she had the guts to say she wouldn’t give in to Housen’s threats of violence, or if there was something else. In any case, there was a way for us to cut right through and force Nanase over to our side.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I understand there are still a certain number of students in your grade who don’t feel an intense sense of commitment to their peers, and wouldn’t think anything of it if their classmates got in trouble. However, you should begin to understand how things are after seeing us. There will most certainly come a time in the future when you will need the help of your classmates,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So…you’re sayin’ we gotta together to get through school without anyone gettin’ kicked out, huh?” said Housen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you really do have so much authority over your class that you view it as your private property, that’s actually rather convenient to this arrangement. With just one command, a substantial number of your classmates will follow, right?” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Housen brought his left pinky up, stuck it inside his left ear and started twisting it around a bit. Then he removed his finger and held it up, pointed it at Horikita, and blew off what was on it in her direction. Sudou’s face tensed up, but he remembered Horikita’s warning, and kept himself in check. His clenched fists were shaking as he held them down on his lap. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita simply endured Housen’s openly vulgar conduct, without turning away. “Can you please stop?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay, so, in the first place,” said Housen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It seemed what she’d said just went in one ear and out the other, because he started speaking as though he were talking to himself. </span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The blood from my wound had also gotten on Housen’s clothes. There was no way he could talk his way out of this. Even if he took the knife back now and stabbed himself in the leg, he wasn’t going to be able to spin this in his favor. Of course, if he </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">did </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">try to do that, I’d just use my full strength to stop him. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Housen, standing across from me right now, could likely sense this much himself. The important part was what came next.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Horikita, Sudou, and I can keep this matter between us,” I told him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What are you plottin’? You’re just gonna throw away this valuable chance to get me thrown outta school?” said Housen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In exchange, I have two conditions.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Two?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He probably knew the first one without me having to say it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’ll agree to initiate a fair, cooperative partnership with Horikita, between our two classes.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, ain’t like I got much of a choice, since if I say no, I’ll get kicked out. What’s the other condition?” asked Housen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I want you to partner with me for the upcoming special exam.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">From the moment I’d first laid eyes on Housen, I had thought that if I were in a position to select whatever partner I wanted, he’d be the guy I’d pick. While I had several reasons for thinking so, the biggest one was that he didn’t seem to care one bit about attracting attention to his problematic behavior. If I were in Tsukishiro’s position, I would have instructed whoever he sent after me to avoid doing anything to stand out. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If negotiations with Horikita didn’t work out, I’d considered reaching out to Housen privately and trying to come to terms with him. In that sense, this chain of events was actually quite convenient for me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…You serious?” asked Housen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’ve only just enrolled here. There are still countless things you haven’t done yet. If you get expelled right now, it’ll be all over. You’ll never get to enjoy any of them. I&#160;don’t know how things were back when you were in junior high, but all that talk about how you’re a match for Ryuuen will end up being just that—talk. People will say you really weren’t that big a deal. And based on what I’ve seen from Ryuuen over the past year, you can’t even begin to compare to him as you are now. He’s strong,” I&#160;replied, taunting him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You…!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Housen Kazuomi was an incredibly proud guy. He thought highly of himself, considering himself a strong person. Even though he might be physically stronger than Ryuuen, it would infuriate him to be told Ryuuen was actually better than him. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">More importantly, though, there was no way he could stomach the idea of being outsmarted by me. If Housen, who had a B+ in academic ability, were to deliberately score a zero on the test, he would inevitable be expelled.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Of course, it was entirely possible he’d try and take me down with him as revenge. While I was as certain as I possibly could be that he wasn’t the White Room student, it was also true that I wasn’t </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">100% </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">certain. I could never fully clear him of that one suspicion, no matter how thoroughly I investigated and examined. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, that had changed now. Even if Housen did deliberately cut corners on the test, the fact that I’d been stabbed remained. If it was clear there’d been something bizarre happening behind the scenes, not even Tsukishiro would be able to force my expulsion right away. The school would certainly want to investigate the matter to figure out exactly what had happened, and why Housen got a zero on the exam. Whatever tricks Tsukishiro tried to pull, I’d hold my ground until expulsion was no longer a possibility. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, all right, pretty good there, Ayanokouji-senpai! Never had an opponent that got my blood pumping like you before. You should know, I ain’t gonna be tryin’ to just make you surrender with brute strength anymore. I’m gonna really beat ya to death, so I hope you’re lookin’ forward to it,” said Housen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That slight hint of him wavering was now a thing of the past. Housen had already changed gears and was focusing on the next battle.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll stay here. There are still some things I should explain to Ayanokouji-senpai,” said Nanase.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? What are you plottin’, Nanase?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ve decided it would be in the best interests of Class 1-D for me to tell him some things. Ayanokouji-senpai and Horikita-senpai are intensely wary of us right now. If that’s the case, don’t you think it would be a good idea for us to make sure they’re cautious of all of the other classes, too?” she answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">While I didn’t understand exactly what Nanase was getting at, Housen seemed to accept her proposal.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Whatever, go ahead,” he replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">And with that, he was the first to leave, heading for the dormitory.</span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">7.3</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">T WAS NOW</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> just Horikita, Sudou, the first-year student Nanase, and me. While I was sure there were a few things for us to discuss, there was something else we needed to attend to first. Namely calming down Horikita, who had lost her cool after seeing the knife piercing my left hand.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wh-what should we do…? The…the knife, I mean, should we…should we take it out?” she stammered. The normally cool and collected Horikita had probably never been in a situation like this before.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No. I know it’s probably not great to look at, but we should leave it alone for the time being.” If we just carelessly pulled it out, it might result in me losing a lot more blood. “More importantly, what about you two? Are you okay?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Looking at your injury, I’d say I’m practically unscathed…” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah… I’m definitely okay too,” said Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He came closer, until he was standing right beside me. His face contorted in disgust when he saw the miserable state of my left hand.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Dude, how can you be so calm with your hand like that?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hm, dunno. I wonder.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I just did what I normally did. Nothing special.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But, dude, like…you are </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">crazy</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> good in a fight, wow…” added Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I just forced myself to stop the knife is all,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…That ain’t how it looked to me though, man,” said Sudou, speaking his mind about what he’d thought when he saw me charge at Housen just a little while ago.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sudou had been in a number of violent situations in his lifetime. I didn’t think I could fake him out, and I probably couldn’t deceive Horikita, either. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I took out my phone with my right hand and dialed the number that would connect me with Chabashira.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m going to need a little help. I’m behind the first-year dormitory building right now. Can you hurry on over? Discreetly, of course. Oh, and also, please bring a bath towel,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Although Chabashira seemed a little baffled by my sudden call, she sensed the urgency of the situation and promised to head over right away. In the meantime, it would probably be best not to move from this spot. It&#160;would be awkward if we tried moving locations and another student happened to see my hand.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even so… Nanase showed no signs whatsoever of being upset. She was completely unfazed, remaining calm and collected even after seeing the knife impaling my hand and the spurting blood. She didn’t seem bothered one bit by the graphic, visually arresting spectacle.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, Nanase, can you tell us what’s going on?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If I don’t, it seems it would only put my class at more of a disadvantage,” she answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You were aware things were going to turn out this way… Is that correct?” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s correct. The idea was that Housen-kun would stab himself in the leg and have Ayanokouji-senpai expelled,” said Nanase. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She didn’t seem to feel guilty or ashamed at all. She explained what they had planned in the same polite tone she usually used.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you saying that the friendliness that you showed us—it was all just part of the act?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, that’s not true. I really do want to join forces with you, Horikita-senpai, and for our classes to support one another. It’s just… The plan to target Ayanokouji-senpai was our top priority,” said Nanase. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Which meant that the reason why Housen and Nanase were so obsessed with our class was because I was in it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why would you do all of that? Also, unlike Ayanokouji-kun, I don’t recall ever forgiving you for what happened tonight. Depending on how things play out, I might consider reporting you to the school right away.” Horikita, unable to understand why I’d been targeted, pressed Nanase to answer.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I think there were problems with how we did things, yes, but trying to get Ayanokouji-senpai expelled from school itself is not counter to the school’s wishes. Very few first-year students know about this, but it’s possible to earn a considerable number of points by having Ayanokouji-senpai expelled,” said Nanase.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Now, the reason why I’d been targeted by Housen finally became clear.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We were given a special exam. For this exam, we were told that anyone who managed to get Ayanokouji Kiyotaka from Class 2-D expelled would be paid twenty million Private Points,” explained Nanase.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What are you talking about? This makes no sense. Who in the world came up with such an incredibly stupid, outrageous special exam?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Nanase didn’t answer that question.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…I’ve told you what I needed to say, for now. I’m sure this will make you far more wary of every class in my grade level, not just us now. Won’t it, Ayanokouji-senpai?” she said instead, turning to me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Nanase hadn’t said much. Really, she’d only told us the bare minimum she needed to. She and Housen obviously knew what was going on, and needless to say, Amasawa was aware of the second special exam, too. Given everything I’d heard, it was reasonable to assume that some students in Class 1-B and Class 1-C probably knew about it, too.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How do you think we could possibly be satisfied with an answer like that? The truth of the matter is, Ayanokouji-kun was seriously injured, and—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m fine. Just getting an understanding of the situation is enough. Thanks for your help, Nanase,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I chose to cooperate with Housen-kun</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;"> </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">for the good of the case, despite knowing how unjust this is. Because the fact remains that if the twenty million points were to fall into the hands of another class, it would create a significant gap between us,” said Nanase.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Twenty million points was essentially a single ticket to Class A. But when you considered things like this current special exam, the more financial power you had, the more advantages you’d gain.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“However, that is not the only reason why I lent Housen-kun a helping hand,” said Nanase.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She spoke softly and calmly, but there was something about the way she looked at me, like she was staring daggers at me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“’Cause I really didn’t… I mean, because I did not think that Ayanokouji-senpai was someone suitable for this school,” said Nanase.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">For the first time, she openly stated her intense hatred for me. But I couldn’t really figure out why. </span></p>
