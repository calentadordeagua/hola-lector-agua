<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 6:<br/>Class D and Class D</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">T WAS NOW THURSDAY</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> and the weekend was drawing near. After class ended, I went to the library with Horikita in tow. It was where we’d be having our discussion with Class 1-D, who Nanase would be bringing with her today. On our way there, we chatted about the special exam.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Have you checked out today’s update yet?” I asked her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Seventeen more partnerships have been finalized. Which brings the total number of partnerships to seventy-three,” replied Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">While there wasn’t anything about the number of partnerships itself that was particularly noteworthy, there was one substantial difference between this last update and the previous two. Namely, the fact that two students from Class 1-D had decided upon partners. There were now some visible signs of activity in a class which had, for the past three days, seemed motionless.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m a little flustered. I thought Housen-kun was planning to wait and see how things would play out a little longer. I tried talking to some Class 1-D students today during lunch, but they just brushed me off, saying they didn’t really know anything about the students who decided on partners,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s hard to tell if they really don’t know, or if they’re just under some kind of gag order.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It wouldn’t be too surprising if there was some kind of rule in place, forbidding them from partnering with anyone or even talking about the whole situation unless one of the smart kids in class got offered a lot of points.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s true. At any rate, it’s great that we’ll be meeting with Nanase-san now. I’m sure she might be able to tell us something about this situation,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita had only ever met Nanase once, and they’d never actually ever had a proper conversation. Even so, Nanase, the person who’d stood beside Housen, seemed to be someone with whom we could communicate. Personally, I’d gotten a strong impression that she was an honest person when we talked. She had a very firm, upright character, vaguely reminiscent of Ichinose somehow.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita and I arrived at the library. When we set foot inside, the first person to greet us wasn’t Nanase, but rather Shiina Hiyori, from Class 2-C.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, hello there, fancy seeing you here,” said Hiyori.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Apparently, this bookworm had come to the library right away after class today.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We might be making a little noise today. We’re going to talk to some first-year students about the special exam,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, is that so? In that case, it might be a good idea for you to use the seats in the far back. I don’t think you’ll bother the other patrons then, and you should be able to talk a little. Also, you’ll quickly notice if someone does try to approach you,” said Hiyori.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I decided to take the advice she had so kindly given me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are things coming along okay in Class C?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, they are. It seems there’s a lot going on right now.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Since our classes were competing with one another, we couldn’t exactly clue each other in on their inner workings. So with that brief exchange of pleasantries, we said goodbye to Hiyori and decided to go take our seats. While I was still kind of curious about what was going on with Hiyori, I went with Horikita toward the seats at the back of the room.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Putting Nanase-san aside, now that we’re getting involved with Class 1-D, it raises the question of whether Housen-kun is going to show up,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. I guess that whether he shows up or not is going to make a substantial difference to how this goes.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Since we hadn’t given Nanase restrictions, there was no guarantee she wouldn’t bring Housen with her. If she did bring him along, we’d just have to go for broke and negotiate for all we were worth, flying by the seat of our pants. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I hope you don’t mind if I ask you something before we start a full-blown discussion. Have you been studying?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, a little bit, here and there. Why, what about it?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I was just wondering whether you’re getting enough time for your studies, considering I have an advantage in this situation by being able to choose the subject.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, what’s this? Taking pity on your enemy?” I teased.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“As if. I’m not so nice as to willingly give up advantageous conditions. This is a contest I must win,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even so, she was apparently concerned about whether I was studying properly or not. Which was to say, she was </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">actually </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">worried that I’d come up with some excuse that I was too busy dealing with the special exam to study, or something like that.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re one to talk, you know. You’re spending a lot of your time getting our class organized,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I always make sure to devote time to my studies. I&#160;have no problems whatsoever,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She seemed to be quite confident that she had managed her time well, with everything she had to do in her daily routine.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, relax. I don’t plan on losing,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, that’s all well and good, I suppose…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She didn’t seem to trust me, strangely enough. She didn’t seem to think that I was taking this test seriously. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">To that point, there </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">was</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> one other thing that I wanted to ask her about. Horikita had a lot of roles to fill, in addition to keeping our class organized. She had to spend time on her own studies and on top of that, she tutored others. I had no idea if she could keep going at that pace all the way up until test day. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Just as I was about to ask Horikita about that though, Nanase showed up at the library, alone. She quickly located us, rushed over toward us, and bowed her head. From the looks of things, Housen wasn’t going to be showing up to this initial discussion.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry to have kept you waiting,” said Nanase, politely.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nah, we just got here ourselves,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita prompted Nanase to sit down in the seat directly across from her and opened the discussion with a brief introductory greeting.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“All right, well… Once again, my name’s Horikita Suzune. Thank you for making the time to talk with us today,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, and I’m… Err, I mean yes, I am Nanase Tsubasa. I haven’t done anything worthy of your thanks, though. If anything, I should be the one thanking you,” replied Nanase.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We were all in Class D, so we all started from an exceptionally humble position. Horikita, after listening to Nanase’s polite and proper words, decided to immediately get down to business. “I’m sorry for how sudden this might be, but do you mind if I ask you some questions?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Not at all, please go ahead,” said Nanase.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“First, just to establish a baseline, I’d like to ask what your class’s policies are. Two students in your class confirmed partnerships today, while the status of the remaining thirty-eight students in your class remains to be seen. You were one of the two students who confirmed a partnership, aren’t you, Nanase-san?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">While it wasn’t clear if it was Housen or someone else, it was clear a member of their class was giving orders here.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s correct. I had a feeling you’d ask me about that. I’m assuming you asked Kajiwara-kun that same question today, yes?” replied Nanase.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kajiwara was the name of another student in Nanase’s class. Apparently, she already knew that Horikita had reached out to Class 1-D students during lunch today. If that was the case, I supposed we ought to assume she also knew that we had reached out to Shiratori and his friends on the first day of the exam period.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m surprised. You seem to have a very good handle on this situation,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Many students are already acting on Housen-kun’s orders,” said Nanase matter-of-factly, openly admitting that Housen was the one taking the lead, rather than being evasive about it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is it because of his aggressive attitude? No, I can’t imagine that’s all there is to it. What methods did he use, exactly?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Nanase looked like she was lost in thought for a brief moment. Then she opened her mouth to speak.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I am terribly sorry, but unfortunately, I can’t tell you exactly what kind of methods he used. Housen-kun brought the class together. Whether the way he did that was right or wrong, I do not know. But letting word of it get outside our class would be an act of betrayal,” said Nanase.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. You’re correct in saying so,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In response to that, Nanase thanked Horikita again and bowed. I supposed that just because we were Nanase’s seniors, that didn’t necessarily mean she was going to tell us anything. Just like in our conversation the other day, she seemed to have firm thoughts and convictions as a member of her class.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, I’ll get right to the point. Is it safe for us to assume we will be able to work with your class, just like those two students who settled on their partnerships yesterday?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“As I’m sure you’ve already heard from Shiratori-kun, our offer is always open. As long as you offer us a set number of Private Points with your proposal, we will accept whatever partnership requests you make, without hesitation,” said Nanase.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So our chat with Shiratori and his friends had reached Housen after all. From that, we could infer a high number of points were paid out to the two students from their class who had gotten partners.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But what I’m asking you about today is different from establishing a partnership contract based on points,” replied Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, I know. I’ve already heard a little about your offer from Ayanokouji-senpai. A collaborative relationship where we would cover for each other—that is to say, mutually work to protect the less academically inclined students in our classes, yes?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. If you’ve come to this discussion understanding all that, there must be some room for negotiation, yes?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There is… Or at least, I’d like to think so.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Nanase’s face darkened. Then she continued speaking.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Housen-kun’s way of thinking is rooted in ideas of intense individualism. And he enforces that ideology. If things continue at this rate, the less academically capable students will be unable to find partners, and get left behind. If they only got their Private Points withheld for three months, that wouldn’t be too bad. But I fear they’ll end up being branded as incompetent for their failure to find partners. Well, no… I suppose that might not be too bad, either. What I </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">really</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> don’t want to happen is for this intense sense of individualism to dominate our class in the future, to the point where we lose any sense of unity,” said Nanase.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita listened to everything Nanase had to say, and then mentally calculated what might happen to Class 1-D in the future.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Right, that is a valid concern,” she said. “If no one in the class helps each other out, the trend toward individualism will naturally accelerate. Once there’s no one around to assist other students, everyone will feel like they must do things on their own. Once that ideology has sunk in, even if someone does ask for help, no one will step in. Which means that if your class were faced with a test that required you to function as one, you’d lose that battle.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Which was precisely why Nanase took it upon herself to negotiate with Horikita alone. She was trying to avoid that fate.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re not afraid of Housen-kun?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No,” replied Nanase, firmly, without a second’s delay.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She hadn’t glanced much at me until this point, but now looked over in my direction. It was the same look she’d given me twice before. I remembered that when I asked her something similar, she responded with </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“I will never yield to violence.”</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> </span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had thought having Kei look into this would give me something to help make sense of this person’s incomprehensible behavior, but this development went far beyond what I had imagined. In fact, it was so disconnected from what I had imagined that it made me wonder if it was actually completely irrelevant.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, that reminds me, I heard that you got yourself a partner for the special exam,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, yeah. Shimazaki-san from Class 1-B, I think his name was. I feel like Kushida-san</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;"> </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">really came through and saved the day for me,” said Kei.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I knew it. I hadn’t heard anything about her going to the study group yet, so I figured as much.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This exam isn’t something you can get through completely on your own, you know. Kei, your grade is a D+. If you don’t consider the fact that you’re not really in the best position here, you could be in for a world of hurt,” I told her.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What? So, you’re saying that you’ll study hard if I’m there?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Yeah, so? I’d work hard in front of my boyfriend,” said Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wasn’t sure if that was true or not, but if that’s the way it was, there was a quick and easy solution.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, how about we… Yeah. How about you come to my room around six o’clock tomorrow?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Considering the fact that we had a meeting with Nanase after class, that should be plenty of time.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I can come hang out with you?!” she exclaimed.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Don’t you “huh” me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll help you study. That should help you get a little motivated, right?” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">First of all, I’d try to get a concrete sense of where Kei’s actual abilities were at. Then, if I found she was at a level where additional study sessions were essential, I’d have to strongly encourage her to go.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re worried ’cause you’d be sad if your girlfriend got expelled, aren’t you?” said Kei rather suddenly and happily, almost like she thought she had the upper hand on me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I could have answered her in a somewhat mean or teasing way, but Kei would probably be more motivated if I told her I was worried.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course I’m worried. If the girl I just started going out with got expelled, that wouldn’t make me happy,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I-I see… I mean, I know, right?! Well, in that case, I guess I don’t have any choice now, do I? I actually did have tons of things planned, but I’ll make a special appearance, just for you,” said Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">While it wasn’t exactly the most honest answer I could’ve given her, it was a small price to get her to make progress.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What should I bring with me?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Everything you’ll need is already here in my room. As long as you aren’t late, there’s nothing else we’ll need,” I&#160;told her.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“All right, I’m hanging up now.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“W-Wait, hold on! Wait! We only talked about the special exam and studying and stuff though!” wailed Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Apparently, she wanted to talk about something unrelated to those matters.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, I suppose you’ve got a point.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Jeez, you really are a piece of work,” said Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">For a while after that, we no longer talked about the exam or about studying. Instead, she continued to shower me with criticism.</span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.09em;">6.2</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">O</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">N FRIDAY</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> the fifth day of the exam period, eighty-one partnerships had been confirmed. Which meant that a little more than half of all students had found partners, including more and more students from within Class 2-D. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The same held true for those close to me. Yesterday, Kei had gotten herself a partner. And now Airi and Haruka from the Ayanokouji Group had both confirmed their partnerships, too.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The driving force behind those partnerships was Kushida. She worked with her kouhai from junior high school, Yagami, to introduce some of the students in our class to kids from Class 1-B. That was a big deal. However, it didn’t solve all our problems. Although Yagami was rising to prominence within his class, he seemed to have no intention of taking on a leadership role, and was only cooperating with Kushida as an individual, at most. It wasn’t like he could provide us with enough students to cover everyone in our class who was struggling. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yagami had only one condition for his cooperation. Namely, that he had to be Kushida’s partner. That had apparently come to pass yesterday, as reflected in the OAA update. Having Kushida partner with Yagami basically used up one of our more academically gifted students, but Horikita didn’t seem dissatisfied by the exchange, considering the level of support we’d gotten out of it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There were still quite a few capable people left in our class, including Horikita herself, Yousuke, Keisei, Mii-chan, Matsushita, and others. In any case, just because a student had chosen a partner didn’t mean they could rest easy. You had to study hard. That was completely unavoidable. If anything, it could be said the competition only really got started once you found a partner. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though I wasn’t speaking to anyone all that much, I could sense a feeling of unity in our class, with people working together. This was possible precisely because we were friends who had been through thick and thin together for a year. And in the midst of all of that…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">One of the students stood up and tried to leave. Then&#160;Horikita, as if she were waiting for the right moment, walked over and called to him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It seems like you still haven’t found a partner yet, Kouenji-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And what of it?” replied Kouenji.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This was an act of intervention on Horikita’s part, with the single, solitary person who wasn’t contributing to our class’s togetherness.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I just thought I’d talk to you and see how your situation is going, as your classmate. So, how are things?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">You generally knew what even the students who worked independently were up to, because they would talk to the people around them. But since Kouenji didn’t say anything at all, we couldn’t tell what was going on with him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re smart. You’ve never even considered the possibility that you might get expelled, have you?” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course not.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I suppose not, no. Even if you were partnered with a student with grades like Ike-kun’s, you would still score close to four-hundred points quite easily. I think you’d be safe,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Generally speaking, you’d think we might want to position Kouenji as one of our most valuable assets, since he was a smart student. I supposed that was the purpose behind Horikita reaching out to him like this, but still…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Fu fu fu</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, you still had to rely on your partner. Students from both grade levels had to work on the assumption that their partners would score at least one hundred and fifty points. It was a ninety-nine-point-nine-percent certainty at best. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In order to make that as close to one hundred percent as possible, the school had implemented a rule stating that students who scored low enough that it appeared to deviate from their expected level of academic ability would be expelled. Because of that fact, Kouenji could afford to be confident. Which meant there was no need for him to go through the trouble of trying to talk to anyone or to build a relationship. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You mean you’re fine no matter who you get partnered with? In that case, why don’t you allow me to decide your partner for you? <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m just irritated by the fact that if he took things seriously, our class would be that much stronger,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There was hardly anything as frustrating as having a secret weapon that you couldn’t use. It was precisely because she had those feelings of expectation that she felt despair when things ended unsuccessfully. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If I were in your shoes, I wouldn’t have counted on him to start with,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It would probably be easier to think of Kouenji as a special case from now, and just leave it at that.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not giving up,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…I see.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was afraid she’d only end up wasting her time and going in circles, but I supposed it was a good thing that she was motivated.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This is a bit of a bother. With this many people around, we might end up attracting unnecessary attention,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, it’d probably be a good idea for us to try to blend in.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Fortunately for us, the seats we had used at the back of the library yesterday were still available. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This was a spacious library, so there was quite a bit of room. That being said, it was still a kind gesture on her part to do that for us.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She must not have intended on staying for awfully long, because Hiyori left immediately after saying that.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“She really is quite kind, isn’t she? Do you think she heard our conversation the other day?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hard to say. Considering how far away we were, I think it would’ve been hard for her to eavesdrop.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Since the seats had been generously reserved for us, we sat in the same spot we did yesterday. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nanase-san is late,” said Horikita.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Should we go looking around the first-year classrooms for the time being…?” asked Horikita.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Tsubaki Sakurako and Utomiya Riku. I’ll have to remember them,” I told myself.</span></p>
