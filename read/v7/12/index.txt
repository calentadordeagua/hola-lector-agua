<p id="auto_bookmark_toc_top" class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 6:<br/>Intersecting Thoughts</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">R</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">OUGHLY TWO HOURS</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> before Karuizawa left to see Ryuuen, Chabashira-sensei lectured us about important things to remember during the upcoming winter vacation.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Parts of the school will undergo renovations, so those areas will be off-limits. Also, all clubs will take the day off after the closing ceremony. Be sure to head back as soon as you can.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">For some reason, our teacher gazed soundlessly around the classroom after she was done speaking. We waited, but she showed no sign of dismissing us. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ike, clearly itching to leave, raised his hand. “What’s the matter, Sensei?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sure that many of you are already aware of this, but your promotion to Class C is almost guaranteed. Well done.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“W-wow. Sensei just praised us. This is rare.” Ike wasn’t the only one who felt that way. I was sure the rest of the class was equally amazed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“However, don’t get sloppy. If you get in trouble over the break, it might affect your class points. Don’t forget, even during the holidays, you represent this school.” With that, Chabashira-sensei dismissed us, bringing the second semester to a close.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This really is unusual,” said Ike. “Chabashira-sensei being nice and giving us a warning, I mean.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, you might be right,” I replied. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As I put my textbooks in my bag, I glanced over at Karuizawa, who was talking with some other girls. She turned and looked back at me. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That morning, I’d received a text from Karuizawa at the email address I reserved for emergencies. The message said that Ryuuen had asked to meet her on the roof at two o’clock today, and that it had something to do with Manabe and her friends. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Having already gotten a message from Ryuuen about the meeting, I was unsurprised. Ryuuen didn’t care if Karuizawa tattled on him. His only goal was to lure me&#160;in. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t reply to Karuizawa’s text. And yet, she left the room with her friends looking satisfied, perhaps because she felt confident that I’d gotten her message. Maybe she intended to leave the classroom and then come back. An hour later, though, almost all the students had left the school building.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, we were talking about going over to Keyaki Mall. What do you think? Want to come?” asked Keisei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sure,” I said. “I don’t have anything planned. I’ll head over after I finish getting ready.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We’ll wait in the hall.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Maybe I should take some textbooks back to the dorm with me, just in case? I might need them over break.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey. Are you busy today, by any chance?” Satou called, sounding rather sheepish.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. I just promised Yukimura and the others I’d go hang out with them,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I-I see. Bad luck, I guess,” said Satou, her shoulders slumping. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Today’s no good, but are you free over the winter break?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I mean, I feel bad for turning you down twice now. If you’re okay with it, Satou, I just thought…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“R-really?!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Y-yeah.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Satou hugged me without warning, leaving me a little overwhelmed. “I-It’s a date!” she said, hopping up and down while blushing.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Why in the world was she so interested in me? I wasn’t complaining, exactly, but there were still people in the classroom. This was kind of embarrassing. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Anytime tomorrow or after is good,” I said. “I’ll send you a message with more details.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Got it! See you later, Ayanokouji-kun!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Beaming, Satou rejoined Shinohara and her group, who eyed me suspiciously before leaving. I went over to Keisei and the others, who were gathered in the hallway, chatting among themselves. I could guess what they were discussing, based on Haruka’s creepy grin and Airi’s downhearted expression. Haruka seemed ready to start trouble as we began walking, so I beat her to the punch.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nothing’s going on,” I told them.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I haven’t even asked you anything,” said Haruka. “What’s up?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nothing’s up. You were about to ask something, that’s&#160;all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I mean, yeah. Just looking at Satou-san, it’s not hard to imagine that </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">something’s</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> going on, right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re such a playboy, Kiyotaka. First Horikita, now Satou?” said Keisei. For some reason, he sounded kind of angry. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I decided I’d better offer some clarification. “She just invited me to hang out with her, that’s all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“A girl inviting a boy out is rare, though. There must be something to it, don’t you think?” asked Haruka.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Y-y-you don’t think that S-Satou-san is interested in Kiyotaka-kun, do you?!” asked Airi frantically. We’d already been through this.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t know how to answer that…” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“A last-ditch effort to find romance just in time for Christmas? Oh, my,” added Haruka. “What an </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">amazing </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">turn of events!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“At any rate, where are we going? The mall’s probably pretty crowded today. Since break starts tomorrow, there should be quite a few students hanging out until late tonight,” said Keisei, pressing us to make a decision.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, can’t we just kinda wander? We don’t have to rush,” replied Haruka.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As we talked, Akito walked along silently, his expression stern. He seemed to be concentrating on whatever was behind us, as if searching for someone.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No sign of anyone tailing us,” he mumbled, sounding relieved.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It seemed that Ryuuen planned on settling things once and for all today. He must’ve decided that following us was no longer necessary.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, you know, even though Keyaki Mall’s got pretty much everything, I think I wanna go off campus after all,” said Haruka, looking into the distance at the main entrance. “I really want to go to Shibuya or Harajuku, or see the lights in Omotesando, you know?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I suppose campus isn’t a good substitute for either of those places, other than the inside of Keyaki Mall…” The school hadn’t really decorated the campus roads for the occasion, so everything looked the same as usual. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m okay with staying here…” said Airi. “Plus, the campus has almost everything we need. Do you feel the same as Haruka, Kiyotaka-kun? About going off campus?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Unlike Haruka, Airi definitely didn’t strike me as the kind of person who liked to hit the town at night. I&#160;supposed it would be okay if I didn’t force myself to be carried along for once.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m happy to stay here, Airi. But I get why people want to go off campus,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I know that it’s in the school rules, but isn’t saying that we can’t contact our families for three years kind of extreme? I mean, you’d think people would be concerned about their kids, right?” Maybe this was a tender subject for Akito, because he was glowering. “My mom’s a worrywart. She’s probably really anxious,” he added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ve heard that the school takes care of that. Apparently, they regularly send out report cards and stuff.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That…might worry her even more. Guess I should study a little harder,” Akito said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Parents probably worry more about girls than boys, though.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah, no worries. That’s not the case with me. My family’s fine,” said Haruka, smoothly dodging the topic. It seemed there might be something that she didn’t want to touch on. “So, are we heading over to karaoke? It might be a bit crowded.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We’re not going to play that punishment game again, are we?” asked Keisei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course we are. So Yukimuu can have his revenge.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As everyone chatted about where we were going next, I stopped in my tracks.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What’s the matter, Kiyotaka-kun?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry,” I said. “I’m heading back.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But it’s only two,” Akito said, checking the time on his phone.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“To tell you the truth, I pulled an all-nighter, and I’m kind of beat. Let’s hang out again over the winter break,” I told them.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Airi looked disappointed, but I trusted Haruka to make sure that she had a good time, even without me around. I said my goodbyes to the group and left, pulling out my phone to call Chabashira-sensei once I was a safe distance away.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hello. I need to talk to you about something. Do you have a moment?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What are you planning? Didn’t you wash your hands of me?” replied Chabashira-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, but there’re still a few loose ends that need tying up. I’d like to discuss this in person. Can we meet at school?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll wait in the classroom,” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Understood. I’ll be there in a few minutes.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I returned to our classroom, which was now empty of students. Chabashira-sensei stood near my seat, looking out the window.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If this is like previous years, it’ll probably snow,” she&#160;said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you like snow?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I used to. Then I grew up and came to hate it.” Chabashira-sensei pulled the curtains shut and turned around. “You said that you have something to discuss. What do you want with me?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I was just wondering. Why do you want to make it to Class A so badly that you’d go so far as to use me to do it?” You’d think a teacher might hesitate to lie and manipulate a student unless she had good reason for it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This school’s designed to make teachers, as well as students, compete with one another. It’s only natural that everyone wants to rise up the ranks.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I can’t imagine that’s all there is to it. If you really aimed for Class A from the very beginning, you’d never have put Class D at a disadvantage the way you did.” During our first midterm exam, Chabashira-sensei had deliberately withheld critical information from us.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s different. It was personal. I have nothing to say to you,” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You hesitated, right? You weren’t sure this class really had the ability to aim for Class A. You weren’t sure it was even worth trying.” I didn’t really care what her secret feelings were. The important thing was whether she could be of use to me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It seems that this is a waste of time. I’m going back to work.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I spoke up again as Chabashira-sensei turned to leave. “If you won’t answer, then you might as well give up on trying to use me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is that what this is about? I thought you already wriggled free of my grasp, didn’t you?” </span></p>

When that happens, Karuizawa will retreat into her shell, and maybe even voluntarily drop out of school. If we can prove Class C was responsible, we might be able to retaliate, but the damage done to each class will be unfathomable,” I told her.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">True enough. A teacher going up to the rooftop alone, not just to stop Ryuuen from bullying Karuizawa, but also to prevent word of Karuizawa’s past from getting out…it was a dream scenario, too perfect to ever come&#160;true. </span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“To think that a student would threaten a teacher. The tables have certainly turned.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you do this for me, I promise I’ll do nothing to hinder or sabotage Class D going forward. I think that’s significant, don’t you?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If refusing to help you with this means that we never make it to Class A, then so be it.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Well, then. It looked like Chabashira-sensei wasn’t going to budge. Fortunately, relying on a teacher for help never once factored into my calculations.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I was just testing you, that’s all. So, why not come observe this little drama’s conclusion?” </span></p>

<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">6.1</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">F ALL WAS GOING</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> in accordance with my predictions, about thirty minutes should’ve passed since Karuizawa went to the roof. Probably part of Ryuuen’s scheme to force Karuizawa to relive her past trauma in order to get her to confess.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What are you planning, Ayanokouji? How long do you intend to wait?” asked Chabashira-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Just a little longer. I’d come this far; I didn’t need to rush to take action. The further I let the situation on the roof develop, the more likely it became that things would go my way. Being late carried its own risk, but a necessary one.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Let’s chat,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Right now?” Chabashira-sensei asked, incredulous.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I ignored her response. “I’d like to discuss something that happened shortly after we started school, back when we wanted you to sell us one point for Sudou’s test.”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It’d been over six months since then. Time sure did fly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s nothing you cannot buy with private points. That’s what you said, right?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s true. Sudou wasn’t expelled, was he?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If purchasing points was permissible, there would never be any expulsions, would there? Any time someone got a failing grade, someone else could cover for them the way we did.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s not that easy to get private points. This year’s Class D has managed to accumulate a decent number, but that’s a departure from normal. Most years, Class D has about half that amount. Besides, not every student in the class gets along. At least not enough to use their private points to save a classmate, even if that means accepting a drop in class points,” she countered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“True. But that’s a flaw in the system, isn’t it? If salvation through private points is always an option, it dramatically lowers the danger of expulsion based on test performance,” I reasoned.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That might be true.” Chabashira-sensei didn’t deny it, but she didn’t look me in the eye, either.</span></p>

It’s not far-fetched to think that it might’ve anticipated someone trying to purchase a test score increase. I would believe it.”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s right. If you can answer, please do.”</span></p>

It was determined in advance that it cost one hundred thousand private points to purchase one test point. If we go from there, it begs the question: can we can purchase one test point for a hundred thousand private points on more than one occasion?”</span></p>

Please tell me if I’m correct.”</span></p>

Some students have been quicker than others on the uptake, though. Additionally, the school has established thousands of detailed rules to cover every possible scenario. Buying and selling points, sweeping violent incidents under the rug, preventing expulsion—there’s a point value attached to every single one of those. In other words, the price of buying a point for the next midterm had already been set; I just hadn’t met the conditions necessary for Chabashira-sensei to disclose that information to me. Now that I knew that, I could begin to devise countermeasures, though the ambiguity would keep us from being too reckless.</span></p>

Chabashira-sensei was probably thinking that as long as my identity remained hidden, she had leverage to make me cooperate. But if I revealed myself to Class C, Ryuuen would know for certain that I was X. Even if he didn’t know for sure, it would all be over as soon as he decided I was his prime suspect.</span></p>

Ironic how quickly she was giving up on her ambitions.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes.”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> </span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">CLIMBED THE STAIRS</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> step by step. It would just look like you guys fought among yourselves, wouldn’t it?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ryuuen ignored me. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This is what happened here,” he told Ibuki, instead. “I tried to punish the four of you for your failings, but you turned the tables and beat me up instead, then forced me to withdraw. I’ll leave it at that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are…are you really okay with that?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Allow me to add just one thing,” I said. “You are free to drop out of school of your own volition and you’re free to doubt me. But I have absolutely no intention of telling anyone about today unless you force my hand. The former student council president has similarly promised to keep it to himself. Nothing about this situation is forcing you to withdraw—but if you do, I won’t stop you.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Then don’t stop me. I don’t trust you,” snapped Ryuuen. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">With those words, he opened the door and vanished from sight. Ishizaki and Ibuki, left behind, looked like they didn’t agree with his actions in the slightest.</span></p>
