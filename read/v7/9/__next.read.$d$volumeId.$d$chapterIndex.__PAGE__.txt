<p id="auto_bookmark_toc_top" class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 3:<br/>Reunions and Farewells</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">“W</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HAT’S UP</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> with those guys?!” Sudou grumbled irritably as he walked into our classroom the next day, bypassing his seat and approaching Horikita. The look on his face made it immediately clear that he was extremely pissed off. “Hey. You got a sec, Suzune?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What’s the matter?” Horikita couldn’t exactly ignore him when he cornered her at her desk.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Those jerks from Class C! That Ryuuen guy and his lackeys. They’ve been followin’ me since this mornin’, trying to pick a fight. They even blocked my way in the hall. They’re seriously getting on my nerves!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You haven’t shouted insults or taken swings at them, have you?” asked Horikita. She gave Sudou a slight glare, prompting his immediate answer.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I didn’t, no way. I completely ignored ’em.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. It seems you followed my instructions perfectly, then,” replied Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What’s she talking about? What instructions?” I asked Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, Suzune told me that whenever I run into something I can’t deal with properly, I should just ignore it,” said Sudou. That was certainly sound advice. If Sudou rebuked the Class C students, it would probably only add fuel to the fire, so to speak. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I guess I did bump their shoulders a little when I forced my way through,” he added. “The students from the other classes knew I was boxed in, so there shouldn’t be anything to worry about, right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t think they’d try anything, no,” said Horikita. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">They’d already gotten the school and student council involved once before, and it had caused an uproar. If Sudou punched someone, that would be bad, but if he just pushed his way through, it should be fine.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, what did they say to you?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“They called me a monkey, an idiot, childish stuff like that. They were tryin’ to start a fight.” </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Smack!</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> Sudou pounded his fist into his palm. I wondered whether this was a continuation of Class C’s plans, like when they’d appeared at the archery club yesterday.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Some Class C guys also tailed Akito—I mean, Miyake,” I told Sudou and Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Miyake-kun? It seems Class C’s quite active these days,” Horikita replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you think they’re plannin’ on staging another fight, like that time they tried to get me in trouble?” asked Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Who knows? I can’t say at this point. But I’ll consider countermeasures, just in case. If they happen to approach you again, make sure you don’t get physical,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I understand. I ain’t gonna break my promise to you. Even if they start throwin’ punches, I’ll stay calm,” said Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He sounded much more mature now, and Horikita seemed to believe what he said. After Sudou finished giving his report, he returned satisfied to his seat and casually started a conversation with Ike and Yamauchi. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Watching him, Horikita spoke up. “I wonder if Sudou-kun’s finally become a normal, well-adjusted person,” she&#160;mused.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah,” I replied. “His speech is still a little crude, but that’s fine.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It looks like it’s time for him to take the next step.” With that cryptic comment, Horikita got a notebook and started scribbling something down.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What are you talking about? What next step?” I asked. When I tried to sneak a peek at her writing, Horikita quickly closed the notebook.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s a topic for another time. Besides, we have more problems than just Sudou-kun right now,” she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t know what she meant, and honestly didn’t care. Lately, Horikita had thought and acted independently of me with increasing frequency. She’d gotten better at communicating with Sudou, Hirata, and the others, too.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“At any rate, Ryuuen-kun’s been a busy bee—we only just got done with the Paper Shuffle,” she continued. “I wonder what he’s plotting now?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There aren’t any exams in the immediate future,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Think back to the time they attacked Sudou-kun. Now, it seems as if Ryuuen-kun’s plotting against Ichinose-san and Class B. He apparently likes challenging his enemies when no exams are involved,” said Horikita. She shot me a withering look, as if to say, “Don’t you already know that?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I pretended not to notice and shrugged the look off. “I wonder what he’s after this time?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you really not know? Or are you just pretending?” Horikita asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do you mean?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“He’s looking for the person controlling Class D from behind the scenes.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In other words, he’s looking for you?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita pinned me with an intense glare. “You can’t use me to hide from Ryuuen anymore.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What makes you say that?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If he thought I was the puppet master, so to speak, he would approach me directly. But he hasn’t done anything of the sort,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Perhaps your strategy during the Paper Shuffle was more effective than he expected? He might be moving more carefully this time. Taking a while to remove the obstacles in his way first,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wonder. I don’t think that’s it, though. It’s more like he lost interest in me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Does this mean you miss Ryuuen’s attention?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Does that mean you want me to kick you?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I do </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">not</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> want to be kicked.” She was definitely the kind of person who would actually kick me, too.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Maybe our class’s clandestine leader foolishly called attention to himself? Play dumb if you like, but do you really want to discuss this here and now?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was just before homeroom, and all our classmates, Kushida included, sat at their desks. It didn’t seem as if anyone was listening in, but this wasn’t a conversation we could risk having overheard. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“At any rate, you apparently understand Ryuuen quite well. I’m not teasing. I mean it,” I added quickly, because Horikita glared at me again.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“His modus operandi has remained essentially the same. If he plays the same tricks over and over, I’m going to learn from it, even if I don’t like it. That’s how I predicted that he’d use Kushida-san during the Paper Shuffle. Of course, it goes without saying that I would’ve preferred that </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">not</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> happen, but…” Horikita trailed off. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">No one liked a traitor. Horikita was probably thinking that we wouldn’t have had to struggle quite so hard if Kushida hadn’t betrayed us.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, Ryuuen had felt secure precisely because of the internal threat Kushida posed. For better or for worse, Kushida allowed us to see our enemy’s attack patterns.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That wasn’t Ryuuen-kun’s only miscalculation. I intended to pull the rug out from under him during the Paper Shuffle,” Horikita continued.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Isn’t that what actually happened, though?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. Honestly, some of Class C’s lowest-scoring students should’ve been expelled, but that doesn’t seem to be the case.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If you could get your hands on a complete set of questions and answers, then there was no need to study. Horikita was saying it wouldn’t have been surprising for some students from Class C to be kicked out of school after having let their guard down. Keisei and the others seemed to think the same thing.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Class C must have some smart people, right? Students acting in support roles for their classmates, unlike Ryuuen,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I suppose so. If they’re putting in that much effort, they’re doing a good job,” said Horikita. “At any rate, I imagine his tricks will only escalate from here.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s not my problem. It’s yours.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I know that. Being your decoy seems to be my fate in&#160;life.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You sound surprisingly accepting.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Because I have no choice but to accept it. It’s not like you’re going to quit now, are you?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This optimism wasn’t a bad thing. Horikita was perceptive. She had quite a lot of potential. If she improved her communication skills to Hirata’s level, she could stand among the ranks of the elite.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, what’s your plan?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“For what?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you have a strategy to counter Ryuuen-kun’s hunt to expose you?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nope.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There you go again.” She huffed, looking irritated. “Let’s change the topic. Are you still participating in those meetings?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Meetings? You mean, with Keisei and the others? Is there some kind of problem?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I can’t imagine there’s much benefit to being in </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">that</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> clique. That study group formed specifically to help Hasebe-san and Miyake-kun in certain subjects, correct? Now that the exam’s over, it’s no longer necessary.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s not about necessity. I just feel at ease when I’m with them,” I answered. Horikita’s life revolved around her quest to ascend to Class A. It was all she ever talked about. Since I didn’t share her enthusiasm on that front, I had little reason to hang out with her the way I did with Keisei and the others. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If Horikita ever came to talk to me about something that wasn’t a class problem, then maybe, I could engage with her the same way I did with Keisei and the others.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Will you cooperate with me?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I will. As much as I can,” I answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She didn’t look very convinced.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">O</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">UR LAST MORNING CLASS</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> ended, and it was lunchtime. As I pondered meeting up with Akito and Keisei, Horikita stared at me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What? Did you want to continue our talk from this morning?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No. I have a request.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If it’s a hassle, I’m going to pass.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It shouldn’t take much time.” Horikita reached into her bag and took out a library book. “Didn’t you say last week that you wanted to read this?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Farewell, My Lovely</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">, huh?” A masterpiece by Raymond Chandler. I’d been interested in it for a while now, but the book seemed strangely popular, because it was always checked out. I’d given up on borrowing it. “I’m impressed that you managed to snag it from the library. Are you offering to lend it to me?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Technically, Horikita should’ve returned it so it could go to the next person on the waiting list. But this seemed like the most reliable method of getting my hands on it, underhanded though it may have been.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you’d like. It’s also due back today. I was hoping you could take it to the library for me, then check it out for yourself,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is this because you don’t want to go to the trouble of returning it?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even if I returned it myself, you’d still need to go to the library to borrow it. From a purely efficient standpoint, this is the correct course of action,” she replied. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">True enough. This just saved Horikita the time and effort of having to return the book herself. You needed a student ID card when you wanted to check out a book, so trying to check out a book in someone else’s name would be impossible. On the other hand, you didn’t need to present anything if you were simply returning a book.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you refuse, I’ll just head over to the library. I don’t know when you’ll be able to get your hands on this incredibly popular book that’s in such short supply, though.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wondered whether this was Horikita’s warped way of showing kindness, since she knew I wanted to read the&#160;book.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Fine. I’ll take it,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thank you.” Horikita handed it to me. “I don’t care when you return it, as long as it’s sometime today. If I hear that it’s overdue, I’ll be coming for you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I know.” I hadn’t borrowed a book from the library before, but I understood the process. If a book was overdue, private points were deducted from your account. “Well, there’s no time like the present. I’ll just go now.”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HE LIBRARY WAS</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> surprisingly empty at lunch, like a cozy little hideaway. Students weren’t allowed to eat in here, so only a few people were around, guaranteeing that the return process would go smoothly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Since I’m already here, I might as well check out another book,” I muttered to myself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Farewell, My Lovely</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> in one hand, I walked over to the mystery section, hoping to find another of Raymond Chandler’s works. As I arrived there, I saw a lone female student struggling to grab a book on a high shelf. It was </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Wuthering Heights,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> by Emily Brontë. A masterpiece penned by the middle of the three Brontë sisters, all of whom were literary legends. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">A conventional blurb could make that book sound like a mystery, but wouldn’t it have fit better in the romance section? </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Wuthering Heights</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> was perched at a strange angle, just barely within reach, which might be why the girl wasn’t using the stepstool. I stood in front of her and grabbed the book.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry, I don’t mean to butt in, but…” I looked at her and trailed off. “Wait a minute. You’re from Class C. You’re…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Shiina Hiyori. I’d seen her with Ryuuen a short while ago. It seemed that she recognized me, too.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re Ayanokouji-kun, right?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. Uh, here you go,” I said, handing her the book.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thank you very much.”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“By the way, that book you have—</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Farewell, My Lovely</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">, right? It’s wonderful,” said Shiina. Her eyes started to sparkle.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I managed to borrow it from a friend of mine today.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wow, that’s lucky. It seems as though Raymond Chandler’s quite popular among the second-year students. I’ve been wanting to reread it myself, but I wasn’t able to find a copy today.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I guess it was bad of me to borrow it from a friend,” I&#160;said apologetically.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s all right. I’ve already read it. Besides, I was fortunate enough to find another good book while I searched for that one. The school library’s quite large. If I tried to read everything on its shelves, I’d probably graduate before I could finish,” said Shiina. She clutched Brontë’s book, a small smile on her face.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. You’re probably right. Sorry for disturbing you, by the way.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She’d come here during the lunch break instead of eating, so she probably didn’t want to waste time chatting to a student from another class. I decided to leave her be.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you just came to return </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Farewell, My Lovely</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> and then borrow it for yourself, you could’ve done that at the service desk. Are you looking for another book to check out?” Shiina asked, stopping me in my tracks.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I thought I’d come back and try another time, so…” I replied. Shiina was already scanning the mystery section. “Hey, what are you doing?” I asked.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Impossible,” replied Keisei. “Like you said, he’s a jerk. Do you really think he’d do anything for our class?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Maybe it’s an act?” asked Haruka.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You mean, his ridiculous personality’s just a front?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Maybe he’s really a cold, calculating schemer. You think?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Everyone shook their heads.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Absolutely not,” said Akito. “He’s nuts.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Besides, Kouenji retired on the very first day of the island test,” added Keisei confidently. “He wouldn’t have known what was going on with us. If there was another mastermind on that island aside from Horikita, it couldn’t have been him.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh ho, I see. You’re pretty persuasive, Yukimuu.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But this is all conjecture based on the assumption that there really </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">is</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> a mastermind, like Ryuuen believes. Even if there is, we’re not sure they were involved in </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">all</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> the tests.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. Yeah, you’re right.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But I </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">do</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> think the mastermind exists,” Keisei added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why do you say that?” Akito asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Just a feeling. I guess it’s because of Class D’s rapid progress.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But how could Ryuuen-kun know for sure that the mastermind isn’t Horikita-san?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Maybe Hirata-kun’s the mastermind?” suggested Keisei. “Back when we were on the island, he got some advice from Horikita-san, I think.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, Hirata’s the one giving the orders?” Akito asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t see him as the type, but it’s not impossible.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It definitely sounds like he’s on Ryuuen’s list.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I think Ryuuen’s got his eyes on about ten people.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Someone from Class C was probably tailing Hirata, too. But he was bound to remain neutral and abstain from conflict, and I barely spoke to him lately. I wouldn’t risk it while Ryuuen and his goons were on the hunt.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“H-hey, Kiyotaka-kun?” Airi timidly spoke up.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hmm?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Please don’t be upset, but I wanted to ask you something,” she said. “Is it possible that maybe </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">you’re</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> the mastermind?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The other group members all looked at me simultaneously.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why do you think that?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“W-well, it’s just…you’re calm, and smart, and…reliable, so… I thought that you gave Horikita-san all kinds of helpful advice, so…” Airi stammered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Does Kiyopon get good test scores?” asked Haruka.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If I recall, not great, not bad,” said Keisei, pushing his glasses up. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“S-sorry. It’s just, well, I kind of thought that, for some reason or another. Maybe because of the advice you gave, that Ryuuen-kun might be after you. I felt bad for you…” Airi continued.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I supposed it was just her nature to ask an honest question. I doubted she meant ill. “Unfortunately, I’m the one always </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">getting</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> advice from Horikita.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I guess Kiyopon does have a mysterious quality, you know? Also, since he’s close to Horikita-san, it’s&#160;reasonable that you’d suspect something,” Haruka told Airi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Come to think of it, that might be right. It might be why Shiina directly confronted him,” offered Akito.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It certainly seems reasonable to be suspicious of Ayanokouji. Even if there isn’t really a mastermind, just being close to Horikita might make people think that he’s the one.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If that’s true, it’s bad news for you, Kiyopon,” said Haruka.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, it really is.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What a drag. Hey, if they give you trouble, feel free to talk to us about it,” said Akito, placing his hand on my shoulder.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. I’ll do that,” I told him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This surveillance couldn’t continue forever. When he saw his opportunity, Ryuuen would definitely choose to attack.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">3.5</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">A</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">N UNEXPECTED PERSON</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> approached me after class the next day: Satou from Class D. Her skirt fluttered slightly in the breeze as she stopped in front of me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, Ayanokouji-kun. Are you free today? If so, would you like to have some tea or something before going back to the dorms?” She twirled her hair on her finger, as if it were pasta on a fork. She was bold—and obviously looking for a date. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita, who sat next to me, didn’t seem to care at all. After gathering her things, she left the classroom. However, I felt the other members of the Ayanokouji Group observing. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Why’s a popular girl like Satou talking to Ayanokouji?</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> they likely wondered. Haruka seemed deeply interested.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t really have any plans. Hanging out with our group wasn’t mandatory, so they wouldn’t mind. I was a little worried about the way they were staring, though.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is this a bad time?” Satou sounded slightly anxious.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry, Satou. Today’s not so good for me,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was a tough decision, but I turned her down—mainly due to the source of my discomfort all day. My shoulders were tense thanks to that person constantly staring at me. Her eyes remained fixed on me even now, as I talked to Satou. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira-sensei was still in the classroom. She pretended to deal with paperwork, but she was faking. She was undoubtedly waiting to approach me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I-I see,” said Satou. “Well, talk to you later, Ayanokouji-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I felt bad for making Satou sad, but this was just terrible timing. I exited the classroom as if I was walking her out, and as soon as I did, Chabashira-sensei followed me into the hall. Clearly, I was correct to think that she had some business with me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I took care to avoid the main hallway, instead heading over to the stairs.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Once we had privacy, Chabashira-sensei called out to me. “Ayanokouji.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you want something?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. Follow me. We need to talk.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s going to be difficult. I promised Horikita I’d meet her,” I answered, coming up with an appropriate-sounding lie.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“As a teacher, I don’t want to be careless. But circumstances being what they are, this is necessary.” Chabashira-sensei, who was usually inhumanly composed, wore a strangely vulnerable expression.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I have a bad feeling about this,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Unfortunately, you can’t refuse. This is extremely urgent,” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Resistance was futile. I decided to follow her, and we moved from the student area to an even more private location.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why are we headed over here?” I asked. “It’s too early to counsel me on my post-high school career, isn’t it?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’ll understand soon.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I tried to lighten things up with a joke, but it didn’t seem like she was going to answer a student’s question. However, rather than what was on the other side of the door, what I was worried about right now was Chabashira-sensei. She sounded almost flustered, and that worried me. She was normally so composed. Whoever she was taking me to see—even if it was the person I imagined—this wasn’t normal behavior for her. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She knocked on an office door. “Principal, I’ve brought Ayanokouji Kiyotaka-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I heard a gentle voice that carried the dignity of age. “Enter.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira-sensei opened the door. A man of about sixty sat on the sofa. He was certainly the school’s principal; I’d seen him multiple times before, at the entrance ceremony and during the end-of-the-semester ceremony. He didn’t look calm, either. In fact, sweat trickled down his forehead. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">One other person was there, sitting opposite the principal.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I now knew the reason I’d been called here.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You two may talk now,” said the principal. “I trust this is acceptable?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Very well. I’ll take my leave now. Excuse me,” the principal said. He bowed out humbly, despite the fact that the person sitting opposite him was only in his forties. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll excuse myself as well.” Chabashira-sensei bowed graciously to the man and left with the principal. The final look she gave me was visibly nervous. As the door closed, the only sound I heard was the heating system’s faint whirring. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As I stood completely still and silent, the man spat out his first words. “How about you take a seat? I came all the way here to meet with you, after all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It had been one year—no, a year and a half since I’d heard this man’s voice. His way of speaking and tone hadn’t changed at all. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not planning to have a long conversation. I promised some friends I’d meet them.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Friends? Don’t make me laugh. There’s no way you’re capable of making friends,” the man sneered. Typical. He just assumed that was true, even though he hadn’t seen me in so long. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Whether we talk or not, it won’t change anything.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Then I assume you’re going to do as I say. No need to discuss anything; I’m busy anyway.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t know what you want from me,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He cut straight to the heart of the matter. “I’ve prepared the documents necessary for you to drop out. I spoke to the principal about it. All I need is for you to say yes.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I have no reason to drop out,” I told him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“For you, that might be true. But I have my own reasons.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He looked right at me for the first time. The sharp gleam in his eye hadn’t faded. If anything, it’d only grown sharper. His gaze was a blade, threatening to slice to the very heart of you. I was sure that gaze had wounded many people.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Does a parent have the right to ruin their child’s life for the sake of their own selfishness?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Parent? You’ve never recognized me as your parent before,” he countered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re certainly right.” I doubted that this man had ever regarded me as his own child. In truth, we only recognized each other as father and son in the most technical way possible. </span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><img height="100%" style=" top: 0px; vertical-align: top; height: 100%;" alt="" id="ClassroomElite-Vol7_INTERIORIMAGES_4" src="/hola-lector-agua/images/books/v7/OEBPS/Images/ClassroomElite-Vol7_INTERIORIMAGES_4.jpg" loading="lazy" decoding="async"/></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The fact is that you’ve been behaving selfishly. I ordered you to remain on standby,” the man declared, no longer urging me to sit. “You defied me and enrolled at this school. It’s only natural that I tell you to drop out immediately.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Your orders are absolute inside the White Room. But outside it, there’s no need for me to do as you say. Right?” I countered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was simple logic. Of course, he wasn’t convinced.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’ve become quite talkative in the short time since we last met. I suppose that’s due to this absurd school’s influence, hmm?” The man rested his chin on his hand, looking at me as if I were less than filth.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Answer my previous question, hmm?” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You mean the pointless query about no longer needing to obey my orders? You’re my property. A man may do as he wishes with his property. Whether I keep you alive or kill you is up to me,” he answered coldly. That he could say such things in a law-abiding country, and mean them, spoke to what a fearsome force he was. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Badger me all you like. I’m not dropping out,” I told&#160;him. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We could keep going back and forth on this, but it didn’t matter. We weren’t going to agree. He knew that, and hated wasting his time on pointless conversations. So, what would he do? </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Of course, he prepared his next attack.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t you wonder how Matsuo’s doing? The person who told you about this school and gave you the idea to enroll?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Not especially.” I remembered Matsuo. His face immediately popped up in my mind.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I hired him to manage you for a year in his capacity as a butler, but he chose to go against his employer.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He said it in one breath, without pausing, then stopped cold after the word “employer,” a pause designed to etch that final word into the listener’s heart. The tone of his voice and the dramatic pause indicated that a conversation of grave importance was about to begin. His dark look was designed to make me fearful. To cause me to wonder just how bad things were about to get.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“He told you about this school as a way for you to escape my control. You ignored your real father’s wishes and selfishly enrolled without my permission. Truly foolish.” He picked up his tea and took a sip. “An outrageous, unforgiveable act. Naturally, Matsuo had to be punished.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It wasn’t a threat. He was merely stating the truth.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You can probably guess what I’m about to say,” he continued, “but he was disciplined and dismissed.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If he went against his employer, that’s an appropriate punishment,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">My butler, Matsuo, was nearly sixty years old. He was exceptionally good at taking care of people, and quite amicable. Any child would’ve liked him. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Matsuo married young, but hadn’t been blessed with children right away. He was over forty when he had his first baby, but sadly lost his wife in childbirth. His son was about my age, and Matsuo’s pride and joy. I’d never met the boy myself, but Matsuo said his son studied diligently every day so that he could achieve great things and repay his father’s sacrifices. His smile as he said those words still burned in my memory.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You know about him, I assume. Matsuo’s son, his pride and joy.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He’d predicted my train of thought. He saw right through me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“When you enrolled at this school, Matsuo’s son also managed to pass the difficult entrance exam for a wonderful, prestigious private high school. He worked exceptionally hard, and achieved it all on his own.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He paused.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But now he’s been expelled.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">His meaning was plain. He’d forced the school to rescind the boy’s acceptance as a form of payback. That was the sort of power he wielded.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So? For a man like you, that’s a mild punishment,” I replied sarcastically. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Matsuo’s son was strong. Even though he was expelled from the school on which he’d pinned his hopes, his determination didn’t fade. He bounced back and immediately tried to enroll in other schools. I did whatever was necessary to crush his attempts to advance. I&#160;made him give up. I did the same to Matsuo, too. I tarnished his reputation, leaving him unable to find a job. His son also lost his way, and is now unemployed,” said the man.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Matsuo and his son had lost everything because of my selfishness. The man before me wasn’t making this up. Every word he said was almost certainly true. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If he came all this way to spout this nonsense at me, then he was in for a disappointment.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I imagine you’re not surprised by any of this. Because Matsuo acted against me, it was necessary that I repay him in kind. However, it seemed that this was more than he could bear. He was always a nice, conscientious man, who lost his wife young and raised his son alone. Racked with guilt for having robbed his child of a future, Matsuo concluded that there was only one way to save him. He begged me to leave the boy alone, and committed suicide last month by self-immolation.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So, that was what he came here to say. That my selfish actions led to a man’s death.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Right now, his son’s working part-time, earning enough to live on and nothing else, with no guarantee of a future. No dreams. No hope. His family’s tragedy is your fault. The boy must surely bear a deep grudge against you. Even in death, he won’t forgive you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The corners of the man’s mouth curved slightly upward in a despicable grin.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The man who took care of you, who saved you, has died. And you show no reaction whatsoever. If Matsuo could see you now, he’d be full of regret.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">What kind of joke was this?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Dead people felt no regret. The man before me was the reason why Matsuo and his son lost everything—why Matsuo killed himself—and he wasn’t even trying to make me feel guilty. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you gave it a little more oomph, it’d be perfect.”</span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><img height="100%" style=" top: 0px; vertical-align: top; height: 100%;" alt="" id="ClassroomElite-Vol7_INTERIORIMAGES_6" src="/hola-lector-agua/images/books/v7/OEBPS/Images/ClassroomElite-Vol7_INTERIORIMAGES_6.jpg" loading="lazy" decoding="async"/></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We headed back to the dorms. It wasn’t even five o’clock yet, but the sun was already setting.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It was really warm today,” said Airi. “Everyone’s out in pretty light clothing, huh?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You could even walk around in short sleeves this afternoon, so that makes sense.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not good with the cold,” said Haruka, sounding melancholy as she looked up at the sky.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t handle it well, either.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, a little chilliness is good for me. It means I don’t sweat during club, which makes practicing easy,” said Akito, making him the only member of the group to prefer the cold.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Looks like it’s going to get cold again tomorrow.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Guess that means I need to buy some stuff to prepare…that’s going to cost me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Our pace gradually slowed to a stroll as we walked and chatted. Then, we heard a voice.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thank you for going out with me today, Sakayanagi-san.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, no, no. The pleasure’s mine.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Turning, I spotted a rather unusual pair: Ichinose and Sakayanagi. Ichinose, noticing our group, raised her hand and waved. Sakayanagi didn’t look in my direction, but rather, gave our entire group a cursory glance as we went past. Even though she’d made what amounted to a declaration of war against me, she’d done nothing since the sports festival. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s unusual to see you in this group, Ayanokouji-kun,” Ichinose said. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Really?” She thought </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> was behaving unusually? The strange sight was the Class A and B leaders hanging out together like friends on a day off.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, based on what I’ve seen, you’re with Horikita-san a lot of the time. This is different,” said Ichinose, taking a long look at our group’s members. “That reminds me. I heard that you won against Class C in the exam. Congrats! We lost to Class A, unfortunately.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Only by a slim margin. Two points. I think we were just about evenly matched,” said Sakayanagi. The battle had been close, and it seemed Class B was just barely lagging behind Class A by the end. “With this win under your belt, Class D might actually become Class C next semester, right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wow! Class B’s going to have to be really careful, or we might get overtaken ourselves!” said Ichinose.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We do intend to overtake you,” Keisei interjected, overtly serious as always. “We’ll make it to Class A eventually.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakayanagi closed her eyes and chuckled. Keisei seemed to find that insulting, but he had to remember that we were still Class D for now. No one in our group was especially close to Ichinose, and since we weren’t really the type to force smiles or engage in small talk, the conversation ground to a halt. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah, sorry. Guess we’re bothering you guys. See you later,” said Ichinose graciously.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakayanagi neither spoke to me nor made eye contact, just followed Ichinose away. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“They’re rivals, right? Those two?” asked Haruka.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s no doubt they’re enemies.” Keisei eyed them both suspiciously, pushing his glasses up. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s just like Ichinose, though, right?” It was a well-known fact that Ichinose could make friends with&#160;anyone. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s just, like, how do I put this?” muttered Airi. “It’s like Ichinose-san lives in a different world from us or something.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“As a fellow woman, I kinda don’t like her.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What? Do you dislike Ichinose, Haruka?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">dislike</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> her, it’s just…she’s way too perfect at everything. You can’t be human without at least a couple flaws, right? I sorta almost hope that she’s actually rotten on the inside.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You have a point. She’s so flawless it’s almost creepy. Saying you hope she’s rotten inside is going a bit too far, though,” said Akito.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s true. I’m just saying that being flawless and totally sweet is boring, even in a manga,” said Haruka. She thrust her hands inside her pockets as she watched Ichinose walk away.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I…I think it’s good that she’s like that, though. If Ichinose-san really was a bad person like Haruka-chan said, no one would believe it,” said Airi. She sounded anxious, as if she really didn’t want that to be true.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I guess so. Maybe there really are unbelievably kind, perfect people in the world. We may just not even know them when we see them,” said Haruka.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We’re going to make it to Class A. When that happens, Ichinose will be our enemy. I think it’s best if we don’t get too close,” said Keisei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He had a point. If Ichinose </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">was </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">as kind and benevolent as she seemed, it would make her a tougher opponent to oppose. Someone as awful as Ryuuen inspired no sympathy, but I wondered whether our class would have the heart to crush Ichinose if it came to it. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If our class was promoted, more conflict down the road was inevitable. We’d be attacked from below by Ryuuen and the newly demoted Class C, who’d be raring for revenge. It was unclear what would happen to Horikita and Ichinose’s currently cooperative relationship in the future, too. In an ideal world, we’d work together with Ichinose to attack Class A. Then, once Classes B and D were promoted to Classes A and B respectively, we’d end our alliance and turn on each other. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Of course, I doubted that things would be that simple.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Looks like the road ahead is strewn with pitfalls, huh?”</span></p>
