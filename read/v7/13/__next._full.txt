<p id="auto_bookmark_toc_top" class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 7:<br/>What Ryuuen Wins and Loses</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HAT NIGHT,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> I dreamed about my childhood. About that time I killed a snake. If I’d grabbed it and taught it to feel fear before killing it, would I still have slaughtered it in the end? </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Freakin’ stupid.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That line of thought was pointless. You only got one life, and it didn’t have an undo button. Some days you won, and some days you lost. Yesterday just happened to be the latter. The number of times I’d been beaten was probably in the triple digits by now. Heck, yesterday hadn’t even been the first time I’d lost to </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Ayanokouji. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So, what made this different from everything that’d happened so far?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">At eight o’clock the next morning, I left the dorms and made my way to the school building. Although this was the first day of our winter vacation, club activities were still being held, so the building had been left open. School rules required uniforms be worn within the building, but I didn’t need to pay attention to that particular rule anymore. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The clubs were already engaged in their morning practice, which generally started around seven. Since Keyaki Mall didn’t open until ten, I was probably the only student heading toward the school building now.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Achoo!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Along the way, I encountered a lone student standing by the side of the path, trembling from the cold. I ignored her and kept going, but she called out to me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You finally came.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I just kept walking.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, wait a minute!” she said, chasing me in a panic. She grabbed my shoulder.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What are you doing? Don’t just touch me like that,” I growled.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t exactly </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">want </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">to touch you. You foisted your phone on me, remember? I’m just giving it back,” said a red-nosed Ibuki, thrusting the phone at me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You could’ve done this at a better time. How long have you been waiting?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Who knows?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That probably meant she’d waited for quite a while. Why was she so sensitive when it came to pointless things like this? I didn’t take the phone, but Ibuki grabbed my arm when I tried to slip past.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you really going to quit?” she asked. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Didn’t you just say you only wanted to give my phone back?” I snapped.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ibuki glared at me. “Do you remember what you said back when you fought Ishizaki and Albert? You said that the strongest person is whoever wins in the end, no matter how many times they lost before. That’s exactly how it went when you fought them.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So what?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you really going to end this after losing to Ayanokouji one time?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I misread the situation and sealed my fate. Besides, I&#160;couldn’t care less anymore,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What? That’s super lame.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t care anymore. Guess that guy was a big deal after all, if he could make me feel this way.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Maybe,” I said, responding to Ibuki with complete indifference.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t give me ‘maybe,’” Ibuki huffed. She hadn’t let go of my arm.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You wanted me to quit school, didn’t you? Isn’t this perfect?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I cooperated with you because you said you’d get us to Class A. Now you’re just going to leave us in the lurch?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ibuki had her outbursts every so often, but it seemed like this had been building for a long time. She had more to get off her chest, apparently, because she showed no sign of stopping. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I tolerated your tyranny. I put up with it because we had the same ultimate goal. Even when Class C got our points docked the other day, and you wouldn’t tell us why, no one complained. We all believed you’d get us to Class A someday. And now you’re just going to drop out? That’s lame. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">So, lame</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She took a breath, and added one more thing. “Is there anything more pathetic?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You really gonna spin this however’s most convenient for you?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I stopped walking. I had to move carefully, because my body hurt all over.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, I told you good-for-nothings that if you followed me, I’d get you to Class A. That was just me dangling a carrot before your noses while I used violence to sow the seeds of fear. You </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">do</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> know about my contract with Class A, don’t you? That was all for me. Just me. I couldn’t give a shit about the rest of you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re saying you planned to make it to A Class alone?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yup. There’s really no way I’d try to take you lot with me. Don’t you agree?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was sure even Ibuki would be convinced by this much.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Eight hundred million private points,” Ibuki said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“After you tossed your phone over to me yesterday, I agonized about whether I should transfer your points to my account. But then I decided to have a look at what else was on your phone.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ibuki turned the screen toward me. It showed the three-year-long strategy I’d been working on. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you were trying to make it to Class A alone, two million points would’ve been enough. So why come up with a strategy that includes this number? Eight hundred million is the number of points necessary to get all of Class C up to Class A, isn’t it? Though I can’t imagine how we’d ever save up that many points…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Quit dreaming. I was only messing around when I wrote that.” I snatched my phone back. “Hiyori and Kaneda will probably lead Class C from here on out. As long as Ayanokouji makes no move, it’s still possible for you to rack up that many points.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s not what I’m talking about.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ugh. Stupid Ibuki. She hadn’t transferred any of my private points at all. What a pain. “What do you want me to say?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you’re going to drop out, then fight me,” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">What an absolutely crazy proposition. Idiots made good pawns, but the downside was that they ran wild every so often, like this.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“With the injuries you got yesterday, and how cold it is today, you probably can’t move too well, hmm?” I mused out loud. I’d noticed already that her grip on my sleeve was weaker than usual. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I pulled my arm free and started forward—and she hit me. I went flying, landing hard on the pavement.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ow. God, I can’t even take a fall,” I groaned. That bastard Ayanokouji had done a number on me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah. That felt great. Well, if you’re going to quit, hurry up and do it,” snapped Ibuki, before turning to head back toward the dorms.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wondered just how long she </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">had</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> been waiting for me.</span></p>

<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">7.1</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">U</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">PON REACHING THE SCHOOL,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> I paid a visit to my homeroom teacher. I’d called ahead and made an appointment on the dorm’s landline. I was doing this the very next day to forestall the complications of the whole mess on the roof as much as I could, especially considering I’d tampered with a security camera and the former student council president knew what had happened.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I gotta talk to you about the thing I reported yesterday, Sakagami.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I understand,” said Sakagami. “Please accompany me to the counselor’s office.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sure.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But first, there’s something we must address.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Would you step outside, please?” Sakagami called to someone in the faculty room. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ryuuen-san…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ishizaki and Albert emerged to join us in the hallway. First that idiot Ibuki, now them? Why were they here?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“They’ve been waiting since early this morning, wondering whether you’d come by. Even when I told them to contact you directly, they wouldn’t listen. Quite troublesome. Before we have our meeting, I need you to do something about these two,” said Sakagami.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What’re you even doing here? Beat it, or I’ll kill you.” I glared at Ishizaki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Um…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“About the surveillance camera being destroyed—did these two have anything to do with it?” asked Sakagami, touching his glasses.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I did that by myself,” I replied. “Come on, let’s get on with it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If Ishizaki and Albert said anything careless, they’d put the noose around their own necks. I shook them off, ignored Sakagami, and started walking to the counselor’s office. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakagami had to suspect something was up, but he told Ishizaki and Albert to head back and followed me. “I&#160;have a general idea of what happened, based on what you said over the phone, but walk me through this once more, Ryuuen. First of all, you admit to defacing the security camera by spray-painting the lens?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. I did that on my own.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay, one more thing. Is it true that a fight broke out between you, Ishizaki, Albert, and Ibuki?” Sakagami&#160;asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, it’s true. I take complete responsibility for it. I&#160;threw the first punch. They retaliated.” No need to involve them in this losing battle.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well then, this’ll be quick.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Please wait, Ryuuen-san! We </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">did</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> have something to do with—” cried Ishizaki, who was still following us.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I kicked him. Violent outbursts before a teacher didn’t really matter when you were going to drop out of school, anyway.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ryuuen?!” rebuked Sakagami. “What’re you doing?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How many times do you plan on making me say it? Did the beating I gave you yesterday not satisfy you?” I spat at Ishizaki, who crouched on the floor in pain. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We’d made it to the counselor’s office. I looked away from Ishizaki as we entered, leaving him and Albert outside. Time to wrap this up. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You can add that outburst just now to whatever my penalty is,” I told Sakagami.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It seems there’s a misunderstanding here, so allow me to offer some corrections,” said Sakagami. “We’ve confirmed that there are inconsistencies in your statement.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? Hold up. Inconsistencies?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Based on my understanding, there’s some kind of problem between you and Class D.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">No way. Had Ayanokouji done something? If he reported what’d happened to the school, then this wouldn’t end with me. The school would punish Ibuki and Ishizaki,&#160;too. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Did they file a complaint or something?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“A complaint? No, from what I’ve heard, a single Class&#160;D student was also involved in destroying the security camera.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What did you just say?” The words made no sense. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Class D already paid the private points necessary to cover the repair. What I wanted to confirm with you was whether you wished to split the blame.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’ve gotta be screwing with me.”</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;"> </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">If you think this’ll prevent me from dropping out, you’re making a big mistake, Ayanokouji.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m dropping out,” I told Sakagami.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s no problem here, though. You still want to drop out, despite that?” Sakagami was no fool. He had to have figured out that something serious had gone down on the rooftop yesterday.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s right. I see no point in staying at this school any longer.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. If that’s your final decision, I can’t stop you,” Sakagami said, pulling out a piece of paper. “Please write your name, student ID number, and reason for withdrawal on this form.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Just a second,” I told him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I picked up a pen, and Sakagami pulled out two more forms. “Once we finish processing your withdrawal, please deliver these to Ishizaki and Yamada.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What? They’ve got nothing to do with this.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re certainly right. But this was their wish. They said that, should you choose to drop out of school, they would quit too. They wouldn’t listen to reason.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That Ayanokouji… Had he put this ridiculous idea in those idiots’ heads? He was basically holding Ishizaki and Albert hostage to prevent me from withdrawing. If I chose to drop out here and now, those two would go with me, and my own withdrawal would become meaningless. I’d be putting the cart before the horse.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Shit.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Personally, I would find it rather regrettable for anyone in my class to drop out,” said Sakagami, his gaze lowering to the withdrawal form in my hand. “As things stand, the matter can be resolved with a simple punishment for damaging school property. This is your first and last chance.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What is the point of me even staying, though?” I muttered to myself. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ayanokouji had my number. He knew I wasn’t gonna cause more trouble for Sakayanagi and the other two.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Fine. I won’t drop out.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I handed the paper and pen back to Sakayanagi and left.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">S</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">OON AFTERWARD,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> strange rumors started spreading among the first-year students—rumors that Ryuuen Kakeru had abandoned his position as Class C’s leader. That Ishizaki and the others were no longer his entourage. That he no longer talked to anyone, but spent all his time&#160;alone.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was like looking in a mirror—a mirror of the person I’d been when I first started at this school. I wondered whether there would come a day when Ryuuen regained what he lost. I </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">was </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">sure of one thing, though. He and I were similar. </span></p>
