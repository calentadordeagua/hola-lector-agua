<p id="auto_bookmark_toc_top" class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 2:<br/>The Sound of Footsteps in the Middle of Winter</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">W</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">E WERE HALFWAY THROUGH</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> December, and it was suddenly freezing cold. More and more students donned scarves, gloves, and long socks. Clouds hung low in the sky. It looked like it was going to snow.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Come to think of it, I’d never seen snow before. I’d watched it on television and read about it in books, but never touched it, never felt the sensation of it on my skin. I decided I’d like to experience it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yukimura Keisei, Hasebe Haruka, Sakura Airi, and myself—all members of Class D—met up at Keyaki Mall after class. (Keisei’s real name was “Teruhiko,” but we’d started calling him Keisei in accordance with his wishes.) Their faces were becoming a familiar sight to me. We’d grown close of late, and now met regularly just to talk, without any specific purpose in mind. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sometimes we hung out for over two hours, and other times, we split after only thirty minutes. They were a casual, easygoing group who came and went as they pleased. We did often hang out together after class on Fridays, because our currently absent fifth group member, Miyake Akito, had a situation to contend with.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I really thought Class C would’ve tried something else by now. Those questions they gave us weren’t easy at all,” said Keisei, just as some Class C girls happened to pass by.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Class C doesn’t seem any more capable of studying than us,” Haruka answered, eyes on her phone. “Miyacchi says he’ll be here soon. Sounds like he just left his club.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She was texting the very person we were waiting on. Akito was the only group member who belonged to a club, and couldn’t hang out with us immediately after class. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But it’s good that we made it through this exam, isn’t it? Besides, I don’t want to see someone expelled, even if they’re from another class,” said Airi. She didn’t have the stomach for cruelty.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, yeah. I get that you want to get along, but that’s kind of hard, with how this school is designed. Rising in the ranks means kicking another class down,” said Haruka.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Exactly,” said Keisei, sounding impressed. “I understand what you mean, Airi, but it’s eat or be eaten at this school. I don’t want us to be eaten.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I suppose,” Airi replied, disheartened.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, wait a minute. What if, after the final exam, all four classes had the exact same number of class points? Then everyone could graduate from Class A… Just kidding. That’ll never happen,” said Haruka.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I think that’d be wonderful, though,” said Airi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Unfortunately, it’s impossible,” said Keisei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How can you be sure?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ve heard the upperclassmen talk about it. If all the classes happened to have equal points after the final exam, the school would hold an additional special exam to determine our rankings,” he said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What kind of exam?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Who knows? I’ve only heard rumors. Apparently, it’s never happened before.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I still think it’s an interesting idea, though,” said Haruka.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, only one class can be A in the end, huh?” asked Akito, arriving to join us.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, Miyacchi. How did practice go today?” asked Haruka.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How did what go?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You know. How did you handle your bow or whatever?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Like normal. Not really great, not really bad. You don’t have to pretend to be interested,” said Akito.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Come on, isn’t it nice to ask? We’re just having a casual conversation between friends, right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, do you know anything about archery?” asked Akito. He sat down, looking somewhat suspicious of Haruka.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You just hit the target with your bow, right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No…but I guess you have the general idea. Just forget about it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, how do I put this? It’s not like I have any interest in archery myself. I never have. I just want to know what youthful missteps led </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">you</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> to care about it, you know?” replied Haruka.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. Come to think of it, why archery? It’s not like this school’s archery club is legendary or anything, right?” asked Keisei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Back in junior high, an upperclassman I was friends with belonged to the archery club. So, I thought I’d give it a shot. That’s all there was to it. No particularly deep reason,” said Akito.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah, so this other student made you want to join?” Airi chimed in more and more often these days. It was a welcome sight, if a surprising one. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Airi, you have a digital camera, right? That’s a fad these days, huh? I suppose I understand why you prefer something like photography,” said Haruka.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What, like posting pictures to Instagram and stuff? That’s more of a girls’ hobby, I guess. I don’t quite get it,” said Keisei. He sounded critical.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, that’s sexist,” said Haruka. “There’re lots of dudes on the ’gram these days, you know.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Really? I don’t think it’s a good idea to make your personal information public like that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What about you, Kiyotaka? Are you into photography?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No. I don’t know anything about it,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After all, at this school, you couldn’t communicate with the outside world at all. The only people who’d see you on social media would be other students.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Kiyopon doesn’t really look the type. Actually, if he used Instagram, I’d be super surprised. Are you secretly the sort of person who impulsively snaps a photo when you’re out partying, or at the pool at night?” Haruka asked me. “Or when you have an ice cream in hand and make, like, a cute face… You ever do anything like that?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No.” I didn’t want her to picture me doing anything of the sort. “Do you use it, then? Instagram?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Not at all. It’s a pain, and I don’t like putting myself on display,” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m with you,” said Keisei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Airi was quiet, but she looked hurt by their dismissal. Although she was taking a break from it at the moment, her hobby was posing for pictures and uploading them to the internet. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, it’s pretty popular worldwide. It’s not that strange a hobby,” I said. I didn’t want to depress Airi. She probably intended to hide her true feelings, but it was obvious that she cared what I said. She always reacted when I helped her, and Haruka and the others immediately noticed it, too.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I’m not the person to ask about what’s cool these days. I apologize to anyone who happens to like Instagram and stuff like that,” said Haruka. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Just because it’s not for me doesn’t mean I should dismiss something other people like out of hand. That’s on me. I didn’t think it through,” Keisei apologized to&#160;Airi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Airi patted her chest and breathed a sigh of relief.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry to change the topic, but I was wondering about something,” said Akito as the discussion calmed down. He sounded slightly irritated. “Doesn’t Class C seem strange lately?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Class C? They’re always strange. What’re you talking about?” Haruka tilted her head to the side.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I knew what Akito referred to. He was describing the people who’d followed us over the past few days. Even now, one watched us from his hiding place: Komiya, a Class C student and one of Ryuuen’s henchmen. He was undoubtedly monitoring our group, but sat just far enough away to be able to deny it as a coincidence. We would risk being labeled the aggressors if we confronted him. Akito understood that we still didn’t have any solid proof. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">More importantly, another person was currently watching our group. Someone Akito hadn’t noticed yet.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“During our study sessions, those Class C students came over and messed with us, right?” he asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You mean Ryuuen-kun and Shiina-san? It’s them again?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, they sent different people this time around. Komiya and Ishizaki showed up today at archery club. They said they came to observe, which the upperclassmen readily accepted. But they just glared at me the entire time,” said Akito.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So, Komiya was following Akito, then? Ishizaki was probably no longer accompanying him to avoid getting too conspicuous. Of all of us, Akito was clearly the most bothered by Ryuuen’s surveillance. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Maybe they’re interested in the club?” asked Airi, who couldn’t possibly know Ryuuen’s plots.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’d be nice if it were true. But it doesn’t seem like it,” said Akito. He rotated his arms, as if his shoulders were stiff. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ryuuen was continuing to pressure us, and lately, he’d turned up the heat. I could almost hear his bold laughter, hear him say, “Sooner or later, you’ll give way.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Did they do anything to you? Like heckle you, or sneeze to distract you when you were shooting? Or throw rocks at you?” asked Haruka.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“They couldn’t do anything of the sort in front of the coaches or senior students, obviously. They left around the time practice ended,” said Akito.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Although nothing had really changed for me personally, Ryuuen and his cronies had clearly marked us since the last exam. I had to assume they marked Karuizawa, too. They’d probably narrowed down their list to a few prime suspects by now—and I was sure that included me. One final piece of decisive evidence would lead them to conclude that I was the one they were after, and Karuizawa Kei could give them that evidence.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The fact that Ryuuen was treading lightly was proof that he thought about this very carefully. How would he go about finding his puzzle’s final pieces? If I examined his movements thus far, it wasn’t hard to guess what he might do. It was a matter of “when,” not “if.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As I pondered that, Akito and the others continued talking. Keisei offered his thoughts about why Class C was bothering us. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Maybe it has something to do with Class D’s growth. We had zero points shortly after we started school, but now we’re within arm’s reach of Class C. Factor in the Paper Shuffle test’s results, and there’s a chance we might overtake Class C by our third semester. They should be panicking,” he reasoned.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re right. We’re going to overtake them. Us, the same people they’ve been making fun of!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But…that shouldn’t be possible, right?” asked Airi, clearly thinking back to when our class point totals were announced. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Correct,” Keisei said. “When the school announced the class point totals at the start of December, Class D had 262 points, and Class C had 542. There was a 280-point gap.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Class D had faced off against Class C in the Paper Shuffle, and won decisively. One hundred points were transferred from C to D, letting us close the gap by two hundred points total. The difference between Class D and Class C was now a measly eighty points.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Class C was currently in the lead. However, something unrelated to the exam was brewing in Class C. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It seems that Class C did something to severely violate the rules. The school’s keeping the details hush-hush, but Class C had a hundred points docked.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wonder what they did? Sounds like a very Class C thing, though.” Haruka seemed amused…not that Class D had any room to talk. We’d managed to lose a thousand class points within a month of starting school.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nah, it’ll be really crowded at this time of day. It’d probably be faster if you got in line. Save me a seat.”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Komiya, after concluding I was heading to the bathroom, followed them. I watched them leave, then headed in the opposite direction from the bathroom, approaching a girl who’d sat by herself while my group chatted.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Can I have a minute?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The girl was Kamuro, from Class A. She messed with her phone as if she hadn’t noticed my presence.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, I’m talking to you,” I said again.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? Me? What?” Her body language told me she’d only just noticed me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I took a chair next to Kamuro. You could’ve cut the tension in the air with a knife.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’ve been following me. What do you want?” I&#160;asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hmm? What’re you talking about?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I saw you after class yesterday. Two days ago, at Keyaki Mall. Four days ago, at Keyaki Mall. Six days ago, after class. Seven days ago, after class. You were always there. That’s quite a string of coincidences, isn’t it?” I asked. On my phone’s screen, I showed her several pictures I’d taken of her spying.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s… But when…?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You were trying to be discreet, so you couldn’t exactly meet my eyes when I looked in your direction. No wonder you didn’t notice me taking pictures of you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, so what if I’m following you? Do you have a problem with that?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Not especially. It’s not hurting me or anything. I’m not going to tell you to stop, either.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Exactly. This is just a coincidence.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But I have to wonder…what will your boss think when she finds out about this?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Boss? What’re you talking about? You’ve been watching way too many movies.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Then I suppose I’ll report this to Sakayanagi. I’ll tell her you’re not really very good at tailing me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait a minute,” said Kamuro, grabbing my arm as I stood up.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re devoted, aren’t you?” I asked. “To Sakayanagi, I&#160;mean. She asked you to tail me day after day, and here you are, still doing your job. You must be close to her.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t give me that. I’m nobody’s lackey.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s really no need for you to lie. You’re spending your precious time doing something as boring as tailing me, and you’re doing it precisely because you trust and respect Sakayanagi.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Absolutely not. I’d honestly never speak to her again if I could,” Kamuro spat.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Then why do you follow her orders?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Does it matter?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you’re not doing it out of goodwill, then it must be because she’s got some dirt on you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What’re you trying to say?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll report your clumsy efforts to tail me to Sakayanagi. I’ll expose your inability to act on her behalf. Then that weakness of yours she’s exploiting will probably come back to bite you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you’re threatening me, too?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Too,” huh? That just about confirmed that Sakayanagi was indeed using some weakness of Kamuro’s. She’d fallen hook, line, and sinker for my ploy.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, what’s your deal?” Kamuro demanded. “Why is Sakayanagi targeting you?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Who knows? I honestly have no clue,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re the Class D student that Ryuuen’s looking for, aren’t you? That’s the only thing I can think of.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What’re you going to do about it?” I chose not to deny it. If Sakayanagi knew what I was doing, there was no point covering it up.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re trying to threaten </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">me</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">, but I could give Ryuuen some friendly advice, if I felt like it,” Kamuro said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you’re threatening me in return, huh? In that case, how about this?” I decided to make her a proposal. “Feel free to tail me as much as you like. I won’t say a word, and I won’t rat on you to Sakayanagi. In exchange, I want you to keep quiet. Don’t tell anyone other than Sakayanagi what you know about me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, those are your conditions?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t think it’s a bad deal.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re right. I have no real interest in helping Ryuuen, either.” Kamuro nodded in apparent agreement and stood. “I’m going home now. I’m tired.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">With that, she headed for the exit. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Whatever Sakayanagi has on her is dynamite, I bet,” I&#160;muttered to myself.</span></p>
