<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 6:<br/>The Ayanokouji Group’s Formation</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"> </p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">IME FLEW BY,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> and soon it was time for Yukimura’s fifth study session with Miyake and Hasebe. We’d held the prior sessions at Pallet, but we decided to gather at the café inside Keyaki Mall today. We anticipated that Pallet would be exceptionally crowded, since all the clubs had ceased activities in order for the students to focus on the final.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wow, it’s even noisier than I imagined,” said Yukimura.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He looked overwhelmed by the number of students in the café. We managed to secure seats, but Pallet was practically at full capacity. It was packed with students from all grades sitting in their own study groups. Despite how focused everyone was on cramming, the library would definitely have been quieter.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We should’ve met in the library,” said Yukimura, echoing my thoughts. “Or in my room.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No way. It’s easier to do it here. Right, Miyacchi?” asked Hasebe.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. Archery club is already quiet and tense enough,” replied Miyake.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Contrary to what Yukimura had imagined, the two of them appeared to be quite content with studying here. No one wanted to be stuck in a stuffy room. The modern method was to study while chatting with friends. Degenerative evolution.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, you’re the ones who’ll be studying. If you say that you can concentrate, then I believe you. Here, I prepared some material,” said Yukimura.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He handed Hasebe and Miyake notebooks full of questions on the humanities. The two accepted the material indifferently. The questions were lined up closely, like food stalls packed together at a festival with a fireworks display. Yukimura looked all keyed up. These problems he’d created wouldn’t be easy to solve.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Whoa. You really went overboard. You’re merciless, Yukimuu!” said Hasebe. Since she didn’t exactly like studying, let alone studying her weakest subject, I was sure this was torture for her. Miyake, meanwhile, looked as though he was about to vomit. He kept his hands over his stomach while he perused the notebook.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How can you be so terrified before you even start?” asked Yukimura.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s just… There are clearly way more questions here than last time, and they look harder.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s a mediocre student’s mindset. Think about what you </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">can </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">do first, and challenge yourself,” said Yukimura, his voice filled with passion.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, are the questions simpler than last time?” Hasebe asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course not.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, I knew they’d be tough.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Well, obviously things couldn’t stay simple forever. Honestly, the problems and tips Yukimura had come up with really were brilliant. He could probably become a real teacher someday if he tried—not that it was any of my business. He chided students, but never gave up on them, and never raised his voice or lost his patience when they didn’t understand something. Had Yukimura, like Horikita, matured? It was incredible.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In the first semester, Horikita and Yukimura had both insisted they were placed in Class D by mistake, as both were superior students. That felt like such a long time ago.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Come on, Hasebe.” Miyake understood that there was no point in grumbling. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wow, you’re getting pretty gung-ho there, Miyacchi. What’s up with you? Is your blood boiling?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I finally got a break from club stuff, but I don’t want to spend all my free time studying. Can I head back after I finish?” Miyake asked Yukimura.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yukimura and Horikita had different teaching methods. While Horikita designated a fixed amount of time for studying, Yukimura was more flexible. His sessions lasted until the students got through all the material, and could therefore finish earlier or later than expected. The method’s effectiveness depended on who Yukimura tutored—if he tried this with lackadaisical students like Ike, for instance, it would be a bust. They’d probably just answer without thinking so they could finish early and escape. Conversely, Hasebe and Miyake could handle it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you don’t have any free time, why not quit the club?” asked Hasebe.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I want to be in the club. I just also want free time,” replied Miyake.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wow, selfish.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">At any rate, if they’d managed to regain their motivation, there was nothing more to say about it. If one or both of them had withdrawn from the study group, who knew what words Horikita would’ve hurled my way. It seemed that Yukimura really had a positive impact on them both. I didn’t think they had any remaining doubts about his method’s effectiveness.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“All right, Ayanokouji. Starting today, I’ll have you do these too.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh…?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You get good enough grades, but your partner is Satou. You’ll need to prepare and review thoroughly so you don’t both get kicked out,” said Yukimura.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, I—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Just do it, Ayanokouji-kun. Let’s all die together,” said Hasebe, dipping her head low so her hair hung down. She grabbed my hand, as if she were a vengeful spirit trying to drag me into a well.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Welcoooome,” she moaned.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">A shiver ran up my spine as I was swallowed by the terrible darkness of the humanities questions.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">6.1</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"> </p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">“T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HAT REMINDS ME</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> There’s a guy named Yoshimoto-kun in Class C, right? Do you know him, Miyacchi?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yoshimoto Kousetsu? Yeah, he’s in the archery club.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, yeah. That guy. I heard that he started going out with a second-year. You know about that?” Hasebe, tired of studying, started to gossip.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nope. But, come to think of it, he’s been in a real hurry to leave as soon as club ends lately. That must be why.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If you were an adult in your thirties, an age difference of a year or two didn’t matter much. For high school students, though, trying to date someone even one grade above you was tricky. At least, I was fairly sure that was how things worked. I was still a budding teen myself, after all.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It seems as if Yoshimoto-kun’s really into it. He said they’re going to get married someday. Jeez, guys are morons, aren’t they?” Hasebe and Miyake’s conversation completely derailed their focus.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Talk about the future later, no matter who’s dating whom. First, get your work done,” said Yukimura.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I know, I know. We’re just taking a short break,” said Hasebe. She didn’t seem to care what Yukimura said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. Very short.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wow, okay, I feel those sarcastic vibes. I’m gonna get a refill.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re having even more sugar? Your coffee’s so ridiculously sweet, I’m shocked you can drink it,” said Miyake.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I struggle to understand why anyone would drink their coffee black.” Hasebe started to get up, but stumbled slightly over the backpack she’d placed at her feet. “Whoa!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She dropped her empty cup. My eyes followed it as it rolled along the ground, until it stopped rolling by a particular student’s feet.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah, sorr—” Hasebe started to apologize. However, the student’s foot crushed the cup.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“My, you seem to be enjoying yourselves. Mind if we join?” asked Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What are you…?” Steeling herself, Hasebe glared at the Class C guys. An understandable reaction. Ryuuen, the one who’d crushed the cup underfoot, had a smug grin on his face. Ishizaki, Komiya, and Kondou, the evergreen trio of goons, stood behind him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There was also one girl with them whom we hadn’t seen before. She stood next to Ishizaki, and appeared extremely calm, her face devoid of emotion. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey!” said Hasebe. “Why’d you stomp on my cup like that?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It rolled up to my feet, so I thought you were throwing it away. I stomped it to save you the trouble,” said Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He kicked the crushed cup back toward Hasebe, laughing. A little of the remaining liquid poured out of a hole in the side and splashed onto the ground. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Miyake slowly stood. “Hey, Ryuuen. I’ve been dying to say this: knock it off.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh? And whom exactly do you think you’re speaking to, buddy?” Ishizaki grabbed Miyake by his collar.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wasn’t talking to you. Lackeys should mind their own business, Ishizaki,” said Miyake, shaking off his grip.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Bastard!” shouted Ishizaki, attracting other patrons’ attention. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Surprisingly, that outburst enraged Ryuuen most of all. “Shut up. Are you really trying to throw down, Ishizaki?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“S-sorry. Miyake was getting full of himself, so I—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Reckless idiots amuse me, but I need you to behave yourself for now.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ryuuen was right to rein in Ishizaki. There weren’t just first-year students around here. Senior students, store clerks, and several surveillance cameras surrounded us. Nothing went unseen here, and if Class C started trouble, they would pay for it. Any testimonies from the onlookers or recordings would make sure of that.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I have no business with you. I’m interested in those two,” said Ryuuen to Miyake, glancing at Yukimura and me. “Did you receive my gift?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Naturally, Yukimura was bewildered. “What in the world are you talking about?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ryuuen looked to me. Without a doubt, the “gift” was the email he’d sent me the other day, the one that said, </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">What are you?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Who knows?” I feigned ignorance. Ryuuen’s methods were heavy-handed. I wouldn’t dig my own grave by answering his questions. Even if he tried to arouse suspicion, he couldn’t claim anything definite. No matter how far he tried to take this, it was shrouded in a degree of secrecy. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, how about it? Did </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">you</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> catch something, Hiyori?” Ryuuen glanced at the girl with him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“‘How about it?’ I can’t say anything at this stage,” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">A lot of the students working under Ryuuen were terrified of him, but this Hiyori was completely calm. She kept looking back and forth between Yukimura and me, though her eyes weren’t entirely focused. What in the world was Ryuuen planning?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Both their faces are weak. I’ll probably forget them immediately,” she added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Heh heh, come now. These are our future friends, after all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yukimura-san, Ayanokouji-kun, Kouenji-kun. Who was the other guy?” she asked.</span></p>

But if we just focus on the things a first-year, second-semester high school student needs to know, then I think so. He definitely shouldn’t expect to fail this exam. What about you, Yukimura-kun? Did you manage to resolve Hasebe-san and Miyake-kun’s issues?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course. Ayanokouji watched everything closely, so he can back me up on that. Right?” Keisei asked me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t think there was a better method we could’ve gone with. I’m not worried about anyone,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m glad. I’d absolutely hate to lose anybody from Class D,” said Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. But are we really going to do okay?” said Karuizawa. She still seemed unsettled. “Look, I hate the idea of having fewer classmates. But this test always gets someone kicked out, right? So, like, there’s no guarantee Sudou-kun and I won’t fail, right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I can’t guarantee that,” Kushida said. “But…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Then don’t act like it’s a done deal.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The somewhat relaxed atmosphere became tense.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You know, Kushida-san, I kinda feel as if you’ve been paying us lip service for a while now,” said Karuizawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“R-really? But I just want everyone to pass,” said Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Man, it sure must be nice. Being smart, I mean. You don’t have any idea what’s gonna happen to me,” snapped Karuizawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s okay, Karuizawa-san. You studied hard in your group,” Hirata reassured her. She still didn’t seem convinced, however.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Kushida-san, I’ve wanted to say this to you for a while now. Don’t you think you’re taking this goody two-shoes act a bit too far?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Uh…r-really?” stammered Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Can you please calm down, Karuizawa-san? We’re in the middle of discussing our final exam. Don’t waste our time,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Be quiet, Horikita-san. Hey, Kushida-san. Are you making fun of me in your head right now? Thinking about how stupid I am?” asked Karuizawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wouldn’t do something like that,” said Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Then don’t patronize me. Tests are always hard for me, no matter how many I take. Are you going to be responsible if I fail?” asked Karuizawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This was getting absurd. Karuizawa’s inexplicable anger baffled not just Kushida, but everyone else. It seemed Karuizawa found her outward virtues insulting. Then, in a move no one saw coming, Karuizawa picked up her glass of grape juice and dumped it over Kushida. The juice soaked into Kushida’s blazer.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Karuizawa-san!” Hirata grabbed Karuizawa’s hand, which still held the cup. “You can’t do this. That’s not okay.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“B-but… Are you saying I’m in the wrong here?” asked Karuizawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry, but yes, Karuizawa-san. Kushida-san hasn’t done anything bad to you,” said Horikita. Even she, locked in a cold war with Kushida, couldn’t defend this.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m fine, really. I don’t mind. Please don’t blame Karuizawa-san, okay?” said Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No. That’s unacceptable. Karuizawa is at fault here,” said Keisei matter-of-factly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Naturally, everyone here was treating Karuizawa as the villain. Anyone would think that Karuizawa was in the wrong because of her self-absorbed comments. Her actions weren’t exactly out of character, though.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, sure. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">I’m</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> the bad guy. Kushida-san is the class’s favorite, after all,” said Karuizawa. She turned toward me, as if I were her last hope. “Hey, Ayanokouji-kun. Whose side are you on?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Whose side am I on? There are no sides. You’re in the wrong, Karuizawa,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I knew it. Everyone is my enemy,” said Karuizawa. She got up and grabbed her bag without a word of apology.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Karuizawa-san. If you leave now, you’ll regret it later. I don’t want that,” said Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, what do you want me to do, then?” barked Karuizawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“First, apologize to Kushida-san. That’s the most important thing,” said Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Karuizawa wouldn’t even listen to her supposed boyfriend. She stood her ground. “So, even though I don’t think I did anything wrong, </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">I </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">have to apologize?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You have to talk to her,” said Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Karuizawa stood completely still and silent for a moment. Then, slowly, she spoke. “Sorry.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, it’s completely all right. I should’ve been more understanding of how you felt, Karuizawa-san,” said Kushida. She could have been angry, under the circumstances, but forgiveness came easily from her lips.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Karuizawa looked guilty as she sat back down next to Hirata. “I guess I lost my cool. Sorry,” she said again. Kushida responded with a gracious smile.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thank you.” Hirata patted his chest and let out a sigh of relief. However, this didn’t necessarily mean that everything was settled.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Kushida-san, do you have a spare blazer to wear tomorrow? Will you be okay?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah, no. My first blazer got damaged, so this was the only one I had left,” said Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The school had provided us with two blazers each when we first arrived, but accidents happened, and students could simply outgrow their uniforms. A store in the Keyaki Mall specialized in student uniforms. However, it took time and cost many points to tailor clothing.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ain’t there a dry cleaner and a laundromat? I could bring your blazer in with some of my sweaty basketball clothes. If I take them in today, you should get the blazer back first thing tomorrow morning,” said Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I didn’t know there was a dry cleaner,” replied Kushida. “That sounds like a good solution.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Thanks to Sudou, we were on our way to resolving the issue. Then, Karuizawa contributed in her own way.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, it’s not exactly an apology, but at least let me cover your cleaning fee,” said Karuizawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Really, don’t worry about it,” said Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’d feel too guilty if I didn’t.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you sure?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. Please let me do this much,” said Karuizawa, and so, she paid the fee.</span></p>

<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">6.7</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"> </p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">O</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">N THE WAY BACK</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> to my dorm after our chaotic meeting, I came across Katsuragi standing next to a water fountain. He didn’t seem to be looking for anyone in particular, so I called out to him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What are you doing?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ayanokouji? Oh, nothing. Just thinking a little about the final exam next week,” he replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In a place like this?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wanted some time alone to think in peace and quiet,” said Katsuragi. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">His thought process was unlike that of a typical first-year high school student. That being said, I couldn’t imagine that the test was so advanced that even Class A would agonize over it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you feel like the exam will go well?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I decided to respond honestly. “Dunno. But everyone’s studying really hard.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is that so? It would be nice if no one were expelled,” said Katsuragi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t feel as if he was particularly concerned for his classmates. “Did something happen?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“When you were in junior high, were you ever class representative or on the student council?” he asked, his voice somber. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, wasn’t interested at all,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ve been a student council member since elementary school. I even served as student council president in both elementary and junior high. But, after coming to this school, I had to make some significant course corrections,” said Katsuragi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Come to think of it, you didn’t join the student council here.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wanted to, but I couldn’t get Student Council President Horikita’s attention.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So far, this had nothing to do with the final exam.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“At first glance, the student council president and class representatives don’t appear to have much authority. Most students think that participating is nothing more than a waste of time and effort. That’s why only a few people want to join the council,” said Katsuragi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I shared the majority’s feelings on that. I didn’t want any management position.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“However, those roles come with certain privileges. There’s a divide between people who hold the positions and people who don’t—one that can’t be crossed. I’ve lost those privileges,” said Katsuragi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But your standing in Class A is still good, right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If that were the case, we absolutely wouldn’t have chosen Class B as our target for the final exam,” he replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">True, someone like Katsuragi would’ve picked Class C or Class D. He’d have chosen the path of strong defense and decisive victory.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is it okay to talk to me about your class’s internal politics?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s fine. You understand what it’s like.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You know, you should cut yourself some slack. You’re trying to singlehandedly carry Class A, but I bet if you relaxed a little, they’d still be fine. The important thing right now is to maintain your position,” I told him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I suppose. Hmm…to be told to maintain my position by a Class D student who should be chasing after us,” he responded, clearly amused.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Perhaps I can see things objectively precisely because I can’t catch up with you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When the two of us got to the dormitory, we found a crowd in the lobby.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s pretty noisy in here. Did something happen?” Katsuragi asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Dunno. Should we try asking around?” I caught sight of the Professor and called to him. “What happened?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, Ayanokouji, is that you? It seems that all us first years somehow received the same missive in our mailboxes.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The same letter?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I slipped through the crowd, went over to my mailbox, and unlocked it. The mailboxes didn’t normally see much use, but students did occasionally get packages, gifts from friends, or letters from the school. Others peered over my shoulder as I opened the door.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I took out a letter folded into fourths and walked back to Sotomura.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is this…?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, it would seem so!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">A few moments later, Katsuragi returned with a similar piece of paper. We unfolded our letters at nearly the same time. They said:</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">First-year Class B student Ichinose Honami may be collecting points illegally. —Ryuuen Kakeru</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sotomura showed us the same message on his paper. Katsuragi mumbled, “What </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">is</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> that guy planning by signing his name to this? If this claim is baseless, the school will take disciplinary action against him.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If he’s risking that, does that mean his claim is true?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I suppose that this is something I could see Ryuuen doing. If people start to suspect Ichinose of fraud, it’ll make her life difficult, regardless of whether it’s true. What Ryuuen is doing could be considered defamation, but he wouldn’t care about something like that,” Katsuragi explained.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was true. While Ryuuen could theoretically lose his reputation if his lies were exposed, he had no reputation to speak of.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, Ryuuen’s back!” said one student as Ryuuen entered the building. I wondered whether he knew what had caused all the commotion.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, Ryuuen. What’re you trying to pull?!” One of the Class B guys grabbed him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hmm? What in the world are you talking about?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This! Your letter! This nonsense you handed everyone!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, that. Pretty interesting stuff, huh?” said Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What’s so interesting about it?! Look, this is going too far!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Fine then, let’s see some proof that Ichinose isn’t collecting points illegally,” Ryuuen replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How about it, Ichinose?” asked Ryuuen, still holding the letter. He looked at Ichinose, who’d just arrived.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No matter what I say to you right now, you probably won’t believe me, will you, Ryuuen-kun?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s right. Besides, it’s up to the school to decide whether you’re doing something wrong.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I suppose so. Sorry, everyone. It seems as though I’m under suspicion. But please don’t worry. I’ll report to the teachers tomorrow and prove that this is just a misunderstanding on Ryuuen-kun’s part,” said Ichinose proudly.</span></p>

<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How exactly do you plan on proving that, Ichinose?” asked Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll explain everything in detail. I’ll tell them how many points I have, and how I acquired them. If I do that, will you be satisfied?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Report it to the school? Why don’t you prove it right now, in front of all of us?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Will you believe me if I just explain it to you, Ryuuen-kun?” Ichinose countered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, I won’t. Spewing lies is probably as easy for you as breathing,” Ryuuen taunted.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Then it stands to reason that, if I report my total points to the school, there shouldn’t be any room for fraud, right?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Heh. I see. I suppose you have a point,” said Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you convinced?!” shouted a Class B student.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Humans are nasty, lying creatures. Isn’t it possible that Ichinose is coming up with some kind of scheme to hide the evidence right now?” said Ryuuen. He was hounding her, trying to back her into a corner.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What is that guy plotting? Even if Ichinose has lots of points, she’s far from the kind of person who’d steal them. Ryuuen has absolutely no chance of succeeding,” said Katsuragi. His expression grew even sterner.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, what can I do to make you believe me?” Ichinose asked Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“First off, say how many points you have right here and now. Second, explain how you acquired those points. I’ll report that information to the school tomorrow. If you do that, you’ll convince the students who distrust you, right?” said Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If they did that, it would dramatically cut down on any opportunities for her to lie or make excuses later. However, I couldn’t imagine that Ichinose would agree to something like that so easily.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I can’t accept, Ryuuen-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you admit that you committed fraud?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s not what I’m saying. It’s precisely because I didn’t obtain my points illegally that I can’t just show you all my cards. Your private points have a big impact on the moves you can make. If I explain everything to the school tomorrow, they should investigate. On top of that, if I </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">did</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> commit fraud, they’d make everything public anyway, right?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s no guarantee that you’ll report your total points to the school like you say you will.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Then go yourself, Ryuuen-kun. Tell them just what you wrote down in this letter,” said Ichinose.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Really? Heh. Seems like you’re pretty confident.” Ryuuen snickered. It was true. A guilty person might have been nervous, yet Ichinose remained as calm as ever. “Well, I’ll be looking forward to tomorrow.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ichinose watched Ryuuen board the elevator with a bold smile on his face.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Once the seed of doubt is planted, that doubt will grow until it’s eradicated. Even an exemplary student like Ichinose isn’t exempt. The deeper the doubt, the greater the loss of trust,” said Katsuragi. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He was right. The same thing applied to politicians. However high their approval rating, one damaging lie could cost a great deal of support. Once the lie proved entirely groundless, approval ratings might rebound, perhaps even soar higher than ever. But, generally, it was hard to entirely dispel allegations once they spread. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The next day, Ichinose did as she’d said she would. The school issued an official notice that there was no fraudulent activity. They cleared her of all suspicion. </span></p>
