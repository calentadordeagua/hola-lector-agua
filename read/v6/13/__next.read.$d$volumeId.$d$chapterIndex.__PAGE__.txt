<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 7:<br/>A Difference in Determination</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"> </p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">IME TICKED AWAY</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> as we continued to cram for the upcoming exams. Students’ spirits dipped under the onslaught of grueling, endless work they had to do. December arrived, and the final exam was less than three days away. Tomorrow, we’d be off for the weekend, the final exam waiting for us on Monday.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">To be honest, the exam itself wasn’t too dangerous. As far as the Class D students were concerned, we were more united than ever. The study groups were producing amazing results. Even Sudou and the other slackers did their best. The issues were Ryuuen and Kushida. They’d been making their moves behind the scenes, without a doubt. However, I could read them pretty easily.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ryuuen’s two goals were to beat Class D, and to smoke out the puppet master hiding behind Horikita. If he wanted to win in overall points, that limited his tactics. The best he could do was get Class C to study extra hard, or come up with extremely difficult test problems, both of which were relatively ordinary strategies.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t know how fully Class C had united, or if they’d studied extensively. We hadn’t seen them in the café, the library, the classrooms, or anywhere else. Was it simple coincidence? Or were the Class C students working somewhere in secret? Even if they did study extra hard, as long as they didn’t surpass Class D, we’d be fine. At any rate, though, I had no idea what their strategy was. I’d be able to figure it out if I thought about it from a different perspective.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thinking about something?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, sorry,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita looked up at me from the base of the stairs. I hurried down after her. She held a large manila envelope stuffed full of the questions she and Hirata had slaved over for the past month. That envelope held Class D’s fate, which was exactly why Horikita kept it as confidential as possible. She wouldn’t even let me see the questions. Ultimately, she was the only one who knew all of them.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What are our chances?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hard to say. Don’t expect too much. The school made many adjustments. However, there’s no doubt that we’ve completed the most difficult part of the exam so far,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She exuded confidence, so she’d probably done a solid job. The issue was what came next. How would we protect these questions? </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">While making our way to the staff room, Horikita and I bumped into Ryuuen in the hallway.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yo, Suzune.” Ryuuen wore a bold smile on his face. He also held a manila envelope.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is this a coincidence? Or an ambush, perhaps?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s inevitable. I was waiting for you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“An ambush, then.” Horikita let out an exasperated sigh and walked past Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re submitting your test problems at the last minute too, huh? Let’s go together,” said Ryuuen, holding out his manila envelope. “Anyone might try and sneak a peek at this, so I understand your caution.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re not worried about any traitors in your own class?” asked Horikita somewhat mockingly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ha. There’s no way anyone would be stupid enough to betray me,” said Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yet you waited until the last minute to submit your questions,” countered Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ryuuen probably couldn’t help but enjoy her ire. Horikita and I walked on, and he followed us.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I really hope that whatever intelligence you squeezed out of those defective pieces of garbage in your class works in Class C’s favor,” he said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita ignored him. “Ayanokouji-kun, have you been studying properly? How are things with you and your partner?” she asked me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I think we should be safe,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thinking isn’t enough. We can’t afford to have a single student drop out. Don’t get complacent, even though I’m sure we can handle whatever Class C throws at us,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Not one to be silenced, Ryuuen responded to that verbal jab. “Oh ho! That’s an interesting remark. You sound pretty arrogant.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hmm, who knows? Maybe it was simply cheap provocation? Just your style,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Maybe so,” Ryuuen replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As soon as we arrived at the staff room, Horikita called Chabashira-sensei. Similarly, Ryuuen called Sakagami-sensei, who appeared first and took the manila envelope from Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Would you please accept this?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. I’ll talk to you later.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After their short exchange, Chabashira-sensei showed up and took Sakagami-sensei’s place. She already seemed to know what we were there for, and her gaze was focused only on the manila envelope. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, there it is,” she said. She paid no attention to Ryuuen, off to the side.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“These are the final questions, Chabashira-sensei,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll hold on to them,” said Chabashira-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ryuuen watched with a creepy smile. As Chabashira-sensei reached for the envelope, Horikita paused.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’d like to ask you one thing, if I may,” Horikita said to Chabashira-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“These questions are linked to either Class D’s victory or defeat. They’re two sides of the same coin. We must avoid a leak at all costs. After I hand these papers over to you, could you please not show them to anyone else? Myself included.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita had negotiated those terms based on her failure at the sports festival. I didn’t know if Chabashira-sensei would understand, though.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You want me to turn down absolutely anyone who asks to see them?” Chabashira-sensei asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Would that be a problem?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s not the issue. I understand that you’re afraid of information leaking, and the school has no right to refuse your request. However, there are conditions,” said Chabashira-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Conditions, you say?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The entire class needs to agree to this. Has everyone approved this measure?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I didn’t receive permission from every individual student, but…I don’t think they’d object. No one wants their class to lose,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You can’t say that for sure. As I may have mentioned before, people are full of surprises. Some students </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">might</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> very well want to lose,” said Chabashira-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira-sensei continued. “On that note, can you guarantee that these are the exam questions your class wishes to use? Not everyone in class saw and agreed to all of them, right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you asking me to prove that? You want me to show the questions to everyone in class and confirm that they’re okay with them?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Not quite. I’m saying that it’s not that simple. I can’t be entirely sure that you, Horikita Suzune, are acting for your class’s sake. That said, I’ll honor your request. If any student asks, I absolutely won’t disclose the questions and answers you created,” said Chabashira-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thank you very much. Now I can face the exam in peace.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“However, I will say this—generally, it’s not ideal to restrict information in this manner. It proves that the class isn’t united,” said Chabashira-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She wasn’t wrong. If everyone on our class was on the up-and-up, then we wouldn’t have had to worry about protecting the information from leaks. Selfishly, I thought that this kind of thing would’ve never happened with Class B.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“A painful lesson to learn. I’ll work harder to bring the class together,” answered Horikita confidently.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira-sensei smiled a bit. “You’ve changed, Horikita.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Some things can’t stay the same.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“As I said, I’ll honor your request. In case of unforeseen circumstances, though, I’d like to add one provision to our arrangement. As long as they have your permission, Horikita, I’ll disclose information to those asking to see the questions. Is that okay? If I didn’t show them to absolutely anyone, that could be a problem for you too, right?” asked Chabashira-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In short, 100 percent confidentiality wasn’t really possible. Our teacher wanted at least some means of disclosing it if necessary.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, as long as I’m present at the time,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course. Someone might lie about having your permission, after all. In the event that someone comes looking for the questions, well, I’ll tell them everything you said. As a teacher, I can’t lie,” said Chabashira-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s all right.” Horikita breathed a sigh of relief. Something had finally gone right. This plan was foolproof, and should’ve precluded the possibility of underhanded tricks like the kind we saw in the sports festival. Even if someone tried to pay to see the questions, it wouldn’t be allowed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, something still felt off to me, though I couldn’t put my finger on what. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Everything was going smoothly. The test questions that Horikita and Hirata had devised were undoubtedly very difficult, and Horikita had put preventative measures in place to ensure that they didn’t leak. So far, so good. Even if Kushida tried to obtain the answers for Ryuuen, she’d need Horikita’s presence and approval. Everything was rock solid. No holes anywhere. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">I see, I see.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita had negotiated flawlessly, but there was definitely something up with our homeroom teacher. You wouldn’t have been able to tell by her body language, though. Chabashira-sensei solemnly accepted the test questions and motioned for us to leave. Still, Ryuuen’s resolute attitude felt off—something about the fact that he didn’t seem flustered at all.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Let’s head on back, Ayanokouji-kun. Our business here is finished.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I ignored Horikita and looked into Chabashira-sensei’s eyes instead. She stared at me in return. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">See it, Horikita. Before it’s too late.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I couldn’t say anything in front of Ryuuen, nor could I stare for too long. Even if we made it through this, our hands might be tied afterward. Horikita started to walk away from the staff room, then immediately stopped dead in her tracks.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Chabashira-sensei. You said that you wouldn’t lie, didn’t you?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s right. That’s required of me, as a teacher,” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, will the school accept the questions I just submitted to you?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita had noticed after all. She’d figured it out herself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Not until we confirm that there isn’t anything abnormal about them,” replied Chabashira-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What’s the matter, Horikita?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita didn’t pay any attention to me. “Let me rephrase that. Have you already accepted other test questions?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Our teacher became quiet. “What do you mean by that?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’d like to hear the answer from your own lips, Chabashira-sensei.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“My response is that the school already accepted and finished reviewing questions from Class D.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Our reality imploded.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So…does that mean someone else already submitted test questions and answers?” asked Horikita. She couldn’t seem to process what was happening.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Correct. The school won’t use the questions you just handed me,” said Chabashira-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Please cancel acceptance of the previously submitted test questions. I have the correct ones here,” said Horikita, pointing to the manila envelope.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, from my understanding of the conversation so far, I knew that wasn’t going to be a simple matter.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Unfortunately, Horikita, I cannot indulge your ego. I already finished reviewing another student’s questions. That student shared similar concerns. They wanted me to keep the questions secret, too. This student said that, in the event that another student selfishly showed up and asked to change the problems, I should merely accept the new questions and hold on to them. They also wanted me to tell them who asked to swap those questions afterward,” said Chabashira-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What in the world?” The fight drained out of Horikita, and she slumped over. This was far too cruel. “What student? Please tell me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Kushida Kikyou.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The answer was obvious. Horikita had thought that she would prevent Kushida’s betrayal. However, Kushida had struck first. Our knowledge of her past made her take bold, drastic measures.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But under the right circumstances, Class D can change the questions the school already accepted, yes?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. However, the deadline is the end of today. If you wish to change the test questions, please bring Kushida here,” said Chabashira-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was hopeless. Kushida wouldn’t agree to that. Even if we started looking for her now, there was a 100 percent chance that she’d turned her phone off and holed up in her dorm room—or somewhere else, even. We couldn’t possibly get a hold of her before the end of the day.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I can only speculate about which one of you is lying, Horikita. I don’t know the truth. I also acknowledge that an unknown third party might be pulling the strings here. If you don’t resolve this dispute within your class, it’ll be bad,” said Chabashira-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How much time do we have left to correct the questions?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Until 6 p.m.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I checked my phone. It was a little before four. We only had about two hours left.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ha ha ha ha ha! What are you even doing, Suzune?” Ryuuen sneered. The guy had undoubtedly known about this situation from the very start; he laughed raucously at our despair. “This is already checkmate, don’t you see? The questions that you struggled so hard to come up with are completely meaningless!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Were you behind this? Did you instruct Kushida-san to do this?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hmm, who can say? I mean, it’s not like </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">I </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">know anything about Class D, right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita raised her voice. “I’m not going to tolerate this outsider eavesdropping on the conversation any further!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh ho, how scary. Guess I’ll just head back to my dorm like a good boy. I sure am looking forward to the exam’s results,” said Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Aren’t you going to look for Kushida, Horikita?” Chabashira-sensei asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I dislike futile gestures.” Even if we did manage to find Kushida, there was no way she’d comply. The game was already over. “Did Kushida-san instruct you not to show the questions to anyone?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, I received no such instructions,” said Chabashira-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was hardly surprising; it only confirmed our suspicions.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Please show them to me, then.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira-sensei showed Horikita the test questions that Kushida submitted. After only a brief glance, one thing became apparent.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“These are incredibly difficult,” Horikita said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. They definitely are,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida’s questions didn’t appear any easier than those Horikita and Hirata had prepared. These were excellently constructed problems. In fact, they were so well-structured that you’d never guess that Horikita herself didn’t come up with them. Since Ryuuen was involved, chances were high that Kaneda made them. While Kushida had attended the study sessions and accepted Horikita’s challenge, she had slipped through with a terribly clever plan.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">No third parties would know the truth. If Kushida’s problems had been so easy that even Sudou could have solved them, she would have been suspected of sabotage. However, if they were similar enough, then she would fly under the radar. These questions made things much, much more complicated. If you knew the answers ahead of time, it didn’t matter how difficult the problems were. As long as all of Class C shared the answers with one another, they would get high scores.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita had promised that she wouldn’t expose Kushida’s past. Hirata, afraid of internal class conflict, probably wouldn’t say anything. That meant that the student who struck first won the day. If Class D lost, Horikita, who’d assumed leadership, would unavoidably take the blame. Kushida would siphon away Horikita’s power while simultaneously using Ryuuen to drive her into a corner. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">None of this was as dire, however, as Horikita’s bet with Kushida. If Kushida and Ryuuen had colluded, there was no doubt that Kushida received Class C’s test questions in exchange for her cooperation. If that had happened, Kushida would most likely score a perfect one hundred points on her math exam. If Horikita answered even one question incorrectly, she would have to drop out of school. Horikita wouldn’t dare break her promise.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, is there nothing more to be done?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida’s preemptive strike had put Horikita between a rock and a hard place. It looked as if she was out of options. This all came down to the naivete of her plans. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">If it’d been </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">me,</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;"> then…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s all right, Horikita. Ryuuen left,” said Chabashira-sensei to Horikita, who still hung her head. What was going on? Chabashira-sensei didn’t look shaken in the least. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry. I wanted to take extra precautions just in case, so I kept up the act,” said Horikita, lifting her head. She didn’t look depressed at all.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">A</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">FTER HORIKITA</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> headed back to the dorm, I thought about what was to come. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Despite myself, I couldn’t help but feel a little excited.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Then why had someone deliberately put down Ryuuen’s name as the sender? Because an anonymous message had less credibility. The allegations might’ve been disregarded entirely. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But what was the letter’s point? To put you on your guard?” said Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Who knows? I’ve thought about it at length, but it’s still unclear. Did the culprit simply want to know whether Ichinose had a lot of points? Or maybe… No, that’s impossible. Couldn’t be something that stupid,” said Ryuuen. Whatever he’d considered was just far too removed from reality.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Either way, the strategy had been well prepared and flawlessly executed. Ryuuen’s interest in the person he called “X” had only increased.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, Kikyou. I don’t know anything about your past, and I’m not interested. However, if you keep trying to get Horikita kicked out of school, you’re going to disappear. Get it?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re in a dangerous position yourself, aren’t you? If Class C loses collective points for this exam, isn’t that bad?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yep. With this stroke of luck, your class probably has a chance of being promoted to C,” said Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How does it feel to be knocked down a peg by the ‘defectives’ in Class D?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ryuuen seemed completely indifferent, even after being hit with Kushida’s tenacious instigation. He’d never cared about that from the beginning.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I feel like I don’t have a care in the world right now. Whether it’s Class A or D, they’re all just letters in the end. We’re only scratching the surface of our battle,” said Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do you mean?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Of course, Ryuuen couldn’t answer her. However, his objective hadn’t changed one bit since he’d started at the school. While there had been moments when he stumbled, his preparations to reach Class A were still progressing smoothly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do your absolute best, and reach for the upper classes,” said Ryuuen, turning and starting to walk away.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait—the cheat sheet! Something’s not right here!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Heh.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What’s this all about? Tell me, Ryuuen!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You noticed?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The contradiction that dawned on her had her reeling with doubt. A new problem was growing.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why did someone in Class D have these test questions? Only you and I should have them. I just can’t figure it out.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Because I gave them to X, I suppose,” said Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you betrayed me?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No. It was a necessary deal.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ryuuen’s eyes lowered to his cell phone. On it were pictures of the questions and answers before he’d changed them. He had sent those pictures to an unidentified email address.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“However, X understands me very well,” he added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He’d received a message from X with the word </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“Transaction”</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> in the subject line. The message read as follows: </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Provide me with the questions and answers that Class C devised for our final exam, or make significant changes to the questions you gave Kushida Kikyou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Normally, Ryuuen wouldn’t dignify that with a response. However, moments earlier, X had freely given him information beneficial to Class C with no strings attached, by warning him that Horikita Suzune had seen through Ryuuen’s plans and taken preemptive action to get her questions approved instead of Kushida’s. That had come as quite the shock to Ryuuen, whose whole strategy depended on the questions he thought Kushida had submitted. Without X’s warning, the Class C students who didn’t study enough might’ve failed. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Knowing that, Ryuuen had had three options.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The first was to defy X and give Kushida the correct questions for the math test, letting her win her bet against Horikita. However, Ryuuen wanted to avoid Horikita being expelled at all costs. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The second was to leave the test questions as they were and let Kushida’s cheating be exposed, so she’d get kicked out of school. However, Ryuuen didn’t want to give X </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">exactly </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">what he wanted. Playing into X’s ideal scenario was no fun.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The final option, and the one Ryuuen eventually chose, was to give Kushida the wrong questions and let Horikita win.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, X protected Suzune while managing to keep you in check, Kikyou,” said Ryuuen. Suzune was fighting on the front lines, but this person was doing some adept work behind the scenes. When Ryuuen had realized that X turned his strategy to use Kushida against him, he’d barely held back his laughter. “But I’m about to drive him into a corner. If he doesn’t reveal his identity—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ryuuen pulled up an image on his phone—the same image he’d sent the mysterious mastermind. The person in the photograph would be instrumental to discovering X’s identity.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Then I’m going to destroy her.”</span></p>
