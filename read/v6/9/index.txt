<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 3:<br/>The Paper Shuffle</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"> </p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">A</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;"> </span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">FEW DAYS LATER</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> our class’s atmosphere was heavy. The feeling wasn’t disappointment so much as nervousness. Class D’s homeroom teacher, Chabashira-sensei, appeared to sense it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Take your seats. It seems you prepared quite a bit in advance for this,” she remarked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The moment Chabashira-sensei walked in, everyone went quiet and a bit stiff. We should’ve behaved like that originally. It was the natural way for a class to be, but Chabashira-sensei didn’t hide her surprise at our mature conduct.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“My, my. You all look so serious. It’s hard to imagine you’re Class D.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s because today’s the day you announce the midterm results, right?” Ike looked slightly nervous. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira-sensei grinned in response. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s exactly right. The school will expel you immediately if you fail the midterm or final exam. I told you as much before, so I imagine it’s fresh in your memory. I’m happy to see that you’ve matured,” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira-sensei was impressed with this new, previously unseen side of her students, but that didn’t necessarily mean that our test scores would improve. At most, we’d just had a change in attitude. Naturally, our teacher said as much.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“However, results are results. If you received a failing grade, you’ll need to prepare for the consequences. So, without further ado, I’ll now post the results. Read them carefully.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Her warning was genuine. The classroom’s surveillance cameras always had their lenses trained on the students, observing everything. If someone acted out after seeing their test scores, the school would undoubtedly respond with draconian measures.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, we can see everyone’s test scores?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course. It’s allowed by the school’s rules.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Regardless of whether the Class D students wanted to keep their personal information private, their grades were posted on the blackboard for all to see. There was absolutely no privacy, nothing concealed. Just like a company might post up every salesman’s profits, our chart exposed the capable and the incapable alike.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">At times like this, the ones who stood out were those with good grades and those with bad grades. The lowest scorers would suffer the most, though, becoming the object of everyone’s contempt. The threshold for failing hadn’t changed from the previous test, but the situation was a little different. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“A score of forty or higher is considered a passing grade for all subjects. Anyone with a score under forty will face expulsion. These scores also reflect your results from the sports festival. If anyone achieved a score of over one hundred points on this test because of their sports festival results, the school would treat them as having received a perfect score.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Meanwhile, the school would deduct ten points from the midterm scores of the ten students with the lowest sports festival results. Sotomura was one of the worst-scoring students in the sports festival from </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">any</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> grade, so he needed to score ten points higher in every subject to compensate. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ike and Sudou wore stiff expressions. A system where students could be expelled immediately if they received a failing grade was a heavy burden. Many students waited with bated breath as Chabashira-sensei slowly posted the scores on the blackboard. However, Horikita didn’t seem nervous at all.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wh-what?! No way!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The results started with those students who scored lowest and went up. Many students naturally expected to see Sudou’s name at the bottom of the list, because he’d scored lowest on the midterm and the final exam. However, the first name to come up was “Haruki Yamauchi.” Next was “Ike Kanji.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Following that came Inogashira, Satou, and Sotomura. Sotomura always had middling scores, but I imagined that the penalties he’d incurred from the sports festival resulted in him ranking this low.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh no! Dude, did I seriously get the lowest score?!” wailed Yamauchi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Fortunately, he’d scored over forty points in every subject, his lowest grade being a forty-three in English. He barely passed overall, with an average just slightly below fifty points. Yamauchi probably felt as if his life flashed before his eyes for a moment. He broke out in a cold sweat.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sudou’s results were more surprising. Until today, he’d always ranked at the very bottom of the class, but now he’d moved up significantly, going to twelfth from the bottom. Even if you took away his sports festival points, his results were still incredible. Everyone looked shocked; he had an average score of fifty-seven points.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I just obliterated a personal best! Dude, I almost got an average score of sixty!” Sudou shouted. He shot up and began literally dancing with joy.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s not good enough to merit such a fuss,” said Horikita. “Especially since your sports festival points helped.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Guh! Y-yeah…” Sudou sat back down, looking despondent at Horikita’s rebuke. He was just like a faithful dog responding to his master’s orders.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sudou got an average of fifty-seven points. Study group gets results,” I remarked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even in his worst subject, English, Sudou had remarkably managed to score fifty-two points. Apparently, Horikita had tutored Sudou and the other failing students. I wasn’t invited to help tutor the group, but that was only natural. From the others’ perspective, I wasn’t in the “smart people” category. Besides, Horikita herself should have been skeptical of my academic ability.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The study group had an impact, yes. After all, taking on the exams without preparing for them would cause you to fail, wouldn’t it? However, other significant factors helped. Sudou was lucky that this midterm test was made up of relatively simple problems,” Horikita said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That might be true.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This midterm had been, without a doubt, a bit easier than usual. Some questions were so easy I thought the school might’ve included them by mistake. That said, Sudou’s hard work definitely paid off. Horikita was confident that the failures had successfully managed to cross the threshold and get a passing grade; hence her composure.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">By contrast, Yamauchi, who had gotten the lowest score, looked like he was unable to hide his frustration over having lost to Sudou by such a wide margin. Horikita had tutored all her students equally, but Sudou had taken things a step further, giving up his free days to study one-on-one with her. The power of love really was a terrifying thing. Little by little, it looked as though his academic ability was beginning to improve.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You have an average of sixty-four points. That’s so mediocre. Why don’t you stop fooling around and get serious?” Horikita asked me. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That was the absolute best I could do.” I usually scored around fifty points, so it’d attract attention if I suddenly got a perfect score. I thought it would be best if I made slow but steady progress. That said, Sudou’s breakthrough meant I could probably afford to increase my score a little more.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s so insipid that you’re still playing the clown. Honestly, I can’t listen to you anymore.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t think you’ve ever listened to me,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I suppose that’s true.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">She openly admitted it…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">At any rate, because the test problems had been simple, there were a few perfect scores among those students at the top of the class. Chabashira-sensei even gave us a genuine compliment. “As you can see, no one has to drop out of school due to their midterm scores this time. All of you managed to pass.” She seemed content, so I assumed she would refrain from any constructive criticism.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Obviously. I’m looking forward to next month’s private points, sensei!” said Sudou, his elbows propped up on his desk.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira-sensei responded with an unchanging smile. “That’s right. Incidentally, there were few real problems during the sports festival. It would be reasonable to expect a private point increase in November. I must say, in the three years I’ve taught at this school, no other Class D has held on this long without any students dropping out. Well done.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira-sensei had never shown us this side of her personality until today. Quite a few students seemed hesitant to believe that it was genuine. In particular, people who didn’t usually receive praise felt a little awkward about it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You praising us feels weird.” Horikita was hard to fool. Although it was wonderful that no one had failed, she understood that Chabashira-sensei wasn’t the type to end on a positive note. The gentler Chabashira-sensei acted, the creepier we all felt. However, Horikita kept her cool.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Our teacher’s hair, tied up into a ponytail, swayed bewitchingly. Chabashira-sensei slowly passed between the rows of desks, as if intending to make a trip around the classroom. When she arrived at Ike’s desk, she stopped.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You managed to pass, but I want to ask you something. What do you think of this school? I’d like to hear your honest evaluation.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I guess…it’s a good school. If you do well, you can get tons of spending money. The food’s tasty, and the rooms are nice and clean,” said Ike. He counted on his fingers as he listed more things. “You can buy games and stuff. Plus, there’re movies to see and karaoke. And the girls are cute.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That last part wasn’t really the school’s doing, of course.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Um…d-did I say something wrong?” blurted Ike, looking fearfully at Chabashira-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No. From a student’s perspective, this is quite a paradise. Even as a teacher, I feel that this school is almost excessively lavish. It treats the students here so well that it almost defies common sense,” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira-sensei began to walk once more, heading for my side of the classroom. I had the feeling she was going to ask me a question in front of the rest of the class. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Don’t talk to me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Fortunately, it seemed my request was heard, and Chabashira-sensei stopped next to Hirata’s desk this time. “Hirata, do you like this school?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. I’ve made lots of friends, and I’m enjoying a fulfilling life.” Hirata’s response was exemplary.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t you feel anxious, knowing that you could be expelled if you happen to make a single mistake?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Whenever I’m nervous, I work harder with everyone else,” he replied. His words of camaraderie rang clear, without a hint of hesitation.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira-sensei returned to the podium. It seemed as though she was trying to confirm something, but I didn’t understand what. Maybe she wanted a better sense of our class’s morale. Was she gauging our stamina, to see whether we could handle what was to come?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Next week, in preparation for the second semester’s final exam, there will be a short test with problems from eight subjects. You’re all likely aware, and I’m sure that some of you have already started studying, but I just wanted to remind you,” said Chabashira-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Geh! And I was just recovering from the midterm! Another test?!” Ike wailed. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We had just entered the chillier months, and students who weren’t adept at studying would only continue to suffer. Obviously, tests were an important part of student life, but in the second semester the intervals between them were rather quick.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That means there’s only a week left until the next short quiz! I haven’t heard about this at all!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Actually, the teachers constantly reminded us about the upcoming quiz. Ike’s obliviousness made me want to sigh deeply.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Saying that you haven’t heard about it won’t do, as much as I’d love to tell you otherwise. Don’t worry, though, Ike.” Chabashira-sensei smiled as if she were throwing Ike a lifeline, but she did nothing purely out of the kindness of her heart. We knew her better than that.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Well, we should have, anyway.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Really, sensei? So, I can relax and take it easy? Woo!” shouted Ike.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira-sensei looked away from him. “There will be one hundred questions on the test, making for a total of one hundred possible points. However, the questions will be on a third-year junior high school level. This test is meant to confirm that you remember your fundamentals. Furthermore, much like the mock test you took in the first semester, it won’t affect your grades. It doesn’t matter whether you score zero points or a hundred. At most, it will be used to measure of your abilities.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“R-really? Good!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“However, it’s far from meaningless—this test’s results will have an enormous impact on your next final exam,” she added.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I nodded. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It can’t have been easy to get that recording. How did you do it? Ryuuen-kun said there was a spy, but you’re not friends with anyone from Class C, are you?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I have my ways. I used what I had at my disposal.” Of course, Horikita didn’t know about the trouble Manabe and her friends had caused Karuizawa back on the cruise ship.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Another thing. I was angry that you came to my rescue like that, because it meant that you thought I would fail. But I suppose I </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">was</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> about to fail, so…I can’t argue with you. Since you forbade me from prying into your affairs, I can’t demand that you tell me more. Though it was troubling, it… Well, if you hadn’t done anything, I would’ve… Well, thank you,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That was an amazingly roundabout way of thanking me.” I’d expected her to give me a lecture, so her gratitude caught me off guard. “I did promise to cooperate to a certain extent, so I could at least do that much,” I added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’d consider it meddling, personally. Are you sure you’re okay with being this conspicuous? Ryuuen-kun is now convinced that someone in Class D is working behind the scenes, and you’re probably on his list, Ayanokouji-kun. I think the peaceful life you wanted is in jeopardy,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">True, my high school life was becoming more stressful than I ever wished it to be. But peace might never have been an option. Chabashira-sensei had already mentioned a certain man who wanted to see me. Then there was Sakayanagi, who knew my past. Ultimately, none of us were clairvoyant; we didn’t know how things would play out. Horikita could become my trump card later on. Now I was scrambling to find a way to get some R&amp;R.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">All the while, Horikita waited with an expectant expression.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Good point. We’ll have to be especially cautious.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You reach into the deep recesses of your mind only to come back with that? I’m starting to understand you less and less,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You never understood me at all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I suppose that’s true.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If I didn’t remember seeing her, then she must not have pried into my affairs. At any rate, Horikita didn’t have time to focus on Ryuuen or me. If she didn’t deal with Kushida, her days at this school might be numbered.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">3.2</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"> </p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">A</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">S SOON AS</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> we entered Pallet, Karuizawa glared at Horikita. “Jeez, what were you even doing?! You’re so late! At least apologize.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Let’s get started. After all, Hirata-kun has club activities, right?” said Horikita, ignoring her completely.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wow, you just ignored me. Typical,” said Karuizawa. “Not even a ‘sorry.’”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Besides Horikita and myself, the group now consisted of Hirata, Karuizawa, Kushida, and Sudou. Horikita was right that there wasn’t much of a grace period before club activities. It was already 3:50 in the afternoon, and clubs convened at 4:30. Hirata, who was in the soccer club, should have been the most anxious, but he remained calm and collected. Maybe he was sincerely looking forward to this meeting, since his eyes shone.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After Horikita took her seat, she immediately launched into the conversation without touching the drink she’d purchased. “Let’s discuss the upcoming short test, shall we?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is it really that important? I mean, the school pretty much guaranteed that our grades won’t reflect our test results,” said Karuizawa. “Having these study sessions one after another now that midterms are over is a huge burden on everyone.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">First midterms, then the short test, and lastly, the final exam. The flurry of study sessions, which left us practically no room to breathe, would probably place an unbearably high level of stress on the students who weren’t doing that well.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wasn’t going to force people to study. However, the school isn’t making us take this test simply to measure our academic ability. We just passed the midterm, after all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We all passed because the midterm had easy questions, right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait, you think the short test will have more difficult questions on it? That doesn’t seem efficient,” Hirata said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If they tried to make the short test more important than the midterms, the school’s priorities would seem completely backward.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The short test might be significant in another way, then. Does the school want to measure something other than our academics?” asked Hirata-kun.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? What? What are you talking about?” asked Karuizawa, perking up considerably as Hirata spoke.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It has nothing to do with our academic abilities at all. The short test’s results will influence partner selection for the final exam. That’s why it matters,” explained Horikita. Sudou looked somber. “Do you understand, Sudou?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Just barely.” Sudou’s grasp on the current situation appeared dubious at best. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There must be some kind of process to select the pairs,” Horikita continued. “If we discover that process, we can gain a clear advantage in the final exam.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What’s that mean, Ayanokouji?” Sudou whispered. He most likely didn’t want to interrupt Horikita’s explanation. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It means that controlling the short test’s outcome is necessary to clear the final exam,” I answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, that’s what I thought.” Sudou’s lie was so incredibly obvious.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita’s read on the situation was true, without a doubt. The results of the short test were definitely going to be used to determine how the pairs were formed, meaning we’d be able to uncover the process in some way. The school had promised to explain things to the students at a later date, so we wouldn’t end up making a strange, complicated decision. I figured I’d see what Horikita had up her sleeve.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So what? Will they match up the people who scored closest together or something?” said Karuizawa. Apparently, she’d been listening intently.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Or maybe they’ll pair someone who got an answer right with someone who got the same answer wrong?” asked Sudou, who was also focused.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Either is possible,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata seemed to have some doubts, since his expression turned serious. “I understand it to some extent, but I’m a little skeptical about this.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“About what? Please, I’d like to hear it,” said Horikita, her gaze encouraging him to speak.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If there </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">is</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> some kind of partner selection method, then I think we should confirm it with an upperclassman,” Hirata said. “If it’s the same test from previous years, then the rules probably haven’t changed. Maybe the teachers would deliberately try to hide that?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida had listened quietly up to this point. After Hirata’s comment, she spoke. “I was a little curious, too. I think an upperclassman I’m close with might be willing to tell me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If it were a simple rule, then we should be able to hear it. Otherwise, the rules could be complex or entirely nonexistent.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wow, just as expected, Yousuke-kun! You’re right!” Karuizawa showered Hirata with admiration, then crossed her arms in apparent contemplation.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita side-eyed her. “As Hirata-kun said, it’s certainly true that we don’t know the facts. However, the school hasn’t tried to stop us from finding out what the rules are. In fact, discovering them might be one of the test’s prerequisites.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do you mean, Suzune? Can you explain it like we’re dumb?” asked Sudou. He looked as if he’d racked his brain so intensely that smoke was about to shoot out of his ears.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, the test begins once we find out the rules? In that case, not learning what they are could be disastrous,” said Hirata. He was probably imagining a scenario in which the school expelled half the class.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This is all just hypothetical, but if we don’t discover the process behind the pairing, the results could be devastating. Or would it? Chabashira-sensei said this is the first time that Class D has made it this far without anyone being expelled. She also said that, in previous years, only one or two pairs were typically expelled, right? Isn’t that strange?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nope,” said Sudou. He banged his forehead against the table in resignation.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see what you mean,” Hirata said. “Horikita-san is trying to say that, if we understand how this all works, we might not suffer any serious harm. Right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Correct.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why do you say that?” asked Karuizawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Because we’re taking the final exam in pairs. Consider the highest average score, and the high difficulty level of problems created by the students. If we take on this test without discovering what the rules are, then it seems like we’ll only see terrible results, don’t you think?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah,” added Hirata. “If two failing students were paired up, it’d be pretty bad.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, if we’re afraid of what could happen, then we just need to find out how they determine the pairs, right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, we absolutely must know the rules first. Again, Chabashira-sensei said that only one or two pairs were expelled in past years. That’s far too few, isn’t it? If the school partnered our class’s weaker students together, it’d probably force close to ten people to drop out,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. So that’s how it is.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yousuke-kun, what does this mean?” asked Karuizawa. “I’m getting a little confused.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, okay. Hmm, how to explain it? Let’s start fresh. Forget trying to figure out the rules for a moment. Imagine if we took the exam without knowing that these rules even existed. What do you think would happen?” asked Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Umm, it’d be bad? If the dumber students got paired, we’d probably see the school expel a lot of people,” said Karuizawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, I think so, too. But, in past years, only one or two pairs from Class D were expelled.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Isn’t that kinda weird?” Sudou seemed to realize what we were saying.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The pairs seem balanced,” Hirata said. “This is the hidden rule we’ve been looking for.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Indeed. That seemed to be the trick.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We can extract this conclusion from all the prior processes and results. The pairs are made up of a high-scoring student and a low-scoring student,” said Horikita. “It can’t be any other way. So, if I scored a hundred points, and Sudou-kun scored zero points, we’d be the pair with the most significant difference between our scores. That means we’d produce balanced results on the test, because our scores would even out.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Karuizawa seemed basically convinced. “I see. But doesn’t that mean the average students are the most at risk?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. The more average someone’s grades are, the greater the danger,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Poor performers would be paired with the top students, but middling students would likely be partnered with one another. It made sense that the questions on the short tests were going to be of a somewhat higher difficulty level. While it might make it hard to accurately measure students’ academic abilities, they probably held some sort of meetings to devise countermeasures.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We need to confirm this with upperclassmen. Then we can plan our next steps. Hirata-kun, Kushida-san, would you please talk to some senior students?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course,” said Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll check with the upperclassmen in my soccer club,” said Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The two of them readily accepted Horikita’s request. Our strategy for dealing with the short test was beginning to take shape. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’d also like to ask one more thing,” said Karuizawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Go ahead.” Even in the face of Karuizawa’s doubt, Horikita was as composed as ever.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“They said we’re being paired up, but what if there’s an odd number of students?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I doubt we need to worry about that right now. At the time of enrollment, all classes from A to D had an even number of students. No one’s been expelled yet, so our class sizes haven’t changed. However…if someone </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">were</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> expelled, it might place their class as a whole in an excruciating situation.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Really? Wouldn’t it suck to suffer just because one person was absent?” Apparently, Kushida thought that the school should be gentler.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We started the year with an even number of students. If someone is expelled or withdraws due to unforeseen circumstances, their class will have to bear the consequences,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">During the test on the island and the sports festival, the school imposed merciless penalties on nonparticipants. Horikita might’ve been right that, if even one student were kicked out, we’d likely suffer serious disadvantages in future exams. She was probably realizing just how important it had been to save Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you understand?” Horikita asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, I guess so. I just feel like thinking about it is a waste of time,” said Karuizawa.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Karuizawa’s doubts had been cleared away, so we moved on to the next item on the agenda.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Now that we’ve discussed the short test’s importance, we can move on. But there’s one more thing. Which class do we nominate as our opponent? In my mind, we should be going after Class C,” said Horikita. Before hearing out anyone’s opinion, she offered her own. “It goes without saying, but their collective academic ability is their big weakness. Class C is inferior to Classes A and B in academics. Looking at the class points so far, that’s plain to see.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She wasn’t wrong. Deliberately challenging an academically superior class would be nearly suicidal. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, Hirata offered his own two cents. “I agree, Horikita-san. But Classes A and B will naturally think the same way. If Class C is so inferior when it comes to academics, then multiple classes will likely go after them. That might be bad news for us.”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see what you mean,” said Horikita. “If our combined score wins against theirs, then the point gap between their class and ours will go down dramatically.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay, but wouldn’t it be a good thing if Class A attacked C? I mean, Class A would have no problem beating them. Then Class C would definitely lose points. Wouldn’t that be good for us?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That depends on what we’re aiming for with this exam. But Class C should still be our target—that hasn’t changed. Let’s assume one class will go after them, whether it’s us or not.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If the goal was to reduce Class C’s points, then it might be better for us to let Class A or B attack them. However, Class D also wanted to gain points. To increase the chances of that happening, it would be better to compete against a weaker opponent. Avoiding Class C would mean having to defeat a stronger enemy.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It seems like everyone agrees with Horikita-san’s plan. So, I’ll go along with it, too,” said Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata didn’t like the idea of things going south, so that was probably why he’d presented an array of possibilities.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thank you. I think we can move on to the next stage.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though there were a few things that tripped us up during our conversation, everyone came together on one course of action. We disbanded a little after 4 p.m. Hirata and Sudou both left for their respective clubs. Karuizawa followed Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I’ll go ask the senior students about the exam and report back,” Kushida said as she left. Nothing else of note from her, as I expected.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We’re counting on you.” Horikita turned to me. “What are you planning to do, Ayanokouji-kun?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nothing. It should be fine if I leave the plans to you and Hirata, right? Honestly, you’ve handled everything so far almost perfectly. You’re confident in your predictions, aren’t you?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So far. But to ace the final exam, we need to be able to tackle it head on,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. If Class D doesn’t study and improve, we’ll get nowhere. But if we can turn things around, then passing the test will be a breeze. If necessary, I can adjust my score in order to team up with a specific person,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, I can count on you?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If it’s within my power, sure. I can join your study meetings if needed. But I won’t be a leader.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Because you plan to act the mediocre student as long as possible, right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I prefer doing whatever is the least hassle.” I thought it was a fair compromise.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, Horikita was the furthest thing from undemanding. “Let me think about it. You’re a member of Class D, after all, and I’d like to give you an appropriate role. I want us to win,” she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll consider it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I did my absolute best to avoid answering.</span></p>
