<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 5:<br/>A Means of Escape</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"> </p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">A</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">FTER HOMEROOM BEGAN</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> at 6 p.m., Chabashira-sensei left the classroom. Hirata, glancing sideways at the perplexed students, stood and went up to the podium. No more time for games. We were about to have a serious discussion.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“During homeroom today, I’d like to discuss our strategy for the short quiz tomorrow. I’ve received Chabashira-sensei’s permission. First, Horikita-san, would you please come up?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As if she’d been waiting for him to say the word, Horikita stood and went to Hirata. Some students probably felt it was weird to see the two in solidarity. Hirata had always welcomed a partnership with Horikita, but until now, she never took him up on the offer. Horikita had always fought alone.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, as a result of the crushing defeat she had suffered during the sports festival, she understood the limits of fighting alone, and it was like she had been reborn. That didn’t mean she had become a perfect being, of course.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The Swiss biologist A. Portmann said it best. He said that human beings are born physiologically premature. He argued that, from a zoological point of view, humans are born about a year early in comparison to other mammals’ developmental states. When a human baby is born, its sensory organs are developed, but its motor skills are not. On the other hand, many large animals, like deer, are capable of moving on their own soon after birth. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita might have been reborn, but she was still underdeveloped. However, she contained limitless possibilities. Perhaps she still felt conflicted, deep down. She was probably struggling. The best course of action would be for her to change and accept it. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I thought Horikita would immediately start discussing the final exam, but she didn’t. “First, there’s something I’d like to say. Please allow me to apologize.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Something appeared to have festered inside her heart for several weeks now. “My performance during the sports festival was disappointing. Though I act tough in front of you all, in the end, I didn’t do anything for Class D. For that, I apologize.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita bowed her head deeply. Naturally, this display shook many students. It was as if she was taking the entire blame for Class D’s defeat. Onodera, who had become a little distant with Horikita after the three-legged race, spoke up in response.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“B-but it’s not like it’s only your fault we lost, Horikita-san. Please, don’t bow to us. You don’t have to,” she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s right, Suzune. I mean, Haruki and the Professor weren’t really much help either.” Sudou’s words were sad but true. Yamauchi and Sotomura shot vexed glares at him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Regardless of whether you win or lose, a humble attitude makes it easier to move on. But that’s not what I’m talking about here. I contributed almost nothing to any area of the festival,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She looked over at Sudou for an instant. By giving him that look, she implied that the one thing she’d gained was becoming Sudou’s ally. Sudou couldn’t have missed that message. While he scratched his cheek in embarrassment, he smiled quietly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But now, I’m finished apologizing. Next, I’d like to use my energy to take on the upcoming short quiz. Unless we all come together as a class, we won’t get through this challenge.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I can understand that, but do you have a plan? Like, we don’t even know how they choose the pairs yet.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Actually, the partner selection process has already been made clear. If we handle things right, each student could feasibly end up with their ideal partner. Hirata-kun, if you would.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata, now playing the role of Horikita’s support, wrote out the rules on the blackboard.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Deciding How Pairs Are Formed:</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">After the school looks at the class as a whole, it will pair the highest-scoring and lowest-scoring students.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">This process will continue with the second-highest and second-lowest-scoring students pairing up, and then the third-highest and lowest, and so on.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">For example, the student with one hundred points will pair with the student with zero points. The student with ninety-nine points will pair with the student with one point.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, there’s the method—and the meaning behind our short test. Simple, right?” Horikita said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wh-whoa! You cracked the code, Horikita! Awesome!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s fairly obvious. However, remember this: though students who earn lower grades will almost automatically partner with students with higher grades, there are always exceptions. I’m going to explain a strategy that we can use to end up with reliable, appropriate pairs,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She’d called it obvious, but that wasn’t the case. The hints were easier to dissect for this than other trials we’d faced in the past, but she had probably only noticed as a result of her prior failures. She walked to Hirata and faced the classroom. Her face betrayed no hint of fluster; she had only the drive to keep facing forward. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’d like to have the students who worry about their grades partner up with high scorers to help them out. However, looking at our grades so far, the truth of the matter is that we can’t cover everyone,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Eleven students had an average score of eighty points or higher. Only six students had an average of ninety points or more. It didn’t exactly make one feel at ease; less than half the class had excelled. Meanwhile, the number of students with an average of sixty points or below made up more than half the class. In other words, it would be impossible to pair every low-scoring student with a high-scoring counterpart.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Instead, therefore, Horikita aimed to create stability by forcing the top ten and bottom ten students into specific partnerships. Hirata listed the lowest-scoring students’ names on the blackboard, one by one.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Um, I don’t really get it. What are we supposed to do?” asked Yamauchi, who knew that his name would have been written on the board.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s okay for the ten lowest-scoring students, whose names are written here, to simply score zero points on this test. You could merely write your name. Since your final grades won’t reflect your scores, that’ll do you no harm. Conversely, the ten highest-scoring students should definitely try to score eighty-five points or above. Similarly, we’ll divide the remaining twenty students in the class into two groups of ten. The higher-scoring of those two groups should aim to score a maximum of eighty points, while the lower-scoring group only needs to score one point. By doing things this way, we automatically achieve a good balance in our pairings,” said Horikita. “I will properly confirm the details with you later, because it’s entirely possible that we’ll run into some kind of hiccup.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The important thing was to ensure that the students who scored zero points and those who scored one point weren’t paired. We needed to ensure that the school partnered the students with the most significant differences in academic ability.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I think this is a great plan. We’ll definitely need a countermeasure for this test.” Hirata didn’t dissent. He wanted to create a harmonious atmosphere. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kouenji usually refused to participate in anything, but he didn’t appear to either support or reject the plan. Actually, it was more like he didn’t seem interested whatsoever. His social skills were even worse than Horikita’s, but for once, it didn’t matter that he was up to his usual tricks. Kouenji never took exams too seriously, but he had avoided the kind of results that would get him kicked out of school so far. However, this time, it was possible that you could still fail the final exam even if you got a perfect score, depending on how well your partner did. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That said, if we got Kouenji into a preselected pairing, he’d probably be fine. In other words, while he might not be interested, he basically seemed willing to cooperate. Actually, we didn’t know how Kouenji would react, in some ways. He might be unpredictable. 	</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you have any objections, Kouenji-kun?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No. What a nonsensical question. Naturally, I fully grasp the situation.” He rested his long legs across his desk and started combing his hair, like always.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Then would it be appropriate for me to expect you to score eighty points or higher?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I’m not too sure about that. Wouldn’t that depend on the test’s contents?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you intentionally scored a zero, and got matched with one of the higher-scoring students, you’d destroy the balance we’re trying to achieve. Do you understand that?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The only thing to fear from this test was someone getting an irregular score. If an academically gifted student like Kouenji deliberately held back, he could throw off the class’s balance. We needed to avoid having highly gifted students like Horikita and Kouenji paired together.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I will consider the matter carefully, </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">girl</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though Kouenji’s response was suspicious, Horikita couldn’t pursue the matter any further. There was no way for us to manipulate the scores on the final exam.</span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">5.1</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"> </p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HE DAY OF THE SHORT QUIZ</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> arrived, and though I expected us to start immediately, Chabashira-sensei had something to discuss first.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We will begin shortly, but I’d like to say something. You nominated Class C as the class you wanted to attack for this test. Since there was no conflict with any other class, your nomination was approved,” said Chabashira-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Did Classes A and B both nominate Class D, then?” It seemed we’d managed to overcome our first obstacle, and Horikita was relieved. “In any case, the fact that we’ll be able to challenge Class C, with their chronically poor performers, is a huge bonus for us.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Class C will attack Class D. They also had no competing nominations that conflicted with that decision.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So, the battle was going to be Class D versus Class C, and Class B versus Class A.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s an ideal match-up,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Seems that way.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In summation, the higher classes had chosen to attack their direct rivals. Each class was fighting to either lengthen the gap between themselves and others, or to shrink it. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakayanagi had likely chosen Class A’s nomination. Katsuragi probably would’ve nominated Class D, as that would give Class A the highest chance of winning. You could sense that Katsuragi’s influence was on the decline.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Class C was our challenger, just as Horikita had hoped it would be.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“At any rate, you look like you’re doing quite well, Ike. Yamauchi, you usually have dark circles under your eyes right before you take a test. Did you happen to come up with some secret strategy?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Heh heh heh. Watch and learn, sensei.” Ike was completely self-confident, as expected. He hadn’t needed to study at all.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">What he should have been afraid of was getting a middle-of-the-road score. The test questions were almost implausibly low difficulty, but he could just write down his name and turn in a blank sheet of paper. If he seriously tried to take this unique exam, it would only increase the risks we’d face.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira-sensei had to see what was happening. “Don’t do something you’ll regret later. It would be better if you took this quiz seriously.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wh-what do you mean? This isn’t going to have an effect on my grades, right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Right. Your final grade won’t reflect your score on this at all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, I don’t need a good score,” said Ike.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sure. If things go the way you expect them to go, that is.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">What Chabashira-sensei said stirred our class’s anxiety. “Should we try to get good scores after all?” muttered Sudou, letting his feelings slip.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t get flustered. There are no problems with our plan,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Her calm, collected response managed to settle the students. Sudou regained his composure instantly. “Yeah. All I have to do is believe in Suzune.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira-sensei scanned the class to confirm that everyone had returned to normal, then produced the quiz papers. “Well then, let’s begin. I needn’t remind you that no form of cheating is allowed. If someone gets caught cheating, then there will be no mercy.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The teacher handed stacks of paper to the students in the front row and had them pass the tests back. Because we had to keep the paper facedown until the test started, I immediately flipped mine upside down when I received it. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Aren’t you worried that you’re wrong about how the pairs are selected?” asked Chabashira-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Not at all. I’m confident that I’m right,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She was unfazed by what Chabashira-sensei said. Ike and the others could stand tall because of their leader’s confidence.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Class D was changing. They were slight changes, but I figured that our homeroom teacher had taken notice. After all, she was watching over us day in and day out.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Begin.” Chabashira-sensei gave the signal, and the test started. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I slowly turned the papers over. “Oh…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I couldn’t help but make a noise. I probably wasn’t the only one who was surprised. Though we’d expected that the difficulty level would be rather low, it really was extremely easy. Even small children could have completed this test. Of course, there were a few difficult questions in there, but even someone like Ike could easily score around sixty or higher if he didn’t panic.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was a very tempting trap. If we’d gone rushing into this test thoughtlessly, it could have been a disaster. However, that was exactly why Horikita came up with her strategy.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">5.2</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"> </p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HE SHORT QUIZ</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> ended without incident. The results were announced the very next day. Class D had previously sat all its exams while lacking any kind of cohesion, but we were so united this time that it felt almost too good to be true. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Putting aside the pairing system, having to create test questions, and the competition that was to come, the simplicity of these rules was a real godsend. It was simple now: take the test, achieve the necessary grade. We’d been doing the same thing over and over during the nine or so years we’d been in school anyway.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The best part is that it looks like I didn’t need to get involved,” I muttered. I really was grateful for that. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I will now announce the pairings for the final exam,” said Chabashira-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The short quiz’s results were as follows: Horikita Suzune and Sudou Ken, Hirata Yousuke and Yamauchi Haruki, Kushida Kikyou and Ike Kanji, Yukimura Teruhiko and Inogashira Kokoro.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The pairs were almost perfectly in line with what we’d predicted. My partner was…Satou Maya.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“God really does have a bad sense of humor.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">How had it ended up like this? I couldn’t help but wonder. Satou noticed that she was paired with me, and looked in my direction with a smile. I raised my hand to let her know I’d noticed, too.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It looks like Kouenji-kun acted just as we hoped he would this time.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kouenji was partners with Okiya. It seemed he’d gotten a sufficiently high score. Well, he’d scored highly on every test thus far, so it was more like business as usual. Kouenji crossed his arms, grinned, and let out a laugh.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The results indicate that some among you understood this short quiz’s purpose,” said Chabashira-sensei, sounding quite impressed as she glanced over the list of partners. “Students were to be paired based on the difference in points, with the highest scorer partnered with the lowest, and so on. If students had the same scores, the pairings would have been determined at random. It’s probably not necessary for me to explain the system, but I thought I would anyway.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It shouldn’t have come as a surprise, but knowing that we’d been right on the money was a relief. “There don’t appear to be any blatant problems with the pairings,” I said to Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. Everything’s been going so smoothly it’s almost scary. But the real challenge starts now. How will we come up with our exam questions, and how will we overcome the final? You’re paired up with Satou-san. You should be fine.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I hadn’t intentionally chosen Satou or anything like that, but those of us with middling scores had muddied the strategy a little. That was just statistics at work, and in a way, it was kind of convenient. Still, I wasn’t entirely safe—she might fail the exam. I needed to keep my scores high from this point onward.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll hold study groups until the final exam to raise our class’s average scores,” Horikita said. “If Hirata-kun and Kushida-san agree, I’d like to hold two study sessions per day. There will be a two-hour session beginning at 4 p.m., right after classes end, until 6 p.m. The other two-hour session will be held from 8 p.m. to 10 p.m. We’ll each lead a session in turn. I’ll be counting on you, Hirata-kun,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Because of my club activities, I’ll take the second study group. Let’s all work together and do our best,” Hirata replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Things were certainly moving forward. More people could tutor the study group now, so we were able to utilize this kind of strategy.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita and Hirata discussed the study sessions’ format until they nailed down the finer details. Horikita would handle the first session, while Hirata volunteered to take the second. That way, they could support the entire study group and provide guidance to the more anxious students at the same time. Kushida would attend both sessions and adopt a unique role, tutoring the students who were worried about whether they could reach fifty points.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There were quite a few girls who scored around this middle range, including Onodera and Ichihashi. That being said, this plan wasn’t free of problems. Compared to the first-semester study group, significantly more students wanted a tutor, and only three could handle being tutors. Naturally, the more students there were per teacher, the lower the quality of instruction would be. When lunch came, Horikita met with Hirata and Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Dang it! Suzune’s not leading the second group? There goes my motivation, man.” Sudou couldn’t attend the first session due to his club activities, and was clearly unhappy to miss Horikita. Then again, the old Sudou probably would’ve lashed out by now.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No matter who’s teaching, it’ll bother me if you don’t try. Okay?” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay. We’re a team, after all,” he replied. Horikita had excellent control over the untamed stallion that was Sudou. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Bravo.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Your efforts reflect on me,” she added. “I’d appreciate it if you understand that. Besides, I’ll try to make appearances at the evening sessions as often as I can.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita laid out the finishing touches to her request, raising Sudou’s motivation sky-high.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah! All right, I’m ready for this! I’m countin’ on you, Hirata.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Likewise. Let’s do our best together, Sudou-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Now that he was partnered up with Horikita, he was </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">really</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> fired up. Just then, however, an interloper came onto the scene.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Excuse me, may I talk to you?” Miyake Akito asked, approaching Horikita. I hadn’t ever really spoken to him before. He looked apologetic.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What’s the matter, Miyake-kun?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Miyake was with Hasebe, a beautiful girl who was a frequent topic of discussion amongst the boys. These two normally kept quiet and rarely interacted with anyone. It was unexpected for them to approach Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait, you two are paired together for the final exam, right?” asked Hirata, creating an opening for conversation. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, yes. We’re partners, but we’re equally good and bad at all the same subjects. We’re a little nervous about how we’ll perform in the final, so we wanted to ask for your advice.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Miyake handed Hirata their completed answer sheets for both the short test and the midterm. Their scores on the short test stood in stark contrast, with Miyake getting seventy-nine points and Hasebe getting one point, as intended. This was the product of Horikita’s plan to pair the high and low scorers together. However, their midterm scores were sixty-five and sixty-three, respectively. There was almost no difference in academic ability between them. They were both exceptionally average, but they’d still been split between the upper and lower groups. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">At first glance, it looked as though they could probably score high enough to pass the final, but there was a problem. Miyake and Hasebe got the exact same kinds of questions wrong. They were equally bad at all the same subjects. To pass, they needed a score of at least sixty points for every subject. This would be a perilous bridge to cross.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see,” said Hirata. “This is a little unexpected. Let’s make sure to check the other pairs later.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sorry to bother you, Hirata, really,” replied Miyake. “I’m always causing trouble for you, between the cruise ship and the sports festival.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That made me think back to the sports festival. I could only vaguely recall the situation at this point, but Miyake withdrew from the final relay because he hurt his leg. It looked like he moved without any problems now, so his injury must have completely healed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Please, don’t apologize,” said Hirata. “If I had trouble, I’m sure you’d do the same for me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The answers that Miyake and Hasebe got right and wrong on their sheets were extraordinarily similar. In fact, they were so similar you might think the same person took both tests. Even if it was possible for someone to adjust their scores, not every matchup would be perfect. Having an irregular pair like this was unavoidable.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This is really unfortunate,” added Miyake. “I don’t want to complicate your plans or mess up the study groups.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">You could tell that the two were by no means unintelligent. They were different from Sudou and the rest, who struggled with studying. The problem was that these two were too similar. Because of that, group tutoring was going to be insufficient. One-on-one tutoring was necessary.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Kushida-san, could I ask you to take on additional students? These two should have a pretty strong foundation that won’t bring down the group’s overall cohesion,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sure,” replied Kushida. “If you’re all right with that, Miyake-kun and Hasebe-san, I’ll do it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Miyake didn’t appear to accept or reject Kushida’s offer, but Hasebe spoke up. “I’m going to pass, I think. I don’t get along too well with Ichihashi-san and the others.” Fortunately, Ichihashi and the other girls weren’t in the classroom and didn’t hear that remark. “Besides, I’m not really good at studying in a group with a lot of people.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It seemed Miyake was the one who had wanted to come to Hirata for help, while Hasebe hadn’t been too keen on it. I’d thought it would be the other way around.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But the two of you have trouble with the same subjects. If you take the final exam as is, even if you clear the overall score requirement, you might score under sixty points somewhere,” Horikita said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, I know,” replied Hasebe. She averted her eyes from Horikita and started walking away.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Where are you going?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Miyacchi, I’m sorry to have wasted your time, but I guess this just isn’t the right fit for me,” said Hasebe. With that, she left the classroom.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry, Horikita,” Miyake muttered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t mind. Well, even if it’s just you, would you mind studying with Kushida-san?” asked Horikita. If Miyake improved his weakest subjects, he could effectively cover for Hasebe, too. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll pass. I don’t really feel like I could study with a group of just girls. I’ll try and do it on my own,” he replied. He got up, grabbed his bag, and left. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita couldn’t force anyone to study. If a student didn’t participate of their own free will, it would be nearly impossible to get any results. It would probably lower the serious students’ morale to boot.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What now?” asked Hirata. “I think we should follow up with those two.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. If only we had another tutor,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She glanced at me, so with my own eyes, I sent her a message saying “absolutely not.” Putting aside whether I could even do the tutoring, I wasn’t sure I could communicate with Miyake and Hasebe. Well, at least they would disregard my existence after this point.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll see if I can make time,” Horikita muttered. After giving it some thought, she’d concluded that she just had to take it upon herself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t think that’s a good idea. You’ll overwork yourself. If you do that, you won’t be an effective tutor. Besides, Horikita-san, you also have to create Class C’s test problems,” said Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But what other choice do I have?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She’d responded bluntly under the assumption that there was no one else for the job. While Hirata could advise her not to, he couldn’t force her. Evidently, Horikita would take on Miyake and Hasebe, and that would be that.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, I’ll take care of tutoring them,” said Yukimura, inserting himself casually into our conversation. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We’d be glad to have you aboard, Yukimura-kun. You’re diligent and academically gifted. But are you all right with this? I thought you didn’t really like this kind of thing?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If I don’t help out where I can, we won’t be able to pass the test,” Yukimura said. “Same goes for you, Horikita. You can’t do everything yourself.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yukimura might’ve chosen to intervene because he’d seen Horikita change since the sports festival.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s just one problem. I can teach Miyake and Hasebe how to study, but I’m not friendly with them. After seeing what happened here earlier, I get the feeling that talking to them will be tricky. I was hoping you could do the work of convincing them to study with me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So, he had a condition. Well, it was a small price to pay, under the circumstances. Horikita was overjoyed. Yukimura was like the cavalry in a movie—the sort who arrives in the nick of time, charging over the hill to save the cornered protagonists.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Got it. I’ll think of something,” Horikita promised. Yukimura left the classroom, and she turned to me. “Is it okay to be optimistic for now?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Not necessarily. Think about it: you don’t know how to talk to those two, either,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hirata-kun, do you think Miyake and Hasebe will listen to Yukimura-kun?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not sure. All three of them are loners,” said Hirata. “It’ll all come down to whether they can see eye to eye with Yukimura. It might make them a little anxious.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita turned to me after a moment of thought. “Hey, Ayanokouji-kun. Would you manage Yukimura-kun and the others?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Manage?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You were Yukimura-kun’s roommate on the cruise ship, so I thought you might have broken the ice with him. Miyake-kun and Hasebe-san could be difficult, but with you as the middleman, I think it’ll be easier for us to communicate with them,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Well, it was probably as good a plan as we had. No one else here would be able to act as the go-between for Horikita. Still, why me? I’d been happy not getting involved for once.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You don’t seem pleased with this. Are you unwilling to cooperate with me? All you need to do is help corral them into studying with Yukimura. I didn’t say I wanted you to teach them.” Although that was true, just managing Miyake and Hasebe likely wouldn’t be an easy task. “Can I count on you?” Horikita asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Her request was morphing into a threat. All I could do was nod. If I accepted, she could save face, and the plan was pretty low risk. I just didn’t want to have to do anything truly exhausting, like tutoring or preparing test questions.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll do what I can.” I sighed, but made sure that Horikita didn’t notice.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;"> </span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">STARTED PREPARING</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> immediately. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hasebe isn’t like that,” said Miyake. “Maybe she just went on ahead of us?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why would she need to?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“A bunch of different reasons.” Miyake seemed to understand Hasebe quite well, and wasn’t especially worried. </span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yukimura seemed to take this as a personal attack, as if she found our company embarrassing. “So, you hate being seen talking to us?” he asked.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, all the seats at Pallet will probably be taken while we stand around talking. Let’s keep moving,” I suggested. I understood how Yukimura felt, but we needed to stay on target. Classes were over for the day, so students were starting to trickle into Pallet one after another.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, you’re right… It’d be a hassle if we had nowhere to sit. Let’s go,” replied Yukimura, regaining his composure and taking the lead. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You should be a little more careful about what you say,” Miyake told Hasebe.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Was it that annoying? I’ll think about it, I guess,” she replied. Evidently, she hadn’t been intentionally rude.</span></p>

I hardly understood how this group had come to be in the first place. Still, we had to get down to business.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Um, I guess I’m counting on you, or whatever,” said Hasebe.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Well…I mean, I didn’t talk to Hasebe on a regular basis, or at all. So, maybe it was understandable that she had that kind of impression. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Miyake brought up the sports festival. “But Ayanokouji was awesome in the relay. Everyone’s eyes have been on him since then.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Seems that way. But I went to the bathroom during that race, so I missed seeing it. It all feels kinda bizarre to me. Didn’t you compete against the former student council president? That’s what everyone was buzzing about right after the festival ended,” said Hasebe.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Did you do track and field back in junior high, Ayanokouji? After seeing you run, a talent scout from the track and field club came looking for you,” said Miyake.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah, yeah. I got some offers. But I refused,” I replied. The track and field club’s enthusiasm was only temporary. They couldn’t keep trying to recruit me forever. The people in the club probably weren’t talking about me anymore. Even if someone was fast, if they had no interest in joining a club, hounding them to join would be pointless.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“To be honest, I’ve never been in a club before,” I added. “I don’t really know much about that stuff.”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yukimura just listened, never speaking once. Hasebe, disinterested, switched the conversation’s topic to Miyake. “Miyacchi is in the archery club. Is it fun shooting bows every day?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wouldn’t do it if it wasn’t fun. By the way, you don’t shoot the bow, just arrows,” he replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Well, he’s not wrong there.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m just not interested in clubs, I guess. I’m fine with spending time by myself,” said Hasebe.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, Miyacchi. Is it okay for you to miss your club stuff?” asked Hasebe.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I took time off.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wow. You shouldn’t do that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“When something takes priority, I focus on it. Besides, my club’s pretty lenient, so I won’t be in any trouble.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Excuse me? I’d like to say something before we begin,” said Yukimura. Finally, he’d spoken up. He focused not on Miyake or Hasebe, but on me. “No hiding anything, Ayanokouji.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? What do you mean?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Studying. Horikita says you’re quite capable.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ugh, Horikita,” I muttered. What a blabbermouth. I needed to give Yukimura something if I wanted to earn his trust. “Well, I’m relatively good at memorizing things. I think I can get a pretty high score if I concentrate.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you the type to hide his abilities?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I can’t hold a candle to you, Yukimura. Please don’t expect too much of me. I’m not very good at teaching,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You should take this study group seriously, then. With me tutoring you, you’ll definitely score higher than you did on the midterm. Even if it’s just by one point.” Yukimura turned to Hasebe and Miyake. “Did you bring your answer sheets from the midterm and first semester exams like I asked?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah,” said Hasebe.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Miyake nodded as well. They took their test papers from their bags and handed them over. I glanced at their papers and scores. </span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Miyake and Hasebe had scored relatively high in math, earning around seventy points. But they only got about forty points in language and world history. It was obvious why the two of them were worried.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I didn’t know you two were so close, but you certainly do share the same strengths and weaknesses,” added Yukimura.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, Hasebe came and talked to me when I was studying in the library one day,” said Miyake.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Miyacchi and I are both independent people. We don’t really fit in with everyone else,” Hasebe added. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I feel the same, in a way,” said Miyake. “Even in </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">this</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> group, I feel awkward and distant.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The two of them were certainly distant from the rest of the class, and they didn’t belong to any specific clique. Was this the reason why?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, why did you agree to join us?” asked Yukimura.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Because this isn’t really a club, I guess. It’s just a study group. Besides, it’ll be quiet with just the few of us, right? When I study by myself, nothing bothers me or gets in the way. I think I’ll have to process my new study method, then. Sorry, but I’ll need a little time.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Got it. How about we have a little tea break?” Hasebe asked. She immediately took out her phone and relaxed. I supposed it was easy for anyone to pass the time nowadays, as long as you had a phone. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Would it be appropriate for me to take mine out now, too?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Suddenly, I felt as if someone was watching me. I turned around. Several male students, each on the phone with somebody, were in fact eyeing us. I recognized three of them; all were from Class C. I only recalled the name of the one in the middle, Ishizaki. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hopefully, they weren’t about to drag me into another troublesome mess. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This is good. We’ll definitely want to test the school,” she said. “Hirata and I are already making progress coming up with questions to use against Class C, but I’d like to know just how far we can go. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s no way to be sure,” I replied. “But trying to anticipate the kinds of questions Kaneda would come up with is certainly one way we can handle these study sessions. It’s not the worst, right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I suppose. If we imagine that this test will be full of difficult questions, we might need to try to get eighty or ninety points,” said Horikita. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If this exam ended up being far more difficult than anything the school itself would have created, there would probably be a limit to how many points we could get.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“By the way, how did the study group go, if you don’t mind my asking?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There really wasn’t any reason for me to hide it, so I filled her in, though I did exaggerate a little. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I doubt they’re doing it on purpose, but it’s quite the coincidence,” she mused.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It wasn’t unusual for people’s strengths and weaknesses to overlap, but this degree of similarity was uncanny.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I know, right? For the time being, I’ll see what I can do. They seem easy enough to supervise, anyway,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thank you. Also, there’s one more thing. On the days when Yukimura-kun’s study group doesn’t meet, would you please come to mine?”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s still within our terms. You don’t have to tutor. I just want you to manage everyone,” Horikita replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The word “manage” was vague, so vague that I had absolutely no clue what she meant. I understood it as much as I understood the expression “more than friends, less than lovers.” Which was to say, not at all.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do you mean, ‘manage’?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She sighed heavily.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Too many people need tutoring, compared to the number who can teach. I can’t keep my eye on everyone, no matter how hard I try. I’d like you to make sure everybody studies properly,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The teachers manage to teach dozens of students all by themselves, right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“At the risk of sounding self-aggrandizing, it’s not as though even our teachers can watch everyone by themselves. That’s why you have some students, like Ike-kun, who fall behind. It’d happen regardless of whether we have surveillance cameras—which we do. Even if they fool the teacher into thinking they understood, in the end, they struggle to keep up,” said Horikita. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I’d thought my argument was swift and decisive, but she had managed to topple it in one fell swoop.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yukimura-kun isn’t accustomed to tutoring, and I’m having trouble with the sheer number of people I have to teach. Ike-kun and Yamauchi-kun are especially problematic. They have shorter attention spans than a kindergartener.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ike and Yamauchi were attending the study sessions, but they were apparently just goofing off and doing whatever they wanted.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Any objections?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“None.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Excellent.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s okay if I don’t go to the night sessions, right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s fine. The nighttime study sessions are significantly better than the daytime ones, anyway, though some of the girls in that group can be troublesome,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I guessed that some girls only showed up at the sessions to be around Hirata and didn’t mind that Karuizawa was there. I didn’t see the harm in those girls chatting with a hot guy like him. Surely it wouldn’t be so bad if the ladies’ man controlled by Karuizawa happened to become more popular in Class D. Regardless, that had to be an interesting dynamic.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I realized that Horikita didn’t mention Sudou among those causing issues. “Has Sudou been behaving?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. He’s taking this seriously, though he still hasn’t reached junior high level yet.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Subjects aside, it seemed as though he was really trying his best to improve his attitude.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m counting on you.” I still didn’t have a good feeling about this. “Oh, yeah. While we’re on the subject of the study groups, what about Kushida?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do you mean?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Has nothing changed with her?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course not. I do think that she’ll help. She also promised to attend the study sessions every day,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That wasn’t quite what I was asking. But I supposed that, as far as Horikita was concerned, there wasn’t much to worry over. After all, it was just the very first day of the study sessions. There wasn’t much opportunity for her to investigate further. From my point of view, though, we simply couldn’t kick back and casually watch this problem develop.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Have you started creating test questions?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course I have. Mine, Hirata-kun’s, and Yukimura-kun’s will form the fundamental basis. I wanted more people to help, but the more classmates I involve, the greater the risk that the problems will leak to Class C,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She was exactly right. The questions and the accompanying answers were the key components of Class D’s defense. Even if we tried our hardest to study and come up with a way to attack, we wouldn’t stand a chance if Class C conquered those. We can’t allow our test questions to leak, by any means. We also needed to consider that we might run into someone snooping for information.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’d certainly be difficult to rule out any possibility of leaks if we factored in Kushida. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, you’re right. She can’t just act freely, however. As long as we don’t ask for her help with the test questions, it should be okay.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This chat about Kushida and what she might do next was pure speculation on both our parts. “Our test questions and answers are Class D’s lifeline. If that information leaks, we’re doomed,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Setting her desire to make an ally out of Kushida aside, she needed to focus on the topic of leaks. We couldn’t just ignore the potential dangers.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll keep things locked down. But that alone won’t solve the issue, will it?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not worried about the ‘creating questions’ part. If anything, I fear what comes after we submit them to the school. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida had employed a similar tactic with the participation table during the sports festival. It was more than likely that Ryuuen would make a similar request of her again.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, the only way we can deal with this is by talking it out with Kushida,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But what do we do if she leaks our questions to Class C?” I countered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t want to think about it,” she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You have to. This is about all of Class D. No matter how much we study or improve, if our opponents get a hundred points across the board, we have no hope of winning,” I told her. If Class C memorized all our answers, defeat was certain.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see what you’re saying, and I understand your anxiety. But I’m dealing with it. It’s already past ten o’clock. I’d like to come up with at least one more question before I go to bed, so is it okay if we stop here?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I agreed, and she hung up. My phone’s battery was low, so I plugged it in to the outlet on my bedframe to charge.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We were now facing an ordeal like that of the sports festival. Just like our participation table, the questions we’d craft for the final exam were our lifeline. Surely Ryuuen and Kushida would come up with two different strategies this time around.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita said she was working on countermeasures, but I didn’t know how well they’d go. I wasn’t criticizing her strategy to persuade Kushida directly, but rather, didn’t have much to say about it. If I had to bring Kushida over to my side, I’d threaten her like Karuizawa. No—I’d probably need to do something far worse to make Kushida surrender to me. However, I didn’t know the details of Kushida’s past, and she might not fold under pressure. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What should I do?” I muttered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Unfortunately, I couldn’t come up with a solution. Then, I received an email from Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After the sports festival, I’d asked Manabe and her friends for Ryuuen’s email address and sent him a message. I hadn’t received a response until now.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">What are you?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was the entire message. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Another meaningless email…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wasn’t nice enough to answer him, and besides, he couldn’t trace my email. It was a dummy account. He should’ve known that, so I figured he was playing games.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I decided to go to sleep.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Satou looked a little depressed. Being in the library was kind of annoying; I had to whisper to avoid bothering other students, and as a result, got far closer to Satou than I normally would’ve. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Clearly the stress had been insane. How had she kept this act up for so long?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“My blog saved me. It was the only place where I dumped out this hidden stress. I could tell it all my most painful secrets. Of course, I posted everything anonymously, you know? But I wrote the facts just as they were, and it made me so happy when I received encouragement from people I didn’t even know. Then, one day, a classmate discovered my blog. Even though I hadn’t named anyone, it was clear my posts were all based on real events. I’d badmouthed everyone so much that they were obviously going to hate me for it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s how this incident started?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The next day, my posts were shared with the entire class. Everyone condemned me. I’d helped them so much, and yet they all turned on me. Selfish, right? The boy who’d told me that he liked me actually shoved me. It was understandable, though, since I’d posted that his romantic confession grossed me out and I wanted him to die. One girl I’d comforted after she was dumped even kicked my desk. I’d posted in detail about why she was dumped and made fun of her. Over thirty students decided I was their mortal enemy that day.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She could have never won that fight to begin with. I could only see it ending with the class forcing Kushida out.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, how did you make it through? Violence? Or lies?” That was the mystery that Horikita and I still didn’t know the answer to.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Neither. All I did was tell the truth. I revealed all my classmates’ secrets. Who hated whom, who thought whom was a disgusting creep. I exposed truths I hadn’t even written on my blog.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We hadn’t anticipated this. Truth was a weapon that you could only obtain through trust. Horikita and I didn’t have that. Although truth might seem harmless, it was actually a powerful double-edged sword, only usable at the cost of more trust.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“At that point, my classmates stopped being angry at me and started hating each other. The boys got into fistfights, the girls pulled each other’s hair and shoved each other over. The entire classroom descended into complete chaos. It was honestly incredible.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, that’s how it happened.”</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><img alt="" height="100%" id="ClassroomElite-Vol6_INTERIORILLUSTRATIONS_8" src="/hola-lector-agua/images/books/v6/OPS/ClassroomElite-Vol6_INTERIORILLUSTRATIONS_8.jpg" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style=" top: 0px; vertical-align: top; height: 100%;"/></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"/>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Because of everything I exposed, the class could no longer function. The school rebuked me, but my blog was anonymous, and all I technically did was tell my classmates the truth. They weren’t really sure how to punish me.” Kushida spoke indifferently, but every word she uttered carried weight. “I still don’t know that much about my fellow Class D students. However, I know enough to destroy a few people. That’s my only weapon.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">A threat. If we told anyone about her, we should prepare for the consequences. If Kushida felt it was necessary, she would tear a rift in Class D right after we’d finally started bringing everyone together. If that happened, the harmony we’d established would disappear.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It was a mistake to use the internet to vent my feelings. Everything you put up there is saved forever. That’s why I stopped blogging. Now, I blow off steam by saying what’s on my mind out loud when I’m alone.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Like the first time I’d seen that other side of her. She was probably spewing all the cruel insults she could muster.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you want to remain as you are right now?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This is my reason for being. More than I love anything else in the world, I love everyone respecting and noticing me. When people trust me with their secrets, I feel joy that surpasses my wildest imaginings.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Knowing the anxiety, suffering, embarrassment, and hopes that people held deep within their hearts was Kushida’s forbidden fruit.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Boring, isn’t it? But to me, it’s everything.” Kushida’s smile disappeared. Now that she’d told us about her past, we became true enemies in her eyes. From this point onward, she wouldn’t show us the slightest compassion. “Don’t forget. If I score higher in math, both you and Ayanokouji-kun will drop out.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">And that was that. Satisfied, Kushida left to return to her dorm. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I turned to Horikita. “Are you sure about this? Kushida’s involved with Ryuuen. She could get all of our questions and answers.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you knew it was dangerous, why did you join? Because you believe I won’t lose?” Horikita countered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, yeah.” Truthfully, I went along with the bet because I had some ideas of my own.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even though Kushida might be able to help Ryuuen-kun, I really have to wonder whether it’ll come to that,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do you mean?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do you mean?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Certainly, if she gets ahold of the test questions, victory will be within Kushida-san’s grasp. In which case, I would definitely have to drop out. But do you think that Ryuuen-kun really wants me to drop out?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s doubtful.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ryuuen wanted to entrap Horikita, but he wasn’t trying to get her kicked out of school. He hoped to see her brought low, and her expulsion would end those aspirations. Besides, would he really let Horikita go without finding out who was working with her in secret? Would he let her get expelled without exposing me first?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But what if Kushida lies to get the questions from him? She might say that she wants to improve her personal score, and keep our bet’s details secret,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ryuuen-kun would see through something like that. If Kushida-san asked for the math answers, he’d want to know why,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re probably right. But this is dangerous.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There were no guarantees. Kushida might successfully convince Ryuuen to help her. I hoped Horikita would consider that, but harping on it would be too harsh.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’ll always be danger, no matter the challenge at hand. It helps to have things you’re willing to put on the line.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita hadn’t expected me to actively take part in the bet. However, she was resolute in her conclusion. She’d made her offer sound more credible to Kushida by promising to keep her past a secret, swearing to drop out if she lost, and having the former student council president stand as a witness.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s no turning back from this. We definitely need to win.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita was making her biggest gamble yet.</span></p>
