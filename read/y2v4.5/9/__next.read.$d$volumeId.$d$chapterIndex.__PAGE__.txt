<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 3:<br/>The Beginning of a Short-Lived Vacation</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">F</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">OR MANY STUDENTS</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> each day spent on the uninhabited island felt like an eternity. In contrast, each day on the luxury cruise ship seemed to be over in an instantaneous flash of light. How could the flow of time be so different when it was still the same twenty-four-hour period? I supposed the most crucial factor was that here, we rarely thought about the time for most of the day. During normal school days and special exams, we frequently thought about the passage of time. On&#160;the other hand, on days off, we usually did not, and so the&#160;difference in our perception of time was striking.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Today was the second day of our festive holiday. The number of students that passed by one another in the&#160;ship’s corridors increased dramatically. Many students’ fatigue had finally abated, and they finally started to enjoy their vacation in earnest. Even someone like me, who had been spending most of my time quietly all by myself, received a message from a rather unexpected someone inviting me to hang out with them. It was none other than Vice President Kiriyama, from Class 3-B. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The invitation said to meet him by the pool. I wondered if he intended to have a light-hearted chat while we floated around on pool floats, or maybe to deepen our bonds of friendship by way of a game of volleyball. I immediately banished such incomprehensible predictions from my mind though. Although he was inviting me out by the pool, I was sure that his intentions in inviting me weren’t just to “hang out.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I could have refused his offer, of course, or I could’ve ignored it. But whatever I decided, I was sure that I was going to have to hear what he had to say anyway. Depending on how things played out, it was entirely possible that I could have been called out in a far, far more unpleasant way than this. I responded to his invitation with a simple, indifferent “Yes,” and promised to head over to meet him at the appointed time. Besides, there was a significant possibility that this could solve the mystery of the nagging stares from the third-years that I felt the other day.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Kiriyama, huh…” I muttered to myself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Right now, I was in the break area next to the fitness center. Before me was the monitor where the results of the special exam were posted. It seemed that most of the other students had already finished checking their exam results because I was the only student here now. The number of teachers monitoring the students checking their results had similarly been reduced down to one person. I&#160;had already committed the exam results to memory more or less, but I slid through the results once more. Once the positions of the top groups were shown on the screen, I focused on Kiriyama’s group’s results.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The overall rankings had already been announced out in front of front of the entire school earlier, with Kouenji Rokusuke coming in first place by himself. Nagumo’s group came in second place, and Sakayanagi’s group came in third. I saw that Kiriyama’s group had come in fourth place by just a slim margin, at only six points behind. His group had a total of 255 points. That meant Sakayanagi must have gotten the lead over him at the last second and clinched the third-place position to secure herself a place at the podium. The difference between third and fourth place was not limited to a mere difference in ranking, but also a number of benefits.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Naturally, this must have been quite frustrating for the third-year students,” I mused.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">First place has slipped away from Nagumo, and Kiriyama had missed getting into the winner’s circle entirely. Moreover, all the students who had been expelled had been third-years, which was quite an anomaly. I still had about twenty minutes to go before I was supposed to meet with Kiriyama, but I decided to head to the pool area ahead of time. I wanted to confirm that I wasn’t simply being overly self-conscious in noticing the stares I was getting, but rather that there was some kind of plan in motion.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The answer was immediately apparent, without any need for careful observation or scrutiny. Within a few dozen seconds of me arriving by the pool, I had attracted stares from an unspecified number of third-year students from all over the place. Students who were engrossed in conversation, students who were swimming, it didn’t matter. As soon as they noticed my presence, they started to observe me closely. The glances I felt yesterday hadn’t been mere coincidence.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I know I came here to find proof, but I feel like I got it almost too fast,” I muttered to myself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This gave me such an intense feeling of unease that it actually made me want to grumble about the situation. I was just supposed to be here as an inconspicuous, nondescript student, someone who blended into the background, but now I was sticking out more than anyone else. Even if I wasn’t trying to think about what was going on here, I naturally wanted to find out what was behind this and what the true intent was. The orders had come from Nagumo, most likely, but at this point in time, it was completely unclear </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">what</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> those orders were exactly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Many students were directing obvious glances my way, but I continued to deliberately pretend not to notice anything. It was much easier to play the part of a foolish, thick-headed person. However, I knew Nagumo must have assumed I’d be aware of the strange looks I was getting from all these students. On top of that, I wouldn’t have been surprised if he was enjoying seeing me being the center of attention. Regardless, the best plan of action for the time being was to just completely ignore the stares and let the moment pass. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I looked around the pool to see if anyone else was around besides the third-year students, and I saw Ichinose and some of her classmates. Ichinose alone had just so happened to notice my presence, and our eyes met. Her shoulders twitched, and then she proceeded to hide behind her classmates like she was trying to escape. Ichinose’s sudden, bizarre movements prompted a response from her companions, who asked her if something was wrong.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I supposed Ichinose reacted that way because she had confessed her romantic feelings for me back on the island. It was no wonder that simply locking eyes with each other at a distance like we just did would make things feel awkward. If Ichinose were the only one here, I would’ve done things differently, but since she had classmates with her, I figured I’d keep my distance for the time being. Even if I left her alone right now, I had already planned to meet with her on the evening of the day after tomorrow. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I saw a few of my own classmates around here and there, but unfortunately, I couldn’t find anyone with whom I was particularly close.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Seems like things are starting to get quite serious, Ayanokouji.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I turned my head to glance over in the direction that voice had come from, slightly diagonally in front of me. When I did, I saw Kiryuuin, lounging on a beach chair on the deck.</span></p>

Physical Ability,&#160;A. Adaptability, A+. Societal Contribution, A+. Truly impeccable.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I know. As far as OAA is concerned, he is by far the top student in our school.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">A small number of students, like Sudou and Kiryuuin, had A+ rankings in certain areas. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It would be best if he simply left someone like me alone,” I answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If that’s the case, then you made the wrong choice somewhere along the line,” said Kiryuuin.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was painful for me to hear that, but still, she spoke mercilessly. </span></p>

However, Nagumo is in a completely different category. I believe that he’s the type of person that </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">you</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> are not good at dealing with. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The other students were just looking at me, but I hadn’t realized how stressful and repulsive it would feel. To put it another way, I was being forced into an environment that I had never experienced before in my entire life. The only way for me to escape this situation was to stay hidden away indoors, which wasn’t a realistic solution here. </span></p>

No matter how underhanded the method might be, his priority is to win in the end.”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">A</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">FTERWARD</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> I didn’t really feel like playing around. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If I said that I had plans, then Ichinose and the other girls would surely refrain from asking me to stay, out of politeness.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“W-well, see you later then, Ayanokouji-kun,” said Ichinose, still sitting pretty.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She and the other girls waved goodbye, seeing me off as I left the room.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"></div>
<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">3.3</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">“P</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HEW…</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> I felt like I was about to break out in a weird sweat,” I muttered to myself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">No, actually, I had already broken out in a sweat. Less than thirty seconds after Himeno had left the room, I&#160;also managed to escape from the devilish room 5034. I figured that for some people, that situation would’ve been heaven. But for me, personally, it was a painful place to be. You couldn’t call me particularly good at getting close to people, after all. It would have been a different story if I were to completely create a new role for myself, but since I was operating on the premise of playing the part of an inconspicuous high school student, it wasn’t that easy for me to change.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, I supposed that in a way, the fact that I had little connection with Ichinose’s class up until this point </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">might have helped me get closer to her. With Ichinose at the center of her class, I could see, albeit just faintly, what kinds of kids were beside her. What did she have, and what was she lacking? At this point in time, I knew the strengths and weaknesses of Ichinose’s class. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Students who could speak up and make themselves heard were essential, no matter who would be the leader in the future. The only person that I could think of at the moment who could do that would be that guy Kanzaki. However, with the class centered on Ichinose, the girls’ collective voices seemed to be just as strong as the guys’. Kanzaki, as an individual, was the type of person who could speak up to Ichinose, but whether he could appeal to the class as a whole and whether he could control the girls was another matter.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hm?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Himeno had complained of a headache earlier and said that she was going back to her room, but she was walking in a different direction from the guest rooms. She left and turned the corner in an instant, but considering that distinctive hair color of hers, there was no mistaking her. There was something about Himeno that I felt was off during the girls’ party just moments earlier. Since I was somewhat curious about what was up with her, I decided to follow. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I found myself at the aft deck. It was nighttime, and there was no sign of anyone else around. As I continued to look at Himeno Yuki’s side profile from a distance, I&#160;recalled her profile in OAA.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.00em;">2-B Himeno Yuki</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Academic Ability: B– (63)</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Physical Ability: C (51)</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Adaptability: C+ (58)</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Societal Contribution: C+ (58)</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Overall Ability: C+ (57)</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Other than her high level of academic ability, she wasn’t particularly skilled or lacking in any way. She had no outstanding abilities as far as I could see. However, that was just a view of her abilities only as far as the school’s metrics were concerned. It was possible that any student had invisible strengths and weaknesses hidden away. I decided that I wanted to investigate a little further, and that direct contact would be the fastest way.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What are you doing?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh…? What?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She averted her eyes, looking somewhat uncomfortable. Considering that she slipped out of the room after saying that she had a headache, it was strange for her to be in a place like this.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Your headache all better?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Gawd…” </span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span><img alt="" height="100%" id="INTERIORIMAGES_2" src="/hola-lector-agua/images/books/y2v4.5/OEBPS/Images/INTERIORIMAGES_2.jpg" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style=" top: 0px; vertical-align: top; height: 100%;"/></span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"></div>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Her short mumbling was almost completely drowned out by the wind, but it sounded like she was telling me to shut up. There were several guys and girls who used harsh language in our school, but in Himeno’s case, it seemed like her way of speaking was a defense mechanism—a way for her to keep people away rather than meaning to be hurtful.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, perhaps because she was concerned about her public image, she coughed, and then directed her gaze over to me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I just stopped here because I thought the breeze would help me settle down. Okay?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you get headaches often?” I asked. “You said something earlier that gave me the impression you do.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I tried pressing her for more details, but she must not have wanted to engage in conversation with me any further because she fell silent. Even during the girls’ party earlier, she hadn’t spoken up at all except to say that she was leaving. In addition, I had noticed that the other girls generally didn’t really start conversations with Himeno either. It wasn’t like she was being excluded on purpose or anything, as Ichinose would never tolerate such a thing. And if their relationship was that bad, I was sure they wouldn’t want to show that to someone from another class, like me. In that case…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I supposed that they must have somewhat forcefully invited Himeno to come out to the post-exam party then, twisting her arm a bit. If I framed what happened as the girls doing it because they wanted to get their classmate to have at least some fun, then it all made sense.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I get migraines. That’s what they say,” said Himeno, her comment brief and muddled.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you have migraines, then cooling down is the right choice,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Migraines are caused by the dilation of cerebral blood vessels due to things such as changes in female hormones, fatigue, lack of sleep, and so on. Blood vessels are less dilated when cool and more dilated when warm, so stepping outside to feel the cool breeze wasn’t a bad idea.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That is, if she really did have a migraine.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Pain in the…” she mumbled.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Aren’t you just using headaches as a way to get out of uncomfortable situations?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? You callin’ me a liar?” she huffed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Himeno had been acting relatively indifferent up until this point, but the look on her face changed as soon as I had pointed out that she might have been stretching the truth. She was an unusual type for Ichinose’s class, which was full of mild-mannered people. My intuition had not been wrong.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You look pretty angry. That mean I hit the bull’s-eye?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wrong,” said Himeno. “Actually, you know what? Ugh, my head’s startin’ to hurt again. I’m going back to my room.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry if I offended you,” I said. “But would you mind listening to me for just a minute?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Himeno, her hand held up to her forehead, turned and shot me a disgusted look. “Look, my headache’s getting more intense. Okay?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“‘Sorry,’ huh… So, you assume I’ll listen to you if you say sorry?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You don’t seem to want to talk.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After trying to engage in conversation a few times and getting a couple of responses now, I could start to see that this person in front of me was the real Himeno. This seemed to be her true self. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see,” I said. “Well, nothing I can do, then.”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Liar?” I repeated. “All I said was that you ‘might’ be faking it. Besides, as long as I feel like that might be true, then I have the right to raise the question, don’t I? You can just prove it to everyone at a later date whether it’s true or not.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s no way someone can </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">prove</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> they have a headache, though,” said Himeno.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Maybe,” I answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The hell? Everyone was all praising you up and down, but you sure have a nasty personality.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, at the very least, I wasn’t being praised for having a </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">good</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> personality though, was I?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t really want to say this myself, but really, all Shiranami did was express her gratitude to me for helping her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Guess so,” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Anyway though, you’re a strange one, Himeno,” I told her. “It’s like you don’t seem like someone from Ichinose’s class.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Strange?” she scoffed. “If you ask me, the people in my class are way too nice. Our class is always getting together in big groups to do stuff. And sure, that’s fine. But the issue is that each time we do something, it takes friggin’ forever, and nobody </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">ever</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> leaves.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I supposed that if I had to repeatedly go to meetups I didn’t like, I couldn’t help feeling fed up either. Ichinose’s other classmates, however, enjoyed those gatherings. That was probably why no one wanted to leave each time they met, which resulted in each meeting getting drawn out for long hours.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you don’t like it though, you don’t have to participate. Right?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You really think I could get away with something like that?” said Himeno. “Even if it’s annoying, it’s important for me to keep in line.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I suppose so.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Her whole class was united, and there was an especially strong kind of camaraderie among the girls. It would take courage to toss a stone and cause a ripple, even if you felt dissatisfied with things on the inside. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Himeno. Maybe our chance encounter right now could change the direction things were headed in. Normally, I wouldn’t get too deeply involved with a member of the opposite sex like her unless it were a special situation. However, I figured that it wasn’t a bad thing to deliberately take a step forward here.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Of course, if this ended up making trouble for her as a result, then, well, c’est la vie. There was no getting around&#160;it. </span></p>

The ship wasn’t actually shaking from that, of course. </span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Really? You don’t look it though.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You were even better than I thought. You must be under a lot of stress.”</span></p>
