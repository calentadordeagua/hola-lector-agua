<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 4:<br/>Everyone’s Holiday</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">B</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">EING ON THE CRUISE SHIP</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> came with the problem of where to have lunch every day, and what to eat. The school provided buffet-style meals to us every morning and night, free of charge. Students were free to visit the buffet or not, if they wished. Aside from being free, the food was also exceptionally delicious, so it was quite popular amongst the students. The school divided the breakfast buffet service into three separate admission blocks between seven and nine in the morning. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Each block of time was limited to sixty minutes. It&#160;operated on a reservation-style system where we used our phones to reserve whatever time we wanted. I generally had breakfast at eight o’clock in the morning, but since I was late to make a reservation, the eight-to-nine block had filled up, so I ended up having to eat a little earlier at seven o’clock in the morning. As a result, I was feeling strangely hungry by the time noon rolled around. Perhaps my body was craving energy because I’d only been meeting my minimum caloric intake requirements during the uninhabited island exam.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Dining in the open-air café was a popular option, but, regrettably, the meal prices were high. If you wanted to have a lunch combo set with a drink included, you would need at least 2000 points. If you wanted to enjoy a meal together with friends, the café would be a fine option, but unfortunately, I was alone today. It made sense for me to be as frugal with my points as possible, considering the costs, so I was extremely grateful for the concession stand kiosks.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In short, they were places where you could easily purchase items such as rice balls, sandwiches, and so on, just like at a convenience store. I quickly marched up to the kiosk and paid 250 points for a rice ball and a small tea. Then, plastic bag in hand, I went in search of a place to eat. I figured using any suitable break area that was available would’ve been fine, but most of those places were occupied, and I felt a strong aversion to sharing such a small space with someone else. I figured that if you wanted to find a place where you didn’t mind being near strangers, at least to some extent, then being outside was generally the best choice.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As I continued searching, I arrived at a spot on the deck on the sixth floor near the bow of the ship, overlooking the ocean. It didn’t cost anything for me to use this place, so I figured it was an appropriate place to eat the light meal that I had purchased from the kiosk. I’d&#160;been thinking I would enjoy the magnificent view of the ocean while enjoying my little snack, but it seemed like I picked the wrong time of day to do it. There were many other students who had come here to take in the view, and it didn’t seem likely that I’d be able to relax.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Though the deck was spacious, if there were many people on it, it would obviously be a challenge to find space. I&#160;scanned the area and found an empty bench. And, next to it, I saw Nanase from behind, seated on the adjacent bench. A sandwich and a carton of milk were placed beside her. She must have purchased them from the kiosk too. It was kind of funny—this was the opposite of yesterday, where she was the one who found me. Aside from Nanase, there were many second-year students here as well, who, like her, were enjoying their lunches while taking in the view of the ocean. For example, I spotted my classmates Ijuuin and Okiya, as well as Sakayanagi from Class A, and Nakaizumi and Suzuki from Ryuuen’s class.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ultimately, people tended to think in the same ways. I didn’t move from my spot and turned my gaze out to the ocean. Surely, a meal would be utterly delicious if you were to eat it while taking in this spectacle. However…just as there were many students from my own grade, there were many third-year students here too. Some of the third-year students had noticed me, though it was only a handful of them. They immediately began to turn their watchful gazes in my direction. On the other hand, if I were to leave immediately, that would mean that I didn’t like the way they were looking at me, and they’d know that I had decided to run.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Doing so might encourage the third-year students further as they would likely conclude what they were doing was effective. Come to think of it, Nanase seemed to have something she wanted to talk to me about the other day. I remembered that we were interrupted at the time because Kobashi had come up to speak with me, so I decided to call out to Nanase now. It would at least provide me with some pretext to go over and talk to her. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nanase.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When I called her name, she turned back with a start.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah, semffai!” she said in surprise.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Apparently, I had gotten her attention just as she had a mouthful of sandwich, and she looked at me while trying her best to keep the contents from spilling out. I&#160;felt a little sorry for her when I saw her hurriedly trying to swallow her sandwich. I was just using her as a means to combat the third-years, but from the look of things, I had put her in an unnecessary panic.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, sorry. Should I come back later?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though I said that, I knew that there was no way Nanase would allow it, considering her personality.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Pleaze whaid a momend, howd om,” she sputtered. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She couldn’t spit the food out now that she already had it in her mouth, so she quickly started to swallow it down.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Gulp!</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> …Um, sorry, excuse me,” said Nanase. “I was, um, well, to tell the truth…just having lunch.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She said that like she was confessing some kind of secret, but I could tell just by looking that she was eating. Actually, I knew it the minute I saw her from behind.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Um, is there something I can help you with?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I felt like there was something a bit strange about Nanase, when I saw her so flustered like this. Her eyes were restless, darting all over the place. She seemed to be unable to concentrate on having a conversation with me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, not exactly, it’s just that you seemed like you wanted to talk to me yesterday,” I replied. “I was wondering what about. You just kind of left after Kobashi came up to talk to me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh,” said Nanase.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Her train of thought was slow. Her words weren’t coming out right away.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sorry, but it’s already been resolved, so would you mind just forgetting about it?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It is? Well, in that case, all right.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had been planning to talk to Nanase about whatever it was because she had helped me out in many ways, but if the issues had been resolved, it wasn’t of any consequence. More than that, though, the biggest reason I decided to let it go was that I sensed that it didn’t really matter anymore. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry for bothering you so suddenly,” I said. “Anyway, I’m going to head back inside the ship. There are more people than I thought there’d be, so it doesn’t seem like I’ll be able to relax here.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see,” said Nanase. “Well then, I will see you later, senpai.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As soon as I finished getting my stuff together, I left. I looked back out at the deck once more and saw that Nanase was once again facing forward and had resumed having her lunch.</span></p>

<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">4.1</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">N THE END</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> I made my way back to a less-populated area—the aft of the ship, on the fifth floor—to find a place to have lunch. It was the same place where I talked with Himeno the other night, and I already confirmed that few people usually went there. For a few minutes, I&#160;just stared out at the rough waves created by the ship as it sailed through the waters, forgetting about my original purpose for coming here. Then, an unexpected visitor approached me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re going to have lunch all by your lonesome here? In a place like this?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sakayanagi? What a coincidence.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As I recalled, she was on the same deck as Nanase earlier.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s correct. Well, that’s what I’d </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">like</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> to say; however, I did chase after you, Ayanokouji-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She chased after me? But her legs were in such bad shape that she wouldn’t have been able to match even my slowest walking speed. There was no indication that she had assigned someone to tail me ahead of time either.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It was simple deduction,” Sakayanagi said. “You made an appearance at the deck near the bow of the ship earlier to have lunch. But you gave up on the idea after seeing how many people were there, didn’t you? It wasn’t difficult to predict where you’d ultimately decide to go, given the fact that you were carrying a light meal and you were looking for a place with a view of the ocean.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She had perfectly read my behavioral patterns and had arrived here based on them. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see that you also wish to enjoy your meal while taking in the view, Ayanokouji-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Unlike back at the bow of the ship, the view here isn’t top-rate,” I said, “but there aren’t that many opportunities to gaze out at the wide-open ocean like this.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There was no guarantee that there’d be another uninhabited island exam this time next year. There were other school trip events planned for our second year, sure, but the details remained unknown for the time being. Perhaps this would be the last chance I’d have to gaze out at the ocean in this fashion.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sure there will be many more sights like this ocean view for you to see in the future,” said Sakayanagi. “In&#160;that sense, you were probably correct in choosing this school, Ayanokouji-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, that’s right. I think so too. You know, though, I actually had seen the ocean before, prior to coming to this school.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakayanagi was, surprisingly enough, a little shocked by that. Well, no, to be fair, it might have been reasonable for her to have been surprised. The truth was that I didn’t leave the facility even once until I was fourteen years old, which would have put me in my third year of junior high. That fact was common knowledge to someone who knew what the White Room was generally like. Anyway, I had seen the ocean once. It was when I had the opportunity to be outside for a short while after I was transferred out of the facility.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Although I didn’t have any direct contact with the water, I walked along a path with a view of the sea. However, the first time I saw it, I wasn’t moved or anything. I simply walked through the outside world, emotionless. Nothing more.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Have you heard of </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Beneath the Wheel?”</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> asked Sakayanagi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s a novel by Hermann Hesse, right?” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Out of all of the novels he had written, that work was particularly well-known in Japan. In some languages, it was called </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">The Prodigy</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> instead.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hans, the protagonist of the story, was a genius gifted with incredible talent,” said Sakayanagi. “Though he attended an elite school and had a bright future ahead of him, he only lived for his studies. Eventually, he began to have doubts about himself. He tried to live up to the expectations that were placed upon him, but he suffered setbacks and started to decline.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The protagonist, Hans Giebenrath, met a tragic end. At the end of the story, he fell into a river and died. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What of it?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t think he was a prodigy, myself</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">” she said. “The reason being that real, honest-to-goodness prodigies do not suffer failures like that. And choosing death at the end was just sheer stupidity.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakayanagi seemed to have interpreted his death as a suicide rather than an accidental one.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Some time ago, I told you, ‘People can come to know warmth by touch. And that is precious. The warmth of another’s skin is by no means a bad thing.’ Do you remember that?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, I think I recall you saying something like that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Was that at the end of the third semester of our first year? Right after the special exam ended?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hesse, who wrote </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Beneath the Wheel</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">, was also troubled and suffered setbacks in his life, just like Hans, the protagonist of his story,” she explained. “However, he said that it was because of his family that he was able to keep going. He didn’t feel the need to take his own life.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">From the sounds of things, the author, Hesse, and the book’s protagonist, Hans, had strikingly similar personal backgrounds. You could therefore see the story as the author’s psychological projection of his self. As Sakayanagi looked out at the ocean, she was suddenly hit by a strong gust of wind.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I saw the instant Sakayanagi’s hat started to fly away, and I immediately reached out my hand to catch it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Whoops… That was close,” I said, taking hold of it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If my reaction speed had been even slightly delayed, her hat probably would have been carried off into the vast ocean.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thank you very much,” said Sakayanagi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s not safe to wear that on the deck,” I warned.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Fu fu</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">, yes, I suppose you’re right,” she said. “But it is my trademark, of sorts.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She held the hat in her hands, holding it tenderly against her chest.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Something quite nostalgic sprang to mind, just now.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nostalgic?” I repeated.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, it’s nothing important. It’s just that I have a few memories of the ocean,” said Sakayanagi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I supposed that even a single body of water, though it would look the same to anyone, could hold many different memories to different people. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“By the way, you never told me the reason why you chased after me,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is it so much of a bother that I came after you without one?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was wondering what she was going to say, but she responded with an answer that I hadn’t even considered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You don’t have one?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I just thought that I wanted to talk to you, Ayanokouji-kun. I could have approached you before, at the place you just left. However, I imagine that you wouldn’t want to be seen speaking with me that much, no?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That meant she was trying to be thoughtful and considerate, for which I was grateful. However, I wasn’t a skilled conversationalist, so I didn’t have any particular topics that I could go into with her. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Would you mind if I led the conversation with some idle talk?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s fine. Is it okay if I eat while I listen?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Please, by all means, go ahead. I do not mind. If you simply lend me an ear and listen, that would be plenty for me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I took a single rice ball out of the bag and began to peel off the wrapping with my fingers.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You know, Ichinose came to see me yesterday,” said Sakayanagi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“She did?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakayanagi recalled yesterday’s events, telling me what had happened as she thought back on them.</span></p>

<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">4.2</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">“U</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">M, EXCUSE ME</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">…</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> Sakayanagi-san? Do you have a minute?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After lunch, I was enjoying a nice relaxing break in the café on the ship’s deck when Ichinose-san approached me. Since I was enjoying my tea alone, I had no reason to refuse her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Whatever is the matter?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I already knew what this was all about before she even said anything, but I deliberately cocked my head to the side anyway, making myself appear puzzled.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, about what happened during the special exam… I felt like I needed to apologize,” said Ichinose-san. “I did something really selfish on the last day, and…I am truly, deeply sorry!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ichinose-san bowed deeply with as much respect and intensity as she could manage. She must have been prepared for the worst, assuming this to be a situation in which I would accept no excuses from her. To be honest though, I didn’t think that she would have made careless excuses to anyone. I was sure that she probably felt as though her actions had angered me, the leader of Class&#160;A. And, also, she probably assumed that what she did had caused our cooperative partnership to be dissolved. It wouldn’t have been surprising at all for her to feel that&#160;way.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Please lift your head back up, Ichinose-san. I am not angry about anything at all,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh…?” she blinked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If anything, I must say that I recognize your efforts as a fellow member of the group. You have contributed well. You masterfully played a vital role in the harsh, unforgiving uninhabited island experience by bringing together our disparate allies, and also by getting a high percentage of correct answers on Tasks. As a result, we managed to achieve third place in spectacular fashion. Did we not?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“B-but…” she stammered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, it certainly is true that you acted somewhat selfishly on the final day of the exam. However, the loss our group incurred as a result of that was only a few points at most. If we were to compare that to your contributions, then it’s hardly anything worth chastising you over. If we had fallen into fourth place by a slim margin, then you </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">might</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> have been on the receiving end of some criticism, but that didn’t happen,” I answered, cutting her off.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But that’s a conclusion we can only make in hindsight…” she insisted.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wow, so cooool!” </span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But hey, sorry, but like, I’m feeling kinda sentimental right now. So can we do this another time?” asked Amasawa.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Just a feeling, more or less. Maybe after hanging around you for so long, I’ve developed a bit of a strange intuition. To be honest, I don’t want to get involved with her any further.”</span></p>

And if that was the case, it was possible that Sakayanagi could provoke Amasawa into making a move. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“She noticed that I was following her though, didn’t she?” said Kamuro. “Are you going to use Hashimoto?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If I use him, I suppose he might be able to skillfully talk his way out of things even if he is discovered, but…” </span></p>
