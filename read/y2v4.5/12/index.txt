<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 6:<br/>A Treasure Hunt of Girl Troubles</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HERE WERE NOW</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> only three days of vacation remaining on the cruise ship. The days were too much, passing by too fast. Early that morning, just around the time that everyone was beginning to lament how little time they had left on the ship, the school sent out an email to all students simultaneously. Hondou, who was the first to turn his phone on, read the message aloud.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“They’re holding a game, a treasure hunt starting today at ten in the morning…” he said. “What’s this about?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We all saw the unfamiliar word “game” in the email, and at the same time, we each proceeded to carefully read through the message.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.00em;">Treasure Hunt</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This is a bonus game that anyone may freely participate in</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.00em;">Participation Requirements:</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> Available to men and women. 10,000 Private Points required for participation, per person.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.00em;">Date:</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> Today, August 8.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">For more information, come to the venue. You must arrive on the fifth floor by 10 a.m. You are free to choose whether or not to participate after hearing the instructions.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“For a minute there, I thought this was gonna be another special exam. That part aout how anyone can participate sounds interesting though, doesn’t it?” said Hondou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So, we were able to freely choose whether we’d like to participate, and the only risk that individuals had to bear was paying the 10,000-point fee, huh? The exact details were unknown at this point in time, but considering that it was called a treasure hunt, it was probably safe to assume that the payoff was greater than the fee. I was expecting it to be something simple, like getting Private Points.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Personally, I thought it would be a good idea to be proactive and participate if there was a chance to earn a temporary bonus since I was constantly short on funds. The fact that it only cost 10,000 points to participate also sounded reasonable. Miyamoto and Hondou naturally seemed inclined to participate, and after they had finished eating, they started talking about entering together. I was thinking of inviting Akito to join me too, but…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t worry about me,” said Akito, exhaling sluggishly as he lay on the bed. “You go have fun…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Akito was sick in bed with a fever. Perhaps he overdid it at the private pool yesterday.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If they hadn’t forbidden us from bringing personal belongings onboard, I would’ve lent you my game console,” said Hondou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I really don’t think I could even play a game, how I feel right now…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Akito buried his face in his pillow, sounding somewhat exasperated. We let him sleep in bed. After finishing our meals, we relaxed in our room, killing time until about 9:50 in the morning. Then, the three of us decided to make our way to the venue without Akito, though we felt somewhat sorry for him.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"></div>
<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">6.1</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">A</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;"> </span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">LARGE CROWD OF STUDENTS</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> was packed into the designated venue. I wondered how many people were going to participate, and it looked like roughly half the entire student body was here. I expected even a few more, but I supposed that the students who weren’t interested in treasure hunts might take this opportunity to enjoy the pool or other places since there wouldn’t be as many people in those spots right now. Since this event was open to everyone, the students could spend this day however they wished.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Shortly after the cutoff time came, a great deal of commotion came from the stage in the front. From the looks of things, Takatou-sensei, the homeroom instructor in charge of Class 3-A, was going to explain how this game worked. It looked as though almost all the faculty had assembled here, but I couldn’t see Acting Director Tsukishiro, nor Shiba, the homeroom instructor for Class&#160;1-D. If Shiba were also employed by </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">him</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">, then it wouldn’t be surprising if he had resigned after what happened. In fact, I imagined his appearance and his role might have become known to both Mashima-sensei and Chabashira now.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Good morning, everyone,” said Takatou-sensei. “As it is now 10 a.m., we will no longer be accepting any more students beyond those who have gathered right now.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">One of the other teachers, who had been standing near the entrance, slowly closed the doors. I supposed that even though this was a game where participation was voluntary, rules were rules. They weren’t going to allow any latecomers, even if they were only a second late.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Before I begin explaining the rules, I would like to tell you exactly how this treasure hunt game came to be,” Takatou-sensei said. “This event came as a result of a suggestion from Student Council President Nagumo-kun, who thought that we should offer students an interesting and enjoyable recreational activity as a way to deepen ties of friendship, especially after competing against one another by grade level while living on such a harsh uninhabited island. Please say a few words, Nagumo-kun, if you would.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After his name was called by Takatou-sensei, Nagumo got up and stood in front of everyone who had come to participate.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I am pleased to announce that we are hosting this bonus game with the school’s full support,” he said. “This proposal stemmed from the student council’s raison d’être, its commitment to enriching and improving our lives at this school. During the uninhabited island exam, each grade level was locked in fierce competition with the others, but in this treasure hunt, it is possible for you to team up regardless of grade level. I urge you all to take advantage of this and participate in the game.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He concluded his remarks with a short statement that sounded typical of a student council president. That reminded me of what happened yesterday, when Nagumo appeared before Ichinose and me. Ichinose was also a member of the student council, and she was sitting by the faculty, listening to the proceedings. From what I could see from where I was standing, she didn’t seem any different from normal, but…I still remembered the tears that Ichinose had unexpectedly shed yesterday. The wounds inflicted upon her heart surely hadn’t been trivial. I’m sure she was trying to act natural, but it would take some time for her to heal. When that time came, the love she felt for me might disappear, or she might even become hostile toward me. Whatever kind of changes she underwent, I was sure this was going to be a major turning point for the future for her. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After Nagumo finished speaking, he handed the microphone back to Takatou-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Members of the student council will not be eligible to participate in the treasure hunt as they will be managing operations for the event, meaning that they are giving up their holiday to handle clerical work. I ask that you please treat them kindly,” said Takatou-sensei. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Several student council members, including Horikita and Ichinose, were called over to assemble next to Nagumo.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Now then, I will provide you with an overview of how the treasure hunt game will work,” Takatou-sensei said. “The rules are not complex at all. In fact, they’re exceedingly simple.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He raised his right hand. He was holding a square-shaped piece of paper between his thumb and forefinger. It looked to be roughly five centimeters in size. There was a QR code printed on the paper.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There are one hundred of these stickers with QR codes on them placed all throughout the ship,” he explained. “Those of you participating in this treasure hunt game will be tasked with finding these stickers. You will be awarded Private Points as a reward for scanning the codes via a dedicated app. However, you can only scan once per device. Please note that the results will be reflected immediately, and the points will be paid out to you right away once you’ve accessed the site. Of course, once a QR code has been scanned, it will be rendered invalid from that point on. Even if someone were to scan it using another device, they would not be given any rewards from it. Also, we ask that you please refrain from any illicit activities during this event, such as removing stickers without permission or using a pen to make the code unreadable. Engaging in such behavior will warrant severe punishment, even though this is just a game.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">I see.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> The rules were exceedingly simple, and luck was an important part of this game.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The lowest number of Private Points you can been awarded is 5,000 points,” Takatou-sensei continued. “There are fifty of these stickers available, exactly half of the total amount. The second most common prize is worth 10,000 points. There are thirty of these stickers available.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Unfortunately, this meant that if you got one of those fifty low-level stickers, you would be losing money. Even if we were to find one of the 10,000-point stickers, which were only 30 percent of the stickers out there, we’d only be breaking even.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“As for the remaining twenty stickers, there are ten stickers worth 50,000 points, five stickers worth 100,000 points, and three stickers worth 300,000 points. The final two stickers are the highest, at 500,000 points and one million points respectively. It would be safe for you to assume that the more difficult it is to find the hidden code, the more Private Points you will receive from it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">With roughly two hundred people participating, that meant that one in every two people would receive nothing. But if you were to find the sticker that was most difficult to track down, you’d end up with a million points. That was an amount that you couldn’t easily obtain, even in a special exam. In that case, I wouldn’t be surprised if people felt it was worth the risk, even though half of the people here would get nothing, but…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There are one hundred stickers, but there are two hundred or more students here,” Takatou-sensei said. “So, it is inevitable that some students will not get prizes. However, we have implemented a way for you to avoid that risk. Participants may pair up with another student from any grade level. If any person in a pair scans a QR code using their phone, the reward for that code will be paid out to both members. So, for instance, if the reward is 30,000 points, both of those students will be awarded that amount.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If everyone were to pair up and scan all 100 QR codes, then 200 people would be able to receive rewards. The possibility of taking a loss, without even getting a single point for your efforts, would be greatly reduced. The only disadvantage was that if both members of a pair were to find QR codes, they might have a dispute over which one to scan. Despite the fact that handling that disadvantage would require some coordination, it seemed that pairing up was highly advantageous.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Also, please note that the areas where the QR codes have been placed were determined in advance,” he added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though he said earlier the codes were placed all throughout the ship, there were still some areas that were naturally inaccessible to us. Takatou-sensei continued his explanation, using the screen. To briefly summarize, stickers with QR codes would obviously not be found in the restrooms or in guest rooms. Also, employee-only floors and guest rooms were also excluded from the treasure hunt, as expected. The stickers were not hidden on levels that were off-limits to students. Takatou-sensei had strongly emphasized that placement of stickers was limited to public spaces and areas that students were allowed to go to.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And also…we will be providing you with these as well,” said Takatou-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Just after he told us that, faculty members began handing something out to us. Not too long after they started, I&#160;received one myself: a piece of paper folded in two. It&#160;was a map of the ship that had been slightly modified. The areas where stickers were placed had been highlighted in color. There were also some unfamiliar-looking text and figures on the document.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Essentially, this game is mostly about luck. However, we have incorporated a few elements where a little bit of skill will be involved.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Takatou-sensei was likely referring to the text and figures on the map that had just been handed out to us.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There are three riddles on the maps you’ve been given. If you solve them, you’ll be able to find the hidden locations of a total of three QR codes. Please assume that you likely won’t be able to find these locations without solving these riddles.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So, by the sounds of it, out of a hundred total QR code stickers, there were three that had been specially prepared. I skimmed through the three riddles and put the piece of paper in my pocket.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Registration is open now, and will remain open for the next thirty minutes,” Takatou-sensei said. “Please indicate whether you will participate via your phone. Also, if anyone is unable to turn on their phone because their device is out of power, please notify the nearest teacher as soon as possible.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Students took out their phones one after another and began registering. There were several students who left the room, but it was a certainty that almost everyone here was going to sign up. The treasure hunt game would be over by five o’clock in the afternoon, and we’d need to scan a QR code by that time. I wasn’t going to be left out, so like many of the other students, I took out my phone and decided to register.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, with this many people here, there was also the highest number of stares yet to be directed at me over these past several days. When it was on as large a scale as this, the fact that they were looking at me was naturally going to be noticed by students from other grades. I wasn’t sure if they were all coordinated or if they had been given certain instructions in advance, but the stares directed at me now temporarily diminished, and the third-years began to avert their eyes as the other students started to follow their gazes. Apparently, at this current stage, they weren’t going to let it be known that they were monitoring me yet.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">They seemed to be waiting for a more effective, more damaging moment. As long as I didn’t know what their ultimate goal was, I needed to be well-positioned as well. I had to act while under the assumption that everything about me was being secretly reported. My girlfriend Kei was also among the participants here, but we didn’t even exchange glances—since we hadn’t announced our relationship publicly yet, we decided to refrain from making explicit eye contact. Of course, even though we were told that we </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">could</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> pair up, she and I wouldn’t. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Apparently, Satou also knew about this third-year student named Tatara. He had generally slightly above average scores in OAA overall, ranging between B and&#160;C. He also had long hair in an unusual hairstyle for a boy. I&#160;wondered what that kind of hairstyle was called… I&#160;didn’t really know very much about those kinds of things.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“He was too excited, and it made me recoil a bit. I&#160;was trying to turn him down in a roundabout way,” said Matsushita.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t get it, though. I went back to investigating underneath the sofa for the time being, which I had been in the middle of doing before.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, Ayanokouji-kun, don’t you think a code probably wouldn’t be in a place like that?” Satou said. “Even if there is one, I think it probably wouldn’t be a big one.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was true that somewhere underneath a sofa could have easily been chosen as a stereotypical hiding place for a QR code. In fact, you could see a QR code peeking out underneath the sofa, if you crouched down and looked at a slight angle. Of course, I was not going to be scanning this code.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The important thing here is the school’s pattern,” I&#160;replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Pattern?” she repeated.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“When they decided to run this game, the way they decided upon the values for the codes was important,” I&#160;elaborated.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Um…?” She cocked her head to the side, not quite understanding me. </span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Naturally, they’d be putting high-value QR codes in difficult-to-find locations,” she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s right. Then, the next question is, who would determine what constitutes ‘difficult-to-find’ in this case?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This time, it was Satou who got the answer in before Matsushita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The teachers!” exclaimed Satou. </span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sure it would be a lot of work to put up a hundred stickers with QR codes, right?” she said. “I don’t doubt that it was the teachers who put them up, but it’s hard to imagine that only one or two people could do it. Even if they split up the work and did it during the dead of night last night, they must have sent out several people to do the job…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, did they carefully decide on where to place the QR codes throughout the ship while the students were engaged in the uninhabited island exam?” I pondered. “Or did they suddenly entrust the job to the teachers in charge of this task? Once we know the answer to that question, it’ll probably be easier for us to guess where the stickers might have been placed.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sorry, I’m not following a single word you two are saying…” said Satou, sheepishly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The layout of the pathways and the decorations that have been put up are all fundamentally the same, right?” remarked Matsushita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Did you understand what Matsushita-san means by that?” asked Satou, turning to me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, yeah,” I answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s amazing, Ayanokouji-kun!” Satou exclaimed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Anyway, I think it’s an interesting perspective to take, but considering this is meant to be a treasure hunt, it’s probably fine to be a little more casual about it, right?” I&#160;said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…I suppose, yeah,” said Matsushita-san.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Once that had been said, she wouldn’t be able to continue this conversation any longer. Anyway, she likely just figured it was better to try and reason things out a little so that we wouldn’t regret it later.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Anyway, too bad for me,” she sighed. “Guess someone beat me to it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“T-too bad?” repeated Satou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I think I’ll go search around for a dependable partner myself. Anyway, see ya!” said Matsushita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Standing around here talking would only result in all of us missing out on opportunities anyway.</span></p>

<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">6.3</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">L</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">ESS THAN AN HOUR</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> had passed since the treasure hunt began. By now, many of the participants had scattered, and there were no longer people clustered around in groups of dozens of more. However, I did see students passing by constantly, searching intensely in the same locations. Speaking psychologically, it would be difficult for someone to just go ahead and scan the very first QR code they found. Even if you considered that QR code you discovered to have been difficult to find, you wouldn’t have any frame of reference to compare it too. There was probably a certain percentage of students, me and Satou included, who could have actually discovered the 500,000- or one-million-point codes already but refrained from scanning them or decided to ignore them for now.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Good morning, Ayanokouji-senpai.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hm? Oh, morning, Nanase,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had thought I sensed a presence approach me from behind, and it turned out to be none other than Nanase. I&#160;guess, once again, that we were setting yet another new record for consecutive run-ins since the vacation began, huh.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Who’s she?” asked Satou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">For some reason, Satou seemed visibly cautious of Nanase, shooting her a glare. For Nanase’s part, though, she didn’t interpret the look Satou was giving her as anything offensive and simply bowed her head respectfully.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“My name is Nanase Tsubasa, from Class 1-D,” she announced.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hmm… It’s hard to believe that you’re a first-year,” remarked Satou, eyeing a particular area of Nanase’s body.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Nanase simply cocked her head to the side, puzzled.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You think so? I don’t think I look nearly mature enough to be mistaken for someone older, though,” she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“H-huh? Who says you’re not mature?” Satou exclaimed. “You certainly look </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">mature</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> no matter who you ask!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Really? Hearing you compliment me like that makes me quite happy,” said Nanase. “I’ll do my utmost every day so that I may become more mature.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s no point in trying to become even more mature, is there? Err, I mean, how exactly are you going to do that?” Satou leaned forward a bit; it sounded as though she wanted to become more mature herself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s difficult to explain in concrete terms, but… Yes, I&#160;believe that mental growth is essential.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“M-mental?” Satou repeated in surprise. “So, not like, drinking milk or getting a massage every day?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s certainly true that such actions that promote physical growth are connected to becoming mature, but in my case, I think it’s coming from the mind,” said Nanase.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hm… This is the first I’ve heard of that. But it’s strangely convincing.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">It’s nice that you’re so impressed, Satou, but I don’t think you and Nanase are on the same page here…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you treasure-hunting too, Nanase?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? Oh, um, no, I’m not,” she replied. “For some reason, I just felt like taking it easy today.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It sounded like she wasn’t taking part in the event. But in that case, why did she come here?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m glad to see that you’re looking well again today, Ayanokouji-senpai. Well, I think it’s time I were going now.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Not long after I parted ways with Nanase, I happened to walk past Nakaizumi as well.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nakaizumi?” I muttered aloud.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hm? Is something up with Nakaizumi-kun?” Satou asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was trying not to pay it much mind over the past few days, but it didn’t seem to be a coincidence after all. It&#160;wasn’t merely by chance that I was running into Nanase every day. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">First and foremost, Nanase was deliberately making contact with me so that she could check how I was doing each day. It was only on the third day that I was the one who found Nanase instead, when she was having lunch out on the deck. But even if I hadn’t gone over to talk to her then, it was safe to assume she would’ve come to me. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">And then there was Nakaizumi, who was following after Nanase. He might not have been following Nanase every time she was out, but he was certainly up to something. In all likelihood, it was Ryuuen’s shadow looming behind Nakaizumi. I wondered if Nakaizumi was investigating my relationship with Nanase, but so far, he hadn’t seemed to be paying me any mind. In that case, it was better for me to assume that that he was only keeping tabs on Nanase.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wanted to try to deduce what reason they could have for tracking her. Ryuuen was looking for the perpetrator who had injured Komiya and Kinoshita. If that was what this was about though, Nanase was completely innocent. Simply getting testimony from Sudou and Ike would make that clear. In that case, why would Ryuuen be keeping an eye on her? Both Nanase and I knew that she saw Amasawa that day, but if Nanase knew more and was keeping that information to herself, it would be a different story. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even if I pondered the matter further right now, I wouldn’t find anything out. I decided to just file that away in the back of my mind for now.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I-I found one, Ayanokouji-kun!” Satou exclaimed happily, pointing. “In a kind of hard-to-find spot!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was on the other side of a standing lamp, in a place that was almost completely out of sight. Hidden under the shade was a sticker with a QR code. Fortunately, there didn’t seem to be anyone else but us here at the moment.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But we won’t know how many points it’s worth until we scan it, right?” Satou added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s the hard part,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had a feeling that this wasn’t the most commonly found QR code, as it seemed somewhat difficult to find, but was hard to judge since it wasn’t actually </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">that</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> difficult.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What should we do?” asked Satou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Let’s see…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Nonetheless, I had no doubt that this QR code was too good to simply throw out. I took out my phone, turned on the camera, and pointed it at the code.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? W-wait, is that okay? To go ahead and scan it?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Actually, I’m not scanning it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Satou blinked. “Huh?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I pressed the button to take a picture, saving a zoomed-in photograph of the QR code.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What are you doing?” asked Satou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m keeping pictures of QR codes that seem like they might be worth a lot of Private Points, like this one,” I&#160;explained. “That way, if we can’t find a better code later, then we can just have you scan the QR code from the photo I saved, Satou.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? R-really? Will that still work, scanning it from a photo?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“As long as it’s a clear shot, it should work without any issues.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It would be inefficient to come all the way back here looking for a QR code we found before. Besides, our rivals might beat us to it anyway. However, if we found multiple codes and saved them, we could just scan any of the ones we had if we needed to. If we got a hit with one of those codes, we’d be in luck. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even with just one phone, it was possible to point the camera at the QR code and display the URL. However, our phones weren’t capable of copying the URL if we didn’t access it first, which meant that if we wanted to keep the URL, we’d have to manually type it in later. And if you accidentally touched the URL, you’d start the process of redeeming the code, and then the points would be transferred into your account.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The school said that there were benefits to pairing up, but they weren’t just talking about the fact that you share points,” I added. “Using two phones means we can use time-saving techniques, and we can prevent accidents from happening.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I figured some students who were panicking and made a mad dash for codes may have overlooked this fact. I was sure that many other students were probably employing these techniques though. Now, we just had to hope that no one else found this QR code. If anyone spotted us standing here looking at this standing lamp, this hiding spot would be immediately exposed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Let’s move,” I said aloud.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay,” replied Satou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We headed to a different floor and began searching for QR codes once again. I was groping around underneath a sofa, and something caught my attention.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Looks like there’s one here too,” I remarked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s kind of an easy pattern, isn’t it?” she pointed out. “I mean, that there’s one under this sofa here, like the others.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Satou, would you stand watch for a minute?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sure, but what’s up?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I sat in front of the couch and lowered my face down to take a peek.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I thought we couldn’t expect much from these kinds of QR codes though?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“For the QR code here, yeah,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I started to run my hand not against the floor underneath the sofa, but rather, along the bottom of the sofa itself. Usually, I looked at the floor underneath, but I hadn’t looked at the underside of the sofa before. I&#160;supposed it might have been more correct to say that I </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">couldn’t</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> look at it, rather than I </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">wasn’t</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> looking at it. But when you touched it with your hands, you’d notice that something felt different. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The underside of the sofa should be fabric and even, so something wasn’t right. Here, there was something that caught your fingers if you ran your hand along it. It was a five-centimeter square. There was a sticker placed here. I took my phone in my hands, held it underneath the sofa, and took a picture. By using the light from my phone camera’s flash, I was able to get a picture of the QR code in the dark.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wow, there really was something there!” Satou said. “It’s a QR code! You normally wouldn’t find one there, for sure!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If I had been participating in this treasure hunt game alone, it wouldn’t have been easy for me to scan this QR code. Sure, I could have still saved a photograph of the QR code by taking a picture with the flash on, but I wouldn’t have been able to scan it from my own phone. And even if I tried to flip the sofa over, I’d basically be committing to scanning the QR code without delay as doing something like that would be a big deal and rather conspicuous. However, since I was paired up with someone, I could just have Satou scan it from the photo, and everything would work out smoothly. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It seems like you really think of everything, and so does the school,” observed Satou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After finding a new potential code to scan, we decided to keep moving.</span></p>

<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">6.4</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">E</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">VEN THOUGH THIS SHIP</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> was quite expansive, students couldn’t just freely go wherever they wanted. Inevitably, students were concentrated in the places where they could hang out, so unexpected encounters were common here. One young man headed toward the outdoor café, while another walked back to his own cabin. Two people who had completely unrelated destinations happened to encounter one another in the corridor.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Both were walking down the middle of the hall, and neither showed any sign of backing down. They noticed each other’s presence almost simultaneously and stopped just about a meter apart.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yo, Ryuuen. Thank you </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">so much</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> for what you did the other day.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The first to open his mouth to speak was Housen Kazuomi, from Class 1-D.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You sure it’s okay for you to be up and about? I mean, you may as well stay in bed for another week or so, I figure.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">And the person to respond was none other than Ryuuen Kakeru.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t worry ’bout it,” Housen said. “Besides, even if I beat you half to death right here… Nah, even if I actually kill you, it ain’t even gonna make me feel any better. I’ve got two targets to kill now rather than one, so looks like I’m gonna be busy.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Man, you’d sure look lame if you lost to the same opponent twice,” Ryuuen replied. “Don’t push your luck.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">They were each repeatedly trying to provoke the other, but neither one was ready to start throwing punches.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Heh,” Housen scoffed. “Anyway, loser, I heard you’ve been secretly buyin’ out Free Ride cardholders from the first-years. You bet on that third-year Nagumo, if I remember. Didn’t you make quite a bit from that?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Ku ku</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">. What, someone pissed themselves and told, huh? Who? I made sure to put a clause in the contract that they’d have to keep their mouths shut.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Before the uninhabited island exam started, Ryuuen approached first-year students who had the Free Ride card and had them sign contracts. Those students were apparently supposed to give up all the points they earned from whatever group they had designated if that group won anything. If the group they picked only gotten into the top fifty percent, they would have only earned 30,000 points. In other words, if you were going to get even more value out of that, then some people were going to have to give up their claim to it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In the end, Ryuuen had designated Nagumo’s group, and had gotten 280,000 points across all the students he had contracts with. Most of Ryuuen’s classmates were unaware of this fact, and only the people he used to execute his plan knew about it.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That was a fast response.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was confident that I could control other people, at least to a certain extent, but it was safe to say that the sole exception to this was Kouenji. I had run many simulations in my head on how to control all my classmates, and Kouenji was the only one who, no matter how many times I tried, evaded my control. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even if you give up, I won’t,” said Horikita. “His strength is essential.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Trying to control what couldn’t be controlled was, simply put, a contradiction.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even if it’s a waste of time?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re saying we don’t need Kouenji-kun?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I think that as long as he doesn’t cause any harm, the best course of action is to leave him be,” I replied. “And&#160;now that Kouenji has been given a Protect Point, it’s become even more possible for us to do just that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That certainly is a rational line of thinking, I suppose,” she conceded.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If our class couldn’t win without Kouenji, then I could understand why you’d be so worked up about it. However, your class has already grown to be capable of competing against the other classes. And it will continue to grow from here onward.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s certainly true. Compared to a year ago, the class has become much more dependable,” said Horikita. “While striving for Class A is my priority and my ultimate goal, I want to keep the class together as one. I want to lead the class in such a way that everyone works together.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That meant she didn’t want anyone to be left out, not even Kouenji, huh. Horikita was looking straight at me when she spoke. Her gaze was so piercing that it almost made me choke on my words. If Horikita could reel in the man known as Kouenji to her side, he certainly would make an irreplaceable and dependable ally. However, clearing that hurdle was probably going to be more difficult than reaching Class A.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In the past, I wouldn’t have taken her statement seriously and I would’ve just dismissed what she said as nonsense, ramblings about something beyond her means. Horikita’s growth was slow, but she was progressing step by step. Well…despite that, I still couldn’t say for certain that even Horikita would be able to move Kouenji someday. Honestly, Kouenji was the only guy who just wouldn’t fit into my calculations.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What’s up?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hm?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You just looked like you were thinking about something.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nah, not really,” I said. “I was just wondering how I should use these Private Points I’ve gotten.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…I see. Well, considering you’ve been giving half of your earnings to Kushida-san, you should cherish the Private Points you’ve received today and not spend them recklessly,” she warned.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s true. I’ll plan to do just that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I decided to walk away quietly, since hanging around here any longer would only mean getting in the way of Horikita’s work.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"></div>
<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">6.9</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">T WAS NOW AFTER</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> five o’clock. I was getting together with a certain someone that I had been planning to meet before the dinner hour started at six. Just after I left my guest cabin to make my way to the fifth-floor deck, I happened across Sudou. He had just come out of his own guest cabin two doors down.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s almost time to eat. Where ya off to?” he asked, presumably thinking that I was on my way back to my room.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Just going for a little walk before dinner,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Dude, you kinda sound like an old grandpa,” Sudou said. “Welp, I’ll see ya at the restaurant then.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We exchanged a few words and were about to go our separate ways, but Sudou must have remembered something because he called back over to me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, sorry, dude! Actually, to tell you the truth, something kinda surprising happened!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You mean how Ike and Shinohara are going out now?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“H-how the heck did ya know already?!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I just happened to hear, by coincidence.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Still man, that sure was a surprise for me,” said Sudou. “He beat me to it… Anyway, more importantly, he said he wants to study together. He wants to join Suzune’s study group.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was unexpected. Or I suppose I should say that was quicker than I imagined. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Low academic ability is fatal here in this school, after all,” I reasoned.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Many students were in danger of being expelled because of that. A student’s main responsibility was to their studies. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s a precious time for me, since I can be all alone with Suzune,” said Sudou. “But if he’s motivated, I ain’t got no choice but to support ’im, right? So, Kanji’s gonna hit the books hard, starting in the summer term lead-up.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">From what Sudou was saying, he really was planning to start his studies right after our trip was over. Whether or not Ike demonstrated immediate results depended on his efforts, but he might show some growth early in the second semester. Both Sudou and Ike could undergo a profound change thanks to their respective romances.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You might have another person joining your study group too,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? Seriously?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ike’s not the only student who’s started thinking that they’d like to be tutored by Horikita.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sudou came over and grabbed hold of my shoulders, with a serious look on his face. “It ain’t a guy, is it?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No… It’s not. It’s Satou. Just Satou.” I hadn’t intended to say her name, but I felt like I had been forced into revealing the truth against my wishes because of the intense pressure.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“A girl, huh,” said Sudou. “Well, in that case… Wait, Satou, huh? But if it’s not just me there, but Ike too, she probably ain’t gonna join the study group then, will she?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t you think you’re making some assumptions here? Besides, she sounded determined.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hmm. Well, all right then, that’s good. I ain’t gonna lose though, no matter who joins,” he said with a forceful snort, showing off his strong resolve to continue his studies.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Isn’t it pretty tough for you though, with your club activities going on at the same time?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well yeah, it’s not easy,” he admitted. “But hey, I’ve always had enough stamina to brag about it. The first time I really tried stretching my brain, I got super sleepy in just a minute. But now, I can go on and on and… Well, actually, I can only concentrate for about an hour or so, but still.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Still, if you could concentrate and study for that long, you wouldn’t have any problems. Studying for an hour, taking a break, and then studying for another hour… If you repeated that process, that would be more than enough.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But, y’know… Damn, dude. I can’t accept the fact that Kanji got a girlfriend before me,” said Sudou. He was smirking, but he sounded frustrated too, like he was deeply lamenting that fact. “I’m gonna hold a grudge about that and give him some </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">thorough</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> training. Gonna put that Spartan training from the basketball club to&#160;use.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">They were partners in crime with a love-hate relationship between them. From the sounds of it, Sudou was going to take good care of him (or rather, torment him).</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In moderation though, right?” I said. “I mean, it’s not exactly easy to start liking studying, when you used to hate it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, I know, man. I mean, I was super against studying myself</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">” said Sudou, sticking out his tongue like he had just swallowed something bitter.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After Sudou and I parted ways, I reached my intended destination. I saw Kushida up ahead on the deck and hid myself from view. It was already about five minutes after the time we were scheduled to meet, which naturally meant that she had been waiting for me for at least that long. I took out my phone and called her. She picked up after about two rings.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hello?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After I confirmed that I had heard her voice, I walked out onto the deck where Kushida was. Mobile phones were designed to prioritize phone calls because of their intended use. Even if Kushida had turned on the recording function on her phone, once I called her, it would have automatically shut off. Since I called her, that meant that the conversation we were about to have right now would be between me and Kushida alone.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry I’m late, Kushida,” I said over the phone. “I’m on my way there right now. Are you already there waiting?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. Um… Oh, over here!” Kushida looked to her left and right, and, spotting me, waved me over. I didn’t end the call on my phone but proceeded to run over to where she was. We then each hung up, at about the exact same time.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry to have made you wait. I got a little lost,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh, even you make mistakes too, Ayanokouji-kun. Anyway, what’s up? You said that you wanted to talk.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ve been wondering about what I should do over the past couple years, but in the end, I decided that I should just come out with it and lay it all out,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hm? Lay it all out? What?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Did you know that I participated in the treasure hunt?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. You partnered up with Satou-san, right?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She gave me a puzzled look, as if to say, </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“What about it?”</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">, not understanding what this conversation was leading to.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The QR code I scanned earned me a reward of 100,000 points,” I said. “Meaning that I came away with 90,000 points after subtracting the participation fee. If you divide that by two, you get 45,000 points. I thought that giving you half would be the right thing to do, Kushida.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After telling her all that, I held out my phone, showing her a record of deposits and withdrawals into my account. It clearly showed that 100,000 points had just been deposited into my account a little while ago.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait, what? It was just a game though. I don’t think you need to worry that much about it,” said Kushida. She was surprised about the unexpected turn this conversation had taken and held her hands out in front of her to show that she was refusing to accept the points.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“To be honest, I thought so too, at first. Well, I tried to think that was the correct answer, but I couldn’t help but feeling like it was a dishonest, unfair way of thinking. I figured there was a possibility that you’d say you didn’t need the points, or that if I kept quiet, you wouldn’t find out about it. It’s because I felt ashamed about my own way of thinking that I decided to give you half.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">No matter how much I tried to reason with her, it would probably be difficult for Kushida to accept them.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“To tell you the honest truth, though… I want you to take these points as a token of my good faith,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Good faith…?” she repeated.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m giving you half of the Private Points I earn so that I can buy my safety from you, Kushida. If I demonstrate that to you here, then I think that you’ll show me the same.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I appealed to her with my eyes, as if to add, </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“Am I wrong?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Besides, it never hurts to have more Private Points, even if only a little. Right?” I added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s true, but isn’t this quite distressing for you, Ayanokouji-kun?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s fine. It’s nothing compared to fighting with you, Kushida.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Somehow…the opposite seems a little scary,” she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do you mean?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You know, Ayanokouji-kun, lately I’ve been hearing about how you’re such an amazing student. Are you really giving me half of your Private Points because you&#160;want to make a truce with me?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In my opinion, it’s more dangerous for me to make an enemy of you, Kushida. I interact with you every day, compared to students like Sakayanagi or Ryuuen. I only fight with them during special exams,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Although she was somewhat wary, Kushida nodded her head, seemingly satisfied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I understand. Still, are you sure you’re okay with this?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Using my phone, I transferred half of the Private Points I had received from my account to hers, just like I had done time and time again.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I hate to say this after giving you points, but if I do ever have money-related troubles, I might come to you for help,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She giggled, as if she found my pathetic comment amusing. “Oh? That’s kind of uncool, Ayanokouji-kun,” she teased.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Still, I think what you’re doing is much, much smarter than what Horikita-san is doing,” she went on. “I can’t say I dislike your approach.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That so?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Honestly, from my perspective, you’re the only person I don’t want to make an enemy of either, Ayanokouji-kun. I hope we continue to get along.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. I’d like to keep this relationship going. You scratch my back, I’ll scratch yours.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">And with that, Kushida and I went our separate ways, as if nothing had happened.</span></p>
