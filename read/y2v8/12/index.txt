<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 6:<br/>School Trip Day Four</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">T WAS THE MORNING</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> of the fourth day of the school trip. Tomorrow, it would already be time to go back to school. Since this was the second time we were going to&#160;have complete freedom, I wanted to make it a day with no regrets. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The results of yesterday’s sightseeing tour showed that ten groups, meaning half of the twenty total groups, had managed to score twenty points or more, and so all those people had earned 30,000 Private Points. On the other hand, group fifteen, which Mii-chan and Miyamoto were in, hadn’t finished the tour in time and was thus disqualified, so they were unfortunately spending today in study hall at the ryokan.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I felt a little sorry for them, but that’s just how things were. I hoped that once their study hall was over, they could take a nice, long soak in the hot springs and enjoy the trip as much as possible, even if it was only a little. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The large bathhouse was being cleaned right now, so I quickly got changed. I was thinking that maybe I’d watch some TV or something, like yesterday, but Kitou had apparently gotten to it before I did, and was staring at the screen with rapt attention. I didn’t know what it was exactly, but it looked to be some kind of special feature on fashion that had caught Kitou’s interest.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, Ayanokouji,” Watanabe said. “Sounds like they’re having a snowball fight outside!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Snowball fight?” I repeated.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Watanabe, who had also finished changing, showed me his phone. From the looks of things, some people were going to have a snowball fight starting now, and everyone was free to join in.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That sounds interesting,” I said. “Think I’ll go check it out.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ryuuen, Kitou, what about you guys?” asked Watanabe.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kitou was too absorbed in his TV program to answer, but Ryuuen quickly headed for his usual spot in the room, making a clear statement that he was going to pass.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay, guess it’s just you and me then. Let’s go,” said Watanabe.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah,” I replied. The two people we had left behind were like water and oil, but I’d just place my trust in their consciences. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When Watanabe and I exited the ryokan together, I saw that a considerable number of students had already gathered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Good morning, Kiyotaka-kun, Watanabe-kun,” Yousuke called out to us. He had been standing near the entrance, his phone in his hand.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There really are a lot of people here,” I remarked. “Is&#160;everyone </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">that</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> interested in a snowball fight?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t think it’s that simple. This is a snowball fight where contestants gamble Private Points,” Yousuke explained. “Well, though I say gamble, you only have to pay a thousand points to qualify. The winning team basically takes points from the losing team.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">I see.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> Even if you lost, you wouldn’t lose that much. If&#160;you won, you’d receive enough money to get a souvenir or two. With how casual this was, it was no wonder that people were so excited.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But still, is it okay for people to do this?” I asked. “Even though it is a wide-open space, we’re still on the premises of the ryokan.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, it’s fine,” Yousuke said. “I asked just to be safe, and the staff said that they don’t mind as long as we do it early in the day. I think that the fact that there isn’t anyone staying at the ryokan other than us students, who are here for a school trip, was probably a crucial factor, too.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The rules were unquestionably plain and simple. You weren’t allowed to catch snowballs; you could only dodge them. Students who were hit with a snowball had to leave the field. However, snowballs needed to be a certain size. For example, if you just tossed snow that was like powder, making it spread out in the air like a shotgun blast, it wouldn’t count, even if you did happen to hit someone. It&#160;seemed like whether a hit was official was determined by both student self-reporting and referee judgment. Well, I supposed that only a couple people would intentionally cheat just for a few Private Points.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How many people are planning to participate?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“About thirty right now, I think,” said Yousuke. “Are you going to play too, Ayanokouji-kun?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I…” I trailed off. I was thinking that I should probably decline, but still, I had to wonder. A snowball fight. If I chose to sit this one out, then I might not ever get a chance to try it again. “I want to try, but I don’t have a team.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t worry. I’ll assign you to a team that’s short on numbers. Just wait a little bit,” said Yousuke.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He seemed to be the one taking care of the bothersome matters, for which I was immensely grateful. I supposed that was probably why he was hanging out near the entrance. Taking care of everything on your own could come with a lot of difficulties, but knowing Yousuke, he might feel more relaxed if he could manage everything on his own. As I was waiting, for what I anticipated to be roughly ten minutes or so until the cutoff for signing up, Horikita showed up. She must also have come after hearing about the snowball fight.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ve already heard as much, but wow, there really are a lot of people here,” she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you thinking of joining too, by any chance?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, actually… It is a school trip, after all. If there’s an open spot, I think I’ll enter.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It sounded like she hadn’t originally planned to join in, but she changed her mind after seeing that the snowball fight was more popular than she had expected.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, you’re on, Horikita!” Ibuki emerged from the crowd to challenge Horikita, as though she had been waiting for her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…So, you’re here too, Ibuki-san,” said Horikita. “Seriously, you always seem to just show up out of thin air everywhere I go. But whatever. It’s just a simple game anyway, but if you want, I don’t mind taking you on.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The instant that Horikita responded, Ibuki clenched her fist tight. “Sure, it may just be a game or whatever, but a loss is a loss. Don’t whine and make excuses like a little kid afterward, ’kay?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I should say the exact same thing back to you,” Horikita replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yousuke must have been watching those two closely. When I snuck a glance at his phone, I could tell he took their situation into account, because he had assigned them to different teams. It likely wouldn’t have been overly exciting if they were on the same team. Since I was sneaking a peek at his phone anyway, I whispered into Yousuke’s ear and asked him for a small favor.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Just then, Kushida appeared with Yamamura, Nishino, and Amikura in tow.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Good morning, everyone,” she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, Kushida, I was wondering where you were. Looks like you invited Yamamura and the others, too, eh?” I remarked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? …Oh, uh, yeah, I did.” I expected her to respond with her usual smile, but Kushida averted her eyes, mumbling. Then she quickly looked back with a smile. “Nishino-san and Yamamura-san said that they’d just wait in their room until it was time to go, but I told them that’d be a waste.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That was the right decision,” I agreed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We had been spending time as a group, and our relationship had been improving, little by little. It was more worthwhile for us to spend our time together, whether we were actually participating in the event or just spectating.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You doin’ this too?” Ibuki asked Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hm? The snowball fight?” Kushida replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. It’s been decided that me and Horikita are gonna battle it out,” said Ibuki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see,” said Kushida. “But I think I’ll pass, actually. I’d feel bad if I hit someone with a snowball. I don’t think I could do it—I’d feel sorry for them.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh?” Ibuki gestured like she was grossed out, as though Kushida’s behavior was thoroughly disgusting to her. Seeing that, Horikita thrust her hand into Ibuki’s side with a quick chop.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ow! The hell was that for?!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m your opponent, remember?” Horikita said. “If you think about unnecessary things, you’ll lose in no time.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No way am I gonna lose. I am definitely gonna make you cry!” shouted Ibuki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">I see.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> I had thought that the sense of distance between Horikita and Kushida had changed recently, and it looked like Ibuki was involved, too. The three of them were twisted, for sure, but strangely enough, they might have been doing a decent job of straightening themselves out.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Lit: “Those crooked three, but strangely enough they might be purifying themselves well” Ayanokouji is basically saying that they might be helping each other become better people, in a way, in his view.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The number of students participating continued to increase bit by bit. In the end, there were forty-two people spread across six teams. There were four teams of seven people that had formed on their own, without any assistance. And the two other teams were made up of the odd ones out, like me. The snowball fight wasn’t conducted in anything like a tournament setting. It&#160;would just be single rounds.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Perhaps Yousuke had done this because he thought it would be more exciting, but he decided to have the Horikita-Ibuki grudge match as the third and final game. For the first game, there was a team of seven boys led by Ishizaki and Albert, and then another team of seven boys, led by Sudou. It truly was a clash between men. Right from the start of the game, snowballs flew with incredible force from both the left and right side.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There were a total of fourteen people throwing snowballs at each other, after all, so it would be difficult for everyone to avoid them all. Within about ten seconds or so, six players from both teams were gone already. Incidentally, the incredibly enthusiastic Ishizaki was one of the players who had left the game in those ten seconds. As for Sudou, he seemed to be putting his frustration over being rejected by Horikita into his snowballs, because he was taking out opponents on the opposing team one after another. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, Ishizaki’s team still had Albert in play, and, demonstrating a level of nimble maneuverability you wouldn’t expect considering his massive frame, he avoided being hit. He was putting up a good fight, having taken out two people himself. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yamamura was quietly spectating the impressive battle, so I decided to try getting a little closer to her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s getting exciting, isn’t it?” she said, after noticing my presence. Her facial expression was pretty much the same as usual, with just some slight variation, but she did seem to be enjoying herself somewhat.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, it looks that way,” I agreed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yamamura exhaled deeply, breathing into her palms. I noticed that she wasn’t wearing gloves, even though she was supposed to buy another pair at the ski resort.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Did you forget your gloves again or something?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes,” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I went to take off my gloves, but Yamamura stopped me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sorry. That was a joke. I did bring them with me,” she said, taking a pair of gloves out of her pocket. She wore a faint smile.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you make jokes too, eh, Yamamura?” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“… I certainly don’t look the type, it’s true,” said Yamamura. Her smile had instantly disappeared; she must have been regretting what she said, feeling like it was an unnecessary comment. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, no, it’s good,” I said. “I kind of feel like we’ve bonded a little bit as a group.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was a change that would have been unthinkable on the first day of the trip.</span></p>

Afterward, I made sure to buy some souvenirs for first-year students during the time I had left. Now, all that we had left on such a fun fourth day of our school trip was tonight.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After I got back from taking a soak in the large bath, I received a message from Sakayanagi. She said that she wanted to see me, and I responded to her request, heading toward the lobby where we had arranged to meet. It was still only around eight o’clock at night now, but there seemed to be much fewer students out and about today. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I figured that students must have a mountain of things to talk about tonight, whether they were at the buffet or in their rooms, since it was the last night of our trip. Perhaps the other students had realized that as well, since I hardly saw anyone in the lobby. That made it a rather convenient location for us. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakayanagi was sitting in a chair, quietly waiting for me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Did I keep you waiting long?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Not at all,” she replied. “Thank you for going to the trouble of meeting me here.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though there weren’t many people around, me and Sakayanagi being together would certainly attract a fair amount of attention. With that in mind, I would have preferred to make this short, but… </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I know it only lasted a short time, but did you enjoy the school trip?” asked Sakayanagi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, I did,” I said. “I’ve learned a lot about many things I hadn’t ever experienced before. More importantly, though, it was a genuinely valuable experience for me to be able to interact with students from the other classes. I feel like I even know Yamamura and Kitou a little now.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I decided to specifically mention those two by name, but Sakayanagi’s expression remained the same as always.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see,” she said. “I’m not particularly surprised, Ayanokouji-kun, considering you have an insatiable appetite for knowledge.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I decided to try pressing her a little further. “Are you close with those two?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“None of my classmates are given special consideration. I think of them all as equals. If you were to ask if we get along, then yes, we do. But if you were to say we aren’t that close, I’d have to agree with that, too.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Was that vague answer a lie? Or the truth? Singling someone out for special treatment would likely cause jealousy or similar feelings to stir amongst the other students. Sakayanagi might really have been telling the truth when she said she looked at everyone equally as their leader.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Can I ask why you called me?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, that means we’re already finished with the small talk? Are you in a rush, by any chance? I suppose if Karuizawa Kei-san were to see us here like this, she would have some suspicions about our relationship,” said Sakayanagi, with a devilish little chuckle. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not keen on the idea of being seen meeting with the representative of Class A one-on-one. You know?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hee hee! I’m only joking. I understand.” Sakayanagi took a moment to suppress an amused smile. Then she said, “I’ve come to understand many things during this school trip. I thought that, before we return to campus, I would talk to you about the person who contacted you during the Sports Festival, Ayanokouji-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She was referring to the time she and I had both been absent from the Sports Festival, when we talked in my room. So…she wanted to talk to me about the identity of the person who’d spoken to me from the other side of my door, hmm?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. I am interested in hearing that,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m glad. So, you’re also interested in knowing whose voice that was, Ayanokouji-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ve been thinking about it more than a little.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Including what I had sensed in Nanase, it remained unclear whether the person who had spoken to me then was an enemy or not.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“First, though, allow me to ask you something,” said Sakayanagi. “What kind of person do you think he is, Ayanokouji-kun? Is it possible that he shares your origins, like Amasawa Ichika-san and Yagami Takuya-kun?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, I don’t think so,” I replied. “If it was just that you and this other person recognized each other, Sakayanagi, then sure, I wouldn’t be able to rule that idea out. But this person called my father ‘Ayanokouji-sensei.’ That makes an enormous difference.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Meaning what?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If this person were a White Room student, he wouldn’t have called him ‘Ayanokouji-sensei,’ I mean.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was a common thread shared amongst all of those who were raised in the White Room.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But that’s not ironclad proof, is it?” she said. “If he were from a different generation from you, Ayanokouji-kun, then there might be significantly different policies in place, no?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s true that I can’t say it with one hundred percent certainty,” I admitted. “It’s just my own subjective feelings here, at best. But considering the major fact that this person came to speak with me last year, when that man was… I mean, when my father was visiting the school, I can guess that he’s on my father’s side. And there’s the fact that you yourself said he sounded familiar. That could mean that he’s someone close to the political or financial world, right, Sakayanagi?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It went back to the fact that this person had deliberately called him “sensei.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Though she was a little surprised, Sakayanagi closed her eyes and nodded happily. “Exactly correct. I suppose it might have been unnecessary for me to think about providing hints or advice after all, then. Yes, I already have an idea as to the person’s identity, but I have not yet confirmed who it is at this time. I wanted to clarify that here and now, which is why I called you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I turned my attention to the cell phone that Sakayanagi had placed in her lap.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But before we make everything clear, I’m calling someone who should know him,” she said. “They said that they would be here shortly.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you’re saying that there’s someone in our grade who has some connection with our visitor?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It sounds as though no one is coming to mind as a possible candidate, Ayanokouji-kun,” she said. “Is that correct?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was. I had absolutely no idea who in the world she was referring to. Of course, whoever it was who’d come to talk to me then, he was spending his days at school as a first-year student, so it wouldn’t be strange if he was close with someone from my grade. But I didn’t think that was what was going on here. There was no reason for Sakayanagi to call this person here unless they at least knew more about the situation than I did. Who else aside from Sakayanagi would know about the White Room, the identity of my father, or both those things?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In the meantime, let’s continue our idle chitchat, shall we?” said Sakayanagi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That sounds like a good idea,” I agreed. You couldn’t say that passing the time in silence was a wise way to do things on a school trip.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How did you feel about the group assignments this time, Ayanokouji-kun?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The effect of the assessments we filled out in our individual charts must have been significant,” I said. “I got the sense that the group assignments weren’t reflected in just our own groups; it seemed like the school matched up students who had given one another extreme assessments, from what I could tell.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I feel the same way. There were the students who were rated the best, and the students who weren’t. And there was a middle group that didn’t fall into either category. I’m sure that it might not have been applicable to every group, but I think that bias was most certainly in effect. I’m sure that they made combinations that were likely to have an impact on the future.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Now, on that note, there’s something I’d like to ask you,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That makes me happy to hear. Please go ahead and ask me anything you wish.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What’s your take on the final exam?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was sure that the formations of each group for the school trip would surely have an impact later on. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakayanagi, looking pleased, closed her eyes and nodded contently a few times. “It truly is quite fun talking with you, Ayanokouji-kun. You and I always seem to be thinking the same thing. I’m sure the final exam this year will be more arduous than last year.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakayanagi was predicting that it wouldn’t be a surprise if one or two people got expelled.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’ve got a Protect Point, Sakayanagi, so you’re safe and secure, but the fact remains that if you lose, you’re still going to lose Class Points,” I said. “Aren’t you worried that the commanding lead you’ve held until now will fall apart?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you think that I’ll lose in a direct confrontation with Ryuuen-kun? It’s already a foregone conclusion that I will win.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So, like Ryuuen, Sakayanagi never even imagined that she could be defeated, huh?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s true that he makes some interesting moves,” she admitted. “There is an expression that comes to mind: giant killer. And it certainly seems like he has the ability to take down strong opponents at times. However, that is not going to happen in a confrontation with me. At the very least, next year, I will be the one competing with your class, Ayanokouji-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Unshakeable confidence. There were cases where things could end in a draw, but those could be seen as exceptions. It was unlikely that the school would make rules that would allow for a draw to happen easily in a final exam setting. That’s what I’d observed from last year’s battle with Class A.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Or, perhaps… Do you think that I will lose?” asked Sakayanagi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Who can say?” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I couldn’t say anything right now, when I couldn’t see what was going to be on the test. But if I told Sakayanagi that, she’d likely feel all the more disappointed. It would be nothing more than a suggestion on my part that Sakayanagi might lose, depending on the nature of the exam. Besides, no matter who won and who lost—</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“From your perspective, Ayanokouji-kun, whether he falls, or I do, it won’t interfere with your plans either way,” she said. “Isn’t that right?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was precisely because our thoughts were aligned that Sakayanagi understood what I was thinking here, too.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“However, Ayanokouji-kun, the future will not always necessarily go as you expect.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do you mean?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Just as I asked her that question, though, she brought her index finger up to her lips. Apparently, the visitor she had been expecting had arrived.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry to have kept you waiting,” said Kanzaki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He must not have heard that I was going to be here, because he seemed somewhat surprised as he came over to stand next to me. Still, though—Kanzaki? In all the times I’d been in contact with him up until this point, I’d never once gotten the impression that he had any special connection with my past.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Now that we have all the necessary players assembled, let’s get started, shall we?” said Sakayanagi. “Oh, first, though, would you mind coming over this way, Kanzaki-kun?” She beckoned him over to her with a broad smile.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What in the world is going on here, Sakayanagi?” Kanzaki didn’t appear to understand the situation. He crossed his arms in suspicion, seemingly unable to wrap his mind around what was happening here. I felt the same way, as I wondered if there was any particular meaning behind having Kanzaki positioned next to her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“First, Ayanokouji-kun, what do you think when you see Kanzaki-kun and me together like this?” asked Sakayanagi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do I think?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Please tell me your honest opinion,” she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“All I can say is that something feels off</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em; letter-spacing: 0.60mm;">,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">” I replied, “because I’ve never seen the two of you interact before today.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That fact became even more apparent when they stood side by side like they were doing right now.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, I’m sure it would feel that way. From the perspective of the students at our school, there is no point of connection between Kanzaki-kun and me. We don’t hold the same position in our respective classes, and I don’t believe that anyone in school has seen anything resembling a personal friendship. In fact, I’ve hardly spoken to Kanzaki-kun at all since enrolling in this school.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It sounded like she was trying to say that they had spoken with one another </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">before</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> coming to this school.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How many years has it been since I’ve spoken to you like this?” asked Sakayanagi, turning to Kanzaki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s hard to recall, exactly,” he replied. “If we’re not counting speaking through an intermediary, then it must have been at least three or four years.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">From the sounds of it, not even they clearly remembered when they spoke last.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“May I ask how you two know each other?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We’re connected through our parents,” said Sakayanagi. “Though, that being said, it’s not as if there is a direct connection between the Sakayanagi and Kanzaki families. When you have parents who are rather well-known in their own right, you are often invited to parties and the like.”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Considering that Sakayanagi’s father was chairperson of this school, and also that he knew about the White Room, I had no doubt that the Sakayanagi family was one of some renown.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So, what they shared was the fact that their respective families were business leaders. That would explain why I’d had no suspicions about Kanzaki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What in the world is the meaning of this conversation?” Kanzaki demanded. “What’s the point of allowing Ayanokouji to hear something like this? Wait, before getting to that, I’d like to hear the reason why you called me here.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The conversation we’re having right now is related to the reason I called you here,” said Sakayanagi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t understand what you mean,” he said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I was wondering if you might be so kind as to tell us about Ishigami-kun, who is also enrolled at our school,” said Sakayanagi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">With that, Kanzaki’s expression stiffened more. “You…want to know about Ishigami?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ishigami? No one who had that surname came to mind among the second-years. The only student who did was a first-year.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…I see. So, that’s it,” Kanzaki said. “You’re also interested in Ishigami, then?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t mind you interpreting it that way,” replied Sakayanagi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But still, why Ayanokouji? He shouldn’t have any point of contact with Ishigami. I can’t imagine that he would interact with people from other grade levels for no reason. Or, if he did, it would be because there’s some kind of trouble. It’s hard to imagine even Ryuuen, or better yet, Ayanokouji, would waste his time with something so pointless,” said Kanzaki, simplifying and explaining the situation as he saw it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Not right now, but in the past,” said Sakayanagi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What…?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you still not understand? You should have deep feelings for the name Ayanokouji as well,” said Sakayanagi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do y—wait, you couldn’t mean…” Kanzaki, as though he had just realized something, alternated looking at Sakayanagi and me, back and forth. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You were quite slow in noticing,” Sakayanagi remarked. “Well, that is understandable, I suppose.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…So, that’s what this is.” It sounded as though Kanzaki grasped what Sakayanagi was saying. He looked up at the ceiling for a moment, seemingly exasperated, before turning back to look at me.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There was one thing I could understand from what Kanzaki just said. Namely, that Kanzaki happened to know of a person with the name Ayanokouji, or he was acquainted with that person. And it was no longer necessary to even guess that the person in question was my father. That man had strong connections to business leaders. It was an inevitability.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Does this shake off the feeling of discomfort you had from seeing me sitting next to Ayanokouji-kun just now?” asked Sakayanagi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes,” Kanzaki replied. “I had thought that you were simply interested in Ayanokouji’s abilities, but now I see that’s not it. When did you know that he was Ayanokouji-sensei’s child?”</span></p>

Right?” Sakayanagi didn’t say anything about the White Room, instead answering in a way that made it sound like she was pretending we were childhood friends.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Meaning he’s not just your average nobody. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“My assessment of you has changed over and over in my head, but now, it’s finally settled,” Kanzaki said. “If&#160;there’s a child of Ayanokouji-sensei in Horikita’s class, then they must be formidable.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He sounded happily convinced. He must have had a high opinion of my father.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Now then,” said Sakayanagi. “Now that we’ve managed to correct that misunderstanding, let’s continue with the conversation, shall we? Ayanokouji-kun, you do not know of Ishigami-kun, correct?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s the first I’ve ever heard of him,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Apparently, the person who had approached Sakayanagi and me back then was this Ishigami character.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“He is a young man who idolizes your father, Ayanokouji-kun. Kanzaki-kun, you know him quite well, don’t you?” asked Sakayanagi, turning to Kanzaki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Yes. He certainly seems devoted to Ayanokouji-sensei. I didn’t have the courage to go up and speak to Ayanokouji-sensei, but Ishigami was different. At some point, he just went over and started talking to him. He was very proactive in doing so.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ishigami-kun is one year younger than we are, and is currently a first-year student at our school,” Sakayanagi added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So, someone who idolized that man had enrolled at this school, and, for some reason, had contacted me several times. He even indirectly helped eliminate Yagami during the Cultural Festival. The goal of this young man named Ishigami was yet to be seen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sure you’ve had opportunities to interact with first-year students, but when exactly did you take notice of Ishigami?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I recognized him immediately when I looked at OAA,” Sakayanagi replied. “But he’s not the type of person to come out in public, so I haven’t had the chance to speak with him. I have communicated with Class 1-A through Takahashi-kun, but it seemed as though Ishigami-kun intentionally avoided contact with me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It sounded like Sakayanagi herself didn’t want to forcefully get in contact with him either, then.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is he an exceptional student?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I am sure that Kanzaki-kun, who is much closer to him than I, would know much more about that, yes?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kanzaki was entrusted with providing an explanation, but he didn’t look pleased at all. In fact, he looked the exact opposite. “It’s not as though we’re close,” he said. “I just happened to go to the same cram school as him, is all. But Ayanokouji, to give you an honest answer: yes, he is, without question, a genius. He came up with ideas that I could never even have dreamed of, and speaking from experience, after seeing him up close, he’s exceptional.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">While it sounded like he didn’t like Ishigami, Kanzaki accepted the truth about him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There you have it,” said Sakayanagi. “Now, what you’ve heard reflects Kanzaki-kun’s thoughts and viewpoint, but I think it might serve as reference.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But still, I have to ask: so what? Can’t you just leave Ishigami alone?” asked Kanzaki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Can’t you imagine what might be going on, though? He’s someone who respects Ayanokouji-kun’s father. If&#160;that’s true, then it would not be surprising if he had enrolled at this school to see the son’s abilities for himself.” Sakayanagi still kept information about the White Room hidden as she skillfully guided the conversation forward.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ishigami came to see Ayanokouji’s abilities…? Well, yes, I suppose I can’t say that’s impossible.” It sounded like Kanzaki was somewhat convinced that could be true, after applying what he had heard and comparing it with the Ishigami that he knew in his mind.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We are competing with one another in our grade level,” Sakayanagi continued. “Kanzaki-kun, though your class is a step behind, it is still unclear who will emerge victorious. Under such circumstances, don’t you think it would be unfair if Ishigami-kun were to launch some unnecessary trap for Ayanokouji-kun sometime in the future, in order to find out his abilities?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I do understand what you’re trying to say. But still, why are you supporting Ayanokouji? It shouldn’t matter to you what happens to a student in a rival class.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ishigami, if left alone, could go ahead and sabotage a student from a rival class. It was obvious to anyone that that would be an inherent positive for Sakayanagi under normal circumstances.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I just genuinely want to have fun,” Sakayanagi replied. “It is my role to bury Horikita-san’s class, Ayanokouji-kun included. Don’t you think it would be frustrating if someone were to suddenly come in from out of nowhere and snatch your goal away from you?” After letting out a brief chuckle, Sakayanagi offered her gratitude to Kanzaki. “You have my thanks, Kanzaki-kun. From here forward, I think that Ayanokouji-kun and I will devise countermeasures to deal with Ishigami-kun, just the two of us,” she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She had expressed her thanks, yes…but she was also strongly implying that anyone not involved in the rest of the discussion should leave immediately.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I have no intention of getting involved with Ishigami, so I appreciate that,” replied Kanzaki without hesitation. “Let’s chat again in the near future, Ayanokouji. I’d like to ask you all sorts of questions about your father.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kanzaki had a burning desire to talk about my father, but unfortunately, I didn’t know anything. At any rate, I figured that it was fine to just respond with a gentle nod right now. With that, Kanzaki walked away.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Now then, Ayanokouji-kun. Let’s find out if Ishigami-kun really is the correct answer,” said Sakayanagi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What are you planning to do?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We’ll try asking the person in question directly, of course,” she said. “That would be the quickest method, no?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Cell phone in hand, Sakayanagi’s fingers moved smoothly across the screen as she entered the eleven-digit number. Apparently, she had done her research, and she had already obtained Ishigami’s phone number. She put the phone on speaker, and, after she hit call, the person on the other end of the line picked up the phone in just a couple rings.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ve been thinking it was about time I ought to be getting a call from you, Sakayanagi.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">His tone also seemed to suggest that he had anticipated a call. The voice I heard was unmistakably the same as the one I’d heard last year, when I had been called on the phone, and when I had been contacted in my room during the Sports Festival.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You are very quick on the uptake, it seems,” said Sakayanagi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">I asked that if anyone other than a first-year requested my number, it be reported to me ahead of time.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I suppose I should say that I expected nothing less from you. I’ve heard talk of you both within and outside,” replied Sakayanagi. She had been constantly collecting information, like a spider tracking the ripples in its web. “Couldn’t you have approached me earlier, then?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">I was purposefully avoiding contacting you. You don’t need to get involved with me either, do you?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Let’s not be hasty, now,” Sakayanagi said. “I was just thinking that I needed to confirm whether you will stand in Ayanokouji-kun’s way in the future.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, I have to ask, what do you plan to do if I do get in his way?” </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">asked Ishigami.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I do not believe that Ayanokouji-kun will be defeated by anyone other than me, but I would still feel uncomfortable if someone were to butt in. If you do intend to intervene, then I might have no other choice but to stop you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“You, stop me? You should ignore me rather than try to do something as pointless as that. I chose this school because Ayanokouji-sensei recommended it to me. So that I could spend my time here as a normal student.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So, Ishigami had come to this school with similar intentions to my own. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s safe for you to assume that there’s no possibility of Ayanokouji being removed from this school at this time,”</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> he added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“‘At this time,’ hm? That choice of words bothers me,” said Sakayanagi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“It means that, in the unlikely event that Ayanokouji-sensei gives me instructions to eliminate him, I will do so. That’s all.”</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> Ishigami spoke in a calm manner throughout. I couldn’t imagine that he was lying about anything.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It sounds as though you have become quite loyal to him in no time at all,” Sakayanagi observed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t go any further with this, Sakayanagi. Especially if you wish to stay by Ayanokouji’s side.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The only thing that was clear was that Ishigami was warning her very strongly that she wouldn’t make it through that situation unscathed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not going to tell you to keep my identity hidden. Sooner or later, Ayanokouji will find out about me. So, you warn him for me. Ask him, what’s the best option to protect his life here at this school? Well, actually, I suppose if he’s listening in on this conversation now, then you won’t have to.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He likely had no proof, but he was taking into consideration the possibility that I was eavesdropping right now.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I will be sure to tell him if I feel so inclined,” said Sakayanagi. “I would be incredibly happy if you would permit me to say hello to you at school sometime.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakayanagi must have decided that was enough, because she went ahead and ended the call right there.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, it is him, after all,” she said. “Well, it sounds as if he wasn’t really trying to hide it in the first place.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s what it sounds like, yeah,” I said. “If he came to this school to enjoy student life to the fullest, then, for my part, I have no intention of getting involved with him in the future.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">At the very least, I hadn’t sensed any danger from Ishigami in the interactions that we’d had up until this point, that phone call included. Now that there was a possibility that my father hadn’t been trying to get me expelled in the first place, there was no need to panic.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. If that’s what you choose, Ayanokouji-kun, I will respect your wishes,” said Sakayanagi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m grateful to you. Thanks to you, I was able to find out about Ishigami,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well then, now that we have some degree of perspective on the matter, I wouldn’t want to trouble you much longer. However, as one last thing, may I continue from where we left off before, and finish what I was about to say earlier?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“About how the future might not always go the way I think, right?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was certainly true that I was curious about the way Sakayanagi had phrased that. However, just as we were about to continue the conversation, someone called my name at the worst time.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“A-Ayanokouji-kun!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was Amikura who was calling out to me. She walked quickly through the hallway toward me, seeming a little flustered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Um, have you seen Honami-chan?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, I haven’t. Is something the matter with Ichinose?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, it’s just, the school trip is already almost over, right? So, we were thinking that we’d try to get everyone in class together and chat until lights out, but we couldn’t find Honami-chan.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There must have been a fair number of people searching for her, because even as I was speaking with Amikura, other girls from Class D hurriedly walked up to her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“People have already checked the baths and her room,” said one of the girls.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I heard that she looked like she was really down this evening, like she had a lot on her mind, and…I’m a little worried,” said Amikura.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Then, a girl from her same class came up to the anxious Amikura to tell her the news. “Mako-chan, I just checked right now, and it looks like Honami-chan’s yukata is here. So, she might still be outside.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait, outside?” said Amikura. “But it’s already almost nine, right? Besides, the people in her group are inside, right?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">While we were allowed to be outside until nine o’clock at night, if you were going off and doing things on your own outside the ryokan, that would be a problem.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll check the big bathhouse and stuff one more time!” Amikura, not wanting to waste any more time standing around and talking, ended the conversation there and started walking. Ichinose’s absence at this hour was certainly worrying.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Let’s continue this conversation another time,” Sakayanagi said. “Please search for Ichinose-san. I’m sure that for you, Ayanokouji-kun, Ichinose-san is still someone indispensable, after all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After saying goodbye to Sakayanagi, I exited the lobby. We weren’t allowed to go out and do things on our own without the rest of our groups, and Ichinose was not the sort of student who would break the school’s established rules for no reason. Her fundamental stance on that likely wouldn’t change even if she were in some kind of trouble. When I looked outside from the hallway in the ryokan, I saw that snow was falling heavily. If she really was outside, then…there were only so many places she could go.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After returning to my room and putting on my casual attire, I slipped outside via the rear garden of the ryokan. There was an elevated deck just ahead that offered an illuminated view of the landscape. This was precisely one of the locations that got locked down at nine o’clock at night, which was our curfew. Technically, since the rear garden was within the confines of the ryokan proper, that meant that the area wasn’t subject to the group activity requirements, so you didn’t have to worry about being with your group there.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though the ground underfoot was well-lit, it was still dangerous because of the accumulated snow. Many students had come up here on the day we had arrived at the ryokan or on the second day. For that reason, few students were likely going to come back to check it out again in the cold, snowy weather. Not to mention the fact that it was our last day. I imagined that most would want to spend their time relaxing in the ryokan.</span></p>
