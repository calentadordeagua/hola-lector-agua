<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 5:<br/>School Trip Day Three</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HE BUS HAD DEPARTED</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> the ryokan at nine o’clock that morning. Just under fifty minutes later, it stopped near Sapporo Station, and thus we arrived at our destination, marking the beginning of our day. The Sapporo Clock Tower was nearby, and this area was lined with various other spots that were perfect for sightseeing. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We were supposed to go around in our individual groups yet again, but there was one difference from how things were yesterday: a little test implemented by the school. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Within a time limit, before 5 p.m., we were to visit a total of six spots, in any order, out of a predetermined list of fifteen destinations. For a group to have their visit recognized as official, they had to take a commemorative photograph with the entire group present at a designated photography location at each spot. We had to repeat that process six times. So, groups that allowed their members to split up in an effort to get more points, as well as groups comprised of students who didn’t bring along their phones and selfishly did their own thing, wouldn’t be able to complete the task.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">You would only fail this test if you visited fewer than six spots before the time limit was up. In that case, you would be deprived of your free time on the fourth day of the trip, and you’d attend a study hall which was going to be held at the ryokan until 4 p.m. on that day. Each spot was also assigned a point value. Groups that achieved a total of twenty points or more would be rewarded, with each individual member winning 30,000 Private Points. However, since scores didn’t have an impact on whether we actually passed the test, it was up to the groups to decide whether or not to shoot for the reward.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If the photo wasn’t clear, and you couldn’t identify the people in the shot, it wouldn’t be counted. Whether students wanted to go for the reward was another matter, but if students wanted to enjoy their free time tomorrow to the fullest, they needed to take this test seriously and cooperate with one another to visit the destinations. There were no restrictions on things like the number of times we could use public transportation, but we were prohibited from using taxis. It was also necessary for us to keep a record of how we got to each spot.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was sure that the students would have been happier to have received free time on our third day, but personally, I didn’t think walking around Hokkaido under the conditions set by the school was so bad. If the students were simply given free time to do whatever they wanted, the school trip would’ve likely ended with only limited sightseeing and skiing. I was genuinely looking forward to exploring Hokkaido within the restrictions the school had set. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Once we got off the bus, we were handed a pamphlet. This pamphlet, which was a unique one designed by our school, described the spots that we were to visit. Sapporo Clock Tower, Sapporo TV Tower, and the Hokkaido Museum of Modern Art were each worth one point. Nakajima Park and Hokkaido Jingu were worth two points. Sapporo Maruyama Zoo, the Hokkaido Museum, and the Sapporo Central Wholesale Market Curb Market were worth three points each. Moerenuma Park and Shiroi Koibito Park were both worth four points. Mount Moiwa was worth five points. Sunpiazza Aquarium was worth six points. Jozankei Onsen was worth seven points. And finally, Lake Shikotsu and Lake Utonai were both worth eight points.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was necessary to remember that just getting to a spot wasn’t enough. For instance, in the case of Sapporo Maruyama Zoo, we needed to enter the zoo and take a photograph with the polar bears, or take a picture with the polar bear pavilion in the background, to mark the location as complete.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This is kind of a surprise,” Kushida said to me after we got off the bus. “Well, I suppose it is fitting for this school, but…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">For some reason, though, she was gazing far off into the distance.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m over here,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, sorry. I had no idea.” There was no way that should’ve been possible, and she wasn’t even looking at me when she said that, either. Perhaps she herself was keenly aware of how unnatural it all was, because now she turned her head and smiled at me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’d really hurt to lose an entire day to studying if we don’t do this properly,” she said. “I guess the reason they let us have a whole day of free time yesterday without any restrictions probably has something to do with the sightseeing tour today.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You might be right about that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Now, the question was, what choices would our group—group six—make? The fact that we’d be going around sightseeing itself had been explained to us before we left for the school trip, but it was only on the bus on the way over that we had been told about how this was set up a little like an exam, with free time at stake and Private Points as a reward. Meaning, in other words, that we still hadn’t had the opportunity to establish what our group’s policy would be. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was conceivable that groups who aimed for the Private Point reward might not finish the test in time, so risks were unavoidable. While some groups seemed to stay put and discuss, most started walking, and in the same direction.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It looks like a lot of groups are heading to Sapporo Clock Tower first, since it’s just a stone’s throw from here,” observed Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We could consider focusing on places with high point values, like Lake Shikotsu and Lake Utonai, but that would be a high-risk strategy. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And because it’s more efficient to discuss while walking, too,” I added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Just like Kushida had said, the safest bet would be to go from Sapporo Station to the clock tower, and then, after taking a picture at the designated spot, heading over to the TV tower via Odori Park. You could hit two spots in a short amount of time, and without spending any money. However, it was unclear at the moment whether that would be the ideal scenario if you were shooting for twenty or more points. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">By now, all eight people from group six had finished getting off the bus.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I did a quick search on the map app just now,” Amikura said, “and even if we were able to use a taxi, it seems like it’d take several hours for us to hit six locations with high point values.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was sure that Amikura hadn’t taken things like the time needed to reach the designated photo spot for each of the locations into account in her calculations, either. Even if we were to make full use of public transportation, it would likely be impossible for us to hit only high-scoring locations within the time limit.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Anybody here familiar with Hokkaido?” Watanabe posed his question to the entirety of group six, but he didn’t get any favorable responses.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I, like the other students, had no knowledge of things like how to get around Hokkaido or the most efficient means to do so, which meant we’d have to do some research to find out the answer to those questions. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hrmm. Even if I try and make a route with the map app, I don’t really know where to go, so the order gets all jumbled up.” It seemed like Amikura was struggling with the map app, trying to enter the appropriate locations. Since the spots were scattered in all directions, to the east, west, south, and north of our current location, you would need to start by having an understanding of the relationship of the positions. On top of that, there was no guarantee that public transportation would necessarily be available at each spot, nor that the school hadn’t been mean-spirited, including some very difficult-to-reach spots in the list of places marked in our pamphlets.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, even if we </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">can</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> get Private Points, it’s just thirty thousand,” Watanabe said. “Since we’re here and have the chance to do some sightseeing, why don’t we just forget about the reward and enjoy ourselves?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Watanabe’s suggestion was one correct answer. If we focused only on scoring twenty points within the time limit, our enjoyment of our sightseeing tour would be cut in half. We wouldn’t have the time to take it easy, relax, and enjoy what each location had to offer.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Basically, I guess I’m firmly in the camp of ‘Let’s not push ourselves too hard on this,’” added Watanabe.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I agree. I think it’d be better if we just go to places we each actually want to go to. Like, I want to go to the zoo and stuff</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em; letter-spacing: 0.60mm;">,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">” said Amikura.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We students spent our day-to-day lives on campus, so we didn’t normally get the opportunity to visit zoos, aquariums, and the like. It was only natural not to want to waste the opportunity, since we were here.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Let’s hear everyone’s opinions on where they’d like to go and work from there.” Amikura suggested. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Six of us, me included, quickly agreed with the idea of giving up the points and instead focusing on leisurely hitting the minimum number of spots. However, this was a matter that needed to be discussed by everyone in the group to reach a decision. Kitou and Ryuuen were the only two remaining who hadn’t yet said if they agreed or disagreed with the idea.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Kitou, what do you think?” Watanabe turned to check with Kitou, who had been completely silent this whole time.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No objections,” said Kitou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After getting a favorable response upon asking Kitou, a wave of relief seemed to wash over Watanabe and the others—for the time being, anyway. That made seven. And the final person, Ryuuen… His answer…still had not come.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Um… Hey, uh…” Watanabe seemed to be struggling to ask, so I decided to speak up and get Ryuuen’s answer.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Me and everyone else here share the same opinion,” I said. “Can we take your silence to mean you also agree with us?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, Ryuuen had declared to me that he was going to save up eight hundred million points, so his answer was obvious.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We’re going for the points,” he said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was a simple response. Ryuuen was going to stand against the seven of us, in other words. Of course, we as individuals were each free to think about how we were going to visit these locations. Some groups were likely going to prioritize certain locations for the Private Points. However, additional discussion was going to be needed if opinions were split like this. Watanabe was even more frightened than before, so I followed up on my previous question and asked Ryuuen another.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Can I ask the reason why?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ain’t it obvious? Private Points. I can’t think of</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em; letter-spacing: 0.60mm;"> </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">’em as ‘just’ 30,000 points,” said Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">With two people from each class in the group, each class could get 60,000 Private Points. It might’ve sounded like nothing but dust if you compared that to eight hundred million, but the truth was it was still one steady step forward.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There ain’t no reason not to grab the points that are right in front of your face,” Ryuuen snarled. “So, just shut up and follow me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Although there was a risk of not visiting enough sightseeing locations in time, or not getting enough points due to mismanagement, fundamentally speaking, there weren’t any downsides to the idea of going for the reward; there were only positives. If you followed the&#160;rules and successfully hit enough locations, you would receive Private Points from the school. And it was&#160;also true that doing something that meant not getting points you could have earned would, in itself, be a loss. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, there was no way that Kitou was just going to stay silent and ignore Ryuuen’s heavy-handed behavior overruling the opinion of the seven of us.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re telling all of us to follow you, to satisfy you?” asked Kitou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. Got a problem with that?” said Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Your way of doing things ignores democracy,” Kitou said. “I think this is an issue that should be decided by majority vote.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Like I care,” Ryuuen snorted. “When the hell did this group become a democracy or whatever?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not convinced that you could be this fixated on such a paltry sum in the first place. In fact, there’s no way I can believe that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, what do </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">you</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> think it is, huh?” asked Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had lost count of how many times this sort of thing had happened by now. As Ryuuen and Kitou clashed, no one else could interject.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I can only think that you don’t like how the group came to an agreement, so you said something just to stir things up,” said Kitou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see,” Ryuuen said. “That might be true, yeah. I mean, seein’ that disgruntled look on your face ain’t bad.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If we let these two continue to talk on their own, we’d soon be heading into dangerous territory.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We need some Private Points to even use public transportation anyway,” Kitou pointed out. “If you subtract those from your winnings, you won’t be left with thirty thousand Private Points per person in the end. Even so, you still want to do it?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We didn’t know the precise cost at this point, but we would need to spend some money on transit.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even so, yeah. Hypothetically, even if the reward’s closer to twenty thou, I ain’t planning on givin’ it up.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I realized now that we were the only group still near the bus.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, Ayanokouji,” Ishizaki said. “You’re in a group together with Ryuuen-san, right? Stuff goin’ good?” </span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Despite what she said, Nishino had looked pretty brave to me back there. She talked back to those guys without fear while shielding Yamamura from their harassment. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re a pretty hotheaded chick yourself, though.” Ishizaki guffawed loudly with his cheeks stuffed with crab.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Gawd, shut up,” said Nishino. “Also, stop spraying food everywhere when you eat; it’s disgusting.”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Though Nishino was taking an argumentative tone with Ishizaki, she was engaging in conversation with him properly, each of them taking turns. I supposed they were classmates who knew one another really well, then. I could only go on what I’d seen of Nishino while we’d been in a group together, but though she didn’t talk very much, she didn’t seem to make trouble for others, and she did seem to have a kind side, as she cared for Yamamura in her own way.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was precisely because she could see the impending reality that loomed ahead, then, that she could only sigh when she thought about Ryuuen and Ishizaki’s futures. Selfishly doing whatever you wanted now would mean suffering for it later. That should be common sense throughout all of society, whether or not you were a delinquent. Putting aside the entertainment and creative industries, where talent was key, and the sports industry, where physical ability was most important, the higher your level of education, the better. The harder you&#160;worked in your studies, and the more knowledge you accumulated, the more likely you were to start from an easier position later in the working world.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When I had finished my meal and was about to leave, I happened to spot a particular young man: Katsuragi. I saw that he was eating alone at a table in the corner, silently scooping food into his mouth. I was curious about how he was doing, so I thought I’d observe him for a little while, but then I saw something strange. When Oda, from Ryuuen’s class, spotted Katsuragi and tried to go over and talk to him, Matoba from Class A candidly strolled up to talk to Oda, stopping him in his tracks. Then, after something Matoba said to him, Oda headed toward a different student while still keeping an eye on Katsuragi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was almost as though Matoba had done that deliberately, to keep Oda from making contact with Katsuragi. It&#160;wasn’t just once, either; this happened two or three times in a row. Matoba was a member of group two, along with Katsuragi. It wouldn’t have been surprising if Matoba had been sitting down at a table together with Katsuragi, but it was actually the opposite. It seemed that there were some people in Class A who did rather spiteful things. I would’ve been fine just leaving things be, but I decided to try making contact with Katsuragi at least once. Just then, Matoba, sensing that I was going to approach him, came up to me.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">I see.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> If Matoba said that it was a problem for group two, which they were both a part of, then even Katsuragi’s classmates would have no other choice but to back down. That was why Oda understood right away and left. So, was this the collective will of Class A at work, or was this an action taken by Matoba alone? And was there an agenda to defeat Ryuuen’s class behind this, or not? In any case, from a third party’s perspective, what Matoba was doing could only been seen as an act of malicious bullying. </span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t understand what that’s supposed to mean.”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Besides, I had been able to catch a glimpse of Ryuuen expressing his concern for Katsuragi in his own way, as he was unable to sit around and watch Katsuragi eat his meal all alone. <p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">5.2</span></span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">R</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">YUUEN AND I</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;"> </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">stayed with Katsuragi until he finished his meal, and then we left the dining area. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">By third wheel, he meant me, of course. Kushida put her palms together in an apology to me, and so I figured that I should get out of here. The two of them walked side by side over to an area where there weren’t any people around. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hm. I have to wonder about that, though,” he said. “You never know till you try, after all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">What we needed now wasn’t some half-hearted attempt at getting him to stop what he was doing with words, but rather, a complete shutdown of his actions.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you do decide that you’re going to expose her, no one can stop you,” I admitted. Kushida couldn’t hide her anxiety and humiliation at my words, but I lightly tapped her on the shoulder and told her not to worry. “But if you do that, you won’t be able to achieve your goal of fighting against Sakayanagi in the final exam.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What? How can you know that for sure?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Because I’ll deal with the situation in a way that you wouldn’t want.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In an instant, Ryuuen’s smile was replaced with a look of suspicion. It was just like the look he’d had when he had abducted Kei in the past, when he hadn’t known what fear was—or actually, perhaps even more suspicious than then. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hah,” he scoffed. “What’s this? Now, that’s a look you haven’t shown me in quite a long time, eh?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I moved in closer to Ryuuen, inserting myself between him and Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Come on. Even if I </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">do </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">choose to stay silent right here and now…there ain’t no guarantee that I won’t expose her later, right?” he said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ryuuen looked like he had fight in him, but eventually, he backed down, throwing his hands up. “I’ll let it go,” he conceded. “I didn’t even feel like attackin’ your class with the whole Kikyou thing in the first place anyway. Well, actually, I guess I should I say that I don’t feel like it </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">anymore</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do you mean?” asked Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If it ain’t somethin’ I can get Ayanokouji with, then it ain’t a weapon I can use.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh…?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I dunno if you know this, Kikyou, but yesterday, this guy over here told me somethin’. He said that he doesn’t want to expel you anymore. So, even if I attacked your class usin’ your story, it wouldn’t affect </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">you</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> anymore, would it, Ayanokouji?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s right,” I replied. “I’ve already thought of countermeasures.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It don’t make any sense to use a strategy that won’t work and only have it comin’ back to bite me in the ass. Right? I’ve learned the hard way that I ain’t gonna defeat ya with half-assed plans.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It wasn’t at all that Ryuuen was giving in and turning servile, of course; I was sure that he was going to come back and challenge Horikita’s class with some elaborate scheme that even I hadn’t considered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m headin’ back to my room now,” he said. “Bye, Kushida. Enjoy the rest of your time here at this school as much as ya can.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ryuuen headed back to the room, his attitude seemingly saying, </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Don’t try and stop me again.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> He’d even gone from calling her Kikyou to calling her Kushida. Was that the flip side of what happened when Ryuuen completely lost interest in someone? </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Only Kushida and I remained, standing there in silence.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why…did you come to my rescue?” said Kushida. “There’s no benefit in it for you, Ayanokouji-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There is a benefit for me, though,” I argued. “It’s that you are an indispensable asset to the class. I don’t think that Ryuuen would have actually gone through with exposing you, even if I hadn’t come, but I wasn’t sure what you were going to do. You were probably wondering if there was anything you could do to get him to keep his mouth shut, right?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Well, yes, I…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ryuuen isn’t an opponent that you can compete with. You would’ve found yourself in trouble if you rushed into a battle that you didn’t start and got yourself blown up. That’s why I decided to say something.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you’re saying that you could do something, then, Ayanokouji-kun? Well, yes… The truth is that it seems like you did.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“At the very least, I think that at this point in time, Ryuuen isn’t a big enough deal for me to see him as a formidable opponent.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“H-huh? What is that supposed to…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Anyway, you don’t need to cross that dangerous bridge anymore. Just take care of yourself for now.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“When you say something like that, it really makes me uncomfortable. You need my help with the class that badly?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s that too, yes.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That ‘too’?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I feel like I can work together well with you, Kushida, now that we speak frankly with each other,” I explained.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Being able to see her hidden side had also made it easier for me to infer what she was thinking.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Just stop,” she protested. “There’s no way that anyone could seriously think something like that after finding out what I’m really like.” She herself was painfully aware that she had a personality that wasn’t well-liked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s not true,” I replied. “To be honest, I like you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What is… I don’t know how serious you’re being about this. I can’t trust you, Ayanokouji-kun.” Normally Kushida would’ve thought it sounded like a joke and laughed at me, but right now, her expression was stiff.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s the truth. There are some people out there in the world who are more comfortable with the real you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s—” Kushida looked at me like she was about to say something, her mouth open. But she stopped moving. Then, she suddenly started walking toward the wall.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…What?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Suddenly, she thrust out both arms in front of her, hands open wide, pushing the wall as hard as she could with both palms. “I’m okay, I’m okay…” she mumbled to herself, her movements coming to a complete stop.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I watched her, wondering what was going on. After Kushida caught her breath, she turned back around to look at me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I just got a little dizzy! But I’m okay-A! I mean, A! Okay!” she sputtered. Despite her attempts to tell me I didn’t need to worry about her, her voice shot up in volume in a very strange way.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Are you really okay?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida showed me the face she usually wore out in public, though she really didn’t appear to be okay to me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. I’m fine!” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“O-okay.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In Kushida’s case, it was honestly very difficult to read her emotions.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I kind of feel like I’ve been saved by you, Ayanokouji-kun… Thanks,” she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I feel like you’ve been thanking me more these days, Kushida,” I remarked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, you might be right about that… Anyway, I won’t be getting involved with Ryuuen-kun anymore from now on.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s good.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay, I’m going to head back to my room. See you tomorrow.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, tomorrow.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida walked down the hallway. From the look on her face, she seemed like she made a complete recovery. However, she stumbled while on her way down the hall, and she took a massive spill, causing one of her wooden sandals to go flying in spectacular fashion.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you okay?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Fine! Perfectly! Fine!” She gestured to me not to come over with a wave of her hand, then staggered back to her feet and put her wooden sandal back on.</span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">5.3</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">S</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">INCE I HAD A MEETING</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> with Horikita, I waited in the hallway to the guest rooms with my back against the wall.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry for being a little late.” Though Horikita apologized as soon as she appeared, she hadn’t actually been especially late, so there wasn’t really an issue. “Getting straight down to business, let’s—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you really plan on having a long conversation out here?” I asked, interrupting her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We were near the guest accommodations, so students were constantly coming and going from their rooms. This was one of the least appropriate places to talk about things you didn’t want to be overheard.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, this definitely isn’t a good place to talk,” Horikita agreed. “Let’s see, somewhere appropriate. Let’s head over to the vending machines to get a drink. We can talk while we walk, so that’s perfect, no?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I figured that was fine, and didn’t really have any objections, so I agreed. You were more or less guaranteed to attract attention when you stood around and talked. But if you chatted while walking, you didn’t have to worry about it. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“They sell fruit milk in the vending machines out in front of the large bathhouse,” I suggested. “It’s delicious.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had been told that it was the thing to drink after taking a dip in the bath, and after trying it for myself, I really felt like it was true.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thank you for your childish commentary,” said Horikita. “But that’s not something you drink in the middle of the night, is it?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Was time an issue? Well, actually, I supposed that maybe fruit milk just wasn’t that special from a girl’s point of a view.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even so, the vending machines at the main bath are some distance away, so sure, let’s walk over that way,” she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She began to walk slowly, showing me that she was prioritizing talking for the time being.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“About what happened at the Cultural Festival,” she began. “I’ve been wanting to talk to you about that, but I haven’t had the opportunity to. It’s been bothering me for a long time now, and I couldn’t find a suitable time to bring it up until today.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You must have been extremely exhausted back then,” I said. “You let me see your sleeping face, completely unprotected.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Do you want to get kicked?” said Horikita. She got into a stance that demonstrated that she was more than willing to do it, and I immediately raised the white flag and surrendered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Forgive me,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ugh, talk about negligence on my part,” she sighed. “A&#160;blunder. To think, I let a boy see me sleeping. You have sullied me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you really that bothered by it?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It is something to be bothered about… But it doesn’t matter right now. I want to ask you about something that happened that day.” Horikita tried to brush her embarrassment aside with a wave of her hand, and her&#160;expression turned serious. “That incident that took place in the student council office that day. You had something to do with it, didn’t you?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">What happened at the Cultural Festival that day in the student council office… There was only one thing she could be referring to.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Did you get Yagami-kun expelled?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why do you think I had something to do with it?” I wasn’t exactly dodging the question, but I was interested in hearing the reason she had concluded that.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t know whether you knew this or not, but it’s possible that Yagami-kun was trying to get you expelled,” she said. “In fact, the things he said and did in the student council office support that idea.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It sounded like Horikita had come by several pieces of information in her own way, without my knowledge. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I didn’t know about Yagami, no,” I replied, “but it doesn’t sound like it should come as that much of a surprise. You already knew firsthand that Housen tried to get me expelled, after all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“For the twenty million Private Point reward, yes,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, Yagami was participating in that test too, and keeping an eye out for an opportunity. Right?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I considered that, too. But there are too many things that seem unnatural. More than anything else, I didn’t get the impression that he was trying to get to you in order to obtain money.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">From the sounds of it, as someone who was present at the scene, Horikita understood the situation in more detail.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There are lots of questions that I’ve been curious about the answers to,” she added. “But those aren’t what I want to know most.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Then, what do you want to know?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Your true identity. I can’t possibly imagine that you’re a normal student, like everyone else.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, that’s a rather troubling question. If I’m not normal, then what kind of student am I?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…I don’t know. It’s not about whether you’re exceptionally talented. It’s just, I can’t even begin to imagine what sort of person you are. It’s impossible for me to decipher.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So, she wanted to know the answer to the question of who the person called Ayanokouji Kiyotaka was, hm?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s really nothing all that special to say about me,” I told her. “Honestly, I just don’t have anything to even talk about.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, if I were to ask you questions, would you give me answers?” she said. “Where you’re from, what elementary school you graduated from, your junior high school. Have you participated in any competitions or anything like that in the past? Did you study on your own? Did&#160;you go to cram school, or did you have a private tutor?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I bet that people didn’t even ask you such detailed questions when you went to a formal marriage interview.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I understand what you’re trying to get at here, but I don’t feel like responding to so many tedious questions,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita pursed her lips in a blatant show of dissatisfaction.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Which is why I’m going to disclose some information,” I added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…What kind of information?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“For example… Ah, okay, I’ve got it. Things like how I was involved in what happened with Yagami, just as you guessed.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re not joking, are you? So, was it because Yagami-kun was trying to get you expelled?” </span></p>

President Nagumo, Ryuuen, and the others being there in the student council room—I had orchestrated all of that. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Up until this point, there would have been no point in me telling Horikita all of this. However, by giving her this data, I could indirectly show her what kind of person I was. I did so to create the possibility that she could make use of this information, when the time eventually came for us to face one another.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s no connection between President Nagumo and Ryuuen,” I added. “I talked to them separately, that was all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, that feeling I had back then, that something was off… I think I understand what that was coming from,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“By the way,” I said, “we’re almost at our destination.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We had arrived at the rest area on the second floor of the large bathhouse where the vending machines were located. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was only for a brief instant, but the teachers exchanged sharp, angry looks, sparks flying.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Anyway, Horikita-san, it really seems like you’ve become a true, proper leader,” Hoshinomiya-sensei said. “Are you feeling comfortable in Class B now? I just wanted to ask, speaking as the teacher of the former Class B.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Not particularly, no,” Horikita replied. “What I’m aiming for is Class A. Where I’m at now is nothing more than a checkpoint.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That goes without saying, yes,” replied Hoshinomiya-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Disregarding their conversation, I reached for the remote control that connected to Chabashira-sensei’s massage chair. It seemed like there were five levels of intensity, and right now, it was set on level three. Obviously, the stronger the intensity, the more effective it would be. For some reason, I found myself feeling curious about how powerful the fifth level would be, so I decided to test it out.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Mm! Eeek! Mmmph?!” Chabashira-sensei practically leapt out of the seat in surprise, and the machine started to let out loud rattling sounds. I had thought it would only be about a 40 percent increase in functionality, but it might’ve been more than that.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“A-Ayanokouji, wh-what are you, mm, doing?! T-turn it back!” she shouted.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She reached for the remote, clearly flustered, and forcefully tugged on the cord it was connected to, causing it to fall out of my hands.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“U-ughh! Eek! Ah… H-hurry, get it!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, please don’t yank it out of my hands,” I said. I picked up the remote control and turned it back down from level five to level three.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huff, huff…h-huff… What the hell did you think you were doing…?!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well… I was just curious, I suppose. I figured that the stronger the intensity, the better.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course not!” she snapped. “The right level of intensity depends on the person!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She looked really angry now, her face bright red, more demonic than I had ever seen her before. Apparently, that had been much more stimulating than I had expected it would be.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What are you doing?” asked Horikita, exasperated. Apparently, my loud exchange with Chabashira-sensei had attracted her notice. “Sorry for interrupting your break,” she said to the teachers. “Let’s go, Ayanokouji-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you two going into the baths?” Hoshinomiya-sensei asked. “You can’t go in together, you know.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita ignored the nonsense the teacher was talking and motioned that she was going to keep walking.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait, Horikita-san.” Hoshinomiya had been joking around just moments ago, but all of a sudden she had a serious look on her face.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I certainly do think that your class is growing at a remarkable rate, Horikita-san,” she said. “And you’re right, Class B is a checkpoint that you need to reach in getting to Class A. That much is obvious, but still, I also think what you’ve done is wonderful, and very admirable, too.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It sounded like she was praising Horikita, but her words were loaded with meaning.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Chie, don’t say anything unnecessary,” snapped Chabashira-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Come on, what’s the harm?” Hoshinomiya-sensei said. “I’m just trying to tell her what I think, is all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t know what you’re trying to say, but it’s not like we can freely say whatever we think,” said Chabashira-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Please tell me.” Horikita cut in, urging her to speak. She must have been curious about what Hoshinomiya-sensei was going to say next.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“All right, in that case, I’ll continue,” said Hoshinomiya-sensei. “So, yes, speaking as a homeroom instructor in charge of a class, I do have a lot of thoughts. The teachers too, from Class A to Class D, are also competing against one another like you are. If I were to use an analogy here, you could say that it’s like we teachers are playing the card game daifugou against each other.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Playing…daifugou?” echoed Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you know the rules?” Hoshinomiya-sensei asked her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, kind of.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that sense, we use the cards we’re dealt to fight and decide who comes in first through fourth place, and in this school, we spread that fight over three years,” Hoshinomiya-sensei explained. “In daifugou, you use cards numbered one through thirteen… Putting aside regional differences in rules or special rulesets for the time being, essentially, cards with higher numbers are stronger, and cards with lower numbers are weaker, right? So, if a&#160;student who only has a value of three clashes against a student with a value of six, the student with six will win, of course. Mashima-kun’s Class A, for example, would tend to have a certain range of cards. He’d be dealt more tens and elevens. On the other hand, going further down, like to Class D, you’ll see more lower-value cards, like threes and fours. Well, that’s the way it has historically been at this school, anyway.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hoshinomiya-sensei reached for the remote control for her massage chair and raised the intensity one level to level three. I was reminded again of just how intense level five was.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course, students are changing every day,” she continued. “I think that there are cases where kids who were threes or fours grow into twelves or thirteens, or in rare instances, become the most powerful number, twos. So, class fluctuations do happen, and sometimes, Class&#160;D can become Class B. Of course, that is extremely rare, though.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That meant that what Horikita’s class had accomplished was unprecedented.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But what’s important is that everyone is fighting on equal footing,” said Hoshinomiya-sensei. “Namely, that every class is always fighting within the same boundaries, with numbers one through thirteen. We can’t have any unfairness or misalignments in a particular class, can we?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, you’re right,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But don’t you think that one card that shouldn’t have been added to the deck was mixed into your class, Horikita-san?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“A card that…shouldn’t have been added?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hoshinomiya-sensei was smiling, but she was directing her gaze toward me. “That’s right. It’s unfair. Sae-chan’s class is the only one that has the Joker.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita also noticed Hoshinomiya-sensei looking at me, as though her gaze was naming me aloud.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Chie. Cut it out already,” snapped Chabashira-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, I’m entitled to grumble a bit,” Hoshinomiya-sensei complained. “Even if we fight smart, fight desperately hard, a single Joker can turn the whole situation upside down. Actually, wait. It’s much, much worse than just playing daifugō. In cards, after you play the Joker once, it goes away. In this case, you can keep playing it repeatedly. There’s no way we can win.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">What she had just said, as a homeroom teacher, could have been interpreted as her declaring that her own class had already lost.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Putting aside whether what you’ve said is right or wrong, what would you do if a student from Class D heard what you just said?” asked Chabashira-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">An admission of defeat. If students from Ichinose’s class heard it, they’d inevitably be shocked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Yes, you’re right. Sorry about that. The massage chair’s probably just making the alcohol hit me harder.” So saying, Hoshinomiya-sensei switched the power off. “You got the Joker because of luck, Sae-chan, Horikita-san. If you use that to get into Class A, well, that’s just plain unfair, isn’t it?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It would be obvious to anyone that it was meant as a snide remark.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s enough, Chie.” Chabashira-sensei had raised her voice, coming awfully close to sounding threatening. I’d never heard that tone of voice from her before. Hoshinomiya-sensei must have sobered in an instant, because she hurriedly jumped out of the chair.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Welp, I’m heading back to my room! Farewell!” she exclaimed. Clearly slightly peeved, she waved to us and walked down the hall with long, exaggerated strides.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sorry about all that.” Chabashira-sensei got up from her own massage chair and spoke on her colleague’s behalf. “I know she already said as much herself, but it was likely just the alcohol talking.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t mind. I just disregarded it as nonsensical drunken ramblings,” said Horikita flatly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In response to Horikita’s sudden, biting comment, Chabashira-sensei was a little shaken, and coughed. “You’re pretty harsh,” she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It seemed like you were a little concerned about what she was saying before, sensei,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s not as though I don’t have any thoughts on the matter, to be honest,” Chabashira-sensei admitted. “Things are vastly different now than they were with the class I oversaw three years ago.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was certainly true that there were powerful cards in Horikita’s class.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t know whether Ayanokouji-kun is a Joker or not,” Horikita said, “but I won’t deny that he is a powerful classmate. However, I’m not going to hold anything back because of that.” She didn’t even so much as look at me as she offered her thoughts to Chabashira-sensei. “I will fight with everything I have, using the cards that have been dealt to me in your class, Chabashira-sensei. Because I am aiming for Class A.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. Of course, that’s the goal,” said Chabashira-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, I was sure that Chabashira-sensei herself likely thought that Horikita wasn’t sufficiently prepared for it. Class A, led by Sakayanagi, also had a lot of great cards. Even if you won one game, there was no telling what would happen in the next ten or twenty.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well… I’m going to go after Chie,” Chabashira-sensei said. “If I leave her alone, I’m afraid she might drink herself silly until morning.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">With that, Chabashira-sensei set out after Hoshinomiya-sensei. It sounded like she couldn’t abandon a friend and former classmate.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I turned to Horikita. “Well, guess that’s it for today, Horikita.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s still a mountain of questions I want to ask you, though, Joker-san,” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Since we’ve come all this way, I want to hop in the bath again,” I said. “Besides, there are more people here now.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was starting to catch glimpses of students who wanted to enjoy a bath before going to bed, as they trickled in.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, can I assume this means that you’ll talk to me later?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I nodded in response, and then went through the curtain leading to the men’s bath.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">E</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">LEVEN P.M</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> was drawing near, and it was about time for lights out. Kitou stood up without a word, heading out into the hall carrying several magazines that he had borrowed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“He’s been reading in the room almost this whole time, huh?” I mused.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He must’ve liked reading. Unlike me or Hiyori, though, Kitou didn’t look to be the type to read books from the library. When he returned a few minutes later, he had a new magazine in his hand, maybe so that he could read it as soon as he woke up in the morning. The magazines that Kitou read strongly reflected his personal tastes, and most of them were what you’d call fashion magazines.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Would you let me read that for a sec?” I asked him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was expecting him to tell me to get one myself, but Kitou silently placed the magazine on the table. I supposed I could take that to mean it was okay for me to read it if I wanted. I decided to give the magazine a brief read, for the just over ten minutes we had until lights out. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It featured things like trendy clothing and accessories. To be honest, I couldn’t really understand the meaning of the pictures in the magazine, or the articles, either. However, I could tell that Kitou had a strong emotional attachment to these magazines. Kitou’s outfits, which to me seemed like eccentric fashions, were filled with his own sense of style and his feelings. It would have been interesting if Ryuuen, who often quarreled with Kitou, said that his interest in fashion was stupid; however, Ryuuen hadn’t made any such heckling remarks.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Soon, it was time for lights out, so we shut them off and lay down in bed. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I quietly stared up at the ceiling for a while, my vision gradually adjusting to the darkness. It didn’t seem like everyone was asleep yet, and I wondered what they were thinking about. Just as I did, Watanabe spoke up.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You know, we’re going to be third-years in just half a year,” he said. “Even while we’re competing to get into Class A, we’ve still got to think about our future, like if we’re going to college, going into the workforce, all that stuff. I still can’t imagine what I’m gonna be after graduating high school. And there’s nothing in particular that I really want to do. What about you, Ayanokouji?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Higher education…I suppose,” I answered. I figured I’d say the goal that sounded the safest. “Though I haven’t decided on what specific university.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What about you, Kitou?” asked Watanabe. He probably wasn’t confident that he’d be able to get an answer from Kitou, but he asked outright anyway, not afraid.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…I’m going to be a fashion designer,” said Kitou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh?!” Watanabe was doubly surprised, as not only did he not think he would get an answer, he was also shocked by the answer he got.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sure you’re surprised,” Kitou said. “I can understand that. I don’t think anyone would expect it, judging from my appearance.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“W-well, no, it’s… It kinda hurts to admit it, but I suppose not, no…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, it was easy to understand once you considered Kitou’s personal fashion sense and the contents of the magazines he often read.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Heh heh. I think if you said that you were gonna become a killer for hire, Watanabe’d find that easier to swallow,” said Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was worried that Ryuuen butting in with that comment might make Kitou angry, but I didn’t see him move.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“D-don’t worry about it, Kitou. I mean, Ryuuen’s always sayin’ stuff like that.” Watanabe tried to smooth things over, but Kitou really didn’t seem like he cared.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m used to it,” he said. “Most people are surprised when I tell them about my dreams. They find them hard to swallow. Even if I’m heading straight ahead on that path, I can’t imagine that people will accept me readily.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There should be no such thing as prejudice, but it certainly did exist in this world. For someone who looked as tough and frightening as Kitou, I was sure that the hurdle to some professions would naturally be quite high. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But that won’t matter if I graduate from Class A,” Kitou went on. “I can still leap into that world, no question about it. Once I jump in, all I have to do is silence everyone around me with my skill.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It sounded like, for Kitou, just getting his foot in the door, achieving that first part, was the most challenging task.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wow, you’re really giving a lot of thought to the future… I mean, that’s awesome. You’ve got a real dream,” said Watanabe. He was surprised, but he was impressed by how much thought Kitou had given his future compared to himself, and he offered him genuine praise. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Children grow older whether they like it or not, and they are faced with the necessity of entering society. It was the same even for Watanabe, who didn’t have a goal right now, and for Ryuuen, who wasn’t talking about his.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You know, I just kind of asked without thinking, and…now that I know, it kind of makes things harder,” mumbled Watanabe, forcing a laugh as he looked up at the ceiling. “I mean, all of us here, we’re in different classes, right? So, if you think about it, only one of the four of us can graduate from Class A. I bet everyone here’s got a dream that they want to achieve, but, like, if you take that spot in Class A, that means someone else isn’t getting it… It’s complicated.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If you were classmates, you could share dreams. But if you were rivals, you couldn’t. That was how this school worked. Some students would be smiling, while others would weep. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wondered if spending the night together with students your age normally led to this kind of conversation. It reminded me of the night that I’d spent talking with Keisei and the other guys at camp last year.</span></p>
