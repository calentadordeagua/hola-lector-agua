<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 4:<br/>School Trip Day Two</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">T WAS NOW THE MORNING</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> of the second day of our school trip. After we finished eating breakfast and getting dressed, we killed time in our room relaxing, waiting until the bus bound for the ski resort was set to depart. Watanabe and I were innocently watching TV. On the other side of the television screen, celebrities were reading off summaries of the morning news and providing their own innocuous, tepid commentary. After a while, the vibe of the programming suddenly changed rather dramatically as they entered a segment of the program that was a special feature on kittens.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Meanwhile, Ryuuen was in the same room, sitting in the armchair as though it was his own personal seat, while Kitou was staring fixatedly at a magazine, reading one by one through a stack of them that were available to borrow for free from the ryokan. They all appeared to be fashion magazines.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“He looks so unsettling like that. It’s hard to imagine that he’s just reading… It feels like he’s studying a manual on how to murder people,” Watanabe whispered into my ear. He had spoken in a quiet murmur so as not to be overheard, but in an instant, Kitou shot him a penetrating glare. That must have terrified Watanabe, because he averted his gaze, hiding in my shadow.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I mean, he’s definitely done </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">that</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> to somebody, don’t you think?” he squeaked, terrified.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I felt my shoulders being shaken, but I wanted to concentrate on this kitten segment on TV, if possible.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, Kitou. Things ain’t clear for you either yet, right? With how yesterday’s pillow fight turned out? Have a throwdown with me today.” As though he were attempting to call forth a storm to raze this peaceful morning, Ryuuen approached Kitou and proposed they fight.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Needless to say, this was not a welcome development for me and Watanabe.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Fool,” said Kitou. “Do you intend to jump straight into the jaws of death yourself? If you wish to feel regret, then I shan’t stop you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Heh heh. In that case, do yer worst,” said Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What kind of competition do you want?” asked Kitou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, ain’t that obvious? We’re gonna go skiin’ soon, so that’ll be it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">From the sounds of it, Ryuuen was proposing a simple time trial, to see who could finish faster. Kitou might not have been a beginner, but it was clear based on what I’d seen yesterday that Ryuuen was highly skilled, at the very least. There was no need for Kitou to willingly go along with a proposal that would essentially drag him onto Ryuuen’s playing field. However, Kitou forcefully shut his magazine, the look on his face unchanging.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You think you can beat me in skiing?” he said. “I will crush your hubris.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Apparently, he was going to accept the challenge, not showing any sign of running from it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 0.83em;">“Hey, you guys shouldn’t fight, y’know? Hey, you two listening?”</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> said Watanabe in a tiny voice.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m pretty sure they didn’t hear your words of warning,” I remarked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Watanabe had spoken so incredibly quietly that if a child heard him just now, they would probably have said something like, “Wow! An ant is talking!” I had been sitting next to him and had only just barely, ever-so-slightly managed to make out what he said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m just picturin’ you lookin’ pathetic, crawlin’ down the slopes on all fours,” sneered Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Laughable,” Kitou scoffed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As Ryuuen and Kitou traded verbal jabs, I could tell that they were both heating up. Kitou stood, rolled up the magazine that he had borrowed, and drew closer to Ryuuen while holding the magazine out as if it were the blade of a sword.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“When you lose, you’re going to be as quiet as a little kitten for the rest of this trip,” said Kitou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wondered if Kitou had unknowingly been influenced by the segment on cats on the TV, but that’s what he was demanding from Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? Well, if ya ask me, I’ve been plenty quiet already,” said Ryuuen, batting away the magazine forcefully with a </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">slap</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Can’t you just leave it at that for the time being, please? I’d like to watch this segment on cats,” I said, urging the two of them to keep their distance from one another and not to fight.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“W-wow, you’ve sure got guts, Ayanokouji. They might turn around and make you their target, though, don’tcha think?” said Watanabe, worried.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nah, I don’t think so. There’s no benefit to either of them in going up against someone like me,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Besides, the general Ryuuen-versus-Kitou schema was going to remain unchanged, unless someone made a big show of butting in.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Anyway, now that you two have calmed down, I can keep watching this special on—” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Before I could even finish that sentence, the cats had already disappeared from the TV. I guessed that that these things called special features didn’t last for very long, because it was over in just a few minutes.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry, that’s too bad, Ayanokouji,” Watanabe said. “You like cats?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nah, not really,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait, you don’t?!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had just wanted to watch, more or less. I didn’t hold any particular fondness for the animal known as the cat. I would likely have felt the same way if it had been a special feature on dogs or even hippopotamuses. The people on the program had been talking about topics cheerfully, in a friendly fashion, for quite a while, but then there was a news flash.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“Now for some breaking news. Former Secretary-General Naoe, who had been undergoing medical treatment for some time, has passed away at a hospital in the Tokyo metropolitan area. Here is a comment from Prime Minister Kijima’s office—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There were flashes of lights going off, and at the same time, a very stern-faced man was speaking.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“‘Judge a man by living with him, and try a horse by riding him.’ Those are words Naoe-sensei said to me shortly after we met.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Just when the Prime Minster began speaking about the deceased, the screen suddenly went black.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Time to get on the bus,” said Kitou, remote control in his hand, his finger on the off button.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Awright, Ayanokouji, let’s get this show on the road,” said Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was a little concerned about their contest, but I figured I’d still try and enjoy skiing in my own way.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"></div>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">4.1</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">W</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HEN WE HEADED OUTSIDE</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> we found a slight problem waiting for us. Apparently, the bus had gotten stuck in traffic and would be arriving about ten minutes late. There were a lot of students waiting for the bus heading to the ski resort, but if you looked back, you’d see that people were flooding the entrance, trying to head back inside.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s cold, but I guess it’s safer waiting outside,” said Watanabe.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He let out a deep sigh, exhaling a cloud of white, as he looked gloomily up at the sky. It was unfortunate that we’d gotten outside a little earlier than the other students, but there was nothing we could do about that now. Even if we did try and head back to our room, we couldn’t exactly relax with only five minutes to spare. We—that&#160;is, group six—decided to wait outside the building, just under the eaves.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, since we’re waiting out here anyway, why don’t we try making a snowman?” asked Amikura, suggesting to the rest of the group that we could spend the time efficiently.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That sounds like fun,” Kushida said. “Nishino-san, Yamamura-san, would you like to join in?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Sure, I guess,” replied Nishino.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had expected her to refuse the offer, but surprisingly enough, she seemed okay with it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And what about you, Yamamura-san?” asked Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, thank you… I’ll pass,” said Yamamura. She’d turned down the offer like I thought she would, albeit somewhat modestly. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The other three girls moved to an out-of-the-way spot and started scooping up fallen snow. From the looks of things, they weren’t planning to make a small snowman. They were hoping to make a fairly large one.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, Ryuuen-kun, why don’t you come over and make a snowman together with us?” Kushida called. “I think it’d be fun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida had to know that there was absolutely no way Ryuuen would take her up on that offer, but she invited him anyway to show off the good-natured heart of her public-facing persona. Even the other students nearby were watching the situation with concern, perhaps because they couldn’t imagine Ryuuen working hard to build a snowman. What Kushida said just now was almost certainly payback for yesterday. It was a show of confidence, telling him that if he said something careless, she wouldn’t back down, she’d fight.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I figured if I tried to rein her in a bit, that’d make her sit there quietly, but I guess I had the wrong idea, huh?” Ryuuen muttered quietly to himself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was certainly true that if this had happened before her classmates had learned the truth about her, Kushida might just have sat there and put up with whatever he said. Ryuuen must have been able to feel that there was something strange about that, but there was no way I could unravel this mystery for him. I couldn’t give out information the other classes didn’t know, like what happened in our class during the Unanimous Special Exam.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Needless to say, there was no way Ryuuen would accept Kushida’s invitation. He didn’t even react to the snowman, instead just looking far off into the distance. Meanwhile, though, a certain someone continued to gaze quietly at the snowman as it was being constructed. That certain someone was none other than Yamamura, who had been distancing herself from us bit by bit without being noticed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Phew…” Yamamura exhaled deeply into her cold hands while she observed Kushida and the others making the snowman. “Phew…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida and the rest building the snowman were, naturally, wearing warm-looking gloves. Even after taking a glance around the surrounding area, I noticed that none of the students outside except for Yamamura were going around without gloves. Well, that made sense. In this wintry weather, no one would go bare-handed for a long period of time unless they had a special reason. I remembered that Yamamura had been wearing gloves before, during yesterday’s skiing lessons. Even if she could rent gloves for skiing once we got there, why wouldn’t she bring gloves along to wear on the way?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If she simply forgot, it’d be fine to just go back and get them, so maybe there was some kind of reason for it. She looked dazed, staring out into space while exhaling deeply over and over. Although I was curious about Yamamura, more and more students started coming outside to wait for the bus. Then, I heard a familiar voice.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s like everything is covered in a blanket of snow.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The owner of that familiar-sounding voice was none other than Sakayanagi Arisu. She was in group four. If&#160;I recalled, Hondou and Onodera from Horikita’s class should also be in that group… And almost as if they’d been summoned, those two showed up just as that thought crossed my mind. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sakayanagi and the rest of her group all gathered without really interacting with any of the members of group six. Since Sakayanagi couldn’t ski, she was probably going to do some sightseeing.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Not too long afterward, the bus bound for the city arrived, although the bus headed to the ski resort was still on its way. The teacher leading the students instructed them to board, and the students began to get onto the bus one by one. Cane in hand, Sakayanagi walked alone on the snow-covered path, which she wasn’t accustomed to. As I watched her, I felt a little uncertain, like it was risky… And my prediction proved to be on the mark, because Sakayanagi slipped and fell, landing gently on her backside. Fortunately, the gentle cushion of snow protected her from the impact, and she didn’t seem to be hurt.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you okay…?!” Tokitou from Class C, who was also part of group four, had been walking a little behind Sakayanagi when she fell, and he rushed over to her. He seemed to hesitate for a moment over what to do, but then he offered her his hand.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was beginning to see that this combination of factors had resulted in the creation of the student known as Yamamura, who was lacking in presence and difficult to stay aware of. </span></p>

I’ve only got about a year left at this school, and it’s gettin’ a little late for me to make a move.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was certainly true that the cooperation of his classmates would be essential if he was serious about raising the viability of that strategy. Just like Ichinose had gradually pooled Private Points from her classmates bit by bit, with their trust, Ryuuen also needed to work with his classmates if he was going to reach that target. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“When you say that you want to double-check something, are you asking me if I’ll help you with getting to eight hundred million points?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You realize I’ve been showin’ your class a whole lotta kindness, in my own way, right? For a while now, too, like with the Sports Festival and the Cultural Festival. And I’m even takin’ Sakayanagi on in the final exam. I got that all taken care of for you. You ain’t dissatisfied with that, are ya?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Indeed, since we had that discussion with Ryuuen last year, Horikita’s class had been able to move about so freely that they had almost forgotten that Ryuuen was even here. If Ryuuen had remained as belligerent as he had been in our first year, things would not have gone as smoothly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And you sure seem to be gettin’ along good with Kushida, huh?” Ryuuen added. “Even though you were so enthusiastic about gettin’ her expelled.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry,” I said simply. “Sometimes you have to change your policies.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Perhaps Ryuuen really liked what I’d said, or maybe it just resonated with him, because he laughed and clapped several times.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If I wanted to, I could destroy Kushida, no problem,” he said. “You know that, right?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ryuuen was one of the few students outside of our class who knew the truth about Kushida. He could have done something anytime, but he didn’t, which was likely the result of that promise.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you’re asking me to fulfill that promise,” I said. “And you’re going as far as using threats. Aren’t you being pushy?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t give a damn if I’m bein’ pushy. So, are you gonna do it? Or not?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We had only made a verbal agreement back then, but Ryuuen was making it clear that he’d show me no mercy if I violated it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Before I give you my answer on that, let me ask you, what happens after you defeat Sakayanagi?” I inquired.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Isn’t it obvious? After I defeat Class A in the finals, it’ll be a one-on-one battle with my class against yours. The way I see it, it’s all part of the story I got laid out, leading up to where I beat you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So, that’s what he was thinking, huh? Looking at everything so far, though, I didn’t really have any doubts.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This sounds a little too convenient,” I said. “You stepped off the stage once before—you were supposed to have taken on the role of laying the groundwork for Kaneda and Hiyori. But now, you’re back at center stage. The point is, if you wanted me to fulfill my promise to you, you were supposed to back off. If we get to a situation where we become Class A and you become Class B, Ryuuen, then I’d be inevitably handing the win over to you if I helped you. Wouldn’t I?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Only then could we create a situation where we could talk about working together toward the eight hundred million points.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You don’t like it?” said Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Obviously not. If your class and Horikita’s class seriously do clash, and as a result you win and move up to Class A, then we’ll be the only ones to look like fools. Or are you saying that you promise to bring the students from Horikita’s class into Class A if your eight hundred million points plan works?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ryuuen’s smile vanished and he shot me a sharp side-eyed look. “Absolutely not. The extra Private Points belong to me and my people, obviously.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Those points were currency that could live on even after graduation as money, so he had no intention of using them for saving students he had no business with.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, if you lose, we bail you out, and if you win, you abandon us… Hm,” I said. “Yeah, that’s a proposition I don’t need to bother considering. Therefore, I can’t cooperate with you on the plan to save up eight hundred million. However, you are free to attack whatever class you want from this point onward; I have no right to stop you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Heh. Guess you ain’t such a naïve moron after all, eh, Ayanokouji?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s because this issue doesn’t affect me alone.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Suppose I can’t argue with ya on that, then. Just have to pretend that conversation never happened and drop it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He had backed off much more easily than I expected. He seemed to have already known that I’d turn him down.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even though our negotiations have broken down, do you still plan on saving up the eight hundred million points?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not plannin’ to change my strategy at this point in the game,” he replied. “My main goal is to save up the eight hundred million. After that, I’ll defeat both you and Sakayanagi. And if I don’t spend any of it and get to Class A, I can graduate with tons of cash. Ain’t that right?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">His plan, which could only be described as a pipe dream even in the best of circumstances, had now been replaced by another ideal scenario. All the same, Ryuuen boldly proclaimed that from here on out, he was going to try and accumulate eight hundred million points.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ve been throwin’ money around so far, pullin’ Katsuragi out of his class and into mine, and usin’ those first-years, but now’s the time I start collectin’. I’m switchin’ things up to a strict Private Point system.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If he was that eager to collect Private Points, there was going to be some risk involved. Ryuuen’s irregular, incoherent thoughts and behavior here were casting a strange shadow over my own thoughts.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t give me that look, like you’re so surprised that I let you get away without fulfillin’ your promise to me, without even pushin’ for a compromise.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, yeah, of course I’m surprised. I’m not seeing the true nature of this conversation,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s simple. It’s just that you breakin’ our contract was a foregone conclusion. I can’t crush you if I’m still partially connected to you. But if you break things off like this, well, that’s a different story. Now I can thoroughly wreck you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So, in other words, he was choosing a resurrected obsession with victory against me over aligning our mutual interests. He’d said something similar on the bus before; once again, he was declaring war. Even so, I wasn’t completely convinced. There was some kind of agenda in this story, though I likely wasn’t going to get an answer even if I pressed him on it here and now.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s fine to look ahead to the future,” I told him, “but you’ll have to beat Sakayanagi first before thinking about a rematch with me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ha. I know that chick is clever, but that’s all she is,” Ryuuen scoffed. Clearly, he had absolute confidence in the final exam’s battle.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Ryuuen, you were defeated, and then you were resurrected. I’ll also admit that your talents have surpassed my expectations.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was true that the success story of Ryuuen Kakeru remained steadily on track.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Whether or not he could overcome the barriers at the very end was another matter. I had wondered if this discrepancy, his failure to recognize obstacles as obstacles, might eventually have an effect in the arena of battle. Of course, those omens and signs would change again, depending on how Sakayanagi perceived Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You head on back first, Ayanokouji,” ordered Ryuuen, and with that, he walked toward the restrooms.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hiyori, who had been watching us from a seat somewhat far away, noticed me and waved. Apparently her group had also come to ski. I gently raised my hand in response, and then returned to my group’s table. Yamamura had already returned as well, and she was silently fiddling on her phone with a blank, nonchalant look on her face.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Where’s Ryuuen?” asked Watanabe.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Seems like he’ll be coming back after stopping by the bathroom,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Are you okay?” Watanabe worriedly looked me over, examining my body carefully.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“He didn’t smack you or anything, did he?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t worry. We just talked a little, is all,” I assured him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I sure hope that was all…” said Watanabe.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yamamura had finished her meal after slowly picking at it here and there; she and Nishino both grabbed their trays.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll…go put my tray away,” announced Yamamura.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She and Nishino had ordered food from the same place, so they were apparently heading back there together.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kitou gave me a penetrating look. “Ayanokouji, if Ryuuen has something over you, don’t hesitate to talk to me about it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Perhaps he felt like Watanabe’s way of addressing the matter was too lax. I kind of wished he could have said that before Ryuuen had called me away, though, if at all possible. When Ryuuen returned shortly after, Kitou turned his gaze away from me to look at him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’ve run away from me and decided to intimidate people from other classes now?” he said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What?” said Ryuuen. “Heh heh, don’t worry, Kitou. I’m gonna bring you Class A bastards down, just you wait. Sakayanagi ain’t nothin’ more than a steppin’ stone for me. I’ll show you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You can never defeat Class A,” huffed Kitou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Heh. I wonder about that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ryuuen appeared calm—or perhaps it was better to say that he was putting on a show to appear calm and confident. He might have sincerely believed he was going to win, but there was no actual evidence to back that up. Of course, it was possible he had some information that I didn’t, but in a simple comparison of their abilities, Sakayanagi was a cut above Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It doesn’t have to be the final exam,” Kitou said. “Come at us anytime.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Kitou, you son of a bitch, you ain’t got that kinda authority,” snorted Ryuuen. “All you got goin’ for you is that you’re a loyal dog. You’ll be causin’ trouble for your master if you make careless remarks like that, y’know?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">At being called a dog, Kitou placed his large palms on the table and stood up. “I alone am more than enough to defeat you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh? So, what, you’re sayin’ you wanna go for round three?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Their pillow fight had resulted in my pillow getting damaged. And thanks to me interfering on the slopes before, they hadn’t been able to settle things by skiing. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Come on, you two, get along,” Kushida cut in. “There are already rumors floating around that our group is pretty dangerous.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even some members of the general public in the vicinity had begun watching the standoff between Ryuuen and Kitou with curiosity. If they continued to be so showy, I supposed it was only a matter of time before the teachers heard about it. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Come to think of it, aren’t Nishino-san and Yamamura-san taking a while?” said Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Now that you mention it, yeah,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">All they were doing was returning their trays; it shouldn’t have taken them more than a minute. But there was no sign of them. Kushida looked around for them.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, there they are,” she said. “But it looks like they’re talking with some boys I don’t know.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida pointed toward Nishino and Yamamura across the crowded food court. They were surrounded by five guys who, judging from their age, appeared to be students. And they had a threatening air about them.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey now, what’s this?” Ryuuen said. “Looks like Nishino is in trouble. Let’s go help her.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’d be better not to do anything when there’s a large crowd of people around,” I told him. “If you recklessly start a fight, there’ll be trouble.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In spite of my warning, Ryuuen and Kitou had already gotten up from their seats. Those two, who would never listen to anything resembling caution in the first place, headed over to where Nishino and Yamamura were standing. They didn’t even bother to communicate their intent to each other. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Kushida, you and the others wait here.” I instructed her, Amikura, and Watanabe to stay put.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As I caught up to Ryuuen and Kitou, who had rushed off to Nishino and Yamamura with strong, fast footfalls, I could overhear the conversation at the scene.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re not gonna apologize for bumpin’ into my shoulder?” said one of the guys. “You got ramen broth all over my clothes.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Apparently, it wasn’t Nishino who had started the trouble here. Instead, it began with Yamamura, who had bumped into these guys.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Aren’t </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">you</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> guys the ones at fault here, for not even noticing Yamamura-san standing there while you were walking?” replied Nishino.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The guys just laughed unkindly in response, mockingly touching their shoulders as if Yamamura had hurt them.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nah, I mean, she’s practically a ghost,” another guy said, “so of course we couldn’t see her. Right?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…I am really…sorry.” said Yamamura, in a tiny voice.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Most likely, she hadn’t apologized just once or twice. But the guys carried on, acting as though they hadn’t heard her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, we’re here on a school trip from Gifu,” one of them said. “Let’s hang out. We’ll forgive you if you hang out with us.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Nishino was standing in front of Yamamura to protect her, blocking their way. But then one of the guys forcefully grabbed hold of her arm.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? You’ve got to be joking,” she said. “Who’d wanna hang out with people like you?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She shook her arm free from her attacker’s grasp, swinging wide, and ended up lightly slapping one guy’s cheek.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That hurt.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The guys had been smirking and chuckling crudely throughout this whole conversation so far, but now, the looks on their faces had suddenly changed. Immediately afterward, one of the five flew forward with great force.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wh-what the hell, dude?!” exclaimed one of the others.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You took the words right outta my mouth, moron,” said Ryuuen. “What business do you got with my people?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was none other than Ryuuen who had driven his foot straight into that guy’s back in spectacular fashion. He quickly grabbed hold of another one by the collar.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t squawk in front of women like some stupid little bird,” he said.</span></p>

<div style="page-break-after: always;"></div>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wh—? We’ll kill you!” one of them shouted.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Go ahead and try,” Ryuuen sneered. “Hey, if you want, I’ll let you get one shot in. How about it? You want a souvenir from your school trip, don’tcha?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Then, Ryuuen presented his own left cheek, tapping it lightly with his finger—</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">tap tap</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">—as though he were offering it up.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Heh! In that case, don’t mind if I do!” shouted the guy he’d provoked. “I’ll sure as shit take a swing at you!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He drew his arm back, swinging wide.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, that’s—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Don’t think that Ryuuen will actually let you punch him,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> I thought to myself. But I couldn’t give him that advice in time. Ryuuen watched his opponent’s grand, but ultimately futile, wind up—then grabbed hold of him by the shoulders and drove his knee straight into his stomach with furious intensity. The student from the other school doubled over, rolling on the ground in agony.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Heh. Looks like some slightly interestin’ stuff can happen even on boring school trips like this,” Ryuuen said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He’d started to find enjoyment in an inevitable situation. To think, I’d encountered people from another school for the first time in my life, and it ended up turning into a disturbing display of violence. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">One of the guys balled his hands into fists, taking swings with his left and right. There was no way that this was going to be a one-on-one fight. It looked like they were hoping to win by outnumbering their opponent. But just then, Kitou slowly stepped in. The guys from the other school were clearly panicked and flustered by Kitou’s face and intimidating presence, which made it hard to imagine he was a high schooler.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It looks like…he’s going to help us,” said Nishino quietly. She’d walked over to me, still holding onto Yamamura’s shoulders, protecting her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Kitou is Yamamura’s classmate,” I replied. “It’s only natural that he wouldn’t be able to just stand by quietly if he saw she was in trouble.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Fortunately, both sides seemed to understand that further fighting inside the food court wasn’t a good idea, so Ryuuen and the others all walked outside, single file.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Should we call an adult?” asked Nishino.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nah. Now that they’ve gotten to this point, there’s no stopping it. It’s best to just get them out of the public eye and go at it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">From what I saw, the other side had superior numbers, but none of them looked like that much of a threat. If&#160;Ryuuen and Kitou teamed up and fought together, it shouldn’t take them much time to clean up. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">About ten minutes later, Ryuuen and Kitou had returned. And they brought their defeated opponents with them. Then, they made them get down on their knees in front of Yamamura and Nishino and beg their forgiveness. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">From the looks of things, they had thoroughly beaten their opponents until they broke their rebellious spirits… Well, if you looked at it that way, then it was a problem, but maybe it was necessary, for Yamamura and Nishino’s sake. The students from the other school were made to promise to never show themselves to Yamamura and Nishino again, and then they scattered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s definitely not a boring group,” said Kushida, in a whisper. Hearing her impression, I could only agree.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">A</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">FTER SKIING FOR AS LONG</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> as time permitted, we returned to the ryokan just before seven o’clock in the evening. I hadn’t had my fill of skiing yet, but perhaps it was better to leave things there, with some reluctance to part from the activity. Our second day was nearing its end, and time continued to march steadily on through the evening. Sudou had invited me out during dinner, so we headed over to the baths together afterward and, after washing up, were enjoying a soak in the hot spring.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Whoo! Man, this hits the spot!” exclaimed Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The hot bath might’ve been especially effective for Sudou, who worked up a sweat just about every day at basketball. Using both hands, I repeatedly scooped up the hot water and splashed my face, feeling my fatigue melt away. After we’d spent some time just lazing about in the pool, Hashimoto from Class A arrived and got in next to me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“’Sup?” he said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I responded with a gentle wave of my hand, and Sudou did the same.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hashimoto looked thoroughly exhausted, rolling his shoulders and letting out a deep sigh.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Man… I am completely, totally beat after today.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Did something happen?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If only it were ‘something,’” he replied. “It’s more like I’m rackin’ my brains over what to do about one specific problem in my group.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I hadn’t said anything out loud, but I had been very curious about Hashimoto’s group from the start.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Because Kouenji’s in your group, you mean?” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Exactly. You know how it’s like, in our free time, we’re supposed to be together as a group? Well, if this was a sane person we were talking about, we’d be having discussions as a group, right? But we’re basically being forced to go along with him to every place he wants to go.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kouenji obviously wasn’t the type of person to obediently follow along with what other people wanted, and it sounded like that hadn’t changed even when he was together with a group comprised of students from all the classes.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It looked like your group was at a ranch today, so you could experience horseback riding,” I said. “I’m guessing that’s what Kouenji wanted, then.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait. How do you know that? Oh, hang on… Yeah, you saw </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">that</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> happen, so I guess it’s not surprising that you know.” Looking like he was at his wits’ end, Hashimoto submerged half of his face in the water.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I just saw him gallop past,” I said. “Did he actually come back after that, like he was supposed to?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hashimoto stayed submerged for about ten seconds; then he shrugged his shoulders and surfaced.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“After about an hour or so, yeah,” he said. “The rest of us didn’t have the mental and emotional capacity to handle horseback riding, so we just stood around, waiting.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hearing about how Hashimoto’s group had spent their free time, it sounded like they had suffered through hellish experiences one after another since this trip had started. Sudou brought his hands together in prayer, offering his condolences.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, I was trying to put together plans to have lunch at this famous place that I happened to see on TV, at least,” Hashimoto said, “but Kouenji seriously had the nerve to just say, ‘I’m going skiing’ and take off. That selfish jerk just up and left before we could even argue. Now I’m so completely exhausted that I don’t even have the energy to have fun anymore. And that is how our second day has come to an end.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If the group had ignored Kouenji and gone ahead to have lunch at that famous restaurant, or anything else, then they would’ve been in violation of the rules. What an incredibly tragic story.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You guys are his classmates,” said Hashimoto. “I was wondering if you might know some way of dealing with him.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We had already passed the halfway point of this trip; there were only two days left. At the very least, I was sure that his group would want to choose something they’d all like to do during their free time on the fourth day.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“He’s just uncontrollable, dude. There ain’t nothin’ you can do,” said Sudou, offering his honest thoughts. It sounded cold, but after having spent a long time with Kouenji, Sudou had given up on him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What about you, Ayanokouji?” asked Hashimoto.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s not realistic to try and convince Kouenji,” I agreed. “Honestly, I don’t think there’s anything you can do.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…What a cruel reality,” said Hashimoto.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But there might be one way, in an emergency,” I added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What is it? Tell me!” Thrilled by the prospect of having some hope, no matter how small, Hashimoto begged me for a way he could free himself from his situation. There was one way he could guarantee that he’d get some free time, provided he could tolerate the disadvantages that came with it. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After I finished telling him, Hashimoto nodded, seemingly convinced. “Well, I guess that’s probably the only thing left I could do.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You should discuss what to do with the rest of your group,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll do that. I’m seriously considering it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hashimoto, lost in thought, disappeared into the water once more.</span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">4.6</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">A</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">FTER TAKING OUR TIME</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> and enjoying ourselves in the bath for about an hour or so, Sudou and I put on our yukatas. We each took a bottle of the free mineral water from the cooler and chugged while holding our hands on our hips. I felt the water cool my flushed body as it went down my throat.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay… I’m…ready for this, Ayanokouji,” said Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, it’s finally time, eh?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Perhaps it was because we had spent so much time in the bath, but Sudou’s face was a little red. Or perhaps it was because he was nervous from imagining what was going to happen next. The time had come for him to formally tell Horikita how he felt about her. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He drank down about half of his remaining water in one gulp. “Urp! Okay! Let’s go!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He slapped both of his cheeks with a </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">smack</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> to get himself fired up, as though he were heading off to a basketball game after this.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, what are you planning to do, specifically?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was just after nine thirty at night now. Horikita probably wasn’t asleep yet, but I was sure that many students were relaxing with their friends in their rooms. I couldn’t imagine her having fun and making noise with others, but it wouldn’t have been unusual for her to just be watching over others warmly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, first things first, I guess… I’ll try givin’ her a call.” Phone in hand, Sudou brushed past the curtain and exited the men’s bath. Immediately after exiting, he made the call. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The phone didn’t ring that many times before Horikita picked up, causing Sudou to panic a bit.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…H-h-hey, it’s me. Where are you right now?” he asked, sounding flustered. “Oh, in the lobby? Okay, can you wait there for a sec? I’m, uh, heading there right now.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sudou hung up, breathing hard. He looked at me as he walked away. “So, y’know how there’s a part of the lobby in the ryokan where they sell small souvenirs and stuff? She says she’s there.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t just tell her how you feel right away the second you see her there, okay?” I suggested. “People would probably be watching in the lobby. And that’d make Horikita feel awkward, too.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Y-yeah, I know that, dude.” Telling someone how you felt about them was a major event, one that required not only consideration for the person expressing their feelings, but for the recipient, too. “But I dunno where should I do it…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nobody should be going through the corridor leading into the rear garden at this hour,” I told him. “That could be a good place, right?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If you went from the rear garden up the stairs to the higher level, there was a little wooden deck with a pleasant view. However, since you couldn’t head out into the rear garden after 9 p.m., there shouldn’t be people around.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Man, I knew I could count on you, Ayanokouji. You’re a good pal to have.” Sudou flashed a smile and gave me a big thumbs-up. It was a rigid, nervous smile, though. “Nice.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Still anxious, Sudou rushed over to the lobby. Once we arrived, I noticed that Horikita was already waiting nearby, checking out the souvenirs. I moved some distance away and found a blind spot to stand in. There was one employee in the lobby, as well as several students, some of whom were browsing the souvenirs and some sitting in the chairs, chatting happily. Seeing that, I felt affirmed in my prediction that this wouldn’t be a suitable place for a romantic confession.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Somehow, albeit through the added use of gestures, Sudou successfully managed to call Horikita to come to the corridor leading to the rear garden. The two of them walked over side by side. Normally, it probably would’ve been better if I stopped following them at this point, but getting told off by Sudou would be annoying. I masked the sound of my footsteps as much as possible as I followed them in order to bear witness to his heroic spectacle. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Just as I’d expected, there didn’t seem to be any people in this corridor anymore, and both Sudou and Horikita stopped in the middle of the empty hallway.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita turned back toward Sudou with a curious look. “What’s up?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wondered if she had just been in the bath up until a little while ago, like Sudou and I had. Her hair had a glamorous shine to it, even in the dim lighting.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay, here is good.” Normally, Sudou’s confident attitude was his main selling point, but now he spoke quietly, perhaps because he was too nervous to speak up in front of the person he liked. There was only the subdued, gentle background music that played over the speakers and the quiet chatter of people at night in the ryokan, so he most likely wanted to avoid carelessly shouting in a loud voice, even in a place without anyone around. That was the right amount of consideration.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I, uh… Um…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Seeing Sudou act so hesitantly, Horikita cocked her head to the side, puzzled. Right now, though, she didn’t seem particularly irritated, or press Sudou to get on with it. I supposed that also demonstrated the trusting relationship that the two of them had built. Back when they had first met, Horikita would have rushed Sudou along, telling him to spit it out already, no questions asked. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Just then, my phone vibrated. Even though I had put it on silent mode, in this quiet stillness, it was still possible that someone might hear it. Therefore, I immediately powered my phone off without even looking at the screen. And it…didn’t seem like they noticed me. I was safe, for the time being.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, Suzune.” I had expected Sudou would launch straight into his romantic confession, but instead, practically squeezing the words out, he asked, “I, um… I’ve changed, right? I was just curious about…how different I am now from when we first met.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you still worried about what people think of you?” asked Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, there’s that too, yeah.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I figured he must be trying to stall until he could build up the courage to tell Horikita how he felt about her. At&#160;the same time, though, it seemed like Sudou himself remained aware of what he was doing.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, let’s see,” Horikita said. “Taking an objective view, you’ve changed greatly, more than anyone. And not in a bad way; in a good way. I’ve been by your side for a long time, watching you, so no one can guarantee that more than I.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Those were Horikita’s honest feelings. Well, no, it probably wasn’t just Horikita who felt that; I was sure most people living on campus shared that same opinion.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Th-that so?” asked Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But don’t be conceited. If I were to speak frankly, you started off in a negative position, compared to the people around you. Don’t think that just because I’m telling you that you’ve moved to a positive one since then, that makes you better than other people.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The negative effects Sudou’s behavior and attitude had originally had on the people around him could bias them when they saw how he was now, making them more impressed by how he’d changed. However, just as Horikita had said, those negatives that Sudou had accumulated over time hadn’t necessarily just disappeared, either. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. I mean, that sounds totally right to me.” Those harsh words had Sudou feeling dejected, but he nodded, accepting them completely. “God, it’s so embarrassin’. All the stupid stuff I’ve done, I mean.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Tardiness, missing class, coming in last place on written tests, using all kinds of foul language and insults, carelessly getting physically violent—no matter how much Sudou reflected on those things, the past wouldn’t change, and he was ashamed of the path he had trod before.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita nodded, and then smiled warmly, eyes creasing. “You really do have a humble heart, don’t you?” She herself might not have realized this, but she had also changed quite a lot. Her change wasn’t nearly as great as Sudou’s, though. “You don’t carelessly hurt others or cause trouble anymore. It’s okay.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Apparently, Horikita had misinterpreted the situation, thinking that Sudou was uncertain about his past and how much he’d grown, and that he had come to her seeking advice. Sudou seemed to realize that Horikita had misunderstood as well, because he shook his head from side to side, flustered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Th-that ain’t what I wanted to talk to you about, Suzune.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s not?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I…I, uh… Well…” He must have remembered what he had declared to me earlier, because he suddenly thrust out his right hand. But his words didn’t follow; his outstretched, open hand remained in front of him, and that was it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What? What are— ” Horikita, unable to comprehend what was going on, tried to question the meaning behind Sudou offering his right hand, but just then…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I like you! Please go out with me!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sudou had freed himself from the feelings of embarrassment that kept the words stuck in the back of his throat. He shouted too loudly, but… Well, I supposed we’d just overlook that part. In any case, even if someone did overhear, I could stand watch and protect him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wha—” Horikita, who hadn’t had even the slightest expectation that someone would express their romantic feelings to her, froze on the spot, completely taken aback.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you do want to go out with me, I’d like you to take my right hand!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait, hold on… Are you seri…?” Horikita immediately swallowed her words. She could sense Sudou’s passion and enthusiasm, and she could tell his feelings were genuine; she knew that it would’ve been rude to ask him something like, </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“This is some kind of joke, right?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She stared at Sudou’s right hand, closing her mouth. I had expected her to reply to him immediately, but she just silently stared at his hand.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After the confession he’d made, I was sure that the longer this silence continued, the faster Sudou’s heart rate would climb. This moment right now was by no means a pleasant one; it was probably painful. But even someone like Horikita should be given as much time to think as she needed. When telling someone you had romantic feelings for them, it didn’t come down to the emotions of just one person or the other. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita must have eventually sorted out how she felt, because she slowly started to speak, choosing her words very carefully. “Never once in my life did I think that someone would tell me they had a romantic interest in me,” she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Now that Horikita had heard and acknowledged Sudou’s passionate feelings, how would she respond? Would she return those feelings? Or would she reject him? Or would she choose to put it off until later? As the silence dragged on, Sudou’s right arm gradually started trembling. It wasn’t due to his arm getting numb; it was because of nervousness and fear. And the frustration of not getting a response, whether Horikita would reciprocate or not. Sudou, believing that Horikita would take his outstretched hand, kept his head down.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sudou-kun. Thank you for liking someone like me.” Horikita offered her gratitude. However, she wasn’t showing any signs of taking Sudou’s outstretched hand. “But I’m sorry. I…cannot reciprocate your feelings.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was the conclusion that Horikita’s thoughts had led her to.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I-I see… Um, if it’s okay, could you…maybe tell me the reason why?” asked Sudou hesitantly, his right hand frozen in the air. He still couldn’t lift his face.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The reason… Okay. It’s not as though I’m particularly dissatisfied with you, Sudou-kun. It’s—”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita stopped for a moment.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“To be honest, I must confess that I’ve never fallen in love with another person in my life so far,” she said. “Even now, I still haven’t had that feeling, and I have no idea what it’s like. I think that even if I went out with you, Sudou-kun, now that you’ve told me your feelings, there could be a possibility that I’d fall in love with you in time. But…I think that I’m probably waiting for that moment that I fall in love with someone naturally, by instinct, rather than trying to trigger romantic feelings to happen that way.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She was expressing her thoughts to Sudou, but it seemed like she was also confirming them for herself. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was the reason she had turned him down. She wanted to keep waiting for her first love. I was sure that was a hidden feeling that Horikita wouldn’t let strangers hear about.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see… Thank you,” said Sudou. “For telling me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Perhaps it was because Horikita had told him so much, expressed her stance so clearly, but he didn’t persist.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’ve really shown me your courage and your feelings very strongly,” said Horikita. With that, she hurriedly grabbed hold of Sudou’s right hand, which was right on the verge of going limp. “I’ve heard your feelings. Thank you for liking me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sudou’s trembling right hand said it all.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Thinking that it was about time to go, I decided to head back. I figured that I’d hang out and look around the souvenir shop while I waited for Sudou to collect himself and return.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That reminds me,” I thought aloud. “Nanase said something about chocolate-covered fries, I think.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I tried doing some searching, but they must not have been carried at this ryokan, because I couldn’t find any. In the meantime, I figured I’d check on my phone and see if there was a store nearby that carried them.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Whoops…” I muttered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When I turned on my cell phone, thinking I’d check the local stores, I saw that I had many messages and missed calls. They were all from Kei, of course.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“Where are you?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“I didn’t get to see you at all yesterday or today.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you busy?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“I wanna see you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“I wanna see youuuuuuuu.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">And so on. When I opened the app, the messages, which had been sent seconds apart from each other, were marked as read. Then, immediately afterward, my phone rang.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“Grr—!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wondered if a cat’s snarl would be an appropriate metaphor to describe what that sounded like.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you mad?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">I’m not mad, </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">but</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">—!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">I see.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> The only thing I was certain of was that she did in fact seem extremely angry.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“Can’t we be together for even just a little bit?!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry,” I said. “We’re on a school trip, and there are lots of things I have to do.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“I know that, but still!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ve already gotten information about group eleven from Kushida, so I know for certain that you’ve been handling yourself well, Kei. That’s why I wasn’t worried about you at all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Hmmmm? You certainly sound like you’re having fun with Kushida-san! She </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">is</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;"> cute, you know! You cheater!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We’re in the same group, so of course we’re going to talk. Besides, you already know what kind of person Kushida is, don’t you?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“That doesn’t matter. She’s got big boobs, too! …Kiyotaka…you’re… Ahh!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I get it, I get it. I can take some time now, so let’s meet up somewhere.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“Really?! Well then, I’ll come visit you!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was an extremely calculated act on her part, and once I offered to meet, her voice immediately turned cheerful.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Actually, it’s probably better that we don’t meet in my room, don’t you think?” I said. “Ryuuen’s in my room, too.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Oh… I see.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Where are you right now?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“In my room, but the three other girls are probably still in the bath. I was with them until just a little bit ago. But I wanted to talk to you, so I left before they did, Kiyotaka-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kei used to be very self-conscious about her scars, but apparently, she had managed to completely move past that.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I have the key to my room, so I’m going to stop by there first and drop it off</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em; letter-spacing: 0.60mm;">,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">” I told her. “I’ll contact you afterward, so wait for me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had been waiting for Sudou at the souvenir shop for a little under five minutes now. Since there was still no sign of him coming back this way, I got curious, and decided to check the corridor leading to the rear garden. When I got there, I saw Sudou standing there alone, in the same spot where he’d stood when he told Horikita how he felt about her. There was no sign of Horikita, though, so I figured that she had already headed back.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sudou?” I felt bad, since Kei was waiting for me, but I drew closer to Sudou and called out his name.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah, damn it!” he shouted. From what I could tell from the sound of his voice, he probably had a look of frustration on his face. “I knew it was hopeless, but…!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I could tell that there was vexation in Sudou’s face when he turned around to look at me, but he had a bright smile. “I’m sorry,” he said to me. “I just couldn’t forget the feeling of Suzune’s hand, and I was spacin’ out.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, that’s what happened.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You saw, right? It was a spectacular failure.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even so, it was an honorable defeat that you should be proud of.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">What I saw was a truly manly expression of romantic love, from start to finish.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Y’know, before, I thought, like, ‘Even if she rejects me, I’m not gonna give up. I’ll become an even better me next year and try again,’” he said. “Stuff like that. But I ain’t gonna do that. It’s pointless. At the very least, I realized that I just can’t reach her.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sudou seemed to have sensed something that I, who had been watching from a distance, could not.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It ain’t a question of, like, if I’m givin’ up or not. I still love her, those feelin’s haven’t changed, but it’s just, I dunno, I feel like she’s this beautiful flower I long for that’s out of my reach.” Sudou couldn’t seem to sum up how he felt very well; after saying that, he laughed a bit.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What are you going to do about Onodera?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Man, how should I know? Even you don’t know how she really feels. You don’t really gotta me ask me that, right?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s true.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I guess it is what it is. Stuff will happen as it should,” he said. “Onodera’s good people, and we got the same kinda interests. Now that I’m not gonna be droolin’ over Suzune all the time, I feel like I can hang out with Onodera fairly, y’know?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Whether or not that developed into love was a secondary concern.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m just tellin’ you this now,” Sudou added, “but I’m gonna keep workin’ hard on my studies from here on out. I was doing it for someone else before, but starting now, I’m givin’ it my best for myself. My goal is to get to around Hirata’s level.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’d be another pretty huge jump,” I remarked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If Sudou did manage to get over that wall, then he’d eventually reach the top level in our grade, alongside the likes of Horikita and Keisei. It seemed like he wasn’t going to stay discouraged after being rejected, but instead, he was able to look to higher goals.</span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">4.8</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em; letter-spacing: 0.60mm;"> </span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HURRIEDLY RETURNED</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> to my room, where I saw that Horikita was standing in front of my door.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What are you doing?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Waiting for you,” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“For me?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had a bad feeling about this, so I tried playing dumb, but Horikita’s expression stiffened.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You are a mean one, Ayanokouji-kun. You were watching, weren’t you?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Watching what?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You were in the souvenir shop earlier, weren’t you? If&#160;it was anyone else, it might have been just a coincidence that you were nearby, but with you, I can’t imagine that’s it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">What a high-handed way of thinking. She’s right, but still. If I need to take similar measures against Horikita in the future, I’ll have to take care not to be seen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re thinking about how you’ll have to be careful not to be discovered next time,” said Horikita. “Aren’t you?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Impressive.” I clapped my hands out of earnest admiration, praising her perceptiveness. “Sudou asked me to. He said he wanted me to watch him tell you how he felt.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even so, don’t you think that shows a lack of consideration for the girl involved, i.e., me?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I suppose I can see that,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see Sudou-kun still has a way to go,” she sighed. “He loses points for asking you to watch him.” Though she sounded exasperated, she didn’t seem that angry.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So? Did you come all the way here just to complain that I was a spectator?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes,” she replied, clearly and without hesitation. Then she added, “I’m half-joking. Actually, there is something I wanted to talk to you about. But it looks like you’re in a hurry to get inside.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, not exactly, but…if possible, could we talk tomorrow?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why’s that?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“A certain someone came to me before you did and is very demanding,” I explained. “I haven’t seen her at all in two days, and she has taken great offense.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. Karuizawa-san.” Horikita pondered for a moment. I wondered if she was thinking of telling me to put off seeing Kei until later, if this was an important matter. But she said, “All right, tomorrow night, then. I’ll forgive you if you can promise to meet me then.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“All right. I promise.” I had no other choice at this point.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After leaving the key with Kitou, who was already in the room, I went to meet Kei. Though we were already recognized as an official couple by many, we couldn’t just go out and about anywhere we pleased, like Ike and Shinohara. We decided to meet up in the area that had multiple reservable private baths. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As soon as we met up, Kei scolded me harshly, but she immediately switched to sweet mode. After I embraced her and got her into a better mood, we spent some time relaxing together for a while.</span></p>
