<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 3:<br/>Self-Explanatory School Trip</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">T WAS THE MORNING</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> of the first day of the school trip. A total of four buses had gathered, and all the second-year students were wearing their casual clothes as they stood in line. It was just under five degrees Celsius that morning, and it felt like the cold was occasionally prickling my skin. However, the temperature was going to be even lower in Hokkaido.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The school made sure that the students didn’t forget their gloves and coats and such, just to be safe. We were also going to do a final check of our luggage, including clothing, and confirm that we had essential items with us, such as our cell phones.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Mashima-sensei, the homeroom instructor for Class&#160;2-A, addressed us in a loud, clear voice before we boarded the bus. “First and foremost, I’m relieved to say that no students will be absent from the school trip due to illness,” he said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The homeroom instructors for the second-year classes then proceeded to oversee boarding for the four buses. Mashima-sensei boarded bus number one, Chabashira-sensei boarded bus number two, Sakagami-sensei boarded bus number three, and Hoshinomiya-sensei boarded bus number four. In short, they boarded in order based on the class rankings, from A to D. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As I waited to board, I went ahead and checked the schedule on my phone for what was going to happen next. The bus would head to Haneda Airport, and we would take a plane to New Chitose Airport. Once we&#160;landed, we’d board a local bus there, and head to the ski resort for our first day. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I quietly browsed the page where the list of groups was displayed. There, I saw I had been placed in group six, along with seven other students. From Class A, there were Kitou Hayato and Yamamura Miki. From Class B, me and Kushida Kikyou. From Class C, Ryuuen Kakeru and Nishino Takeko. And from Class D, Watanabe Norihito and Amikura Mako.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t have any complaints about the group the school had assigned, but I hadn’t imagined that I’d be placed in the same group as Ryuuen, who was perceived by many as the biggest troublemaker. As for Kitou, Yamamura, Watanabe, Nishino, and Amikura, I didn’t know much about them at all, because I hadn’t ever interacted with them before. Still, I was sure I could come to understand them during our time as a group. After all, it had been decided that these groups would spend almost the entire four nights and five days on the school trip together.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was certainly a strange group, and it was difficult to determine whether or not the members’ relationships would be solid. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Incidentally, the numbers I had assigned to the students in the group were as follows: Kushida, 6; Watanabe,&#160;18; Amikura, 14; Ryuuen, 6; Nishino, 18; Kitou, 9; and Yamamura, 14. I ranked them chiefly based on OAA, the metric derived by the school, rather than whether I was personally close to them or not. Of course, I had assigned the highest ratings to Kushida and Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But…the seven other people in my group hadn’t necessarily given the same ratings I did. In Ryuuen’s case, in particular, I wouldn’t have been surprised if a lot of other people had given him an especially good rating, even the students who disliked him. Kitou especially, as someone who stood by Sakayanagi’s side, would likely have given Ryuuen a respectable number. Ryuuen had the qualities and characteristics of a leader, so it would make perfect sense for him to be given a high ranking—or, if not that, at least a fairly good score. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Of course, in the end, all this was nothing more than my own speculation. I knew that they had based these groups on the numbers we came up with the other day, so it wasn’t like they were entirely random. Still, I might not be able to find an exact answer as to how the groups were formed no matter how much I let my imagination run wild.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t even know five out of these seven people…” I muttered to myself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Well, technically, could I even include Ryuuen among the people who I knew? I’d thought that I had expanded my circle of friends in my own way over the past year and a half, little by little, but I supposed that wasn’t really the case when it came to the other classes. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Anyway, it seemed like it was going to be time to board the buses soon. The students all began gathering with their close friends.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There were no assigned places for us to sit on the buses. The me from ages long past would have personally appreciated having a fixed seat. But since Kei currently occupied the position of my girlfriend, it was, of course, easy to know who was going to sit by me in the end. Kei waved at me and came to stand next to me, almost as though we had already decided upon it in advance. However, Yousuke happened to appear at almost the exact same time that Kei did.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Kiyotaka-kun, could I have a second of your time?” he asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hm?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s about where we’ll be sitting. I was just wondering, if you wouldn’t mind, could I sit next to you on the bus on the way to the airport?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Next to me?” I repeated. “Why?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Sitting next to Yousuke?</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> That was quite a coveted spot. Someone like me claiming that seat would most likely provoke some animosity from other people in our class. The fact that Mii-chan was in love with Yousuke was now public knowledge, since Kushida had exposed her secret, but I didn’t imagine that she had the courage to openly invite him to sit next to her. Even so, she was eyeing him like a hawk, and she wasn’t the only one. I could tell that many girls were looking this way with great interest, as if to prove me right.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yousuke looked me in the eyes and pleaded with me. I guessed he must be concerned about the firestorm that might ensue as people competed for this seat, and this was his plan to avoid any issues.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s tough being popular, huh?” I remarked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not trying to be popular.” He wasn’t being smug at all; he simply stated it plainly. His ability to pick up on the unwritten social rules of the class was truly incredible. He cared about others’ feelings as if they were his own, and always did his best to avoid conflict.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, Kei, is it okay if I let Yousuke sit next to me, then?” I asked, turning to her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Say </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">what?!</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> Nah, just kidding. It’s easier that way, right? It’s okay.” Kei seemed to be very receptive to Yousuke; she was indebted to him, after all. “In exchange, though, Kiyotaka has to sit in the aisle seat. Because I’m gonna sit in the aisle seat on the opposite side.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Well, I supposed that was a simple yet satisfactory solution. As a result, four of us all sat together in a row a little past the middle part of the bus, with Yousuke on the far left and me sitting next to him in the aisle seat, Kei in the aisle seat on the other side, and Satou next to her. A few minutes later, all four buses were fully loaded, and we departed for the airport. We weren’t allowed to get up from our seats while the buses were in motion, but we were free to chat amongst ourselves, and to enjoy whatever food or drink we brought with us on the bus, too. So, some students almost immediately started taking out their snacks.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Now it’s starting to feel like a trip, huh?” said Yousuke happily, noticing the mood around him. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I supposed that for this young man, who considered the happiness of others to be his own happiness, it must have been comforting to feel other students being so merry.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ahhh, it still would’ve been awesome if I had been in a group together with you, Kiyotaka,” lamented Kei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The boy from our class who she had been partnered with for her group was none other than Akito—someone she hardly ever talked to.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Like I told you, this is a good opportunity, though, isn’t it?” I said. “We don’t get many chances to interact with people from other classes.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wasn’t really looking for that opportunity, though… Tch.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I guess she had been expecting me to say that I was going to be lonely without her too, because she pursed her lips in a frustrated pout. Still, I was sure that she remembered very clearly what I told her the other day. Kei’s eyes were going to be especially important when it came to learning about the other classes’ situations. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Incidentally, Yousuke was in the same group as Matsushita, and Satou was with Okiya.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, Ayanokouji-kun,” Satou called over, “how have things been lately with you and Kei-chan? Is everything going well?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, obviously it’s going well, right?” Kei interrupted. “You don’t even need to ask.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Maybe it’s just that Ayanokouji-kun is being extra careful,” teased Satou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Don’t be stupid. We’re doing well because we’re super-crazy-head-over-heels in love,” said Kei. “Y’know?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We continued engaging in completely childish conversation like that until we reached the airport.</span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">3.1</span></span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">O</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">NCE WE TOUCHED DOWN</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> at New Chitose Airport, we started lining up in the airport lobby. On the buses bound for Haneda Airport, we’d been with our own respective classes, but from this point onward, we were to move with our respective groups. Mashima-sensei oversaw groups one through five, Chabashira-sensei was in charge of groups six through ten, Sakagami-sensei was in charge of groups eleven through fifteen, and Hoshinomiya-sensei was in charge of groups sixteen through twenty.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Once everyone in your group gets together, we’ll begin seating arrangements,” Chabashira-sensei told us. “Please talk with your groups and decide where you’d each like to sit.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Eight bus seats were allocated to those of us in my group, group six. We were going to have to determine ourselves where we’d each sit out of those eight seats. They were spread between two rows at the front of bus two, with two seats on each side. I went over to the area overseen by Chabashira-sensei to join group six.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida came up to me. “Looks like we’re in a group together, Ayanokouji-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Looks like it,” I said. “I’m guessing you’re fine with any group, then, no matter who you’re paired up with?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, pretty much. But… Well, I’m not exactly thrilled about Ryuuen-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t know specifically to what extent Kushida had shown her true nature to Ryuuen, but I knew that they had worked together for a time. That might make it difficult for her to be partnered up with him now.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“He probably won’t be a scary opponent to deal with anymore,” I told her. “Besides, Kushida, you aren’t the cowardly type to begin with. Even if he were to make some careless remark, it probably won’t have any effect on our classmates.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I know. It’s Ryuuen-kun, after all, and he’s reaching for Class A—I wouldn’t be surprised even if he tried to threaten me at some point. I wasn’t sure how to deal with the situation, but maybe things have eased up.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It seemed like Kushida had built a firm resolve; even if her true nature was exposed, it wouldn’t have that much of an effect. It sounded like she was prepared for that.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Kikyou-chaaan!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">A boy and a girl from Ichinose’s class emerged from the crowd of students, waving at Kushida. Watanabe Norihito and Amikura Mako. Kushida and Amikura seemed like they were awfully close, almost like it were completely natural, and they took each other’s hands in clear joy at being in the same group. On the surface, they were acting like they were close friends, but when I thought about how Kushida probably felt nothing at all deep down, I felt like this was an utterly amazing sight. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Watanabe called out to me, “I hope we all have a nice time together over the next five days.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I responded to him with a gentle wave. I had never interacted with him before this point, so this would be a good opportunity to get to know what he was like. With that, half of our group had assembled. The next person to appear was Nishino, and following a little after her was Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida took the initiative and greeted them with a smile. “Good morning, Nishino-san. And to you too, Ryuuen-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Watanabe and Amikura followed suit and greeted them as well.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Hi,” said Nishino.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This girl, Nishino, struck me as somewhat awkward, perhaps because she had hardly ever interacted with Kushida or Amikura before. Ryuuen, on the other hand, didn’t reply to anyone, and stood some distance away from the rest of us.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Now all that’s left is Kitou-kun and Yamamura-san, right?” Kushida said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“They’re already here,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When I pointed behind Kushida, she noticed that the two of them were already standing there, lined up quietly. Yamamura, though, wasn’t looking at anyone; she walked over while keeping her eyes downcast.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It looks like everyone is here,” said Kushida, “so let’s figure out where we’ll be sitting right away.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Having someone in the group who could take the initiative at times like these was a major plus. I was a bit concerned about what Ryuuen, the leader of Class C, was going to say if he had any issues with the arrangements, but…surprisingly enough, he never interrupted. Was it just that he had no intention of leading the other classes, or did he think that there was no need for him to bother with something so trivial as seating arrangements?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Maybe it’d be a good idea for the boys to stick together and the girls to stick together,” suggested Amikura, picking up the baton from Kushida. “How about that?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What does everyone think?” asked Kushida. “No objections?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">No one objected to the idea of boys and girls sitting separately. Neither Nishino nor Yamamura seemed interested at all. As for the boys, they didn’t seem to have even the slightest complaint about what Amikura said. If&#160;they carelessly objected to the idea, then that meant we would be advocating a different arrangement, saying that we boys wanted to sit with the girls.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay, if we’re doing things that way, then it’ll be fine to have the boys and girls discuss amongst themselves, right?” said Kushida, deftly separating the girls from the boys.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It would have been much easier if we just let Kushida make all the decisions on where to sit, as the person who’d been taking charge, but… Well, there was no use complaining about it. Watanabe and I naturally gathered closer together, but Ryuuen and Kitou didn’t budge an inch. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What do we do, Ayanokouji?” said Watanabe. “I’m getting the feeling that this is going to be really difficult.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah,” I agreed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t really mind who I sit next to,” he added, “but I can’t really envision myself chatting away with Ryuuen or Kitou.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But you could see yourself talking to me?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh…? Well, uh… Umm… More than those two, anyway.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Considering who I was being compared to, it wasn’t much of a compliment. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“As long as I’m not next to Ryuuen, I have no complaints.” He quietly mumbled the most troubling statement possible, and then returned to his original position.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…What do we do?” asked Watanabe.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If we try and force those two to sit side by side, it’ll probably be trouble,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It seemed like Watanabe was easily able to imagine how that would turn out, and he nodded dispiritedly. “Okay, so I guess we don’t have any choice but to spread ourselves out,” he said. “Where do you want to sit?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m fine with wherever,” I replied. “Sit wherever you want, Watanabe.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wherever…I want, huh?” Watanabe, faced with only two choices that left him at his wits’ end, agonized over the decision for a while before giving me his answer. “In&#160;that case, I’ll sit by Kitou. I mean, that guy seems like he’s generally mature. I don’t think he’ll do anything as long as I’m not antagonizing him or anything.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was certainly true that Kitou wasn’t as scary as he looked. The impression I had was that he was the harmless sort, except when someone was being hostile toward him. Anyway, I figured I might as well go ahead and wrap things up on my end, too. The school trip was going to be long, after all: four nights and five days.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, I know this might not be what you want,” I said to Ryuuen, “but I’ll sit next to you during the school trip, unless something goes wrong. I’ll be as considerate as I can, though, and I’ll give you the window seat. Are you okay with that?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Whatever.” For the time being, Ryuuen was acting as quiet as a lamb. He was also quite incredible in the sense that that he was earnestly sticking to the rules during the school trip, even though, if you thought about it, it wouldn’t have been out of character for him to just skip it without getting permission.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Seems to me like you’re misunderstandin’ somethin’ here, Ayanokouji,” he said. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Misunderstanding something?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The first shots between me and Sakayanagi have already been fired.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ryuuen shot a quick glance over at Kitou. Kitou seemed to have been expecting it, because he glared back at Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see,” I said. “Interactions with the other classes are inevitable during the school trip. So, you’re saying that this is the perfect opportunity for classes to look for openings to exploit in each other?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s an opportunity to see how good that Kitou guy really is,” said Ryuuen. “Dependin’ on how things go down, I might crush him while the time’s right.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was a very disturbing statement. So much so that it made it difficult to imagine that we were about to be embarking on a fun, happy Hokkaido adventure. It&#160;seemed like this wasn’t going to be a simple trip. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If I recalled correctly, Sakayanagi was in group four. Who else was assigned to group four? I sorted through my memories. From Ryuuen’s class, it was Tokitou Hiroya and Morofuji Rika. The second semester wasn’t over yet, but it wouldn’t have been a bad idea for them to have already started investigating the other classes in preparation for the end of the school year. If we ended up clashing with those two classes, who had already prepared for battle, then it’d be quite the challenge. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Once the teachers saw that the groups were finished with their discussions, they started ushering us onto the buses. I ceded Ryuuen the window seat and sat down next to him. On the bus earlier, all together as a class, there’d been a lively atmosphere, but now it was so quiet that it almost seemed like what happened before was just my imagination. Now, as designated by the school, we were in groups with students from the other classes.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It would take some time for the students to get to know each other and to be able to talk to each other casually, since not everyone was close friends with each other. Almost as if to prove this point, nearly half of the students on this bus had preferred to stick together by class rather than separate based on gender. That was an example of what inevitably happened when you couldn’t take the initiative and decide who would sit next to whom, like Kushida did with our group.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even so, all the students were on the same page in their desire to have fun. By the time the bus started moving about thirty minutes later, self-introductions were mostly over and done with, and little by little, students were starting to chat within their groups, rather than with just their own classmates. Then, when we were told that karaoke was available to us, one of the boys picked up the microphone and started singing.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I got a little of that same vibe from that first-year that I get from you, weirdo. How do you two know each other?” I had thought that Ryuuen wasn’t going to speak to me at all during the trip, but he’d just suddenly asked me a question, without any warning. He was leaning forward on his elbows, though, not even looking at me, making it seem like he was talking to himself.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And if I told you that we have nothing to do with each other?” I asked. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’d say you’re wrong. He was gonna try and beat the shit out of a teacher just to get to you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yes, I’d have to say it’d be impossible to imagine there was no connection if you saw something like that.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We’re barely acquainted,” I told him. “Nothing more, nothing less.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you’re sayin’ not to worry about it? Hard not to do that when I’m catching a whiff of somethin’ interestin’ about this.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s no point in turning your attention to the first-years. The important thing is reaching Class A, isn’t it?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I do what I want to do. Sooner or later, I’m gonna beat you to death—it might come in handy then.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">I see.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> So, it wasn’t really that he was interested in Yagami; he just saw him as a means to exploit my weak points. Well, he wasn’t going to find any weakness, but I couldn’t deny that Yagami was a bothersome element.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Those dangerous-lookin’ guys hauling that first-year away was a big enough deal,” Ryuuen went on. “Hell, the school even seemed to be okay with it, like it was no bother. For a second there, I got an idea of who you really are, you shady creep.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s too bad,” I said. “Yagami’s not here anymore.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, it’s true he’s gone, but I hear that other first-year’s still around. Some girl named Amasawa. I can play with her all I want.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">From the sounds of it, Yagami had left behind a bit of information as a parting gift of sorts. It was entirely possible that Ryuuen would try and mess with Amasawa if I remained silent on the matter. If it came to a one-on-one fight between them, I doubted Amasawa would lose. But with Ryuuen, that wouldn’t be the end of it. It was easy to imagine that he’d persistently stick to Amasawa, looking for an opening to exploit, repeatedly trying to get to her. Of course, Amasawa was capable enough to handle that under normal circumstances, but now that Yagami had been expelled, the situation was uncertain.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, whatever. It’ll be a little while until I mess you up, anyway,” said Ryuuen, responding to my thoughts.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There were many things I wanted to say, but it was true that he should be focusing on the battle with Sakayanagi’s class, which was certain to materialize by the end of the year, rather than the more uncertain prospect of facing Horikita’s class.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“By the way, Ryuuen, I actually have one question I’d like to ask you,” I said. “To tell you the truth, it’s been on my mind since this morning.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh?” he grunted.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I reached out and put my hand into the netted pouch on the back of the seat in front of me. I then pulled out a black plastic bag that was bundled in there.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m curious what this bag is used for,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What?” He furrowed his brow suspiciously and then snorted. “It’s a bag you use if you get sick and you gotta hurl. Are you screwing with me right now?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. I suppose, yes, it’s possible someone could vomit if they get motion sickness.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So, this was what was commonly referred to as a “barf bag.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“These weren’t placed on the buses during the Uninhabited Island Exam, for example, though. Are they not always available, then?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had ridden buses several times now, but this was the first time I had seen them placed in the pouches on the&#160;backs of seats like this. I supposed it was done out of consideration for us, the passengers, and for the bus company as well. It would be much more difficult to clean up if someone were to spray vomit all over the seats and the floor of the bus, after all. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though I thought that I’d learned quite a lot, there were still countless things I didn’t understand. If I could make it outside of school, I’d likely encounter the unknown quite often.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re as weird as ever,” said Ryuuen. “What, are you some kinda rich kid who never rode a bus before or somethin’?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s true that I haven’t had much experience,” I admitted.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had seen plenty of children who vomited due to issues of semicircular canal imbalance, but never in an environment where we were allowed to vomit into a bag, like here. I didn’t think it was unreasonable for me to ask, though, since I hadn’t considered that they might be suggesting it was okay to throw up. I had experienced some degree of motion sickness myself a few times, so I’d keep in mind that there were conveniences such as these out there in the world.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">A</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">FTER WE FINISHED</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> having our lunch in the large cafeteria connected to the ski resort, we second-year students were finally about to begin our skiing lessons. We were told that we were forbidden from taking our cell phones up on the slopes due to the substantial risk of losing or breaking them. Some students voiced their dissatisfaction over this—the ones who were dependent on their phones, and the students who were high-level skiers, who claimed that they could handle themselves just fine—but we couldn’t break school rules, so there was nothing we could do. Fortunately, though, the school had also informed us that, from tomorrow onward, we were allowed to bring our phones along if we chose to go to the ski resort by ourselves. However, in the event we lost or broke our phones, it would cost us a considerable amount of Private Points. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I went ahead and put on the skiwear that I had rented, then picked up my ski boots. The exterior of the boots seemed to be made of plastic. Following the instructions, I unbuckled them, opened the inner lining, and put my feet inside. After making sure that the heels fit properly, I straightened the inner lining and fastened the buckles from bottom to top. Lastly, I put on the booster straps and powder guard.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">And with that, I had apparently completed the bare-minimum level of preparation. I tried to walk forward like normal, but that didn’t seem to be the right kind of motion. When I did as the instructor said, though, and walked on my heels, I was able to move easily. Once I was ready, I went outside. We were divided into three separate groups for lessons: advanced, intermediate, and beginner. Having no experience with skiing, I didn’t hesitate to go with the beginner group, joining up with the crowd of others interested in being part of it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I could have looked up skiing in books or online ahead of time, but I hadn’t wanted to pick up any extraneous information before the trip; that way I could learn on-site, since that was how the trip was structured. About 60 percent of the students in our grade had requested to join the beginner’s course. I wasn’t sure if that was a lot or a little, but I was slightly surprised that about 40 percent of students were in the intermediate or advanced levels. It didn’t seem to me that people in the Kanto area had many opportunities to go skiing, but these students must have had at least some experience.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Out of group six, there was no sign of Ryuuen, Kitou, Nishino, or Kushida, perhaps because they were in the intermediate or advanced courses. The rest of us were apparently beginners. There were a lot of people in the beginner’s course, so they had subdivided us into groups of ten. The instructor taught us how to ski, starting with the basics. Though I was deeply curious about the skiing equipment, which I was using for the very first time, I made sure to listen to the instructions attentively. Meanwhile, the advanced group, which had the fewest people out of the three major groups, were given just a brief explanation—then they were free to start skiing straight away, and they were already getting ready to hit the slopes. Ryuuen was among them.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I brushed the snow off the soles of my boots. Then, I took a step forward on my heel, aligning the bindings on the front and back of my boots. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">I see</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">. I walked forward with both feet in the same position. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">I see… Okay, for the time being, I’ll…</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I’d assumed that Ryuuen had already headed toward the lift, but he was still there. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Be careful!” the instructor warned me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I bowed slightly and apologized to the instructor, then proceeded to do as instructed. Afterward, we each got to try some actual skiing. Surprisingly enough, a lot of people fell down. I had a couple of unintentional spills, but I was starting to get a general feel for it. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It took about thirty minutes to get through the lesson. After we completed all the steps, we were free to do what we wanted.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">A</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">FTER THE TRAINING WAS OVER</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> Watanabe and some others got together to head to the beginner’s course, which looked to be a gentle slope. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re not coming, Ayanokouji?” he asked, puzzled.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, okay. All right, see you later, then.”</span></p>

I couldn’t imagine that she had consciously chosen to go for the advanced course. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Seems like Yamamura wasn’t kidding about what she said back on the bus, about how she kind of blends into the background,” I remarked.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I like settlin’ things with my fists, since that’s easy to understand, but I guess we can’t do that, huh?” said Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">At any rate, I was grateful that they seemed to at least have the prudence not to get violent.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thanks, Ayanokouji, for saying what I wanted to say,” said Watanabe.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No big deal; I didn’t really say anything special.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s not true, though. At least to me, you did, anyway. You can take the other edge, if you want.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Was it just the students in Ichinose’s class who all seemed to be fundamentally composed of goodness? I hadn’t even asked him for the space, yet he just gave the edge to me. With that decided, there’d be either Ryuuen or Kitou on the far end, and Watanabe would be next to that person. The person in the third futon would be whoever lost this contest. And then I’d be sleeping on the end closest to the entrance to the room.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Besides, I need to build up a little tolerance anyway,” said Watanabe.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Apparently, one of the reasons why Watanabe had given up that spot was for a personal matter. Of course, I was sure that the idea of being sandwiched between Ryuuen and Kitou wasn’t all that thrilling, though.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In the blink of an eye, Ryuuen was already clutching a pillow in his hands. “I guess people always think of this sort of thing whenever someone mentions a school trip, eh? It’s gonna be a one-on-one fight. And I’m sure you don’t even need to hear the rules, huh, Kitou?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course not,” he replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What?” I cocked my head to the side. “What are you going to use the pillow for?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t know what awaited us now that this change had been made.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Come on, school trips, pillows?” Ryuuen said. “Put those two together and there’s only one thing it could mean, right?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Only one thing? I had no clue… However, all the other students aside from me appeared to understand, and Kushida quickly stood up.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“A-all right then, in that case, why don’t I act as referee?” offered Kushida, seeming regretful at having been placed into such an absurd position. “It might be better if we can have someone judge this impartially.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wow, you’re so disciplined, Kushida-chan, even at a time like this,” marveled Watanabe.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I would’ve loved to have heard what Kushida honestly thought, but the other girls were close by, as well as Watanabe. Anyway, more importantly, I was deeply interested in seeing what they were going to be using the pillows for.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll let you take the first one,” said Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’d best not give it to me,” Kitou replied. “You don’t want to be defeated without taking a single shot, do you? Come at me with no regrets, Ryuuen.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ryuuen smacked the pillow against the top of his hand, smirking, with a </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">pomf, pomf</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> sound.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, I ain’t gonna hold back. I’m gonna kill ya, Kitou!” Ryuuen snarled.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">With that, he swung wide, chucking the pillow like it were a ball. The pillow, which was stuffed with buckwheat chaff, flew at Kitou at high speed. Although there was a fair distance between the two of them, the pillow was thrown with such intensity that it wouldn’t be surprising if whoever was hit by it got knocked off their feet and lost. And yet Kitou calmly and precisely caught the pillow.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Now, I’m going to kill you!” Kitou threw the pillow back, and his own throw had no less intensity than Ryuuen’s. Ryuuen then proceeded to catch it in magnificent fashion, and he immediately switched his stance to throw it again.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Heh, not bad, Kitou! Looks like I might have a little fun! Hraah!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Once again, the pillow was returned to Kitou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This is…” I muttered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“A pillow fight,” said Kushida. “You’ve never done this kind of thing before, Ayanokouji-kun? I had the impression that boys were always having pillow fights, though—you know, on trips in elementary school and junior high, or at summer camp and stuff.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This was the first I had ever heard of it. No one had engaged in anything like a pillow fight even during the mixed camp last year.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Darkness Ball!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Go, my rampaging great serpent, devour him!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Darkness and a great serpent, huh? The humble pillow was being transformed into all sorts of things.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“U-um, this is supposed to be…a pillow fight, right?” mumbled Amikura, watching the pillow fly back and forth, from right to left and back again.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">A one-on-one fight to the death, where no one else would be permitted to intervene, not even in the event of an emergency…is what this wasn’t. It was supposed to be a pillow fight to decide who would sleep where. The deadly “life-or-death battle” raged on for several minutes more, with no conclusion in sight. Neither opponent seemed to be at risk of exhausting their stamina; it looked like they could continue fighting for a long time. However, all the rest of us present aside from those two engaged in combat understood that we were now faced with a dilemma.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is that pillow going to be okay if they keep throwing it that hard? It’s already looking so beat-up,” murmured Kushida calmly, which drew everyone’s attention to the pillow in question.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was sure that no one needed this to be explained, but pillows weren’t items meant for throwing. Now, if you were just gently tossing it, that’d be one thing, but there was no way that the pillow wouldn’t get increasingly damaged after being hurled back and forth with no restraint, full force, in a succession of blazing-fast pitches.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"></div>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span><img alt="" height="100%" id="INTERIORIMAGES_0" src="/hola-lector-agua/images/books/y2v8/OEBPS/Images/INTERIORIMAGES_0.jpg" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style=" top: 0px; vertical-align: top; height: 100%;"/></span></p>
<div style="page-break-after: always;"></div>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That reminds me, whose pillow is that, anyway?” asked Watanabe suddenly, checking the futons that were laid out on the floor.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Out of the four futons there, the pillow was missing from the one on the far edge, the one that Watanabe had let me have.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Guess it’s mine,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t have the item that I should have had on my futon. At that very moment, in fact, Kitou seemed to be clutching it tightly in his hands, pouring yet more of his darkness power into it. I could tell quite well that the pillow was screaming in agony.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m pretty sure you’d get nightmares if you slept with that pillow,” muttered Watanabe.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Well, actually, I’d say that the fact there was no guarantee that the pillow would even keep its shape was terrifying in the first place. Whichever side won, I’d have liked my pillow to be returned to me safely.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hmph!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kitou had poured more intense killing intent into his throw this time than ever before. Perhaps it was because Kitou’s thick fingers were digging into it so tightly, but the pillow exploded the moment it left his hand. The cloth ripped open, and the buckwheat chaff that had been stuffed inside was now scattered across the room. Everyone fell silent as we heard the pieces fall everywhere. The pillow that was supposed to have gently supported my head had now been reduced to a tragic state. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Oh, pillow, I wished so strongly for you to be returned safely to me, and yet you weren’t, were you…?</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">I would like to express my deepest condolences to the victim that was cruelly cut down on this battlefield.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ugh, boys. I can’t help thinking they’re such m… I mean, they’re simply children, aren’t they?” muttered Kushida, quietly enough so that only I could hear her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The buckwheat chaff that had scattered across the room had also become as still as death. Neither Ryuuen nor Kitou seemed to be particularly bothered by that, though, and their fingers were already reaching for the nearest pillow at hand. That was when Nishino spoke up, raising her voice.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, listen. We don’t exactly have all the time in the world here, so can you guys finish whatever this is later? It’s a hassle.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ryuuen ignored her warning and looked like he was going to continue, but Kitou was not. He sat down without a word, deciding to put this matter on hold for the time being. His previous feelings had cooled, and he sensed the frustration of those around him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, this mean you lost, then, Kitou?” asked Ryuuen.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If she says that it’s a bother, then I have no intention of being a bother anymore,” said Kitou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Considering the impression he usually gave me, he had pulled back so quickly that it was almost shocking. Well, knowing things had turned out this way, I wished they hadn’t had the pillow fight in the first place. At the very least, I could have avoided having my pillow becoming a sacrifice, with its innards tragically strewn about.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay, well then… First, let’s get this cleaned up,” said Kushida. “And then, after we’re done, let’s chat.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">With all the guys, except for Ryuuen, and all the girls helping out, we successfully managed to gather the debris from the pillow in short order. I’d have to get a new pillow from the ryokan staff later, I supposed. But I agonized over whether I should tell them the truth or lie. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The buckwheat chaff that had been scattered all over the room was collected and placed in a translucent plastic bag in the trash bin. Then, our discussion began. Kushida, as a matter of course, began the discussion, speaking for the group.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, about our free time, we just need to be back to the ryokan by seven p.m. for the final check-in for dinner, right?” said Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Amikura immediately got into the discussion. “Yeah. It really feels like we have the entire day free in that case, doesn’t it?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We could take a train or a bus or something to get somewhere relatively quickly, but… What do you all think? Nishino-san, is there anywhere you want to go?” Kushida asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wanna go skiing, I guess,” Nishino replied. “We basically just practiced all day today, so I didn’t get enough real skiing in. And we’re here in Hokkaido, after all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I agree with Nishino,” I added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I felt it would be a waste if I’d only spent half a day skiing by the time this trip was over, now that I had gone through the trouble of learning how. Kitou silently expressed his agreement as well with a casual raise of his hand.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sounds like quite a few people want to ski, then,” said Kushida. “Watanabe-kun, Yamamura-san, what about you?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t really have any objections, myself</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em; letter-spacing: 0.60mm;">,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">” said Watanabe. “I mean, we’re going around the city on the third day, so that sounds fine to me, y’know?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t mind going anywhere,” said Yamamura.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even Yamamura, who still couldn’t ski very well yet, didn’t seem particularly reluctant about the idea. Was she just going along with everyone else around her, or did she simply want to get better at skiing? I couldn’t really tell what her feelings were in that area.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Mako-chan, what about you?” asked Kushida.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hmm. Well, I’m not that good at skiing, so I can’t say that I’m really thrilled. But if everyone else wants to ski, I’m okay with it. We are a group, after all,” said Amikura, demonstrating a willingness to concede completely.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida didn’t put forth her own opinion in the discussion, instead turning to Ryuuen, who was sitting in an armchair. “What about you, Ryuuen-kun?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do whatever you want,” he snapped.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Apparently, he wasn’t advocating anything in particular, casually abandoning his right to speak. Once Ryuuen, the most troublesome member of the group, had issued that statement, a wave of relief washed over us. It was probably better to assume that Ryuuen also felt he’d enjoy skiing, rather than just not having any interest in going anywhere.</span></p>
