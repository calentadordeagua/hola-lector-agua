<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 2:<br/>Ibuki Mio has<br/>Surprisingly Good Sense</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"> </p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">“S</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">pecial test.” Those words would normally mean a written exam, or some kind of athletic trial. However, at the Advanced Nurturing High School, special tests weren’t trivial affairs. A contest of survival on an uninhabited island. A battle of wits aboard a cruise ship. Those outlandish tests came one after another during our summer vacation.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Between the two tests, I, a mere first-year student, had only seven days of actual rest. That included today. Soon, the second semester would begin. I’d spent the days off rather simply. I didn’t call anyone, and no one called me. In other words, it had been lonely.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I don’t really care,” I said aloud.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The freedom was enough. I wouldn’t ask for more. Having too many friends wasn’t necessarily a good thing. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The more people I connected with, the more troublesome it would become to manage all those relationships. If a friend called me, I might have been thoroughly overjoyed. But there was also a chance that I wouldn’t have been.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even alone, there were many things for me to do. I was doing one of those things today: using my phone to check my remaining point balance (106,219 points). </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I transferred 100,000 of those points to someone from my class—Sudou Ken. Soon afterwards, Sudou called me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yo, Ayanokouji. What are you doing right now?” he asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nothing much. Was just thinking about what to have for dinner.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Gotcha. I just ate some chicken tenders a little while ago. Pretty basic stuff. Tastes all right, but I’d definitely get tired of them if I ate them too often, so I try to change things up a bit. I can fry ’em, boil ’em… Wait, what the hell am I talking about? I wanted to talk to you about fortune-telling.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Fortune-telling? I never expected those words to come out of Sudou’s mouth. He was the sort of guy who saw the world in black and white. He liked simplicity, like the chicken he just ate.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Truth is, I’ve heard there’s this crazy-accurate fortune-teller over at the Keyaki Mall, but they’re only gonna be there during summer vacation. I guess it’s big among the seniors. Even when I’m doing club stuff, all anyone talks about is that fortune-teller. I got some extra cash, so I felt like having some fun, you know? Let’s check it out together. My treat, of course.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The Keyaki Mall was a complex on the school grounds that students often went to. Because students were forced to live on the school grounds, the school was equipped with all sorts of facilities, although it couldn’t give us all the options of the outside world. We didn’t have idol concerts, amusement parks, or zoos. Our world was small. When something new cropped up, it naturally became a hot topic among the students. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even so, fortune-telling was unexpected. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Since no one had invited me to hang out in a long, long time, I couldn’t hide my happiness. “When are we going?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Tomorrow morning. Sounds like it starts at ten, but apparently, if you don’t get in line early, you’ll be waiting forever. I wanted to get there around 9:30,” said Sudou. Apparently, he’d already planned out a schedule. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m fine with that, since I don’t have plans. But will your club be cool with it?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. Tomorrow’s my day off. The tournament ended, so it’s fine. Besides, we’ve done nothing but train our butts off. If they don’t let us rest a little, our bodies will fall apart,” he told me matter-of-factly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sudou had just played in a basketball tournament. Even though I saw him quietly practicing day after day in preparation, I was admittedly kind of curious about the tournament’s results. One more thing to think about.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Did you have any </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">trouble</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> at all?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I made sure to stress the word “trouble.” Sudou understood what I was referring to.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. It was pretty tough. I mean, they supervise you way more—you have the team captain and the coach watching you. It’s nothing like playing in junior high. I can’t open my mouth at all, except when we need to talk during a game. The school puts a ton of restrictions on us, even on bathroom breaks. I thought it was going to be impossible,” Sudou said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though club activities were separate from coursework, the school rules were still strict.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Anyway, it all worked out. I did pretty well, ’cause I got the chops,” he added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. Well, that’s a relief. What about Yamauchi?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I made sure to delete the data before heading back,” Sudou said, once again referring to our secret business. “I wouldn’t worry. I mean, even I’m not that dumb.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sudou’s school life rode on this. He probably wouldn’t do anything reckless. Even so, it might be a good idea to get in touch with Yamauchi later, to confirm that the data was successfully deleted. Just in case.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“By the way, did you actually get to play in the big game?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. I was the only one out of all the first years to play. I even scored. We still lost in the end, though, so I’m not exactly that proud.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t really understand the intricacies, but a first-year student playing in a game like that seemed like a pretty big deal. I sensed acceptance, rather than frustration, from Sudou. He’d probably practiced hard for the tournament, and saw this as steadily making progress.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He had to have practiced hard, since the first-year students left the school grounds to participate in those special exams. That meant Sudou had had less time to practice than the older students.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, what are you gonna do? Fortune-telling—you gonna go or not?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I don’t really have anything planned. So, sure, I’ll go.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Now that I’d agreed, Sudou cut straight to the chase. “Definitely invite Suzune out, too. Definitely. Understand?” he said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see,” I answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Apparently, Sudou didn’t actually want to go see the fortune teller with me, but rather, with Horikita. He probably knew that, if he invited her himself, his chances of success were rather slim. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, it’s just… I can’t imagine that she’s interested in fortune-telling,” I added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even so, invite her. This is like your one special skill, right?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">What kind of special skill? I really wish he’d quit trying to use me as some kind of Horikita Invitation Machine.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I’ll give it a shot. But don’t expect anything,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Giving it a shot isn’t good enough,” he answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Not good enough?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sudou’s emphatic words contained traces of anger. He seemed to plan on Horikita definitely being there tomorrow. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You absolutely need to do it. If you don’t invite Horikita out, there’s no point,” he told me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Look, I don’t know what plans she might have tomorrow. And I don’t know if she even has any interest in fortune-telling. Wouldn’t it be easier to invite her to go shopping or check out a film festival?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s nothing to worry about. All girls love fortune-telling,” he answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was an extremely broad generalization. I couldn’t really imagine Horikita showing any curiosity about things that ordinary girls liked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Got it? Later, after you invite her, get in touch with me. Okay? You gotta.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">With that, Sudou abruptly ended the call. I’d thought it was strange for Sudou to invite me to check out a fortune-teller. Well, it seemed as though he was really after Horikita. While I did feel a little disappointed, I needed to call Horikita right away. If Sudou found out later that I ignored his request, it would be a huge headache. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita answered her phone immediately.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hey, Horikita. Do you like fortune-telling?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If there was one woman in the whole world capable of destroying anyone’s preconceived notion that all girls liked fortune-telling, it was Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You open conversations in the strangest way,” she answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">True. But I didn’t really have anything else to say. “You’ll be saving me if you give me an answer.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, if I don’t answer you, there’s a possibility that you won’t be saved?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was certainly a possibility. The image of Sudou putting me in a headlock stuck in my mind.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, are you going to save me?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you’re comfortable owing me one,” she answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I’d have to owe her one </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">just</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> for answering a question? I was filled with the urge to hit the “end call” button, but I pictured Sudou’s angry face and resisted.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Fine. If you answer me, I’ll owe you,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita, realizing her answer’s value, paused for a moment. “I see. Well, I’m not exactly an enthusiast or anything, but it would be a lie to say I dislike fortune-telling.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This was unexpected. “Have you ever had your fortune told before?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course I haven’t. I’ve simply noticed horoscopes and such in the morning news.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She was probably talking about the daily horoscope things that were usually based on your birth month. I had a hard time picturing Horikita as the type of girl who would go change her clothes, or buy new accessories, if someone on TV said her lucky colors were red and white.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you addicted to getting your fortune told, by chance?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, that’s not it. There’ve been some rumors going around lately about a fortune-teller. Have you heard?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“A fortune-teller?” Horikita fell silent for a while, then eventually answered in a tone that sounded as though she understood. “Yes, there does seem to be quite the uproar. I’ve heard about it.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I was a little curious. They say that the fortunes are really accurate, so I was wondering </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">how </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">accurate. But I can’t honestly imagine that something like fortune-telling would be so spot-on.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I expected her to agree with me, but she apparently held a different opinion.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Indeed? I think that someone with real power could be accurate.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No way. You’d have to be psychic,” I replied. The power to predict the future by reading a person’s face or hands, or based on their birthdate? That was ridiculous, and I didn’t believe it existed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, that’s not what I mean. Fortune-tellers don’t have the power to divine the future. That’s obvious, isn’t it? That would be as foolish as someone saying that they believe in ghosts. However, the big difference between fortune-tellers and psychics is that fortune-tellers give readings based on a massive amount of past data. In other words, they interpret patterns to understand people.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita wasn’t a fanciful girl. Her answer was grounded in logic and reason. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In other words, you’re talking about fortune-tellers’ ability to cold read, right?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re rather cheeky, but I guess you </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">do</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> know some things.” Horikita sounded slightly amused. “We can’t evaluate ourselves objectively. However, a good fortune-teller can extract information from the person they’re reading through a short conversation. They can pick up on things that the person themselves hasn’t noticed. Don’t you think so?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Cold reading. Quite literally, that term meant reading someone’s mind without prior preparation. It referred to a technique which extracted information through casual conversation, and thus, gave the person being read the impression that you knew more than you did. You used observation and deduction to obtain information about your target, then made the person believe that you could see their past and future. It sounded simple, but actually, being able to extract information without the mark noticing was incredibly difficult. It required a high degree of skill.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m a little interested,” I admitted.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s good. I think you should go,” Horikita said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How about you come along?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you joking?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, I’m quite serious.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I refuse,” she answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She’d shot me down instantly, but I couldn’t just accept that. “I’m a total novice when it comes to fortune-telling. I thought it’d be good to have you along, Horikita, so I understand better.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry, but I’ll pass. You know I don’t enjoy dealing with crowds, right?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was certainly true. Fortune-telling was so popular, a lot of very excited students were bound to be there. Some adults from campus might go, too. I certainly couldn’t imagine Horikita wanting to enter a crowded place like that.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If I pushed any further, I’d probably just end up souring Horikita’s mood. As far as I was concerned, I’d received her message loud and clear. Sudou probably wouldn’t be that big a problem, anyway. Probably. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After I ended the call, I shot him a brief message. I immediately noticed the “read” marker pop up. Shortly afterward, I received a disgruntled reply.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Then forget about it,” the message read.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I existed only as a means for Sudou to invite Horikita. Since I’d failed to invite her, Sudou had no further use for me. Well, it probably would’ve been a little weird for two dudes to see a fortune-teller together.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even so… A fortune-teller, huh?” I murmured aloud.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After that conversation with Horikita, my interest was piqued. I decided to head over tomorrow and check the fortune-teller out.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-before: always;"/>
<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">2.1</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"> </p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">W</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">ho in their right mind thought it’d be a good idea to go see a fortune-teller?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I might have messed up,” I muttered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The intense late-August heat blazed down upon me. I saw a shimmering heat haze ahead, above the concrete, and through the roadside trees. The dorm rooms came equipped with air conditioning, so we didn’t feel the heat much indoors. However, it </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">was</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> summer, and in direct sunlight, you instantly started sweating buckets. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The heat reduced people to complete lumps. I desperately sought out shade. Fortunately, the school boasted a rather expansive campus littered with tons of trees. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was 9:30 A.M., well before most student activities, as I made my way towards the fabled fortune-teller. Fortune-telling services were supposed to begin at ten, but I didn’t plan to stick around for long. I’d quickly get my fortune read, then leave just as quickly. That was my plan. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But, as I neared my destination, I realized that my plans were about to be shattered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I expected Keyaki Mall to be nearly empty. Instead, numerous students lounged around in their summer clothes. I prayed that they weren’t all there for the same reason I was, but I suspected they were. I decided to at least escape the blazing hellscape by entering the building, and started looking for the elevator, since the fortune-teller was on the fifth floor.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Geh.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I let out a grumble. Nearly ten students were hanging around in front of the elevator. I wondered if any of them would understand my plight. Whenever I took elevators alone, I was the sort to push the “close” button repeatedly. I wasn’t good at riding elevators with large groups of people my age. I’d need a great deal of courage to mingle with that crowd.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Although it’d be inconvenient, I decided to take a detour and get on a different elevator. Another elevator, across the way, was far less crowded.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Calm down,” I muttered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Getting to the other elevator took more time and effort, but the peace of mind it brought me was a lifesaver. After I reached the fifth floor, I made my way toward the fortune-teller. However, an even more uncomfortable situation awaited me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There are couples everywhere.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Boys and girls stood in pairs all over. A number of them seemed to be in relationships. Of course, there were groups of only boys and only girls, but those were far fewer. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Seeing a fortune-teller about a couple’s future and romantic compatibility was common, I supposed. However, it made this visit significantly more awkward than I’d anticipated. Not many people had come alone to have their fortune told. Single guys were fewer still.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In any case, a line was forming. I decided to queue up. When I did so, a woman scanning the area called out to me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Good morning. Will your partner be joining you later?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Partner? Uh, no, it’s just me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Of course, it wasn’t exactly odd for her to ask me that question, but she’d put it rather strangely. I wished she’d be more sensitive to single people.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Umm…” The line attendant looked at me with a sheepish expression. “I’m sorry. I’m afraid that, in order to have your fortune read today, you need a partner. So…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I can’t have my fortune read if I’m alone?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She gave a small nod and pointed at a written notice. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Guidance is for couples only. We humbly ask for your understanding</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I now saw why Sudou had insisted on inviting Horikita. He and Horikita would’ve had to line up together and talk to each other.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That means he never wanted me along, from the very start,” I muttered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sudou’s behavior took on an entirely different meaning, now. He’d never really wanted to invite me. He probably would’ve found some excuse to send me packing once he had Horikita to himself. That was pretty sad.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Out of curiosity, is the rule the same for the line next to me?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes. Ukon-sensei is only reading fortunes for couples,” the woman answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I understand.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I bowed my head and slipped out of the line. The students behind me advanced one step forward. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I couldn’t believe I’d fallen for something like this. I’d pictured a lone woman sitting on a street corner, being paid in small coins, but reality had other plans. Apparently, this sort of romantic divination was all the rage right now. I would’ve liked to try the fortune-teller’s services, but there was nothing I could really do. Trying to invite Horikita again would have been a waste of time. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I decided to just slink away quietly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? So, I can’t have my fortune read alone?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I heard an angry voice. Another victim of singleness was apparently standing in the next line. Feeling somewhat sympathetic, I glanced over. Unfortunately, I caught that person’s eye.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was Ibuki Mio, a Class C student. When I pretended I hadn’t seen her, she chased after me. I quickened my pace.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait!” Perhaps Ibuki thought that I was trying to run away (which I certainly was). She grabbed me by the shoulder.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you want something?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Where’s Horikita?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She looked around as she asked this. Ibuki was sort of like Sudou; she saw me as little more than a Horikita whisperer. I wished she’d just go see Horikita directly and not involve me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s not like I’m with her all the time or anything. I’m alone today,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah, I see.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">During the deserted island test, Ibuki had been sent to infiltrate Class D. Her mission was to spy on us and stir up chaos, and she and Horikita ended up trading blows in a fistfight. Since then, Ibuki had been rather hostile toward Horikita. She probably considered them archenemies.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Although Ibuki was her usual crabby, unsociable self, she had pretty good fashion sense. She looked great. With a slightly different attitude, she could have been popular.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Fortune-telling is usually something you do one-on-one, isn’t it? I really didn’t expect this at all. Don’t you agree?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. That’s what I thought, anyway.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, are you going to invite Horikita or something?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">First Sudou, now Ibuki. It seemed like Horikita was the sole topic of conversation whenever I was involved.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, I’m not. If you want to talk to Horikita, why don’t you reach out to her yourself? Try telling her that you want to go have your fortunes read together.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? Absolutely not. It’s not like I have anything to talk to her about.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">If that’s true, then I really wish you’d stop bringing her up.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-before: always;"/>
<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><img alt="" height="100%" id="ClassroomElite-Vol4_5_INTERIORILLUSTRATIONS_" src="/hola-lector-agua/images/books/v4.5/OPS/ClassroomElite-Vol4.5_INTERIORILLUSTRATIONS_.jpg" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style=" top: 0px; vertical-align: top; height: 100%;"/></p>
<div style="page-break-before: always;"/>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wasn’t really that interested in having my fortune read in the first place, so I’m fine. How about you?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’d be lying if I said that I wasn’t annoyed, but…” Ibuki shook her head in frustration. “Well, I don’t really have any choice but to give up. I’m bad at making conversation, anyway.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That answer sounded fishy. Ibuki said she was bad at conversation, but unlike Sakura, she had never struck me as a person who struggled with that kind of thing. In fact, she seemed comfortable talking to me on equal terms—or comfortable being condescending, at least.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You could invite Ryuuen,” I offered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I said that half-jokingly, but Ibuki shot me a scornful glare packed with disgust. “You’re joking, right? I hate seeing his face, even when I have to. I’d rather avoid him during my holidays.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But you were always together back on the ship, weren’t you? Isn’t it normal to think that you two might be close?” I pointed out.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ibuki looked away. “That was because it was my fault that I didn’t discover Class D’s leader,” she answered quietly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If that were true, Ibuki had only been working with Ryuuen to atone for her failure. That didn’t explain everything, but I supposed only Class C knew the real reason. That said, Ibuki </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">had</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> learned our leader’s identity during the survival challenge on the island. She discovered that Horikita was the leader, and she hadn’t been wrong. She would have contributed significantly to her class if I hadn’t thwarted her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wanted to ask. During the survival test, who was Class D’s leader?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Who knows?” I shrugged.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“‘Who knows?’ It’s not like you don’t know.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even if I did, I couldn’t tell you. Honestly, I don’t know. Most of us in Class D have no idea. I think Horikita acted in secret, and somehow managed to pull everything off on her own. That’s the only way I can explain what happened.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ibuki seemed to look right through me. However, I wasn’t the sort of person to be easily exposed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If Ryuuen’s not an option, why don’t you invite a girl from your class? I’m sure you must have a friend or two,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If I did, I wouldn’t be in this situation. I absolutely despise the girls in my class,” she answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ibuki was just like Horikita. Actually, she was probably even more antisocial than Horikita. With the right opportunity, the two might get along.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You know, you’re making conversation with me just fine right now. Shouldn’t you be able to talk to anyone else, too? I don’t think you’re bad at dealing with people,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s not true. When you talk to me, you feel something, don’t you? I’m all prickly.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, yeah. I guess so.” Interacting with Ibuki always made me feel like meat being sliced by a sharp knife. That was the closest I could come to describing her desire for isolation. I’m sure other students felt the same way.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No matter what I do, the mood always seems to turn sour. Understand?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I still had my doubts about Ibuki actually being bad at making conversation, but she was undoubtedly standoffish, even with her own classmates. I could imagine her headstrong attitude challenging the fortune-teller.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you’re bad at communicating, I’m surprised you’d want to get your fortune read.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s the problem. I’m like someone who loves cats, but has an allergy,” she answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That certainly sounded frustrating. “But you did really well spying on Class D,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Crabby and unsociable as she might be, Ibuki hadn’t seemed at all disagreeable while she was working as a spy. Our class had accepted her without suspicion.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s different. In any case, talking to people makes me anxious. And when I’m anxious, it puts me on edge. I hate it. It’s not like I </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">want </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">to be this way—wait, why am I even telling </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">you </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">this? People might get the wrong impression about us!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Flustered, Ibuki abruptly turned away. Really, that should’ve been my line. Everyone who’d been queueing near us had moved ahead, and it was just the two of us. The other students might easily have misinterpreted that.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So, talking to people made Ibuki anxious, huh? If that were true, the solution might be unexpectedly easy. Even if I didn’t know the root cause of her anxiety, I could work with this.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Earlier, you said that being a spy was different, didn’t you?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. Because it’s a fact.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What’s the difference between then and now?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ibuki fell silent for a little while. “I don’t know. It’s just different,” she said finally. It seemed like she’d given up on trying to articulate the difference.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You haven’t given it much thought,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, it’s not like I can explain why they’re different. I was just acting.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, I think it’s simple. The difference between you now, and you then, is awareness.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Awareness?” Ibuki turned toward me, her interest slightly piqued.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Your anxiety comes from being hyper-aware of situations. You project your insecurities onto others, so you freeze up when you meet someone for the first time,” I explained.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What are you talking about? I mean, maybe it’s different for people who are good at communicating, but pretty much everyone gets nervous when they meet someone for the first time, don’t they?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course. I’m the same way. But would you still feel nervous if you were talking to the clerk at a convenience store?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The clerk at a convenience store you visit often. ‘Do you have a point card? Would you like that warmed up?’ You don’t get anxious when the clerk asks you those questions, right?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, th-that’s…” she stammered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If you thought of communication as a skill, like athleticism, it was simple. You had to train your talents. Ibuki got nervous because she was acutely aware of who she was talking to</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">. I wonder what they think of me. I want them to like me. I hope they’re a good person.</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When Ibuki infiltrated Class D, she probably didn’t have time to consider those things. She was playing a part, not thinking about how people would react to her real self. And she usually gave off an outcast vibe anyway, which worked as the perfect disguise.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I suppose, when you put it that way, you have a point,” she muttered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You were ready to talk to the fortune-teller face-to-face. It’s natural to feel anxious, but there’s no reason to. If you don’t think about communication too hard, that should relieve some of the tension.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. Wait, why the hell are </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">you</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> lecturing me?” Ibuki glared, looking ready to pounce. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“When you’ve been a loner for a long time, you understand these kinds of things. You wonder why you can’t make friends, and you think about the differences between the people who make you nervous and those who don’t. Finally, you think about where people come from and where they’re going.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re creepy. You seem like the sort who’ll turn out to be a mass murderer later in life. Were you always like this?” Ibuki asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“More or less.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Things had veered into rather awkward territory. I probably came across as a weirdo.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I’m heading back. How about you?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll probably head back, too. I can have my fortune read alone, anyway. I was really interested in </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">tenchuusatsu</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">, though,” she said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Tenchuusatsu</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That wasn’t exactly the kind of word you heard all that often.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait. You came without even knowing what that is?” Ibuki sighed in exasperation. “Put simply, it’s a type of fortune-telling that tells you what times are unlucky for you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Was it really possible to pinpoint an aspect of someone’s destiny like that? My knowledge of fortune-telling was limited to superstitions like “wear the color red,” and “be careful not to lose something this month.” However, Ibuki made it sound like there was far more to fortune-telling.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s why I came. Fortune-telling isn’t all about romance and such.” Ibuki sounded disappointed as she looked back at the long line. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Maybe some of them came here to check out </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">tenchuusatsu</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">, or whatever it’s called,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even so, I get the feeling that the romance stuff is what they’re after, since the fortune-teller is forcing us to visit in pairs,” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">And with that, Ibuki left.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-before: always;"/>
<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">2.2</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"> </p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">A</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">fter I returned to my dorm room, I did some research into </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">tenchuusatsu</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">. It was an incredibly deep subject. Before 1980, </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">tenchuusatsu</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> had been something of a hot topic worldwide. However, as its popularity boomed, people started to doubt its credibility. There was a news story about a famous fortune-teller who was forced to retire after he dropped </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">tenchuusatsu</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Fortune-telling might have some value, even if depending too much on it was bad. From a true believer’s perspective, it probably seemed fairly accurate.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">With that thought in mind, curiosity overcame me. Yet I couldn’t believe what I read online. It was impossible to divine the future. I wanted to try </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">tenchuusatsu</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> out for myself, to see if it was a lie. I wanted what the fortune-teller was doing to be simply an extension of cold reading.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wonder if </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">tenchuusatsu</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> is only being offered this month?” I muttered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Apparently, the fortune-teller’s crew was leaving when summer vacation ended. There was no information on whether they’d return. A fortune-teller might never visit this school again.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t have anyone to invite. I was completely out of options. Horikita would just turn me down if I asked, and I didn’t have the courage to invite Kushida. I felt like Sakura would probably come with me, but if I brought her to a place positively teeming with couples, it’d make her uncomfortable. After that were the guys, like Sudou, Ike, and Yamauchi, but they probably didn’t want to spend their few precious remaining days of summer vacation getting their fortunes read with another dude.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m stuck, huh?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Besides, I didn’t like this couples-only requirement. Ibuki and I agreed on that. It seemed like a huge turn-off for people with a genuine interest in fortune-telling. I gave up and stopped my online research.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-before: always;"/>
<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">2.3</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"> </p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">he day after I’d given up, though, I found myself moving toward the fortune-teller again. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yet another bizarre meeting; fate had reunited me with Ibuki. We happened upon the same place at the exact same time.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why did you come here again? And by yourself?” Ibuki looked disgusted. I got the impression that I thoroughly repulsed her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I could ask you the same question,” I countered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I said that I like fortune-telling, didn’t I? I thought maybe I could have my fortune read, even if I’m alone,” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So, Ibuki came here hoping the rules had changed since yesterday. I wondered if she really </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">did</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> like fortune-telling that much, and if so, which part.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m going to ask you something directly. Ibuki, do you believe in fortune-telling?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you saying I shouldn’t?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No. But it’s generally not something people start to believe in all of a sudden, right?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Not everyone understood that fortune-telling was the mere application of techniques such as cold reading. Many genuinely believed in divination’s mysterious power.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I suppose a lot of people start out thinking that way. But if you can’t move past that, fortune-telling’s probably not for you,” Ibuki said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you’re saying that nonbelievers aren’t qualified to have their fortunes read?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, that’s not it. Let me put it this way. It’s not like I believe in fortune-telling unconditionally. However, people who are overly skeptical from the very start won’t get anything out of it. People who make fun of fortune-telling often lead lives riddled with contradictions themselves. They’ll say they don’t believe in kami or in Buddha, for instance—but when they’re in trouble, they call on a higher power for help, don’t they?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was a good argument. Gods didn’t exist, and neither did things like ghosts. Yet many who made caustic statements like “God doesn’t exist” would still visit shrines to bring in good fortune for the New Year. They’d pray for freedom from disease, success in business, or fulfillment in love. They’d clasp their hands and say, “Please, Kami-sama, listen to my prayers.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">What you believed in and what you wished for were infinitely different, and no one could deny that.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That said, fortune-telling wasn’t the same as the existence of a higher power. Fortune-tellers were just people, like you or me. Really, I couldn’t help but be skeptical.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do you understand?” Ibuki asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I still had lingering doubts, but I got the gist. I decided to make a suggestion.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The fortune-teller only offers readings to pairs, but they’re not only reading for romance, right?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, obviously.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, why don’t we go together? We’re both genuinely interested in fortune-telling. If we’re not in a relationship that might have future complications, I don’t think we have anything to worry about.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I honestly felt absolutely nothing for Ibuki. My emotions were a flat line, neither good nor bad. It was like dealing with a first-time customer in a store.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, I don’t mind. I would like to have my fortune read, after all. Are you okay with this?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even if Horikita were here, she’s only a friend.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s not what I meant. Some students still hold a bit of a grudge toward me because of what happened on the island.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ibuki was looking out for me, in a way. She was worried that, if my classmates saw us together, they might resent me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t think you really need to worry about that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ibuki craned her neck like she was puzzled. “I don’t understand.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If everyone at this school got along, then yes, what you did would be considered a massive moral violation. However, the school believes that ability is everything. Besides, the classes were competing against one another. Spying and sabotage were natural tactics to employ under the circumstances. Am I wrong?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But a lot of people operate based on feelings, rather than logic, and they won’t be convinced of that. Not everyone’s quite as mentally flexible.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t think people like that would be admitted to this school in the first place.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ibuki crossed her arms and looked deep in thought for a moment. “You’re surprisingly shameless, aren’t you?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“All I am is an unremarkable student. I have no interest in either trying to crawl my way up the ladder or being kicked off it. If working with a student like Horikita lets me coast by, then I’m lucky.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s not uncommon, though. Every student in this school has their eye on the special privileges that come with graduation. But no one foresaw that the school would make us compete like this, so I’m sure most students are bewildered.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Apparently, Class C students weren’t really that different from Class D. That probably meant that Ibuki, who’d caught Ryuuen’s eye early on, must be formidable. In fact, after her identity as the spy was discovered, Ibuki was at Ryuuen’s side on several occasions. She said that she was only helping him to make up for her failure, but it seemed like he trusted her, at least to some degree. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ibuki and I both got in line. The clerk I’d run into the other day came by once again to confirm that we were a pair, then handed us tickets. There were eight couples ahead of us.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Looks like we’ll be waiting for a while,” I sighed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If only one fortune-teller was available, Ibuki and I would have to wait longer than an hour, even if they managed the time efficiently. How would the two of us endure that? I probably couldn’t carry on a conversation for that long.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We’re only together to have our fortunes read, so we don’t really need to make pointless chit-chat, right?” Ibuki asked me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I guess you have a point,” I answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She’d picked up on what I was thinking. Good. I’d been saved a lot of hassle.</span></p>
<div style="page-break-before: always;"/>
<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">2.4</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"> </p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">“N</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">ext, please.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was the middle of the afternoon when I heard that small voice come out of the temporary tent.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry to have kept you waiting.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">In the end, fortune-telling for each pair took approximately fifteen minutes, which forced Ibuki and me to stand in line for even longer than anticipated. Just as I started to wonder if I even cared about fortune-telling anymore, I passed through a curtain and entered the room where the fortune-teller waited.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When I entered, I found a chamber that looked like something out of a television show. It was dark inside, probably around 30 lux. The fortune-teller appeared to be an elderly woman, but a hood obscured her face, so I couldn’t make out her expression. She sat with a thick book, the contents of which I couldn’t guess, and some kind of crystal ball. It looked like one of those balls you chuck around in the hammer throw in track and field. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This was excellent atmosphere.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The crystal ball immediately began to glow, as if it would reflect my and Ibuki’s futures. There were two backless seats in front of the fortune-teller. When we sat down, the fortune-teller chuckled lightly and moved her right hand.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“First…you must pay,” she commanded.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She pulled out a small card reader from under the table and placed it before us. Such a product of modern civilization felt out of place, especially in contrast to the scene around me. Not that I expected the reading to be free, of course.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What kind of fortune are you going to read?” asked Ibuki, presenting her student ID card. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It can be about your academics, career, love life, or anything you like,” the fortune-teller answered with an unsettling grin. The expression lent itself nicely to the atmosphere, but she seemed less like a fortune-teller, and more like a witch. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The list of prices she showed us seemed oddly mismatched. They were separated into several categories. The “Basic Plan” package seemed to include the services the fortune-teller had already mentioned. There were several packages, one of which seemed to be related to </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">tenchuusatsu</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">. There were even options that let you see the end of your life. Of course, since this was a couples’ activity, many options were related to romance.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wondered what a couple would do if the fortune-teller divined that they had poor romantic compatibility. Regardless, every option was rather expensive. We’d be spending well over 5,000 points.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s a lot of money,” I sighed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">For a Class D student like me, who struggled with points daily, this was a tough pill to swallow. Even so, it probably would’ve been pointless to go home without finding out more about </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">tenchuusatsu</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">. I could just listen to Ibuki’s fortune and head back, but then I wouldn’t know how reliable the fortune-teller was. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Just in case, I checked my private points balance on my phone. I had roughly 6,000 points remaining: barely enough.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll do the Basic Plan,” said Ibuki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Despite her unexpected interest in fortune-telling, she didn’t seem to want a detailed fortune.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“And you?” asked the fortune-teller.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll take the same,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It felt as though I was placing an order at a restaurant. I presented my student ID card, and the card reader beeped, indicating that it had deducted points. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Very well, let’s start with the young lady. What’s your name?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ibuki. Ibuki Mio,” she answered flatly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“When I tell fortunes, I see the face, the hand, and then the heart of the person I’m reading. I may see something that you won’t like. Are you prepared for that?” asked the fortune-teller.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Do whatever you want,” Ibuki replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I saw a bit of the fortune-teller’s wrinkled skin under her hood, along with the sharp gleam in her eye. She instructed Ibuki to extend both hands, then revealed her fortune.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“First, a palm reading. You have a long lifeline. You have a long, long life to look forward to. I cannot see you suffering any major illness so far,” the fortune-teller began.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">A typical start. I couldn’t imagine that a person could divine such things just by looking at the lines on someone’s palm. Maybe the fortune-teller based her readings off personal experiences?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If it were me, I’d simply base my answer on the customer’s apparent health. I’d come to a conclusion based on their complexion, build, and so on. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But the fortune-teller continued her reading at length, carefully providing Ibuki with predictions about academics, financial success, love, and so on. The predictions sounded ordinary and innocuous to me, but Ibuki listened with seeming satisfaction. The fortune-teller didn’t have anything particularly negative to say. Most of her predictions were of a bright future. She occasionally issued warnings, but they didn’t seem to pose a significant threat.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thank you very much,” said Ibuki. She bowed her head gratefully. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It now seemed to be my turn, a chance for me to understand this fortune-telling business. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The fortune-teller followed the same process she’d used with Ibuki, and the answers I received were largely indistinguishable from Ibuki’s. Things seemed fundamentally good, but the fortune-teller said that there would be a future time when I would need to be careful to avoid disaster.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see… It seems that you had a harsh childhood,” she continued.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">A rather broad statement. Most people would claim to have gone through one or two harsh experiences during their youth, especially boys. I wanted her to give me more concrete answers. More importantly, I found it mysterious that a fortune-teller, who should have been divining the future, was instead talking about the past.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ibuki sat without once interrupting, or even yawning, and listened intently. Perhaps this was what fortune-telling was supposed to be like. Or perhaps this was a necessary ritual? Maybe the fortune-teller needed to visit the past first. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Humans were creatures of convenience. Once people got the idea into their heads that they’d been promised “good luck,” they freely interpreted the next thing that went their way as a sign of their fortune coming true. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Ah, so my fortune was right,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> they would think. However, the reality was that everyone had “good luck” at some point, because life delivered both happiness and unhappiness in great and small amounts.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This is…” The fortune-teller stopped moving her hands. “You’re the holder of the fate </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">tenchuusatsu</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">!” she exclaimed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wha—? Seriously?” Ibuki balked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though it was my fortune, I was the least surprised person there. </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">Tenchuusatsu</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> wasn’t a word I’d even been aware of until the day before. The fortune-teller and Ibuki seemed way more shocked by this revelation. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“To put it simply, you’ve lived a life of constant misfortune since you were born,” Ibuki explained.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This is incredible!” the fortune-teller exclaimed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It might have been pure coincidence, but that claim was accurate. Still vague, though. Besides, from a cynical viewpoint, more than a few people would call themselves constantly unlucky. I supposed it </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">was</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> risky for the fortune-teller to make an unhappy prediction, though. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, is this fate </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">tenchuusatsu</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> going to apply to me from here on out?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The little girl wasn’t quite right when she said that the </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">tenchuusatsu</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> meant you lived a life of misfortune,” the fortune-teller replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Little girl?” Ibuki repeated, annoyed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Fate </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">tenchuusatsu</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> is certainly rare. However, that doesn’t necessarily mean that your entire life will be marked by misfortune. It’s true that the overall outlook is bad. There are negatives: you won’t have the blessing of your parents, or your family. However, the rest is up to you, individually. You alone decide what you can do and can’t do,” the fortune-teller explained.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She had a grim expression on her face, but I could see compassion in her eyes.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You don’t need to be pessimistic, and you don’t need to act like you’re starring in a comedy, either,” she continued.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I’d heard some interesting things today, but in the end, it was just fortune-telling. I wasn’t on the edge of my seat with anticipation. When I tried to get up, though, the fortune-teller stopped me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I have one more piece of advice for you. Go straight home, without taking any detours. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">What prophetic words.</span></p>

<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">2.5</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"> </p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 3.00em;">“S</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">o, how was your first fortune-telling experience?” Ibuki asked.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Fortune-telling was partly based on templates. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah, I see.” </span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Geh. It’s really crowded again,” I grumbled.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry, but I think I’m going to find another way home. I’ll take a detour,” I told Ibuki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, me too.”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That reminds me, earlier…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The fortune-teller said not to take any detours,” Ibuki said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">My eyes met Ibuki’s for an instant. Whether it was coincidence, or an inevitability, we decided to take the detour.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, this might be interesting. Let’s see how accurate her prediction was,” I mused.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is the first floor okay?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah,” Ibuki said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It seemed as though our paths would soon diverge. I pushed the button for the first floor. The elevator doors closed, and the car began to move. Since Ibuki and I had nothing else to talk about, we rode in silence. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, as the third-floor light blinked on, the elevator made a heavy grinding noise, then stopped.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is it a power outage?” asked Ibuki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Seems that way,” I replied. If this was what the fortune-teller meant about getting stuck, then she was right on the money.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Shouldn’t we use the emergency phone?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There was no need to panic. The elevator had measures in place in the event of a breakdown. Although I wasn’t really good at talking to people, I decided to push the call button.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s no response,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t know whether the phone on the other end was ringing or not, but I saw no indication that we were connecting to anyone at the emergency dispatch center.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is the call not going through because of the power outage?” Ibuki asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No. Elevators normally have a backup battery that lasts for several hours. The fact that the emergency lights are on right now is proof of that battery. I think this means there’s some other internal failure.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I pushed the button meant for the hearing-impaired, but got no response from that, either. Perhaps the control panel itself was dead. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Can you try calling the school from your phone? We should be in range,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m sorry, but would you do it?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I know you don’t want to talk to other people, but can’t you do that much?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ugh,” she muttered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">With a disgruntled look, Ibuki pulled out her phone. When she looked at it, her expression immediately worsened. She showed her screen to me. A message said that the battery was low. Immediately afterwards, it died.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t have anyone to talk to, so I don’t usually notice when my battery is about to die. You do it,” Ibuki grumbled.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Guess I got no choice,” I replied.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Apparently, our situation is far more serious than I originally thought.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">My phone’s battery was at only 4 percent. It was like a flickering candle flame that the wind could extinguish at any moment.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re messing with me,” Ibuki growled.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m the same as you. Since I don’t have a lot of people to talk to, I don’t really bother checking my battery life.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re such a useless man.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You know, you’re being kind of mean, even though we’re both in the exact same position. Okay, where to call now?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I looked around to see if I could find contact information for emergency services, and discovered a ten-digit number near the button panel. However, thanks to some jerk who probably thought they were hilarious, the last four digits were scribbled over with magic marker.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“A prank like this is evil,” I muttered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why don’t you call someone you know?” Ibuki asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Someone I know, huh?” Who would I call? “Maybe Horikita?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Rejected,” she countered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I thought you’d say that.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you call her, then that would mean she’d come to save us, right? Don’t make me laugh.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t think it really mattered who rescued us, though. Besides, it wasn’t as though Ibuki were somehow responsible for this. The elevator simply broke down. There was no reason for her to worry; maybe she just didn’t like the idea of showing weakness in front of her rival.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You don’t want things to get messy?” I guessed. Ibuki nodded slightly. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So, we needed someone who would come to our rescue without causing a scene, huh? That meant those three idiots were out of the question. They’d make a huge deal out of this, and probably tell everyone afterward. Sakura wouldn’t gossip, but the situation would probably fluster her. It’d be difficult for her to help us out.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kushida and Karuizawa were probably likewise unsuited to the task. Someone who would come help without causing any issues? The one person we could rely on was…that guy.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll respect your wishes, but let me choose the person we contact,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“As long as it’s not Horikita.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I called him right away. After the phone rang for a few seconds, the taciturn fellow quietly answered. I explained our situation and asked for help. However, not long after I started talking, my phone died. The screen silently faded to black.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Battery ran out,” I told Ibuki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Did he get the message?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Probably.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The only thing left to do was sit and wait. There was no need to panic—someone would definitely notice us before too long. If we tried to escape the elevator, like in a TV drama or movie, we’d just risk getting hurt. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Then, though, the situation evolved in an unexpected direction. I heard what sounded like loud grinding inside the elevator. The pleasantly cool air emanating from the vent stopped.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No way,” Ibuki groaned.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She finally began to look upset. We were trapped in an enclosed space in the middle of summer. The temperature would likely rise dramatically. The air had only gotten a little warmer for now, but soon, we’d be sweating.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Can we possibly escape on our own?” Ibuki asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Looks like there’s an emergency hatch, but…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Fewer elevators seemed to have hatches nowadays, but there was a square panel on the ceiling. You saw that kind of thing often in the movies, but in reality, it was unusual.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How are we supposed to get that open?” asked Ibuki. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">You couldn’t normally open the escape hatch from the inside. The hatch was probably kept locked from the outside, except during routine inspections.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I think we should wait. In an emergency situation in an elevator, the golden rule is to just wait it out.” That was the safest and surest path.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you can deal with being in a sauna, sure,” snapped Ibuki.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">While we snarked at each other, the temperature continued to rise. I understood wanting to get out, but I also wanted to avoid poor decisions. I took off my jacket and sat on the floor. At times like this, it was important to remain calm.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How about you sit, too? If it’s too hot, take off some clothes,” I offered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh? You can’t possibly be thinking something sleazy in this situation. Are you?” Ibuki asked. She’d interpreted my words in a different way than I intended. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I heard you kept up with Horikita. There’s no way I could defeat someone like you in combat,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, that’s true, but…” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Of course, I’ll turn around if you undress. Relax,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not taking my clothes off!” she snapped.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ibuki quickly sat down with a thud.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We waited quietly for about thirty minutes, but still hadn’t heard from anyone.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This isn’t good,” I muttered. Ibuki’s breathing grew ragged. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sweat poured down our foreheads and dripped from our hair. My shirt was so soaked that it looked like I had stood under a waterfall. The situation had gotten significantly more dangerous than I previously imagined. This elevator was installed into the Keyaki Mall’s external wall. I hadn’t noticed that before, because of the air conditioning, but it would get extremely hot under these conditions.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Children have died after being stuck in locked cars in the middle of summer, and the same danger can apply to adults, too. It felt like the two of us were about to suffer heatstroke.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Guess we’ve got no choice,” I muttered. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wasn’t about to roast to death in an elevator sauna. </span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Let’s try to open the hatch on top,” I suggested. The most important thing was to make an opening, even if we couldn’t escape through it. “The ceiling is about two meters up. Probably like 2.2 or 2.3…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even if I stretched as far as I could, I couldn’t reach that high.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Move,” Ibuki snapped.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I wasn’t particularly knowledgeable about elevators, but I’d picked up a few things. There was a way to cancel a command after you pushed a button for the wrong floor by mistake. The command differed, based on the elevator manufacturer, but if you kept pushing the cancel button, that could stop the elevator.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">When I continued to push the button for the second floor, the light went off.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There should be some commands in express mode, for sure,” I muttered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Express?”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t know whether this elevator had an express mode, though.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Let me try.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay.”</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I’d have preferred to get out of the elevator without resorting to violent behavior, but as long as we could escape, I didn’t really care about the means. </span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That seemed obvious. We likely couldn’t activate that mode through a typical combination of buttons.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, it might be better to exclude complex commands,” I said. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Let’s say that, to use express mode, you had to enter one, six, five, five, four, two, four, and then your destination. That would be difficult to memorize, and would have a height requirement of at least six floors. A code like that would be odd if your elevator only covered three floors.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We should try pushing the emergency button, don’t you think? So, then…one, two, or three? With the open and close buttons, that makes five buttons overall.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I think it’s some combination of those buttons,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If there were more combinations, it would’ve been extremely difficult to test them all. Ibuki started trying out the limited number of combinations. As I watched, I mentally crossed off the ones that didn’t work.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ah, I can’t take it! It’s so hot!” Ibuki whined.	 </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She punched the wall to relieve some frustration. Considering how on-edge she was, I decided to drop it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s not opening. Have we already tested everything?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Almost. If there are any button combinations left…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There was one possibility. I decided to try out one last command.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Why don’t you try pushing the button for your desired floor, plus the close-door button at the same time?” I suggested.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The close-door button? All right.” Ibuki muttered dismissively, but she tested the combination. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I didn’t expect the elevator to respond, but it started to move. Ibuki and I looked at each other. After a few seconds, the elevator arrived on the first floor, and the doors slowly opened. Cool air flowed inside the car. We found two adults looking at us, wearing shocked expressions.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you all right?! Are you hurt?!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We’re okay, we’re not hurt. It was just really hot,” I answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Looking at our sweaty state, they could probably guess how hot it was. They offered us sports drinks right away, and instructed us to head over to the doctor’s office for examination, just in case.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Um, can I ask one question? Could it be that we moved the elevator?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, no, we moved it directly from here.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">A special remote control operated the elevator from the first floor. Apparently, Ibuki and I hadn’t caused the elevator to move using express mode, or anything. We just happened to push the buttons at the exact same time the adults operated the elevator remotely.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You must have had a rough time in there.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah, it was a disaster. Fortune-telling is serious business,” Ibuki said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As I thanked the adults, a man watching from the sidelines approached us. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Are you all right, Ayanokouji?” the large man asked. He seemed more anxious than someone that size should.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You rescued us,” I told him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This man, Katsuragi, was probably the one who’d saved the day. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I got the gist of the situation from the information you gave me over the phone. I’m sure you must be glad to be down,” he replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I have to head to the doctor’s office now, but I’ll thank you properly later,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s no need. You and Sudou have helped me a lot. There are some lines we can’t cross, because we’re from different classes, but I happen to think cooperation’s an excellent thing,” answered Katsuragi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sounds like things went well for you,” I said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yeah. Sudou performed brilliantly. Please tell him once again that I’m very appreciative.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sure thing.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I should thank you as well, Ayanokouji. Despite the plentiful evidence, there must have been considerable resistance to the plan I proposed.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Katsuragi bowed his head in gratitude. I owed him, too. Had I been stuck in that elevator any longer, I might have lost it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you need anything else, please contact me. I’ll assist you with anything…other than exams, of course.” Katsuragi chuckled. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">With that, he turned and left.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Katsuragi and I had started to become real friends. We were as close as I was to the three idiots, maybe even more so. But how did I know Katsuragi’s contact information, even though he was in Class A? And why were we close?</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The story of our friendship had begun a little while ago.</span></p>
