<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">A</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">LMOST EVERY STUDENT</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> in Class C had decided to meet up in the classroom during our lunch break that same day. The students who didn’t bring their own lunches to school were expected to head to the store to get one and then come right back to meet up with everyone in the classroom. I was one of those students, so I left the classroom right away, made my way to a location with no one else around, and messaged two specific people from my contacts.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was able to connect with the first person immediately, since I’d sent them a message on my phone ahead of time. That left the other person. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Once I was done taking care of business, I finished my shopping and returned to the classroom. There were two students who hadn’t returned. One of them was Kouenji Rokusuke, a man who couldn’t be tied down by anyone. The other was Hirata Yousuke. Those two aside, there were thirty-seven students gathered here.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It seems like Hirata-kun isn’t participating after all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Looks that way.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Although some people sounded worried, the clock was ticking. The more time we could spend jointly discussing the events, the better. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Pfft, ‘turn over a new leaf’ he says! In the end, that dude ain’t takin’ this seriously at all!” huffed Sudou.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I could understand why he’d raise his voice in anger. Alternatively, I was sure some students had thought Kouenji might have started taking things seriously, at least on the surface. Reality wasn’t so kind—or rather, I supposed, people didn’t change that easily. Kouenji would probably continue to wiggle out of things, slippery as ever, getting by on vague, half-hearted words. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But I couldn’t imagine such an approach would work forever. Sooner or later, there would probably be another exam like the In-Class Voting test. When that time came, Kouenji would have to pay the price.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Let’s just forget ’im and get this show on the road already, shit.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“He’s not worth getting all riled up over. Now then, I’ve gone ahead and made copies of the manual explaining the events that was provided to us. I’ll distribute those copies to all of you. Read it carefully as you eat your lunch. I&#160;was intending for us to discuss the specifics in detail after class,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Now that there was no one else to take charge, Horikita had no choice but to step in and lead the way.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If there’s something you don’t understand, please feel free to come to me with your questions at any time, even while I’m eating.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It seemed she’d already thoroughly perused the manual and had no lingering questions, herself.</span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">4.1</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">C</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">LASSES HAD ENDED</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> without incident that day. Afterward, Chabashira had told us to send whoever we’d decided would be commander out into the hallway, and stepped out of the classroom. Hirata immediately got up from his seat after she left. One of the girls in class, Nishimura, hurriedly tried to call out to him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Um… Well, about the events, we thought we’d discuss—” she began, trailing off once she saw that Hirata just quietly walked right out of the classroom, her words failing to reach him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hirata-kun…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Nishimura and the others could plainly see the intense, brooding aura of depression surrounding Hirata. The only exception was Kouenji, who remained nonchalantly looking down at his phone, as if completely unaware of this series of disturbances.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I… I’m going to use the restroom. I’ll be right back,” said Wang Mei-Yu, whom everyone called Mii-chan, rising from her seat. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So she said, but she was probably going to chase after Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, seeing as how he’s not being of any use, I suppose I have no choice but to step up and do it myself,” said Horikita, taking the initiative and preparing to head up to the podium.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Sorry, but I’ll leave the discussion part to you. I have commander stuff to take care of,” I told her. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s fine. I suppose we’ll find out who we’re going up against once you get to the multi-purpose room. If you have a choice, choose Class D,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I understand. But don’t expect too much from me,” I&#160;replied, standing up.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Since I was the person who’d taken on the role of commander, I exited the classroom and stepped out into the hallway.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh, it’s you, Ayanokouji. Who on earth did you guys pick as your commander?” asked Chabashira, letting out an exasperated sigh as she looked in the direction that Mii-chan and Hirata had disappeared.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Me. I’m the commander,” I answered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Oh?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira and I made our way to the special building together.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“We have to go all the way over to the special building just to pick out which classes are facing each other?” I&#160;asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’ll also be receiving an explanation as to how things work on the day of the exam,” said Chabashira.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There was hardly anyone in the special building, so my ears picked up the sound of our footsteps much easier.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You went through such an ordeal to get a Protection Point, only to be forced into being the commander. That’s rough.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wasn’t forced into it. I volunteered willingly,” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira stopped walking. “…You did?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is something wrong with that?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I thought you hated attracting attention?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It all came down to whether or not I’d passively accept the role.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. So, one way or another, you were put in a situation where you couldn’t say no.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ultimately, it was much easier for the student who had the Protection Point to become the commander. If that student refused to be the commander, it meant only one person would be safe. The only difference in this situation was whether you let yourself be pushed off that cliff, or took the plunge and jumped on your own initiative.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But no matter how you wound up in the role, being the commander brings with it a great deal of responsibility. If you cut any corners, it’ll spell defeat for Class C,” said Chabashira.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Since there was no else around, she spoke more forcefully.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Was that a threat?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">As I turned to look at her, she gave me a slight smile.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You can interpret what I said however you like. But I am looking forward to it, Ayanokouji. Now I can finally see what you’re capable of.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Chabashira had her heart set on reaching Class A. That part of her seemed to be expecting a lot of me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s no guarantee I’ll win.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You think so? Sorry, but I for one can’t possibly imagine you losing,” she told me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After that, we were mostly silent as we made our way to the special building.</span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">4.2</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">T</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HE MULTI-PURPOSE ROOM</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> was located in the special building. Apparently, it was going to be something of a central hub for this exam.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The other three students have already arrived,” said Chabashira.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The door to the multi-purpose room opened, and I immediately caught sight of the teachers and students representing the other classes. I saw Sakayanagi from Class A, Ichinose from Class B, and Kaneda from Class D. As you might expect, they were all students who had Protection Points. Then I saw two computers, stationed facing each other, each connected to a large monitor. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, now that we have the commanders from each class all gathered together, we’d like to determine which classes will be facing off against each other. We’re going to have you each draw one slip of paper from this box. The student who draws the slip of paper with the red circle on it will be given the right to choose their opponent,” said Mashima-sensei, presenting us with the box with the raffle slips in them.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He urged Class A to draw first, but Sakayanagi refused.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“They say good things come to those who wait. I don’t mind going last. You may go ahead, Ichinose-san,” said Sakayanagi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well then, don’t mind if I do,” said Ichinose, drawing a slip of paper.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After her came Class C, followed by Class D. Since the slips of paper weren’t folded up, we understood the results almost immediately after drawing. Kaneda from Class D was the one who drew the slip with the red mark. Which meant that Class D had won the right to choose their opponent.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It would seem there’s no need for me to check what’s on the last slip of paper then, is there, Mashima-sensei?” said Sakayanagi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Mashima-sensei</span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;"> </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">drew the remaining slip of paper from the box himself. Naturally, it didn’t have a red circle on it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It seems good things didn’t come to those who waited, after all,” he told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not so sure about that. It’s not necessarily good luck to be the one to draw it,” said Sakayanagi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I wonder if that means Class A can really afford to take it easy, no matter who they go up against?” said Ichinose.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh no, that’s not it at all. If possible, I’d like to avoid facing your class, Ichinose-san,” replied Sakayanagi. It was hard to tell whether she was just being polite or genuinely meant it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Please tell us which class you’ll be choosing,” said Mashima-sensei, urging Kaneda to answer. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kaneda responded with a subtle nod. Class D had probably held their own discussions in the morning and after class, deciding which class they’d have the best chance of winning against. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Then I’ll come right out and say it. Class D wishes…to go up against Class B,” announced Kaneda, making a declaration of war on an unexpected opponent.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re sure you want to choose Class B?” asked Mashima-sensei, seeking confirmation of Kaneda’s decision.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes,” he replied firmly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">After making sure, Mashima-sensei finalized the match-ups. If Class D was going up against Class B, it naturally followed that Class A would be going up against Class C.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’d thought for certain you’d go for Class C, but you chose Class B. Why?” asked Sakayanagi, pressing Kaneda for the reason behind his decision.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If we’re to turn our situation around, we’ll need to take as many points as we can away from the higher-level classes. That being said, we’d like to avoid fighting Class A right now,” said Kaneda.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So, having deemed Class A to be an understandably difficult opponent, they opted for Class B. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I see. Well, as far as I’m concerned, this means that you’ve saved me the trouble of facing the powerful foe that is Class B, and for that, I’m grateful. I wish you in Class D the best of luck in your efforts,” said Sakayanagi.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">She gave Kaneda a slight bow of gratitude, but I couldn’t help but be aware of a bit of trickery involved in getting us to this point. Of course, the fact that Kaneda had won the right to choose was entirely coincidental, but the results would’ve been the same no matter who drew the winning slip. I had contacted Ichinose and Ishizaki ahead of time, before classes had ended for the day, telling them I wanted them to back off and let me handle Class A. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ichinose seemed to genuinely want to go up against Class A herself, but she’d agreed to let me handle it, as a way of paying me back. And apparently, Ishizaki and the rest of Class D had already planned to pick Class B anyway, so that was fine. All for the sake of arranging a confrontation with Sakayanagi and Class A.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The only problem would’ve been if I had been the one who won the lottery. Horikita had specifically told me to choose Class D, so I would’ve had to come up with an excuse if that happened. A one-in-four chance wasn’t worth worrying about, though. Basically, this entire lottery had been fixed. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Besides, I was sure Sakayanagi knew I’d been laying the groundwork for this outcome. And so, all the match-ups had been determined ahead of time.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Now then, I’ll explain the system you’ll be using on the day of the special exam. During the exam, you’ll be in the multi-purpose room, using a computer like the two that you see set up here. You will be fulfilling your role as commander here, assigning which student will be in which event, all in real time,” explained Mashima-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The left-hand computer’s screen was projected on the large monitor. While Chabashira operated the computer, Mashima-sensei continued explaining.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This is a list of students in Class A. Using the mouse, you’ll drag and drop the selected student’s profile picture onto the box for a specific event. If you make a mistake or if you wish to reconsider your choice partway through, you can use the mouse to drag the student’s profile picture outside of the box, and then re-select. Or use your finger to operate it via the touchscreen,” said Mashima-sensei.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s kind of like a video game, isn’t it?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It really is!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Ichinose and Hoshinomiya-sensei were engaged in their own fun conversation.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There is a time limit on the student selection for each event, represented by the number you currently see counting down on the screen. The more participants required for an event, the more time you’ll be given to choose. You can expect about 30 seconds per person,” he added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Which meant we’d have three hundred seconds for a ten-person event.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Please note that if you don’t make your selections within the time limit, the remaining spaces will be filled with students chosen at random. Moreover, if you end up selecting too many students for an event, excess participants will be discarded via random selection, as well.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So basically, those limits were iron-clad.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Once the games begin, the action will be shown on the large monitor in real-time.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">A sample video of a shogi match began to play on the monitor. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Information describing how the commander can participate in the match will be displayed on your personal monitor once the match begins.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The image on the large monitor switched back to displaying what was on the left computer screen. The words </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“The commander can pause the game and redo a move once” </span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">were displayed on the screen, probably an example of how the commander could get involved in a particular event, just as Mashima-sensei had just explained.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Please keep in mind that you can confirm the details of these rules and activate them by clicking them.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The large monitor went back to showing the shogi match.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Also, instructions from the commanders to their teammates are relayed not via phone calls, but as texts, which are automatically read aloud via a text-to-speech system. All you have to do is type out the words and press enter, and the message will be played through the participant’s headset.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">So our messages would automatically be read aloud by a machine, huh? Probably to keep us from spreading misinformation or revealing more than what was allowed. Using the shogi game currently on the screen as an example, though the rule stated that the commander could only get involved by pausing the game and redoing a move once, clever wording would make it possible for the commander to basically give the participant instructions for two or three moves.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If the commander deviates from the established rules and involves themselves more than what is allowed, the school may disqualify them for breaking the rules.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I supposed that made sense. It was safe to assume every message the commanders sent out was being reviewed by a third party.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Only one participant will be wearing a headset per event. Even in a team event, that means only one person will be able to receive instructions. The commander will also specify which participant will wear the headset.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It seemed I had my work cut out for me. There were things we could decide beforehand, but we still needed to prepare for the unexpected. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The commander can issue instructions whenever they wish, so long as it is in accordance with the rules.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">We could freely change the display on our own screen, including switching displays, maximizing or minimizing windows, and so on. There were more than a few things we could keep an eye on, from observing the students participating in the current event to preparing for the next event.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thus concludes my explanation of the duties of the commander and the systems at play. Are there any questions?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Mashima-sensei looked around, but it seemed no one had any questions.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That will be all for today, then. In the event you wish to review the operating system, you may return to the multi-purpose room, accompanied by a teacher, up to one week before the exam. That is all.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">And so, having heard how the commander position would work, we dispersed.</span></p>

<p class="P_TEXTBODY_CENTERALIGN_PAGEBREAK"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.27em;">4.3</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;"> </span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">HEADED BACK</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> to my dorm room, texted Horikita to tell her which class we were up against, then immediately started thinking about my duties as commander. Come to think of it, this was my first time tackling one of this school’s exams head-on. To be completely honest, I didn’t really think I could lose a one-on-one fight. But in this exam, I’d be waging war by commanding the entire&#160;class.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I could only fight while staying within the limits of my class’s abilities. Even an unparalleled strategist like Sun Tzu wouldn’t stand a chance of winning if he led an army of children against an army of full-grown adults. Though the commanders’ ability to get involved in the events was key, there were some fundamental things I needed to know before we went into this fight.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">One of those things was an understanding of Class C’s current potential. Who did they like and dislike? What were they good at and what did they struggle with? I&#160;couldn’t find the path to victory unless I understood how to group my classmates together.
