<p class="P__STAR__STAR__STAR__page_break" id="auto_bookmark_toc_top"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.17em;">Chapter 7:<br/>A Man’s Tears</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">W</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">E’D MANAGED</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> to get our hands on some intel through our little chat with Katsuragi. But it wasn’t like Class C had the upper hand. Horikita, well aware of where we stood, was trying to address our concerns one by one.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wait a minute, Hirata-kun,” she said, calling out to Hirata as he stood up at his desk when class ended, about to head back to the dorm. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He was the first student to try to leave. This was also the first time she had called out to him since after the in-class voting exam. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata simply stopped in his tracks but didn’t turn around to face her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I imagine you don’t want to talk to me, so allow me to just confirm one thing. You will not be participating in any of the events Class C chooses. And there are no plans to have you in play on the day of the exam. But things may change, depending on the situation. Sakayanagi-san understands what’s going on with you, so we must assume it’s possible she’ll throw in multiple events that require quite a few people to participate,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">No matter how much Class C tries to accommodate Hirata, there was still the possibility that all thirty-eight students would have to participate.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If that time comes, what will you do? Will you apathetically drag your feet? Or will you put in the bare minimum required of you? I’d like you to answer me that much,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">However, Hirata didn’t answer. A heavy silence filled the classroom. Then, as we felt time start to move forward again, Hirata started walking away.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“So, you can’t even give me an answer, huh?” huffed Horikita, exasperated. Fed up, she averted her gaze, as if to say she had given up.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Hey, uh, maybe we…aren’t gonna win in the end anyway after all… With Hirata-kun being like this, I mean,” said one of the girls in class, her voice filled with concern.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The boys probably felt the same way. The guy who’d been pulling the class forward was absent, and his absence continued to place a heavy burden on Class C’s shoulders, time and time again.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You told me that getting Hirata back to normal depended on the efforts of those around him. But in the end, nothing has changed,” said Horikita, addressing me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m not so sure.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Huh…?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Horikita looked up with a puzzled expression on her face, but she and I were looking in different places.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hirata-kun! Wait!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I had no idea how many times Mii-chan had cried out like that by now. She hurriedly grabbed her bag and chased after him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Mii-chan still hasn’t given up,” I told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll never begin to understand why not,” huffed Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You have work to do, Horikita. Keep Class C together and improve our chances.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">No one in our class could do that right now, except for Horikita. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I followed after Mii-chan, and found her standing face to face with Hirata on the path leading back to the dormitory. This definitely wasn’t some bittersweet confession of love, though. She was going on the offensive to get her classmate, Hirata, back on his feet.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Please, Hirata-kun. We need your help, Hirata-kun…so.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Mii-chan, please, enough. Can you please just leave me alone already…?” Hirata grumbled heavily. He was probably wondering how many times he was going to have to repeat himself. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">His sharp words cut like a knife. I was sure they pierced her heart deeply. However, the determination in her eyes showed no signs of wavering. Even if he pushed her away again and again, Mii-chan continued to persist.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I-I can’t leave you alone… Not when you’re like this, Hirata-kun. I can’t,” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, how can I get you to? Please tell me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, um, by going back to how you used to be, Hirata-kun…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Back to how I used to be? I can’t.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">His cold words rained down on Mii-chan over and over, without mercy.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, that’s not true! I-I believe that you can, Hirata-kun!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I already told you, I can’t. I don’t want you to believe in me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Even so, I do!” she shouted.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata clenched his fist. Depending on how the conversation went, it felt like he might even throw a punch.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Okay, then bring Yamauchi-kun back,” he told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wha…?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s what it’ll take for things to go back to normal.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yamauchi had been expelled. He was never coming back to our class. And in the same way, Hirata was never going back to how he used to be, either. That was the truth he was trying to convey to Mii-chan.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I really wish you could have understood that before you came to talk to me,” said Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He turned his back to her and tried to walk away. But Mii-chan reflexively reached out and grabbed his right arm, holding him back—because if Hirata made it back to his dorm room, then it would mean she’d been unable to do anything to help him today, either.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Will you let me go?” he asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I-I won’t!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Despite the fact that Hirata had rejected her, Mii-chan firmly held her ground. She believed her feelings would surely reach him if she did so. I continued to watch the scene unfold some distance away, deciding I shouldn’t carelessly get too close and get in Mii-chan’s way.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata let out an obvious sigh. Then he raised his right arm quickly and shook it as hard as he could to break free of Mii-chan’s grasp. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Kyah!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It was a forceful move, completely unlike Hirata. The momentum unintentionally caused Mii-chan to fall to the ground. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Leave me alone already. If you don’t… I… I…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Mii-chan looked up at Hirata. His gaze, filled with anger, wounded her once more.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I have nothing else left to lose. If you keep hanging around me…” said Hirata, his words trailing off.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He was about to bring the hammer down on Mii-chan. To say something so devastating that all the things he’d said and done so far wouldn’t even begin to compare. But then, at the last minute, a lone man walked past me. His blond hair fluttered in the wind and he smelled of cologne.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“My, my. It would seem you’re dithering again today, hm? And you’re showing everyone such an unseemly side of yourself,” teased Kouenji, trying to get a rise out of Hirata. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He typically went straight back to the dorms after class, too. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, now, don’t worry about me. Carry on with what you were doing. I’ll simply watch,” said Kouenji.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata wasn’t foolish enough to continue what he was doing after hearing that. Instead, he directed his hostility at the man who was getting in his way. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Is there…something you want from me…?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Want? There’s nothing I want. I already have everything, after all,” replied Kouenji, walking past Hirata and Mii-chan. “But…hmm. Come to think of it, if I had to name something I wanted from you, it would be…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">This was just something he’d happened to stumble into on his way back to the dorm. Nothing more and nothing less. Hirata’s feelings meant nothing to him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re an eyesore, so could you please go ahead and remove yourself from my sight? If this is no longer your ideal school, then why don’t you just drop out?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">How very like Kouenji. He was essentially telling Hirata that he might as well just leave if he was going to keep hemming and hawing like this.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Shut up. You have no idea how I feel,” said Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t know, and I don’t care. However, I can make a few guesses. You’re going to tell me that you can’t simply drop out because it would cause trouble for your classmates, hm? What nonsense.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“P-please stop, Kouenji-kun! Hirata-kun hasn’t done anything wrong!” shouted Mii-chan, standing up, trying to stop Kouenji’s relentless verbal assault on Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Oh ho. It would seem what I said displeases you. My apologies.” Despite the smirk on his face, Kouenji treated Mii-chan with a certain degree of respect. “However, the sooner you forget about Hirata Boy here, the better. He’s already beyond help.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata, who had already been nearing his limit for a while, opened his eyes and closed in on Kouenji.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“N-no, don’t, Hirata-kun!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Mii-chan, sensing something was obviously wrong here, put herself between the two of them. But Hirata pushed her out of the way more forcefully than when he shook her off before, then reached for Kouenji without even looking at her. He tried to grab Kouenji’s collar with his right hand but was quickly intercepted by Kouenji’s left. Kouenji held Hirata’s wrist firmly.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Gr!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Understand, I show no mercy to anyone who comes after me. I don’t want anyone to mar my beautiful face,” said Kouenji. He tightened his grip on Hirata’s wrist, causing an expression of pain and anger to appear on Hirata’s face.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Enough already! God, you’re so annoying, Kouenji…!” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re free to do as you please. But I don’t need to be told what to do by someone who makes a girl cry,” said Kouenji.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He looked over at Mii-chan, who was sitting on the ground. Then he let go of Hirata’s wrist and spoke once more.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You knocked her down. Shouldn’t you extend your hand to her?”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…This has nothing to do with me.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Nothing to do with you, hm? My, aren’t you cruel.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Mii-chan averted her eyes from Hirata, unable to look directly at him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, no matter. You’re free to do as you please, Hirata Boy.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wh-wh-what?!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Kouenji gallantly swooped down and picked Mii-chan up. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, since you’re not going to do anything, I suppose I’ll take care of her myself,” he said.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting__And__Page_Break"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata and Mii-chan were both stunned by this newest move from Kouenji, already someone whose actions no one could ever predict. </span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">I</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;"> </span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">DECIDED TO LEAVE</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> the dorm and look for Hirata. <p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">His heart was on the verge of breaking. Hirata thought our entire class should share everything, both the highs and the lows. He couldn’t bear to see anyone suffer, to see anyone disappear. I was sure he’d been asking himself these questions on loop. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It wasn’t clear how much he’d opened up to Mii-chan or to other students. However, I was sure I knew what sort of things they’d said back to him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“There was nothing you could have done.” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“You didn’t do anything wrong, Hirata-kun.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">“Yamauchi was the one who messed up by betraying the class.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was sure that no matter who he asked, they’d tell him that Hirata was good and the other person was evil. That was never going to change. But, as a result, this problem remained unsolved. It was pointless to tell Hirata to blame someone when he was trying to protect them. If anything, that would just make him retreat further back into his shell.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“There’s something that I want to make explicitly clear. It’s not Horikita’s fault that Yamauchi got expelled, and it wasn’t my fault, either. Do you understand that?” I&#160;asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Yeah. I know there was no avoiding it. There was nothing any of us could do,” said Hirata, before adding that he didn’t blame me either, in a quiet voice.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I probably sounded like I was reminding him it wasn’t my fault for what happened. It might have even sounded like I was asking him if he resented me.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Whose fault do you think it is that Yamauchi got kicked out of our class? Out of school?” I asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I would think…that it could be nobody but himself, I guess,” concluded Hirata, saying something that he didn’t want to admit.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Yamauchi had reaped what he had sown. He had been expelled as a result of his lack of skill and the choices he made. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, that’s wrong.” I rejected his answer. I flatly denied Hirata’s naïve way of thinking. “It’s your fault that Yamauchi got expelled, Hirata.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I…!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He looked up at me. The look on his face told me he couldn’t understand what I just said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you really wanted to save him, then you should’ve done everything you possibly could,” I added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“B-but… I, I tried…! There wasn’t anything else I could have done!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Ichinose from Class B hasn’t lost a single person.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s, but, that’s because she’s a special case. We didn’t have a huge number of Private Points, so I couldn’t do what she did!” he exclaimed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“In that case, the problem is you not leading the class like she did. You should have been saving points all through the year, like Ichinose did, so you could save someone if they were about to be expelled,” I told him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">If he had done so, Yamauchi wouldn’t have been expelled, and we would have still had forty people in our class.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But that’s impossible. We lost our class points almost immediately after we started school here. And even if we hadn’t, there’s no way that our classmates would agree to something like that,” said Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“The fact that we’re at zero class points and the fact that you failed to guide the class into becoming one that would comply with a plan like that are both your fault,” I&#160;told him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">No matter how much Hirata tried to run, the fact he was at fault wouldn’t change.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But that’s absurd! It’s completely ridiculous.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, it is absurd. But there’s no getting around it. You </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">chose</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> to take this path. You should have just kept this fantasy of saving everyone to yourself. If you had, then no one could blame you, Hirata, no matter who got expelled. But if you continue to feel this way about the people around you, then you </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">will</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> take all the blame when you fail. You need to be prepared for that,” I told him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“B-But I…!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I was wrong about you. I thought you were an honors student. A man of character who had the respect of many of his classmates. But that’s not who you are. You’re just a cheap, incompetent sham, who does nothing but talk a big game about stuff he can’t even do,” I told him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Of course, this was just me following an extreme argument to its conclusion. Hirata was by no means incompetent at all. He was an exceptionally talented person, and such a good student that it was hard to imagine he was just a first-year. There was nothing wrong with him saying that he wanted to protect people. And it wasn’t like he was to blame just because he’d failed to do so.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">But even so, I blamed him. I blamed him completely and entirely, putting pressure on him, pushing him persistently until he was about to break apart. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Was I doing this because of Hirata? No. Was I doing this so I could empower him to protect everyone? No. There was no way he could protect everyone. I was sure someone else would be expelled in the future. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I was doing this because, when that time came, Hirata was one of the components we needed to keep the class functioning well.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“How long are you going to sit around and daydream?” I asked him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata hadn’t taken a single step forward since he was back in compulsory education, in junior high. High school was a place where students came of their own volition. A place where students made their own decisions about their education.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“This… This is what…you’re really like, isn’t it? Your words are so terrifying, merciless, and cold…” said Hirata. Tears welled up in one of his eyes, then the other. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You’re free to wish for whatever you want. But if you truly desire something, you </span><span style=" font-style: italic; font-size: 1.00em;">have</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> to keep fighting for it to the bitter end, to push yourself to your limits. If people get expelled in the process, you have no choice but to accept that. No choice but to keep moving forward,” I told him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“That’s…cruel.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“If you stop right now, the students around you will disappear, one by one. Which is exactly why, if you keep your eyes trained on the goal and keep moving forward, when all is said and done and you do reach the end, there will be lots of students standing right behind you.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It took a lot of courage to take the lead and walk ahead of everyone else. You never knew when an obstacle might block your path and bring you down.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But…how do I let myself be vulnerable and say what’s bothering me…? Do I have to keep everything to myself and walk forward alone?” he asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, you don’t. When you’re in trouble, you can rely on your classmates. Horikita, Kushida, Sudou, Ike, Mii-chan, Shinohara. It doesn’t matter who. You can show your vulnerability and vent your frustrations to anyone you want to rely on. It doesn’t matter who’s leading the pack or who’s following. We’re all in this together.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">There was no rule that said the person leading the way couldn’t show weakness. The people standing behind them could lend a hand when they faltered. Our classmates would be more than willing to let Hirata be vulnerable and listen to his problems.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“It’s all right. It’s all right for you to take the lead,” I&#160;told him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">I patted him on the shoulder. With that small impact, more and more tears began to pour from Hirata’s eyes. He was burying the past for good. He’d set down the heavy burden he’d been carrying all this time. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata, who’d been trapped in place, was now able to stand up.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Thank you… Thank you, Ayanokouji-kun…”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata hung his head low, tears streaming down his face. Men are difficult, frustrating creatures who won’t let others see them cry except in special circumstances. Which was exactly why I, too, wanted the kind of friendship where I could shed tears in front of someone. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">No more words needed to be said. All he needed was a friend at his side, someone who he could be vulnerable with and who would listen. By doing that…he could begin walking forward again.</span></p>

<p class="P_Prose_Formatting">&#160;</p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-weight: bold; font-size: 2.40em;">N</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">IGHT CAME AND WENT</span><span style=" font-size: 0.80em;">,</span><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;"> and the next day arrived. The final special exam of the school year was just around the corner. When I arrived at school, there was no sign of Hirata in the classroom. Mii-chan looked gloomy, as expected. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Even though everyone in class had tried to push Hirata to the back of their minds, they were still worried about him. But then—the man who Class C so desperately needed showed up. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">And people were reluctant to even look at him.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“G-Good morning…Hirata-kun,” said Mii-chan.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Sure enough, she called out to him before anyone else did. She pushed aside her sadness and tried her hardest to put on a smile. Seeing that, Hirata approached her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Wha—!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Mii-chan froze for a moment, perhaps remembering what happened yesterday. When Hirata saw her, he bowed deeply, as low as he could possibly go.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Good morning. And also, I’m sorry about yesterday. I&#160;was really horrible to you, Mii-chan,” said Hirata, delivering an emotionally charged apology.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…Huh?” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“You were always, always trying to be there for me, to talk to me, and I ignored you. I’m so sorry,” he added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“B-but, well, I just…” she stammered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">It wasn’t just Mii-chan who was completely floored by Hirata’s clear change in behavior. Everyone in class was bewildered.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Good morning…everyone!” said Hirata. He wore such a bright smile that everything before today felt like a lie.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“H-Hirata-kun?” said Mii-chan, puzzled.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’m all right. I’m all right now,” he replied, reassuring Mii-chan with a gentle smile on his face, before bowing to everyone else. Still bowed, he continued, “I understand it might be too late for me to apologize by this point, but…if everyone is all right with it, I would like to contribute to the class however I can, starting today.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">The guys and girls of Class C exchanged looks. Several seconds passed as they struggled to understand what had just happened. And then…</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Hirata-kun!”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">First, some of the girls in class rushed over toward Hirata’s side. They were followed by both guys and girls. Not a single person was unhappy to see Hirata’s long-awaited return.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“What happened?” asked Horikita, who had been watching from afar, still unable to get a grasp on the situation.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I told you that it would depend on the efforts of everyone around him, didn’t I?” </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, yes, you did but… You don’t think he’s just forcing himself to act like that, or anything?” she asked.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Does it look that way to you?” I replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“No, it doesn’t, I suppose.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Everyone processes things differently. Some might get over it quickly, while others might need more time. Some people are even capable of going right back to normal the day after a big fight, and getting along just fine again,” I&#160;told her.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was just how human relationships worked. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Hirata, after being welcomed back with open arms, approached Horikita last. “Good morning, Horikita-san,” he said, looking directly at her with clear, honest eyes.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Y-yes, good morning.” Horikita seemed shaken, perhaps overwhelmed by how radiant he seemed.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I don’t think that I was wrong in the class trial incident the other day,” said Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“…I see.”</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“But…I don’t think what you did was wrong, either. Or&#160;rather, what I should say is that you did the right thing,” said Hirata.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">That was something he hadn’t been able to face at the time. But now, Hirata had resolved his own issues and come to accept it.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I just hadn’t realized it at the time,” he added.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Did you hit your head? Your way of thinking seems completely different from how it was just yesterday. And it doesn’t seem to me like you’re trying to put on a brave face or anything, but…” said Horikita, trailing off.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">Despite her doubts, Hirata simply gave her an untroubled smile.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I’ll do my utmost to regain the trust that I lost. I&#160;would like for you to give me the details of the special exam later,” he said.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“I understand. After you’ve gotten a grasp on the situation, I’ll test you to see whether or not you can really be of use. Is that all right with you?” she replied.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Yes, of course,” he answered, stretching out his hand. </span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">He was offering a handshake, asking for reconciliation. Horikita accepted. Afterward, Hirata was approached by his classmates once again, one after another. The classroom had become such a bright and cheerful place that it was hard to believe it had been submerged in gloom just a few minutes ago.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Well, maybe this means that we’re finally ready to face the special exam,” said Horikita.</span></p>
<p class="P_Prose_Formatting"><span style=" font-size: 1.00em;">“Looks like it.” One might say Hirata’s return was the greatest boon Class C could have received at this point. </span></p>
